The attached records.md holds the full saved text of every record, each between `===== RECORD <id>` and `===== END RECORD <id> =====`.

For each claim, find each passage in its record and read around it as far as you need: who is speaking, what a number counts, what date it refers to. Then decide whether the record supports the claim as worded, with its status and scope.

The status says what kind of claim it is: fact (it happened, or is so), said (someone stated it; the claim names who), plans (an actor intends it), may (it is possible, offered, optional or signalled, not done), expected (others forecast or schedule it), alleged (an accusation; the claim names who makes it).

Report a claim when:
- the record does not state it, or states it differently: a date, number, actor or order of events that does not match;
- its status is wrong: a possibility or intention stated as done, a statement stated as fact, an accusation stated as fact, an optional step stated as required;
- its scope is wider than the record's: one meeting stated as always, the reports made public stated as all reports, a figure as of one date stated as current;
- a quotation in it differs from the record's words, or is given to the wrong speaker;
- its short form, if it has one, says more than the claim.

A claim marked "checked strictly" carries the story: its status and scope must match the record exactly. A claim marked "checked for a reader's takeaway" is background: report it only when a reader would come away believing something the record does not support.

Do not report: a faithful paraphrase; punctuation inside or around a quotation; a quotation that ends where the record's sentence continues, when the meaning is unchanged.

For each finding: the claim's id, its words, the record and its exact words, and what a reader would wrongly believe. Do not suggest wording; the reporter writes the fix.

End with exactly two lines. First: `CHECKED: <n> claims`, counting the claims you checked. Then: CLAIMS HOLD or CLAIMS DO NOT HOLD.

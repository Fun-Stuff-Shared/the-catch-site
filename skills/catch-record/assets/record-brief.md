# {Event in one line}: the record

Every fact line ends with its source: `[record-id: "words from the record"]` for an admitted record (`admit.py check` verifies each one), `[lead: URL]` for something found but not admitted, with the reason. A fact with neither is not in the record.

## The event

What happened, when, who acted, in three to five dated sentences.

## The people it happens to

The person or people at the center, as the records describe them, and what happened to them, in order.

## How it came to this

The dated chain from the earliest act both sides argue from to the event. Each step: what was done, by whom, and what the actor said at the time.

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|

## Why: the stated reasons

Each actor's own stated reason for what it did, in its own words, attributed. The context the actors themselves invoke (a law, a crime, a campaign promise, a budget). No reason the record does not state.

## The sides

Each side's case in its strongest terms, from its own filings or statements, with its reasons. Then the points both sides agree on.

## The numbers

| Figure | What it counts | As of | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

## What coverage says that the records do not

Each line: the outlet's sentence (quoted, admitted), the record passage that contradicts or qualifies it, and the difference in one sentence.

## What is next

Scheduled and pending: dates, filings, votes, deadlines, related cases. What the event will and will not settle.

## Reactions

Statements by officials, elected officials, the parties, advocates and experts, each attributed and dated.

## Unknowns

What the record cannot answer, each with the searches run and what they returned.

## Leads not admitted

Every lead from the grok sweep or the reviews that was not admitted: the URL, what it claims, and why it is not in the record (unreachable, unverifiable, contradicted, duplicate).

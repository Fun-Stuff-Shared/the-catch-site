# Reconstruction Output Schema

Lead with findings rather than proposed research questions. Adapt the headings to the subject, but preserve the evidentiary distinctions.

## What Is Happening / What This Is

State the reconstructed current situation in a few precise paragraphs. For developing events, include an `as of` timestamp.

## First Signal to Present

Give a chronology of meaningful state changes. Distinguish event time, observation time, publication time, and later revision when relevant.

## How We Got Here

Explain the material precursor conditions, decisions, prior incidents, warnings, regulation, and system history. Separate causal evidence from contextual background.

## Similar Incidents or Cases

Compare the closest analogues. State the level of similarity, important differences, and what the comparison can and cannot establish.

## Prior Warning and Remedy Crosswalk

Use a table when multiple findings or remedies exist:

| Prior finding | Promised remedy | Owner / deadline | Reported status | Functional overlap | Intended effect | Current assessment | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not reduce the assessment to implemented/not implemented. Explain design, scope, operation, effectiveness, and whether the present failure lies inside the remedy’s control objective.

## Why It Became Consequential

Show the propagation chain from susceptibility and trigger through failed controls, containment, response, and downstream effects. Label each important link by confidence.

## Effects Beyond the Immediate Event

Separate immediate, second-order, and longer-term consequences. Identify affected actors and quantify scale and duration where possible.

## What Existing Coverage or Conventional Framing Misses

State the context, connection, or consequence that materially changes interpretation. Do not invent novelty merely because a point received less coverage.

## What We Know, What Is Likely, and What Is Unresolved

Separate established, strongly supported, plausible, disputed, unknown, and contradicted propositions.

## Potential Stories Worth Pursuing

Rank only evidence-backed candidate stories. For each state why it matters, supporting evidence, what remains unproven, and which record would strengthen or kill it.

## Highest-Value Next Evidence

Name specific records, data, reports, hearings, filings, milestones, or observations that could materially change the reconstruction. Avoid generic instructions such as “monitor developments.”

## Bottom Line

Give the strongest bounded reconstruction supported by the evidence. Say whether the apparent connection is direct, functionally overlapping, analogous, speculative, or unsupported.

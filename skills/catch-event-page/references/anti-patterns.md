# Anti-patterns: sentences real reviews cut from real pages

Every example below was on a built Catch page and was cut or rewritten after a review.
The labels come from the codex scan of 2,596 sentences across the 15 story pages built
2026-09-19 (94 tells) and from the sentences Zain cut on 2026-09-19 (21). The voice lint
lists the first three shapes for a reread and fails the fourth outright; none of them
should reach it.

## The page talks about itself, or to someone

The rule: the page never says "this page", "we", "I", "you", "reader". A document's page is
named as the document ("the docket page"). Method and provenance are proof-layer sentences.
What a stranger reads is the event. The lint fails these.

- The release states the figure without a cause, and we will not guess at one.
- The headline of this page states what the published number says; this paragraph states what the people who work with the seasonal factors say about it.
- A further sixteen records, added after two independent reviews of this page, are cited where they are used and listed below.
- The final version is applied to the published series in February 2027, when every month on this page will shift.
- What this page can say beyond that is narrow.
- The update gives no speed for the moment the airplane left the pavement, and this page does not compute one.
- None of it is a finding about Flight 7598, and this page keeps it in four parts, in order: what stands on the record of a legal proceeding, what former employees allege, what management and the federal safety agency said back, and what nobody has established.
- Where this page says AP and another outlet agree, check whether they are the same dispatch.

## "Why it matters" leads and reader gloss

The rule: the page does not tell the reader what to take from a fact; it gives the next
fact. A "Why it matters:" lead is process vocabulary. If the sentence after the colon is a
fact, keep that sentence and drop the lead; if it is not a fact, drop the sentence.

- Why it matters: Finance still puts the same dollar figure on the list after the whole seafood category left it, and it has not published how the figure was calculated.
- Why it matters: a Canadian exporter of the banned lines does not pay a higher U.S. duty.
- Why it matters: Finance's rate-for-rate claim is not a claim against every good in those three baskets.
- Why it matters: the confrontation is no longer only a border duty.

## Mirrored antithesis

The rule: two halves that balance each other ("X is not a slice; the rate is") add no fact.
Say the thing the record says, once. Contrast is allowed only where the record makes the
distinction and the misreading depends on it (writing.md, "X, not Y").

- What is not a slice is the rate.
- All are in the record; the emphasis is the editorial choice.
- They do show that hemp and abortion account for one party's objections, not the other's.
- Roads are the exception to the exception.

## Section wrap-ups

The rule: a section ends on its last fact. A sentence that weighs or sums up what came
before ("the week adds up to", "against that record", "what the record supports is") is
cut; the summary box and the catch cards already restate the lede by design.

- Whether that reaches a household's bill is not measured yet.
- The week adds up to a signed political text that binds no money, and a Commission press release that names €200 million for this year and next.
- Against that record, Nuuk announced new projects on top of an envelope that already exists and a proposal that is a year old.
- The visit sat inside a seven-year grant envelope already in law, a budget proposal presented a year earlier, and a 2026 record of U.S. claims on Greenland.

## Unsupported gloss

The rule: a sentence that reads the record's mood or motive ("did not appear from
nowhere", "for once", "reads as a hiring stall") is not in any record. Cut it or cite the
passage that says it.

- It sits before them on the timeline.
- The split did not appear from nowhere.
- For once the pricing story is in the Fed's own record.
- Frederiksen's "We have been under pressure" and von der Leyen's "no one else" sit on this record, not on a blank week.

## An absence that names a set nobody read

The rule (writing.md, "Absence"): an absence sentence names the set that was looked at and
its date, in reader words, and that set is one the working note's census lines show was
captured and read. A refused, stale or never-fetched set does not go in the sentence.

- "Nothing in the docket entries through September 19 names a demolition date." Every CourtListener docket capture for that story was refused; the docket was never read. Rewritten to the admitted filings.
- "The inspector general's report listings through September 14 carry no such review." No census line read those listings. Rewritten to the admitted records.

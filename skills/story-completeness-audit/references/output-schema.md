# Audit Output Schema

Lead with completeness and discovery, not a generic summary or claim-by-claim fact-check. Scale the sections to the evidence; omit empty low-value sections, but never bury material omissions behind background.

## 1. What You’re Missing

State the most important omitted facts, developments, context, actors, records, counterevidence, or explanations. For each material item give the missing piece, what the investigation found, why it changes or qualifies the story, materiality, and supporting evidence.

## 2. Is There More to the Story?

Answer directly using the closest conclusion:

- No material additional story found
- Additional context without a changed core conclusion
- Materially more to the story
- Significant later developments omitted
- Chosen timeframe materially distorts the account
- Broader record substantially changes the interpretation

State the basis and any important research limitation.

## 3. Completeness Verdict

Classify the supplied account as substantially complete; mostly complete with minor omissions; accurate but materially incomplete; directionally correct but missing important context; selectively framed; materially misleading by omission; fundamentally incomplete; or impossible to assess adequately from accessible evidence.

## 4. Critical and Major Findings

For each finding present:

**Claim or omission**  
**What the account says or leaves out**  
**What the broader record shows**  
**Why it matters**  
**Recommended correction or addition**

## 5. Before, After, and Around the Story

Summarize only material precursor events, subsequent developments, parallel proceedings, missing actors, and adjacent records that help complete the picture.

## 6. Accuracy and Verification

Report unsupported or partially supported claims, factual errors, misleading wording, numerical discrepancies, quotation/context problems, and procedural mischaracterizations after the completeness findings.

Use a compact table when several claims require exact comparison:

| Claim | Article evidence | Best evidence | Passage support | Broader-record status | Materiality | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 7. Source and Corroboration Problems

Identify circular sourcing, syndication, shared source lineages, weak authority, unattributed claims, changed sources, or lack of genuinely independent confirmation.

## 8. Reconstructed Story

Give the shortest accurate version of the fuller story supported by the investigation. Do not merely rewrite the article. Clearly separate established facts, strongly supported interpretations, disputes, and unknowns when that distinction matters.

## 9. Remaining Unknowns

List unresolved questions, inaccessible records, and high-value checks that could still change the conclusion. Do not fill gaps with inference.

## Style

- Lead with the outcome, not the research process.
- Distinguish discovered facts from inference.
- Cite claims near the text they support and link to original records when possible.
- Prefer exact, bounded language over rhetorical certainty.
- Avoid false balance and trivia dumps.
- When no material omission is found, say what areas were checked and bound the conclusion to the accessible record.

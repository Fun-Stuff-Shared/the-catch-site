Fact-checking Stein’s claims about mail-in ballot rejections in North Carolina – PolitiFact

In North Carolina, “thousands more absentee ballots (are) being rejected this election — at a rejection rate twice as high as in 2022.”
Fact-checking Stein’s claims about mail-in ballot rejections in North Carolina
If your time is short
- Absentee ballots are being rejected at a higher rate this year than during the last midterm elections in 2022, state data shows.
- The trend comes after North Carolina officials adopted stricter absentee voting rules.
- Around the time of Stein’s post, the total number of rejections was down compared to 2022 and the rejection rate was up half a percentage point.
North Carolina Gov. Josh Stein said a higher percentage of mail-in ballots is being rejected this year over technicalities — a trend he called “infuriating” while sharing a statistic that wasn’t supported by state data.
“The State Board of Elections’ last-minute changes to voting by mail are leading to thousands more absentee ballots being rejected this election — at a rejection rate twice as high as in 2022,” Stein said in an Oct. 20 social media post.
Data released by the state elections board the next day showed that Stein’s post was wrong to say “thousands more ballots (are) being rejected this election,” but it was right in that North Carolina’s mail-in ballots are being rejected at a higher rate.
The number of overall mail-in ballots has dropped in the state when compared to the 2022 midterm elections. More people voted by mail in the 2022 midterms likely due to lingering concerns about the Covid-19, Jason Tyson, a spokesperson for the state elections board, told PolitiFact. President Donald Trump has also taken steps to discourage mail-in voting.
Through Oct. 20, about 43,500 North Carolina mail-in ballots had been cast, down 38% from the same stage in the 2022 election, according to state data. As a result, the total number of rejected ballots is down about 26%.
Through Oct. 20, officials had rejected 1,349 of mail-in ballots — or 3.1% of those cast through that date, Tyson said. At the same stage in the 2022 election, election officials had rejected 1,829 mail-in ballots, or 2.6% of those cast, Tyson said. North Carolina’s final 2022 absentee ballot rejection rate was 2.2%, according to the U.S. Election Assistance Commission.
The mail-in ballot rejection rate can fluctuate during the early voting period as more voters cast ballots and as voters take steps to fix ballots that elections officials flag as having problems. County elections officials are instructed to contact voters who cast a problematic mail-in ballot to give the voter enough time to correct the ballot or vote in-person instead.
What changed?
Stein is among many Democrats who have expressed frustration with new rules for accepting and rejecting mail-in ballots. The rules will make it more likely that ballots will be thrown out over technicalities that previously were ignored, and were introduced and approved by the state’s Republican-controlled state elections board.
A federal judge recently shot down a lawsuit that attempted to stop those changes from going into effect, saying that since voting had already begun it was too late to change the rules. The state elections board, however, didn’t approve the new rules until the same day voting began.
State law allows registered North Carolina voters to cast their ballots by mail for any reason, but they must follow strict instructions for filling out the ballot and mailing it back. The state elections board refers to this practice as voting “absentee-by-mail,” though North Carolinians often describe it as “absentee” or “mail-in” voting.
The state elections board adopted rules in September that allow election officials to more easily disqualify voters over simple mistakes, such as signing their name in the wrong place or forgetting to seal the envelope for their ballot or the container for photocopy of their ID. Opponents have said the new rules would disenfranchise voters in a state where statewide elections are often decided by close margins. Democrats tend to vote by mail at higher rates than Republicans in North Carolina, state data shows.
“It’s infuriating,” Stein wrote in his Oct. 20 post. “North Carolinians, please make a plan to vote this year, in person if possible. And if you’re voting by mail, make sure you seal both envelopes on your ballot. Don’t let them take away your vote.”
Stein is currently suing over a law that took control over the elections board from him. The governor has controlled elections for over a century, but after Stein was elected governor in 2024 Republican state legislators passed a new law transferring control of elections to Republican State Auditor Dave Boliek instead. Stein has repeatedly accused Boliek of politicizing elections administration in the leadup to the 2026 midterms, which Boliek denies.
Where did the ‘double’ estimate come from?
When asked where Stein got his data about rejected ballots and the rejection rate, Stein’s office pointed PolitiFact to an article by Democracy Docket, a group created by longtime Democratic elections lawyer Marc Elias.
The article cited an analysis by Common Cause North Carolina and the Southern Coalition for Social Justice that documented a rise in rejected midterm mail-in ballots. The groups’ analysis was drawn from only a sample of data. It analyzed data from 27 of North Carolina’s 100 counties. Democracy Docket initially reported that North Carolina’s 3% total rejection rate was double the rate “the state saw” in its 2022 election, according to an archived version of the story.
The article has since been updated to say North Carolina’s 3% total rejection rate was double the rejection rate “the entire nation” saw in 2022. The national average in 2022 was 1.5%, according to the U.S. Election Assistance Commission.
Democracy Docket declined to respond to a request for comment.
Common Cause North Carolina and the Southern Coalition for Social Justice found that mail-in ballot rejection rates were 7% to 8% in Mecklenburg and Forsyth counties. And there are nearly 1,000 ballots that haven’t been rejected but could be spoiled if not fixed by the voter, said Sailor Jones, Common Cause’s state director.
The state elections board expects the statewide mail-in ballot rejection rate to ultimately be slightly higher than the final 2.2% average from 2022, said Tyson, the state elections board spokesman.
Our ruling
Stein said that, in North Carolina, “thousands more absentee ballots (are) being rejected this election — at a rejection rate twice as high as in 2022.”
State data showed that, at the time of Stein’s claim, fewer total mail-in ballots had been rejected in North Carolina compared with the same period in 2022. That’s because fewer people are voting by mail.
The data showed that mail-in ballots were being rejected at a higher rate than in 2022 — but they hadn’t doubled. At the time of Stein’s post, the rejection rate was half a percent higher than in 2022.
Stein’s statement contains an element of truth but ignores critical facts that would give a different impression. That’s our definition of Mostly False.

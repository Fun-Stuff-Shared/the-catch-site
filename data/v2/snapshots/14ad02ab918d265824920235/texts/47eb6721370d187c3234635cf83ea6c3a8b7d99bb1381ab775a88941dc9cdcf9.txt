Recent polls in Georgia show split support one week before early vote

Early voting starts next week in GA. What governor, Senate polls show
Editor's note: USA TODAY has deleted the mention of a poll by Wedgewood Polls in this story. Wedgewood Polls released a statement online on Oct. 20 that said "most" of its polling data was “fabricated.” USA TODAY has removed any reference to Wedgewood Polls data in all stories where it was mentioned.
Election day in Georgia is less than a month away, and early voting starts next week across the Peach State.
While there are races, large and small, happening all over the state, there are seats at the top of the ballot that will dictate the direction of the state not only over the next few years, but well into the future.
Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms, the former mayor of Atlanta, and Republican Rick Jackson, a healthcare CEO, are battling it out for the governor's seat, taking over from second-term Governor Brian Kemp who could not run again. Bottoms easily won her Democratic primary and was polling strong at the beginning of the summer, but Jackson, following his primary runoff win against Lt. Gov. Burt Jones, has made up significant ground in the final weeks of the election. Polls show this race is too close to call, and it may come down to a few key voting blocs.
At the national level, Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff is running for reelection to his U.S. Senate seat against Republican House Rep. Mike Collins, a Trump-endorsed candidate. Ossoff has been gaining national attention not only as he calls out the Trump Administration over high prices, the war in Iran and Trump's relationship with his aide Natalie Harp, but also as 2028 presidential plans start to swirl. Collins, on the other hand, has the backing of the president as he battles a House ethics investigation and accusations of close ties to white supremacist figures.
There will likely be split tickets this November, as the Democrat leads in the Senate race and the Republican has a tiny lead in the governor's race.
Here's what polls have been showing in the past few weeks in Georgia.
U.S. Senate race shows incumbent lead
Polling in the U.S. Senate race between Ossoff and Collins has consistently showed the incumbent Democrat with a sizeable lead.
Ossoff ran in the primary for his party unopposed as he seeks reelection, but Collins was faced with a challenging runoff following the Republican primary. Collins ultimately defeated Derek Dooley, a candidate backed by current Georgia Governor Brian Kemp, for the Republican nomination.
Here are some recent polls for the U.S. Senate race in Georgia:
- Oct. 4 — Decision Desk HQ, Ossoff 52.5%, Collins 43.5%
- Oct. 4 — Silver Bulletin, Ossoff 51.8%, Collins 43.1% (this is an average calculated from many polls, not an independent poll)
- Sept. 22-23 — InsiderAdvantage, Ossoff 50%, Collins 42%
- Sept. 15-18 — YouGov, Ossoff 52%, Collins 43%
Ossoff was first elected in a runoff in 2021, and served a full six-year term. If reelected, he will serve another six years in the Senate. If Collins is elected, he will move from one chamber of Congress to the other, and it's unclear what would happen with his ongoing House ethics investigation.
Georgia governor polls remain tight
For the top seat in the state, the race has been consistently too close to call. Bottoms had a noticeable lead immediately after the Republican primary runoff, but Jackson has not only closed the gap between them, but taken a very slight lead over Bottoms in the last few weeks.
There is a significant number of undecided voters, however, who could swing the election one way or the other.
Here are some recent polls for the governor's race in Georgia:
- Oct. 4 — Decision Desk HQ, Jackson 48.8%, Bottoms 46.8%
- Oct. 4 — Silver Bulletin, Jackson 47.8%, Bottoms 46.9% (this is an average calculated from many polls, not an independent poll)
- Sept. 22-23 — InsiderAdvantage, Jackson 48%, Bottoms 46%
- Sept. 15-18 — YouGov, Jackson 47%, Bottoms 44%
If neither candidate reaches a 50% threshold after the election on Nov. 3, the governor's race will have to go to a runoff at the beginning of December. The polls are well within the margin of error, meaning if the polls are off just a few percentage points, it could change who wins the governor's race.
Important dates for the midterm elections
- Last day to register to vote: Oct. 5
- First day of early voting: Oct. 13
- Last day of early voting: Oct. 30
- Election day: Nov. 3
- Runoff election day (if needed): Dec. 1

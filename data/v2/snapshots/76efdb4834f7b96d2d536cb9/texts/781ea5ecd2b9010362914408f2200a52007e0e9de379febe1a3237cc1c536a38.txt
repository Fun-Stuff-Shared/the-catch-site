DECLARATION OF AMAN BHULLAR
I, Aman Bhullar, declare as follows:
Background of Declarant
1. I am the Assistant Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk of the Information
Technology Bureau of the County of Los Angeles Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk
(hereinafter “Registrar”). In such capacity, I am responsible for the configuration,
maintenance, and operation of election management and tally systems and the
preparation of ballot materials for all federal, state, county, school district, and
special district elections conducted by the Registrar. I have worked at the Registrar
for 9 years. I have personal knowledge of the facts set forth in this declaration
based on my role at the Registrar. If called as a witness, I could and would testify
competently to all facts stated herein.
2. I submit this declaration in support of the Registrar’s Opposition to the
U.S. Department of Justice’s (“DOJ”) Request for Temporary Restraining Order in
the above-captioned case to provide additional context regarding how the
technology behind the Registrar’s election systems works when administering an
election.
3. As explained further below, based on my technical expertise, I have
grave concerns about the potential catastrophic impact of implementing the DOJ’s
request to “set aside vote-by-mail ballots from persons identified by the Federal
Government likely to be noncitizens” based on a list of approximately 20,000
individuals (the “October List”), the majority of which are records for individuals
located outside of Los Angeles County. The Registrar does not make programmatic
changes in the middle of an election cycle and, in fact, has policies against making
programmatic changes during election cycles, consistent with federal and state law.
The potential consequences of implementing DOJ’s demand are severe and range
from halting all counting; halting in-person voters’ ability to cast votes; or
otherwise interfering with the ability to accurately count votes. From a technical

Change Management Process
4. “Setting aside” vote-by-mail ballots pursuant to the DOJ’s request
would require changes to the Registrar’s election systems that are governed by
what is collectively referred to as the change management process. Under that
process, a requested change is first initiated and documented. Programmers then
develop the code necessary to implement the change in the system. The change is
tested multiple times in a non-production environment to identify any unintended
impacts. A non-production environment is a separate computing or software
environment used for development, testing, and staging before software is deployed
to live users. Other programmers then conduct a peer review to validate that the
change is safe. The change is typically implemented during off-peak hours; under
ordinary circumstances, changes are made over a weekend when there are no users
on the system and no processing is occurring. A large change is also accompanied
by a “fail period,” which is a defined window during which a system, service, or
application is expected to be unavailable, degraded, or producing errors, and during
which that behavior is considered acceptable or anticipated. Rollback procedures
are also prepared in the event the change must be reversed.
5. These steps reflect industry best practices. They are well understood
by IT professionals. They all help protect the system and its users from unintended
consequences of software changes.

Adjustments to DIMS
12. Before any ballot could be set aside, DIMS, the centralized voter
registration database and election management application, would first have to be
adjusted. DIMS is a software system that manages voting operations and tracks
voter registration information for close to 5.9 million voters in the County. DIMS
supports essential functions such as maintaining voter records and voter
preferences, conducting elections, managing petitions, facilitating vote-by-mail
processes, content management, administrative functionality, and real-time
information exchange between ePollbooks and the statewide voter registration
database (“VoteCal”).
13. Because DIMS houses all voter registration information, it is the first
step regardless of whether the individual votes by mail or in person . The Registrar
would first be required to place a database flag on these individuals, if they appear
in DIMS. No such flag currently exists, so a new flag would need to be developed
and tested by the DIMS manufacturer, Runbeck Election Services, LLC. The
Registrar would then need to validate and test it in a non-production environment.
The Registrar would also need to work with its third-party vendors for automatic
signature verification, BlueCrest, Inc., and for ePollbooks, KnowInk, to ensure that

Vote by Mail
17. The Registrar would next need to make separate adjustments for vote-
by-mail (“VBM”) voters and in-person voters. For VBM voters, the Registrar uses
the BlueCrest Vantage to physically sort VBM ballots. As ballots pass through the
BlueCrest Vantage, two processes occur. First, the BlueCrest Vantage performs
automatic signature verification. The signature reference files are stored in DIMS,
and the BlueCrest Vantage compares the signature on each ballot envelope with the
signature reference file in DIMS. Second, the BlueCrest Vantage sorts ballots into
twenty (20) separate sort categories, each of which must be programmed. To route
ballots from voters on the October List so that they are not sent to extraction,
BlueCrest would need to develop a new sort scheme for the BlueCrest Vantage.
That new sort scheme would then need to be tested before it could be used. Because
BlueCrest, a third-party vendor, would design the new sort scheme, the Registrar
cannot determine whether the change is feasible in the middle of the election cycle
and would need to further consult with BlueCrest. The Registrar also cannot
determine the downstream consequences of making such a change at this time.

Tally
22.Once ballots enter tally operations, including tally pre-processing,
there is no way to separate or identify the ballot of any voter, including voters on
the October List. No association exists between a ballot and the voter who cast it
consistent with California voter anonymity requirements and . The Registrar
therefore cannot locate or remove the ballot of any particular voter. As a result, to
the extent any individuals on the October List have already voted, their ballots
cannot be segregated at this stage.

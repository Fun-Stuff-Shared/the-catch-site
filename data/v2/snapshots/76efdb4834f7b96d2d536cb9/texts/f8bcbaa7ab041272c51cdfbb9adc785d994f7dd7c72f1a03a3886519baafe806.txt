component of the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). I have held this position

Deputy Special Agent in Charge, Special Agent in Charge, and Deputy Assistant
Director.

functions that support investigative, analytic, and operational activities. My
responsibilities include directing and managing approximately 300 personnel and
managing a budget exceeding $250 million. These resources support the development,
deployment, governance, and sustainment of approved data and analytic capabilities
used in support of HSI’s mission.
5. Throughout my law enforcement career, I have led or supported
investigations and operational programs involving transnational criminal organizations,
human smuggling and trafficking, narcotics smuggling, cybercrime, customs and
immigration matters and financial and identity-related theft.

Election Integrity Efforts
11. It is unlawful for aliens to vote in federal elections. See 18 U.S.C. § 611. To
ensure aliens do not vote in federal elections, HSI is among several federal departments
and agencies receiving and processing publicly available state-eligible voter files (e.g.,
voter rolls) and cross-referencing them with federal immigration databases. HSI’s role in
that initiative includes data intake (including obtaining bona fide voter rolls),
preparation, enrichment, quality review, and routing of leads or records for review by the
appropriate component or partner agency to determine whether aliens are on voter rolls
or otherwise participating in federal elections.
12. HSI’s team of technical and analytic experts evaluate, to the best of their
ability, whether the data it receives indicates whether an alien is registered on a state
voter file or requested or received a vote-by-mail ballot. HSI, together with other federal
agencies, conducts and supports the technical and analytic aspects of the workflow.
Workflow Process Generally
13. Voting records contain various types of information about the listed
individual, including name, address, date of birth or age, contact information,
registration information, voter-history information, and more.
14. After a state voter file is acquired, HSI sends it for analysis to the Fraud
Detection and National Security Directorate (FDNS) within U.S. Citizenship and
Immigration Services (USCIS). HSI sends it to FDNS to determine if the person on the
list has data in USCIS immigration holdings.
15. As I understand, FDNS developed a model that uses information from the
voter roll and combines it with information from a reliable third-party data analytics and
investigative platform to learn more about the individuals. See Bartell Decl. ¶¶ 13-20.
From there, as I understand, FDNS then compares the enhanced voter file data to USCIS
holdings to score the extent to which the individual profile matches an individual within
DHS holdings. Id. ¶ 16. Data profiles include name, mailing address, date of birth or age,
social security number, phone number, email, registration information, voter-history

information, and more. Id. The more data identifiers that match, the higher the
confidence it is that individual. Id. ¶ 18. Naturalized United States citizens are then
removed from the population as they are lawfully registered voters. Id. ¶ 19. The
remaining data are then referred for manual review to confirm the subject is a true
match, and based on all available information, is not a United States citizen. Id. ¶¶ 19-20.
16. Once FDNS completes its analysis, it sends its work product to HSI.
Id. ¶ 20. After an initial intake—which includes a quality review that consists of
identifying duplicate entries, incomplete records, inconsistent formatting, missing
identifiers, and other data quality issues—HSI prioritizes the voter records for review
and prepares the data for additional enrichment using approved analytic capabilities.
17. HSI’s enrichment process focuses on improving the completeness and
consistency of identity and address information and, where lawfully permitted,
identifying additional unique identifiers that may assist in reliable matching of the voters
to their immigration status. HSI’s enrichment process has the capability to perform data
matching across multiple authorized data sources, consolidating disparate records into a
unified set of matching results. The purpose of enrichment is to increase the quality of
HSI’s analysis and to reduce the risk that voters are mistakenly associated with the
wrong immigration status.
18. In short, HSI’s processing adds value by applying consistent quality
controls and enrichment processes before voter records are disseminated as investigative
referrals or routed for another authorized purpose.
19. Once the HSI review is completed, each individual data profile will be
referred to the applicable HSI field office where it will be investigated for potential
criminal violations.

Evidence that Noncitizens in California were Sent Vote-by-Mail Ballots
20. Our office received what appeared to be the California voter roster for the
general election of 2026. The roster appeared credible enough for our office to initiate
our investigative processes in accordance with our standard operating procedures. HSI
initially reviewed the list of millions of registered voters in California, then turned the
list over to USCIS FDNS.
21. I believe that the voter file provided to HSI accurately reflects the actual
voter rolls in California that, as of August 2025, may be used for the 2026 federal
election. Based on my understanding of California law, I believe that California
automatically mails vote-by-mail ballots to participate in a federal election to anyone
registered to vote in California. See 2021 Cal. Stat. ch. 312 (codified at Cal. Elec. Code §
3000.5).
22. After receiving the file, HSI referred it to FDNS to go through the process
described above. FDNS then provided HSI an enhanced file of voter information to
ensure the accuracy of the information. As I understand, FDNS analyzed the original file
provided by HSI in combination with third party commercial data. See Bartell Decl.
¶¶ 13, 16. FDNS immigration officers then manually reviewed the data. Id. ¶ 19. FDNS
confirmed to HSI that the revised list includes persons that FDNS believes includes
noncitizen aliens. Id. ¶ 20.
23. After receiving that enhanced file from FDNS, HSI continued to further
refine the data with federal law enforcement systems. That further refinement resulted in
over 20,000 individuals remaining on the list.
24. On October 13, 2026, I helped facilitate delivery to the U.S. Department of
Justice and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Central District of California that list of
just over 20,000 individuals who HSI believes are (1) registered to vote in California, (2)
were sent a vote-by-mail ballot to participate in the federal elections, and (3) are
noncitizen aliens. On that same day, I understand an HSI special agent delivered that
same list on an encrypted hard drive to the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/

Conclusion
26. HSI works with USCIS to determine whether aliens are on voter rolls to
vote in federal elections. In support of the above-captioned case, HSI received data that,
in my belief, accurately reflects the voters on California’s voter rolls. HSI, together with
USCIS, processed these data to identify voters who are highly likely to be noncitizens
registered to vote in the 2026 federal election.

DECLARATION OF MONICA FLORES
I, Monica Flores, declare as follows:
1. I am the Assistant Registrar-Recorder for the Candidate and Voter
Services Bureau of the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk (the
“Registrar”). In such capacity, I am responsible for voter registration maintenance,
vote by mail administration, data entry and signature verification of all petitions,
election consolidation requests, candidate filing and qualifications, ballot measure
filing, certification of election results, recounts, recalls, and assisting with the
coordination, conduct, and administration of all federal, state, county, and local
elections. I have personal knowledge of the matters and facts set forth herein and, if
called as a witness, could and would testify competently thereto.
2. I submit this declaration in support of the Registrar’s Opposition to the
U.S. Department of Justice’s (“DOJ”) Request for Temporary Restraining Order in
the above-captioned case to provide additional context regarding the Registrar’s
administration of the election process generally and in this current general election
that will take place on Tuesday, November 3, 2026 (“General Election”).
3. The DOJ asserts that the Registrar is “well-equipped to implement the
United States’ requested relief[,]” that is, to “set aside vote-by-mail ballots from
persons identified by the Federal Government likely to be noncitizens.” It is not.
The Registrar has no authority under state or federal law to withhold or set aside the
ballot of a registered voter on the basis of a third party’s assertion that the voter
may not be a citizen, has no information beyond the voter’s own attestation with
which to determine citizenship, and has no lawful means of obtaining such
information. In addition, as explained below, a substantial part of what the DOJ
requests is no longer physically possible, and the remainder could not be carried out
without placing the timely and accurate administration and certification of the
General Election at risk. I also have strong concerns about potentially
disenfranchising voters associated with records on this list and believe, if forced to

What Does the Registrar Do?

registering voters, maintaining voter files, and conducting federal, state, local and

contracting, use and maintenance of equipment and materials used to facilitate the

39. The first step would require devoting substantial resources to
confirming any differences between the October List and our voter registration
database; investigating the reason for any discrepancies; tracking those records;
and communicating such discrepancies back to the DOJ. Indeed, it is inevitable

information may vary slightly (e.g., Robert vs. Bob, Apartment #3 vs. Apt. 3, Sara

is a complex process and extreme care must be taken when the consequences of
errors may lead to voter disenfranchisement.
40. As an additional hurdle, the DOJ has provided a statewide list to Los
Angeles County and Los Angeles County cannot identify the legitimacy of records
outside of its jurisdiction.

Step 4: Manual Segregation
43. The Registrar’s voting system and ballot-processing workflow have no
function for setting aside the ballots of designated voters. Creating one during an
election would require changes to systems and procedures that have already been
tested and finalized for this election, the development and documentation of a new
process, and the training of staff on it, all while the work described above is
required to be completed on the statutory schedule. I do not believe the Registrar
could do that and still meet its post-election deadlines, including the timely count
and certification of the vote.

Conclusion
50. As outlined above, I am not aware of any authority under state or

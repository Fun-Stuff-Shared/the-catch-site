Defendants the State of California and Secretary of State Shirley N. Weber,
Ph.D. (“State Defendants”) oppose Plaintiff’s motion for an injunction pending
appeal, ECF No. 76, because there are no grounds for such an appeal while the
Court evaluates the application for a temporary restraining order (“TRO
Application”).
Plaintiff chose to file this lawsuit on October 15, 2026, seventeen days before
Election Day for the 2026 General Election. ECF No. 1. Plaintiff waited four more
days, until October 19, to file its TRO Application. ECF No. 22-2. In that
application, Plaintiff requested relief “by November 3, 2026, before the end of
voting in the 2026 federal election.” ECF No. 22-2 (emphasis added). All
Defendants opposed the TRO Application the next day, on October 20. ECF Nos.
31-1 and 32-1.
On October 21, this case was first assigned to the Honorable John W.
Holcomb (ECF No. 34) and then transferred to the Honorable David O. Carter
(ECF No. 46). At 6:41 p.m. on October 21, several hours after being assigned the
case, the Court issued a scheduling order setting a hearing on the TRO Application
for Tuesday, October 27, a full week ahead of Plaintiff’s requested deadline for
relief. ECF No. 64. At 8:57 p.m., prior to serving the summons and complaint on
any Defendant, Plaintiff filed a request demanding a ruling on its TRO Application
by noon the following day. ECF No. 67.1 On October 22, merely three days after
filing the original TRO Application, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Appeal, claiming that
the Court has constructively denied the preliminary injunction, even though the
scheduled hearing on the TRO Application had not happened. ECF No. 74. At 6:17
a.m. on October 23, Plaintiff filed its motion for an injunction pending appeal. ECF
1
Also on October 21, all Defendants filed motions to dismiss the complaint, ECF
Nos. 65, 68, requesting an expedited schedule on the motions, ECF No. 57. Despite
claiming in its request for an expedited TRO ruling that this case concerns an
“emergency” that “accelerates in real time” due to Defendants’ ongoing tabulation
of ballots, ECF No. 67, Plaintiff opposes expediting proceedings on the motions to
dismiss, questioning Defendants’ need for “clarity and assurance that they can
continue to administer” the elections according to law, ECF No. 71.

No. 76. Just over two hours later, the Court ordered any additional briefing in
opposition to Plaintiff’s TRO Application by Saturday at 3:00 p.m., and a reply no
later than Sunday at 3:00 p.m. ECF Nos. 77, 78.
The Court should refuse to grant an injunction pending appeal for the simple
reason that the appeal is improper, since there is nothing currently to appeal. The
Court has not “even tentatively decide[d] anything about the merits of [Plaintiff’s]
claim,” let alone issued an order on the TRO Application. Switzerland Cheese
Ass’n, Inc. v. E. Horne’s Mkt., Inc., 385 U.S. 23, 25 (1966). Instead, the Court has
expeditiously set a hearing date four days from now and a full week before the date
by which Plaintiff originally requested a ruling. Plaintiff’s dilatory, after-hours
pivot on October 21 to demand a ruling by noon the next day does not create a valid
basis for its purported appeal, which is without legal effect.
“District courts have wide discretion in managing their docket, and they do
not necessarily deny a motion by failing to rule on a parties’ requested timeline.”
Amazon.com Servs. LLC v. Nat’l Lab. Rels. Bd., 151 F.4th 221, 227 (5th Cir. 2025)
(district court did not constructively deny motion for preliminary injunction by
failing to rule by requested date) (citing In re Fort Worth Chamber of Commerce,
100 F.4th 528, 535 (5th Cir. 2024)). “Appeal cannot be achieved simply by
asserting that the trial court has failed to act promptly as wished by a party seeking
an injunction.” Id.; cf. Mt. Graham Red Squirrel v. Madigan, 954 F.2d 1441, (9th
Cir. 1992) (finding constructive denial where plaintiff moved for preliminary
injunction on August 22, the court scheduled a hearing for September 8, but at an
August 30 hearing on a different motion “announced its decision not to hold a
preliminary injunction hearing” until after the challenged action was substantially
completed); Cedar Coal Co. v. United Mine Workers of Am., 560 F.2d 1153, 1161-
62 (4th Cir. 1977) (finding constructive denial when district court issued an order
that continued preliminary injunction hearing date indefinitely).

Moreover, the Court should consider the litigant’s “diligence in seeking to
expedite briefing and consideration,” such as whether it has made “repeated
requests for a ruling by a specific date.” Amazon.com Servs. LLC, 151 F.4th at 227.
Here, Plaintiff originally requested a ruling by November 3, which the Court’s
scheduled hearing will easily accommodate. Plaintiff’s October 21 request that this
Court issue a ruling on its TRO by noon the next day was not reasonable, and the
Court’s current schedule—where this TRO will be heard in only a few days—
cannot be constructive denial.
The TRO Application has not been denied, either by court order or
constructively, and the Court has not failed to rule. Instead, the Court set a hearing
on the TRO Application to be held four court days after being assigned this case.
There is no interlocutory order to appeal under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1), and the
appellate court therefore has no jurisdiction over this case. Accordingly, the Court
“may disregard” Plaintiff's “notice of appeal and proceed with the case.” Ruby v.
Secretary of United States Navy, 365 F.2d 385, 389 (9th Cir. 1966); see also
Hollywood v. City of Santa Maria, 886 F.2d 1228, 1230 (9th Cir. 1989) (holding
notice of appeal deficient as “premature,” and thus “ineffective,” where it is “filed
before entry in the district court docket of the written disposition of [a] motion”);
see also 16 Wright & Miller’s Federal Practice & Procedure § 3924.1 (3d ed.
2012) (“Appeal cannot be achieved simply by asserting that the trial court has failed
to act as promptly as wished by a party seeking an injunction.”).
There is also no basis for Plaintiff’s alternative request for a 30-day
injunction pending further Ninth Circuit developments. ECF No. 76-1 at 2. In
NetChoice v. Bonta, the District Court issued such an injunction solely to preserve
the status quo. See 761 F. Supp. 3d 1232, 1236 (N.D. Cal. 2025). Here, by contrast,
Plaintiff seeks a mandatory injunction that would require Defendants to undertake
affirmative actions to “segregate ballots.” ECF No. 76-1 at 16; but see Purcell v.
Gonzalez, 549 U.S. 1 (2006).

If the Court finds that Plaintiff has validly appealed, the Court should deny
an injunction pending appeal for all the same reasons that State Defendants have
argued a TRO should not issue: Plaintiff lacks standing; Plaintiff’s claim fails on
the merits; the relief Plaintiff seeks would independently violate federal and state
law; Plaintiff fails to demonstrate irreparable harm; and the equities strongly
disfavor relief in this case. See generally ECF 31-1 (State Defendants’ Opposition
to Plaintiff’s TRO).
For the foregoing reasons, the State Defendants respectfully request that the
Court deny the Application for Injunction Pending Appeal.

President Trump Returns To The Hugh Hewitt Show

Nov 12, 2026  /  Transcripts
Link Copied
Text Size:

President Trump joined me today to discuss topics big and small:

Transcript:

HH: Pleased to welcome back to the Hugh Hewitt Show President Donald Trump. Mr. President, always good to have you. Thank you for joining me today.

DJT: Well, thank you.

HH: It usually takes serious historians 30, 40, 50 years to get their arms around a big presidency like yours. You have 26 months left, so this is a premature question. But when they get to your presidency, what do you think the serious historians are going to say about it?

DJT: Well, I think it’s been a great presidency, and I think we’re going to do fine in the next two years. We have a very powerful power, and that’s the power of veto. And you know, I intend to use it a lot. And then, I think ultimately, deals can be cut, whether it’s on drug pricing, medical healthcare. You know, different deals can be cut. But the power of veto is very important. You know, they have two places, I don’t know how that happened, but somehow you know, when you look at the kind of margins that I won by, I did 30 rallies in about 27 days. I certainly worked at it, trying to get the people. There was tremendous spirit at the rallies. I mean, there was love in those rooms. So, and rooms beyond rooms. I mean, we had 20-25-30,000 people at these things. And it was like a lovefest, but I guess it just didn’t go to the candidate. I don’t know what happened. I think they had a lot of problems with the fact that SAVE America wasn’t passed, and that other things weren’t passed, and I can understand that. I agree with them on that. But I was a little bit surprised that Republicans didn’t do better. And I guess certain candidates, they certainly didn’t like.

HH: Well, do you think you’ve altered fundamentally American politics, because historians don’t care about midterms in the end. 30 years from now, they’re not writing about 2026 or 2018. They’re writing about Donald Trump and the era of Trump. What’s the headline there?

DJT: Well, this midterm was, you know, lousy, but it wasn’t as bad as others, as you know. There were a few others that were, actually in a couple of cases, much worse. But I was very surprised. I’m not used to that. I’m used to, well, every time I ran, we won. When I didn’t run, you know, it seems to be, it seems to be difficult. It’s sort of interesting. But you know, I got a million and a half votes, and like in Texas, I got a million and a half more votes than the candidate in Texas when you look at that, than the Senate candidate in Texas. That’s a lot of votes. And somehow, if we were on the ballot, I think that would have been, I think I’m just, I think my level of popularity maybe is higher now than it was. I’m not sure, but maybe, it feels to me that it would be higher other than what happened at the midterms.

HH: All right, so Mr. President, this will be the age of Trump. It’ll also be the age of AI, or as you’ve rechristened it, SI. As we’ve seen, Chinese espionage is going after AI to try and steal our stuff, or beat us.

DJT: Right.

HH: What happens if we lose our edge in AI? How bad is it for the country?

DJT: We’d be in bad shape. The level of importance to SI, super intelligence, is really beyond, actually beyond belief. I’ve seen it. I study it. I’m genetically blessed. I have an uncle that was probably considered the number one professor in the history of MIT for 51 years he was there. And he got the science award, the same award that I gave to Elon. And you know, I gave them out last week. We gave it to Google, we gave it to a lot of great people. We gave it to Jensen, frankly. That was a little before, but what a horrible thing that is. That’s so sad to see that happen. But my uncle got the same award from Ronald Reagan. They presented it to me when I was there last week. And it was sort of something. So I do have, I think, an inclination. I’m a believer in that, you know, and if you have two great parents that were baseball players, or great athletes, you’re going to do better than somebody that wasn’t there. And I will say that it’s very important. I’ve said whoever wins SI wins, and I mean that. Now we have an artificial barrier. All of a sudden, it was created. Some people say it was created by China. But I don’t sort of think that. I think it was just created maybe out of people wanting to get some money out of this. And I think that’s sort of okay. But we have a barrier, and that’s the data centers. And you know, where they’re built, they’re making a fortune, and people’s taxes have gone down, and they create their own electricity. That was because of me. We wouldn’t have, we don’t have enough electricity to fund any of them. They make their own electricity. They’re making electric plants that are unbelievable, selling the excess electricity back into the, and in some cases, giving back into the old-fashioned grid. You know, we have an old grid. It couldn’t have ever done this. But that was one of the great ideas that I had. This was my idea that they’ve got to build their own, they’ve got to manufacture their own electricity, and that’s what they’re all doing. So that’s a great thing. The water’s been taken care of. Everything’s been taken care of. The one thing that we can do, and one thing that’s happening now is these very rich companies are giving money to people in the community. And all of a sudden, you see people saying thank you very much. I mean, they had a case in, they had a case, Hugh, in Louisiana where it was very unpopular.

HH: Right.

DJT: And they ended up giving the teachers $50,000 each. All of a sudden, it became tremendously popular. So you know, that can help solve the problem, and maybe totally solve the problem. You have to negotiate your own deal, but maybe totally solve the problem.

HH: So if the Chinese get this from us, do we lose the ability to defend the country and our financial systems and our electrical power? Do people understand the risk of the espionage that they’re conducting?

DJT: Well, it could be, and you know, we also go after them. You know, it’s not just a one-sided deal. Somebody said oh, they’re tapping our phones. I think that’s right. We’re tapping their phones, too. And we are, and they are, too. And it’s been that way for a long time. And we do it very well, also. So you know, I just don’t want them to think we’re a bunch of babies that don’t know what’s going on. We have to have, you know, we have to be smart. When the balloon went over this country, that balloon should have been shot down a hell of a lot sooner than it was shot down. But under this administration, we’re very vigilant. Very vigilant.

HH: All right, so you’re meeting with them next week.

DJT: And they do things, but we do things, too, Hugh.

HH: You’re meeting with the General-Secretary next week, Chairman Xi. Any chance you can use their espionage and the expose of it to somehow get Jimmy Lai out of jail? Any way to leverage that?

DJT: Okay, yeah, as you know, I’ve gotten others out. And recently, I’ve gotten others out. Jimmy Lai’s a little bit of a different story. His son is fantastic. He’s working very hard, but, and I spoke at length with President Xi about Jimmy Lai. I don’t know Jimmy Lai, but I know the whole story. And there’s a great level of hatred there. I’m using a nice word. There’s a great level of hatred there. President Xi explained what happened with Hong Kong, and you know, the whole thing, that Jimmy Lai was the leader, and it caused them tremendous distress. And you know, we went through it in great detail. And he agreed to other people. He’s agreed to other people. He has not agreed to Jimmy Lai. There’s a lot of major, you know, and it’s great animosity there. Tremendous, actually.

HH: Well, maybe you’ve got some leverage now. We’ve got them with their pants down on going, well, actually, the chairman of Nvidia with his pants down, but maybe we can go after Xi because we caught him red-handed trying to steal our stuff. I don’t know. I leave that to you. You’re the negotiator. Let me give you a warm-up question on negotiation, Mr. President.

DJT: Go ahead.

DJT: True.

DJT: Has it lost? Okay, let me ask you just if I could. And I’m not supposed to do the interview, but I think the changing of the name, the Guardians, because Mr. Dolan from the team is a pretty impressive guy, a good-looking guy.

DJT: I know him from Madison Square Garden. He’s a friend of mine, who’s a very good guy, had a great year last year with the Knicks. But I guess it’s the same family. But Mr. Dolan ran for the Senate. He ran for a lot of things, and I was always able to take him out by one little phrase. And he did actually well. He’s a pretty good politician. And I beat him every time, you know, with other candidates. In other words, I had another candidate. I thought it was a devastating attack. Would you as a fan like to see the name be back to the original name from 1906, Cleveland Indians? I think there’s nothing wrong with the word Indians.

DJT: Right.

We’re taking out more oil. And you know, think about the war. We’ve been fighting, you know, pretty, you know, at a pretty rapid clip for three weeks. Not seven months, but three weeks. Nobody’s been killed in the last five months. You know, in Russia, they’re losing 25,000 people a month between Russia and Ukraine, mostly soldiers. We haven’t lost a soldier in five months, and we control the Gulf. We won Venezuela, and we control the Gulf in terms of the Hormuz Strait. We control it. Totally control it. But the answer is if I saw things going the other way, I would do that. And it’s possible I would do it right now.

HH: Okay, and are you willing to stick with the blockade? Even if it takes 26 months to collapse that regime?

DJT: Oh, yeah. No, no, well, the regime is collapsing, and their press is much different than the fact. You know, they’ll always make these harsh statements about we are doing well, we will fight back, we will…

HH: Oh, yeah.

DJT: They are not doing well. They are at 301% inflation. Their economy is gone. They’re not paying their soldiers. Their military is wiped out. Their navy is gone. Their air force is gone. Their anti-aircraft is gone. And they’ve lost the Hormuz Strait. They’re not doing well. But they always talk with great bravado like how well they’re doing. And the fake news tries to pick it up as much as possible. But even the fake news isn’t saying that. No, if it doesn’t, if something doesn’t work out, I would not hesitate. No.

HH: All right. If freeing Cuba requires military force before you leave office, will you order that military force?

DJT: Well, I want to get Cuba done before I leave, yeah. And we have a very good, talented guy for Cuba named Marco.

HH: Yeah.

DJT: Because Marco’s parents are from, Marco Rubio, his parents are from Cuba as you probably know. And he would love to do that. So we’re watching it very closely. It’s doing very poorly. It’s essentially a failed, you know, place, nation, whatever you want to call it. It’s failed. And we’re watching it. I would like to be able to do it. I won the Cuban vote by 94% in Florida, and I think I’m more popular now than I was then. And frankly, I’d love to be able to do it for the people, for the Cubans that came and were treated really badly, and they came to America.

HH: So if it took a little bit of military force, a week campaign, you’d be okay with that?

DJT: I would be, I would certainly consider that, yes.

HH: All right, now let me talk about Israel with you, Mr. President. You, they’ve been a modern state since 1948. You are the most pro-Israel president we have ever had. Some worry that Vice-President Vance’s post means that your view, your view, Donald Trump’s view of our ally has changed. Has it? Are you still the most pro-Israel president ever?

DJT: Well, I am. And I don’t understand a couple things. Like I wasn’t on the ballot, but still, if you look at El-Sayed in Michigan, he got more votes than the Republican candidate got by quite a bit from Jewish people. I can’t, it’s not even understandable. It’s a tough group to get. Now I will say this. In Israel, I’m at 99%. Literally, I’m at 99, the most popular person. You know, when this is over, I could go to Israel and be prime minister.

HH: (laughing) Yes.

DJT: No, I’m very much Israel inclined.

DJT: Right.

HH: It’s a disease.

DJT: Yup.

HH: It kills Jews. It actually kills Jews, but it kills countries. It’s everywhere, and it’s spreading. It will actually face your grandchildren, because they’re Jewish. How alarmed are you about the rise of antisemitism in the United States? What can we do about it?

DJT: I’m amazed to see it. I never thought this, this is like Germany in 1938 or whatever. It’s like crazy what’s going on. When you look at the attacks on people, the racial attacks and the attacks, they’re, I guess 57% on Jewish people, and you wouldn’t believe it. So I am very surprised at something like, and it started, I would say, during Barack Hussein Obama’s run. That’s when it started, and it’s continuing, and it’s pretty shocking to me. I’m very surprised.

HH: All right, is there federal…

DJT: We’re doing everything we can to do that…

DJT: …including making, you know, to schools and the colleges, and you know, they’re paying tremendous awards, colleges, for what they’ve done, because they’re antisemitic. Harvard’s antisemitic. And they’re big prices for what they’ve done.

HH: Yes.

DJT: I understand.

DJT: It could happen. It could happen.

DJT: We’re very strong, I’ll tell you, Hugh, this should have been done by other presidents.

DJT: Yeah, I would say in a year. I think a year is a lot of time. You know, in many countries, they leave weeks. DJT: Is that what you figure?

The Vice-President, Secretary Rubio, Bessent, Hegseth, Burgum, Pompeo, Kennedy, Senators Cotton, Cruz, Hawley, McCormick, Senator Sheehy, Governor DeSantis, Governor Ducey, Governor Huckabee, Governor Kemp, Governor Youngkin, Ambassador Haley. They’re all very presentable on a stage, right? They’re not going to be able to dominate a stage the way you did in 2015-16, but they could be pretty good in a debate.

DJT: Well, and the way I did in ’24, and the way I did in ’20. I think I dominated, I think I was even better…

HH: Oh, I’m talking about primaries when they’ve got like 20 people on the stage.

DJT: Primaries, yeah, I see. That was an interesting, 16 was amazing. 16 was amazing. I think they’ll never be another period like that. That was so, did you ever see the ratings? The ratings were like Super Bowl, beyond Super Bowl…

DJT: That’s right. You asked me about the triad.

HH: Yes.

DJT: To this day, that was the only question I probably didn’t answer perfectly.

DJT: Semi-perfectly. You asked…

DJT: You remember that, right?

HH: Oh, very well.

DJT: You remember that, the triad.

HH: Very well.

DJT: Yeah, I remember, too.

HH: I call that, that’s my first Trump tattoo.

DJT: Now, I know, now, I can define triad better than any person that’s ever lived right now. Okay, go ahead.

DJT: Well, they should do it. I mean, whoever heard of it, and that’s like a minimal number where they should do it. We’re in the midst of winning a war. But one of the things I did, the MFN, the drug pricing, that’s going to bring down the cost of healthcare tremendously, because you know, drugs, you know, I brought drugs down 70 and 80% that nobody talks about that, either. I did it through the, I guess you’d say, good negotiating, to be honest, with other countries, and with the drug companies. But we, I’ve got drug pricing reduced, and you take a look at, you know, our Trump, our little Trump site, which turned out to be one of biggest sites anyone’s ever seen, people are buying drugs for 50% and 60% discounts. And they’ve never seen that before. So the drug pricing, which was favored nation, it’s most favored nation, we went from the highest drugs in the history of our country to the lowest drug prices in the history of our country all in a period of the last six months, because I got other nations to agree to it by using tariffs against them. I said I’m going to tariff the hell out of you if you don’t agree. Nobody thought it was possible. Nobody was able to do it. I got it done. Drug pricing, most favored nation, and that’s going to help healthcare and healthcare reform and price transparency and permitting reform, which is a very big thing, and lowering credit card fees, all the things that we should be doing. But think of it. The biggest story is the fact that we went from the highest drug prices in the history of, in the world, we paid the highest in the world, and now we’re paying the lowest in the world. Nobody talks about it.

DJT: And it’ll have a huge impact, by the way, on healthcare.

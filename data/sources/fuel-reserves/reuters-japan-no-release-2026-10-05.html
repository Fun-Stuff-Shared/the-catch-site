Japan has no plans for new oil release from national reserves, official says

By Reuters
October 5, 20264:56 AM UTCUpdated ago
TOKYO, Oct 5 (Reuters) - Japanese Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said on Monday there were no plans ​for a fresh release of crude oil from national reserves ‌as the country had already released supplies.
Kihara's comments come even as the Group of Seven countries, of which Japan is a member, agreed on Friday to release 100 ​million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency reserves and pledged ​to refrain from energy export restrictions. The announcement followed pressure ⁠from US President Donald Trump on the European Union to draw ​down emergency diesel inventories.

It was uncertain how much of the ​new release would come from what remains from the 400 million barrels of oil that the 32 countries that are members of the International Energy Agency ​agreed to release in March to mitigate disruptions from the US-Israeli ​war on Iran.
IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol said last week that members ‌had ⁠released about two-thirds of the 400-million-barrel agreement.
In March, Japan said it would release about 80 million barrels of oil from its reserves as part of the coordinated agreement. Japan also conducted a second ​round of national crude ​oil stockpile ⁠releases in May.
"Japan has been releasing oil from reserves for some time now and there are no ​plans for a further release at this stage," ​Kihara told ⁠a press conference.
The G7 meeting came amid delays in the release of European strategic reserves and growing concerns over soaring diesel prices globally, ⁠with ​discussions focusing in particular on measures to ​address diesel prices in Europe, the US and elsewhere, Kihara said.

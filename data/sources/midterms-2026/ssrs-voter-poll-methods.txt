The Voter Poll by SSRS™
                                           2026 Methods Statement


Overview
The 2026 Voter Poll by SSRS is an independent survey of 2026 voters conducted on behalf of
ABC, the Associated Press (AP), CBS, CNN, Fox News Media, MSNOW, and NBC. Between
October 19 and November 3, the Voter Poll expects to interview approximately 100,000 voters at
voting locations, online, and by telephone producing a national sample of over 75,000 and
state-level samples for all 50 states. The poll should be referred to as The Voter Poll by SSRS or
The <NEWS ORGANIZATION> Voter Poll by SSRS. The news organizations together prepare
the questionnaire. More information about SSRS can be obtained by visiting www.ssrs.com.

The 2026 poll includes representative samples of registered voters in all 50 states and
nationally1. The Voter Poll combines data collected from verified registered voters online and by
telephone, with data collected in-person from Election Day voters (at sampled precincts) in 15
states and in-person at sampled early-voting centers in Alaska. Respondents can complete the
poll in English or Spanish2. The overall margin of sampling error for voters, accounting for
design effects, is expected to be plus or minus 0.6 percentage points nationally. Overall sample
size targets and expected margins of sampling error by state are reported at the end of this
document.


Pre-Election Voter Poll
Overall, approximately 76,130 registered voters will be surveyed online and by telephone prior
to election day. Respondents are drawn from a multi-frame sample that combines a registration-
based sample (RBS) selected from a voter file, obtained from L2, with L2-matched voters
sampled from the SSRS Opinion Panel and Verasight’s Verified Panel. Some respondents to the
pre-election survey will receive a small monetary incentive for completing the survey. Expected
pre-election survey sample sizes by each source and state are reported in Table 1.

recontacted between 5:00pm (local time) on Monday, November 2nd and through near poll

Registration-Based Sample (RBS)
The RBS is randomly selected using stratified systematic sampling from the voter file, which
SSRS licenses, in full, from L2. The combined RBS and SSRS Opinion Panel sample (described
below) is designed to be representative of registered voters in each state. The RBS design
involved stratification by voter-file variables historically correlated to response rates to ensure
groups with traditionally lower response rates are represented in the sample. The final results
are weighted to adjust for probability of selection in the sample.

Sampled voters are invited to participate in the study through multiple contact methods,
including a mailed postcard, SMS (text-message) invitation to sampled voters who have a cell
phone number on file, email invitations to those with an email address on file, and multiple
telephone call attempts to a subsample of voters with a telephone number on file. Upon receipt
of the postcard, sampled voters can complete the survey online (including by scanning a QR
code on mobile devices) or by calling a toll-free number.

Respondents who say they are younger than 18, do not provide an age, or say they are not
registered to vote in the state on file during initial screening, are excluded from the survey.
Those who indicate that they are not the named person on the voter file are also excluded from
analysis.

SSRS Opinion Panel Sample

The SSRS Opinion Panel is a nationally representative panel of U.S. adults, recruited entirely
through probability-based sampling. The majority of SSRS Opinion Panel members are recruited
using a stratified Address Based Sample (ABS) design. Additionally, SSRS recruits panelists from
random prepaid cell phone samples, which helps increase representation for harder-to-reach
populations.

Panelists with internet access participate via web while all non-internet respondents (including
those who have internet but are unwilling to take surveys online) participate by phone. All
panelists sampled from the SSRS Opinion Panel for The Voter Poll are registered voters who
could be matched to the L2 voter file (as of October 20, 2026). All potential respondents are

Verasight Voter-File Matched Sample

Verasight panelists are recruited through both probabilistic and non-probabilistic approaches.
Specifically, Verasight employs random address-based sampling, random person-to-person text
messaging, and online targeting through advertisements. Respondents to The Voter Poll are
recruited either from existing Verasight panelists, or directly into this poll, and then verified to be
qualified registered voters. Verasight respondent-verification includes phone verification when
respondents enter the panel, ongoing passive verification of responses, and data quality checks
at the time of each survey. Verasight respondents are screened for age, state of residence, and
voter registration status. Respondents included in The Voter Poll are matched to the L2 voter file
and deduplicated against the RBS and Opinion Panel samples by using L2’s unique voter
identifier.

Alaska Early-Vote Center Exit Poll

In order to ensure a sufficient number of pre-election surveys are completed in Alaska, in-
person interviews (exit polls) with voters are being conducted at five early vote locations
between October 19th through October 30th. Early vote centers were selected using a probability
proportionate to the size of the early in-person turnout (within the administrative region) in the
November, 2024 general election. For each selected early-vote center, two shifts were randomly
selected for exit polling, yielding a total of 10 early-vote exit polling shifts across the state.
Voters are primarily invited to complete the survey electronically on a tablet. Paper
questionnaires are also available and offered if needed. At each location, an interviewer
approaches every nth voter as they exit the polling place, with a target of at least 50 voters
completing a questionnaire at each precinct. The exact number of questionnaires from each
location depends on voter turnout and respondent cooperation. The number of expected in-
person early-vote questionnaires from Alaska can be found in Table 1.

Quality Control
The pre-election survey includes multiple checks designed to ensure the quality of the final data.
Specifically, the survey includes a question designed to establish that respondents are paying
attention as well as automated checks of missing data and speeding. Cases that fail the
attention-check question, those with over 30% item non-response, and cases with a length less
than 30% of the mean length by mode are flagged and reviewed. Cases are removed from the

Weighting and Combining Samples
The Voter Poll employs a multi-step weighting approach that blends the pre-election survey
samples to be representative of registered voters in each state and then uses voter-file based
statistical models and survey responses to combine the pre-election and Election-Day surveys to
be representative of the estimated 2026 electorate in each state.

Pre-Election Survey Respondents

The RBS sample and the SSRS Opinion Panel Sample are each separately base weighted to
reflect the frame-specific design, selection probabilities into The Voter Poll sample, and
adjustments for nonresponse to The Voter Poll survey. While selection probabilities cannot be
computed for the Verasight sample, Verasight respondents are weighted first to a subset of
demographic and political benchmarks and then combined with the RBS and SSRS Opinion
Panel sample into a single dataset and weighted to match registered voter benchmarks for each
state based on information in the L2 voter file, models based on Census data, and modeled
political characteristics.

Weighting parameters for the registered voter (RV) weight include self-reported demographics
and 2024 vote choice (for validated 2024 voters), along with voter-file based characteristics.
States with larger pre-election respondent samples are weighted to more finely-defined margins
and interactions. Table 2 lists the weighting parameters by state group:

Substate Multilevel Regression and Poststratification
For the seventeen states with the largest Voter Poll samples (AK, AZ, CA, FL, GA, IA, ME, MI, MN,
NC, NE, NH, NV, OH, PA, TX, and WI), the registered voter weight is also calibrated to an
estimate of the 2026 Senate or gubernatorial vote choice by county-based region. Regional
estimates of candidate choice are estimated using multilevel regression and poststratification
(MRP).

Alaska In-Person Early-Vote Exit Poll Respondents
Early-vote (EV) center exit poll data collected in Alaska is weighted separately to voter-file
benchmarks for the EV population by age, gender, race/ethnicity, education, and 2024 recalled
vote. These respondents are then combined with early-voters from the RBS, SSRS Opinion Panel,
and Verasight and adjusted to represent the EV share based on the voter file.

Election Day In-Person Exit Poll Respondents

For in-person Election Day interviews, respondent weights will be adjusted to account for
nonresponse based on interviewer-observed sex, age, and race. These weights are then
calibrated to demographic benchmarks for likely Election-Day voters estimated using voter-file
models and adjusted based on self-reported enthusiasm observed in the pre-election survey
and Election-Day turnout in the sample precincts.

Sampling and Margin of Error

All samples are approximations and subject to, among other factors, statistical error, including
sampling error. Sampling error is affected by sample size, the design of the sample, the
characteristic being measured, and the number of people who have the characteristic. There is
no industry agreed upon approach for calculating margins of error for nonprobability or hybrid
samples. The margins of sampling error reported here are estimates based on the hybrid sample
which includes both probability and non-probability cases.

The margins of sampling error reported provide a general assessment of error ranges that might
be associated with the hybrid data, given the total sample size and weighting variability due to
sampling and calibration.

As with all surveys, The Voter Poll is subject to additional sources of error, including question
wording and order, which could increase the total error. Table 3 reports the estimated margins
of sampling error, at the 95% confidence level, and design effects for the topline results for each
state and nationally. Results for subgroups or questions not asked of the entire sample in a state
will have larger margins of error and wider confidence intervals.

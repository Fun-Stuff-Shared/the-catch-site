Transcript of Prime Minister and Minister for Finance Lawrence Wong's Q&A segment at the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders’ Meeting (ALM) Joint Press Conference with Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese on 24 November 2026.
SBS: I want to raise with both of you the news overnight that Jensen Huang has been reinstated to Nvidia. Firstly Prime Minister Wong, what concerns did Singapore raise with the United States back in 2025 when Singaporean authorities started investigating Alice Huang and Megaspeed for sending Nvidia chips to China? And just as a follow-up to you, what do you say to those analysts who now claim Singapore is the weak link in the AI race between the US and China?
PM: We have made very clear our position. America has export control on its chips. We do not enforce America’s export controls. But we do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies who circumvent America’s export controls. So what we do, is to facilitate checks by America to enforce its own laws. At the same time, we are also very clear we do not want companies to breach Singapore laws. So if there are companies that are caught breaching our laws, we will certainly take very firm enforcement actions. So it is on that basis that we were investigating Megaspeed – which is a Singapore-based entity. And we also were investigating Alice Huang herself. But in the course of our investigations, we were thorough. With Alice Huang, we took her in, we investigated her, but we eventually released her because there was no basis for further prosecution. America understands what we have done.
SBS: Did America do enough to help with the investigation?
PM: We did ask for some information, not all of it was forthcoming. But investigations into Megaspeed are ongoing. With Megaspeed, the chips that they bought from Nvidia, did not even enter Singapore. The chips were routed to other countries. And obviously, we cannot investigate all that is happening in third countries. So we require more information from the US side, and we are continuing to have discussions with American authorities where the end users are, to confirm the verification of various documents. On the Singaporean side, we are focusing on whether Megaspeed might have breached any Singapore laws. And again, we are continuing with our investigations.
CNA: Share with us more about the agreements signed today, how will it impact the everyday lives of Singaporeans back home, and also with the chairmanship next year, welcoming your counterpart – tell us more about how you plan to use Singapore’s chairmanship year to deepen ties with Australia.
PM: Sure, the various agreements that we have signed today, on carbon capture, on AI cooperation, these are all agreements that eventually will mean, ease of doing business for Singaporeans, more opportunities for our companies, more jobs for Singaporeans. And again it will bring our two countries closer together. And we think that will, in the end, create more opportunities for both Singaporeans and Australians. The agreements may not seem like they are things that are immediately relevant to Singaporeans on a day-to-day basis, but essentially, they will open up space for more cooperation and ultimately more opportunities for our peoples. With regard to ASEAN, Australia and ASEAN are long-standing partners. Australia is ASEAN’s oldest dialogue relationship partner. The engagement is deep, but both sides agree, that there is more we can do together; of ASEAN, and of Australia. Singapore, we feel is a very natural partner for Australia in this shared endeavour. Because between our two countries, we share a similar perspective on openness, on free trade, on a rules-based global system and we can work even more closely together. In a way, forerunners. Whatever we do together in our two countries can eventually be scaled across ASEAN. And so whether it is on energy, whether it is on supply chain, whether it is on AI Cooperation, we hope we can show the way between Australia and Singapore first, and eventually have these solutions and cooperation scaled throughout the region.
The Australian: Are you both satisfied that Jensen Huang has not inadvertently assisted AI development in China? Prime Minister, have you sought assurances from the US government on this and what is the point in pursuing global AI regulation when a key leader in the sector allegedly has an affair with a Chinese spy, and there is no sanction?
PM: With regard to Jensen Huang's role, we are not involved in those investigations. It is for the American government to decide, and we leave them to make those investigations and judgment. Nvidia's board will have to do what it needs to do. There may still be ongoing checks. I am not sure, but that is for America to decide; not for, certainly not for Singapore. Where we are concerned, as I mentioned just now, we are partner with the US. We are continuing to do our own investigations where it comes to companies breaching our own laws. We are very clear about that. We are also looking at companies that may be using Singapore and exploiting Singapore's position to tranship and reroute chips through Singapore to other parts of Southeast Asia or to other countries. It is a more complex undertaking because, as I mentioned just now, we do not have full information on where all these chips are sent to; they are not sent to Singapore as a final destination. But the US has growing interest in this area of chips coming through Singapore and being transhipped or rerouted to other countries. We are working very closely with the US administration in that regard to facilitate information sharing, to provide, to work out a mechanism where America can do its own enforcement. So that is the kind of cooperation that is happening between us and America. Where it comes to global AI governance, I think this is an important area for all countries to sit down together. No single country can decide on global AI governance. We all have to work together, and we will continue to do our part in Singapore to facilitate these conversations. Because ultimately, we do need a stronger global framework to ensure the responsible and safe use of AI, and to ensure that we never ever lose control over these very powerful autonomous systems.
ST: My question is for both Prime Ministers. So Senior Minister Lee Hsien Loong, in his Boyer lecture, has urged Australia to deepen its ties with Southeast Asia, and he said that this would give Australia greater room to pursue its own interests with this, you know, amid the US-China rivalry. So my question is, how do both of you see the Singapore-Australian partnership helping Australia deepen its engagement with the region, and beyond the agreements announced today, what more can the two countries do to advance this goal? Thank you.
PM: I could not agree more with what Prime Minister Albanese said. Like Australia, Singapore engages both America and China. I too had very good meetings with both President Trump and President Xi over these past few days. We continue to engage both superpowers. We are friends with both, and certainly we do not want to choose sides. In fact, no country in our region wants to be in a position where we are forced to choose sides. We want to engage both America and China, and importantly, in Southeast Asia, we want to preserve the space for Southeast Asia to have strategic autonomy, to be able to choose our own future, to have the space to manoeuvre, and that requires Southeast Asia to engage all the key parties, key players – not just America and China, but other major powers around the world. And that is why in our chairmanship year we are looking at engaging even more the various key stakeholders and partners that ASEAN has, including Canada, including the EU [European Union], including the Gulf Cooperation Council. And within this context, ASEAN's partnership with Australia is critical. As I mentioned just now, Australia is already a long-standing partner of ASEAN. Australia itself wants to do more with ASEAN. Likewise, ASEAN welcomes Australia's interest to engage the region even more. So I do believe in our chairmanship year, there will be potential for us to bring Australia and ASEAN even more closely together. Bilaterally, we already have a few new initiatives that we announced just now. We will continue to strengthen them, and whatever we do bilaterally, it is not limited to our two countries, but potentially can serve as a lighthouse that can eventually be spread to other parts of Southeast Asia. That has always been the basis of our bilateral cooperation, and we believe between our two countries, we can help show the way for deeper integration of Southeast Asia and deeper integration between ASEAN and Australia. Thank you.

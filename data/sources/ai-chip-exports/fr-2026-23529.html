AGENCY:

Bureau of Industry and Security, Department of Commerce.

ACTION:

Interim final rule.

SUMMARY:

In this interim final rule (IFR), the Department of Commerce is establishing new rules to prevent certain foreign actors from leveraging advanced computing power based on U.S. technology to threaten U.S. national security and foreign policy interests. First, this rule establishes new restrictions which will apply for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau. These restrictions will undermine the ability of companies headquartered in countries of concern from using U.S. advanced Super Intelligence (SI) semiconductors to train advanced SI models. Data centers, foreign foundries, and chip exporters are required to perform robust due diligence on their customers, including any direct or indirect ownership or control by persons in Country Group D:5 or Macau, as part of complying with this new control. The license requirement will not apply to commercial recommender systems, such as those used by online retailers or social media companies, as these functions do not pose the same national security threat. Second, in addition to the current license requirements for advanced semiconductors, a license will be required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil (and any additional country designated by the Secretary of Commerce, in consultation with the Secretary of State or the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs)—countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion. The license requirement for these five countries will not apply to semiconductors destined to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, the United States, provided certain reporting requirements are met. Commerce will be actively monitoring for countries that tolerate evasion of this IFR and will consider a range of consequences for such countries, such as import restrictions, new license requirements on advanced semiconductor exports, downgrading the country's status in the U.S. export control regime, and Entity List designations and temporary export prohibitions for foreign persons involved in such activities. Third, this IFR amends the presumptions which front-end chips fabricators and chip packagers face when exporting advanced-node logic chips. These amendments reduce the number of pathways to overcome the presumptions for chip designers located in destinations which face a license requirement for SI chips. This change gives the U.S. government critical oversight over companies headquartered in certain countries that seek to leverage advanced semiconductor production capabilities in third countries. Finally, the IFR formally rescinds the regulations implementing the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. The AI Diffusion Rule has never been enforced. The regulatory changes in this rule formalize the rescission of the AI Diffusion Rule, which the Department of Commerce Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) announced in May 2025.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Background

In this IFR, BIS is amending the EAR (15 CFR parts 730-774) to establish new controls to ensure that computing power, based on U.S. technology located in foreign data centers, is not used in a manner contrary to U.S. national security or foreign policy interests. This IFR accomplishes this by:

  • Adding an end-use control requiring a license for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) of advanced SI [1] items when there is “knowledge” the items are destined for use in data centers, wherever located, that will provide direct or indirect access to a significant volume of computing power to or for an entity headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or destination specified in Country Group D:5. License applications will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis. These changes are contained in new § 744.24(a)(1). In addition, if subsequent to the original ( printed page 74971) export of advanced SI semiconductors to an overseas data center, that data center starts providing, directly or indirectly, significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered, or with an ultimate parent headquartered, in Macau or Country Group D:5, the owner, operator of the overseas data center, or other party making the transfer will be required to obtain a license. Any data center providing significant volume of compute power to companies headquartered, or with an ultimate parent headquartered, in Macau or Country Group D:5 prior to the publication date of this rule that wishes to continue providing these services must obtain a license with 90 days of the effective date of this IFR or face future export restrictions. However, the license requirement will not apply to `commercial recommender systems,' such as those used by online retailers and social media companies, as these functions do not pose the same national security threat.

This new control is a logical extension of existing controls. At present, a license is required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) advanced SI semiconductors for entities headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country Group D:5 or Macau. Through this rule, BIS is imposing restrictions that will undermine those entities' ability to access significant volumes of advanced SI semiconductors remotely, including through connected cloud computing.

  • Adding a destination-based license requirement for advanced SI items for destinations for which there is an unacceptable risk of diversion—which for the purposes of this IFR are Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil, and Thailand. See amendments to § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A)(1).
  • Rescinding the worldwide Regional Stability (RS) license requirement and other regulatory changes implemented by the AI Diffusion rule, “Framework for Artificial Intelligence Diffusion” published January 15, 2025 (90 FR 4544) (“AI Diffusion Rule”).

By making these changes, this IFR prevents the use of certain SI items in data centers and at a computing power level that can be used for training or running of advanced SI models. Access to such computing power can be used to train and run advanced SI models capable of supporting military- and intelligence-related activities that could threaten U.S. national security or foreign policy interests, e.g., using advanced SI for developing more effective weapons of mass destruction and advanced conventional weapons, coordinated cyber-espionage and targeted cyber-disruption, algorithmic theft, and weaponized disinformation.

Specifically, BIS is enhancing and streamlining the licensing policy on items controlled under Export Control Classifications Numbers (ECCNs) 3A090 and 4A090 and the corresponding .z items by using a combination of more focused and effective destination-based controls and enhanced end-use controls. This IFR amends the EAR to reflect a revision to the license review policy for these items in parts 742 (Regional stability) and 744 (Control Policy: End-User and End-Use Based) to simplify the licensing process for SI integrated circuits (ICs) and other SI-related items. The changes promulgated in this IFR follow extensive U.S. government consideration of the impact of these controls on U.S. national security and foreign policy interests.

II. License Requirements and Review Policy

Changes to § 742.6 Regional Stability

This IFR revises § 742.6 to impose new license requirements and licensing policies for advance computing items identified in paragraph (a)(6)(iii)(A).

First, under § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A)( 1), a license is required for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) to or within Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil, and Thailand. As noted in section I of this preamble, these countries are being added in response to concerns that advanced computing items subject to EAR licensing requirements are being diverted to entities and countries of concern without authorization. See section III of this preamble for a discussion of those controls.

The license requirements for items described in § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A)( 1) do not apply to exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) to an ultimate consignee or end user headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, the United States, provided the reporting requirements of new § 743.10, described in section VI of this preamble, are met by the exporter, reexporter, or transferor. This is explained under new Note 1 to paragraph (a)(6)(iii)(A)( 1) of § 742.6.

Second, under § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A)( 2), this IFR amends the worldwide licensing requirement to require a license for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) to or within destinations specified in Country Groups D:1, D:4, and D:5, excluding destinations specified in Country Groups A:5 or A:6, as well as the United Arab Emirates—to the extent a license would otherwise be required by the final rule “Enhanced Favorable Treatment for the United Arab Emirates Under the Export Administration Regulations” (91 FR 43034, July 14, 2026).

For the license requirements under paragraph (a)(6)(iii)(A)( 1) of § 742.6, applications for exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis to determine whether the transaction is contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States, as described under § 742.6(b)(10)(iii)(A)( 1). Pursuant to § 742.6(b)(10)(iii)(A)( 1), destinations will be evaluated on the effectiveness of their export control regimes and their coordination with BIS on the enforcement of export controls.

III. End-Use Restrictions

A. Changes to Part 744—Control Policy: End-User and End-Use Based

This IFR adds new § 744.24 Restrictions on exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of certain advanced computing items for use in data centers for end-uses and destinations of concern. Section 744.24 provides, in addition to the license requirements set forth elsewhere in the EAR, a prohibition on exports, reexports, or transfers (in-county) of advanced SI items without a license if, at the time of export, reexport, or transfer (in-country), you have “knowledge” that the item will be or is intended to be used in a data center end-use that is prohibited under paragraphs (a)(1) of § 744.24.

You may not knowingly export, reexport, or transfer (in-county) items specified in paragraph (a) of § 744.24 for use in a data center to or within any destination worldwide that will provide access to more than 12,000,000 “aggregate TPP” (either at one data center or through `linked data centers' (including digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked infrastructure) to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5 in supplement no. 1 to part 740 of the EAR. `Linked data centers' are data centers that use networking infrastructure that links two or more separate data centers together to allow physical and virtual facilities to share resources, balance workloads, and replicate data as a unified whole. This prohibition is described under § 744.24(a)(1) and the definition is provided in § 744.24(d). The term “for an entity” as used in this § 744.24(a)(1) ( printed page 74972) applies to instances in which there is “knowledge” that a data center is providing remote access to a non-D:5 customer that is in turn training SI models for, or participating in the training of SI models for, or providing SI models to a D:5 company. This control will cut off additional potential diversion pathways and evasion efforts.

These controls are necessary because providing computer power access at this level or greater will allow for training or running of advanced SI models; the Secretary of Commerce has determined that it could pose a national security and foreign policy threat to the United States when such computing power access will be provided to or for companies headquartered, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5—unless the computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender systems. Access to advanced computing power presents significant national security and foreign policy concerns, primarily by enabling foreign countries or other entities of concern to accelerate the development of weapons of mass destruction (WMD), execute sophisticated zero-day cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, and disrupt global military balances of power—unless the computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender systems.

Consistent with the national security and foreign policy concerns outlined in this IFR, BIS is imposing a notification and authorization requirement for subsequent changes in end-use. This requirement, provided in new paragraph (e) of § 744.24, and described in more detail further in this section, is triggered if there is a change in end-use of the items described in paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. In the event of such a change, a BIS authorization may be required. Relatedly, Note 1 to paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24 provides that the prohibition applies regardless of whether the person in possession of the EAR item was a party to the transaction to the original export, reexport, or transfer (in-country), i.e., the original shipment of the items to the data center. Because this EAR end-use control under § 744.24(a)(1) applies to any subsequent reexport or transfer (in-country), including changing from a non-prohibited end-use to a prohibited end-use, it is important that parties in possession of these items are aware of the need to review § 744.24(a)(1) for any subsequent reexport or transfer (in-country).

The prohibition described in paragraph (a)(1) applies regardless of whether a U.S.-origin item specified under paragraph (a) of § 744.24 has been incorporated or commingled into or with a foreign-made item. Reexporters and transferors may not rely on the de minimis provisions for incorporating or commingling U.S.-origin items specified under paragraph (a) of § 744.24 into foreign-made items where there is “knowledge” at any point in the chain from the development, production, or use of the foreign-made item that the U.S.-origin item will be used in this prohibited end-use under paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. This prohibition under § 744.24 is described under Note 2 to paragraph (a)(1).

Note 3 to paragraph (a)(1) describes due diligence expectations imposed on “U.S. persons” and foreign data centers to ensure compliance with these controls. Foreign data centers will be expected to perform robust due diligence on their customers to identify direct or indirect ownership or control by Country Group D:5 or Macau persons. This diligence will ensure that companies cannot circumvent new controls through corporate restructurings or spinoffs.

This IFR adds an “is informed” process under paragraph (a)(2) of § 744.24 to impose an additional prohibition on persons informed by BIS that a license is required for the export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) of specified items to certain persons or destinations, because there is an unacceptable risk of use in, or diversion to, the activities specified in paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. Notifications made under paragraph (a)(2) that there is an unacceptable risk of use in, or diversion to an end use specified in § 744.24 may be made either individually by specific notice or through amendment to the EAR.

As described under § 744.24(b), no license exceptions may overcome the prohibition described in paragraph (a)(2) of § 744.24, except when noted by BIS. The prohibitions under § 744.24 do not apply when the computing power access is being provided exclusively for a `commercial recommender system' under the (b)(2) exception provided the reporting requirements described therein are followed.

Applications for licenses required by § 744.24 will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis as described under § 744.24(c).

This IFR adds paragraph (d) to define for purposes of § 744.24 the terms: `linked data centers'; and `commercial recommender system':

For purposes of § 744.24, `linked data center' means a data center that uses networking infrastructure that links two or more separate data centers together that allows physical and virtual facilities to share resources, balance workloads, and replicate data as a unified whole. This includes digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked infrastructure.

For purposes of § 744.24, `commercial recommender system' means a Super Intelligence (SI) tool exclusively designed and exclusively used to rank, filter, or suggest commercial products, services, or content to users based on user behavior, transaction history, or item attributes. This term does not include any SI system designed or used to generate novel text, image, audio, video, software, or other content in response to user inputs, or any system that incorporates such model, regardless of whether the system also performs recommendation functions.

This IFR adds paragraph (e) to impose a notification and authorization requirement for subsequent changes in end-use. New paragraph (e) specifies that for any export, reexport, or transfer made on or after November 19, 2026 without a license, i.e., because the exporter, reexporter, or transferor had no “knowledge” that the item is for a prohibited end-use under this section and no other EAR license requirement applies, the exporter, reexporter, or transferor must inform the recipient that BIS authorization is required if the item will be transferred in-country; and that the recipient must follow the know-your-customer guidance in supplement no. 3 Part 732 of the EAR. The notification also must specify that the transferor, e.g., the data center operator, must obtain authorization for this change in end-use. Changes in end-use from a non-prohibited end-use to a prohibited end-use under paragraphs (a)(1) are considered a “transfer (in-country)” for purposes of paragraphs (a)(1) or (2) and require an EAR authorization under § 744.24.

B. Conforming Change to 744.23 for Removal of Worldwide License Requirement in This IFR

This IFR, as described under section II of this preamble, removes the worldwide license requirement from former § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A). As a conforming change, this IFR makes revisions to the advanced computing items end-use control under § 744.23(a)(3) to ensure that a license will continue to be required for exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of these advanced computing items when such items are destined for entities headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country Group D:5 or Macau. ( printed page 74973)

Specifically, this IFR revises § 744.23 “Supercomputer,” “advanced-node integrated circuits,” and semiconductor manufacturing equipment end-use controls under paragraph (a)(3)(i)(A) to specify that this end-use control for advanced computing items includes all advanced computing items ( e.g., those specified in ECCNs 3A090 (except for 3A090.c), 4A090, and related .z paragraph items). Prior to this IFR, there was an enforced license requirement under § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A) of the EAR that applied to all destinations outside of the United States for these advanced computing items when such items are destined to entities headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country Group D:5 or Macau. See the May 31, 2026, “Guidance Regarding Enforcement of License Requirements for Advanced Computing Items for Entities Headquartered in Country Group D:5 and Macau” on the BIS website at http://www.bis.gov. With the removal of the worldwide license requirement this IFR makes a required conforming change to return the license requirement for these advanced computing items to § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(A).

For § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(A), BIS has received inquiries regarding the meaning of the phrase “for an entity” that is headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, either Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5. As stated in BIS's October 2023 Advanced Computing/Supercomputing (“AC/S”) IFR (88 FR 73458) this control was intended to ensure that the national security and foreign policy objectives of the October 7, 2022 IFR and the AC/S IFR are not undermined by Macau, the People's Republic of China (PRC), or other Country Group D:5 headquartered entities setting up cloud servers or data centers in other countries to allow these entities of concern to continue to train their SI models in ways that would be contrary to U.S. national security and foreign policy interests. This end-use control thus is not, and has never been limited only to exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) destined to such entities of concern as ultimate consignees or end users, but extends to exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) on behalf of these entities of concern, e.g., if an entity of concern was the purchaser in the transaction (directly or indirectly) or was the exclusive or primary (greater than 50 percent of total compute) remote end user of the data center computing capacity. Exporters, reexporters, and transferors are encouraged to examine both end-use controls in § 744.23(a)(3) and in new § 744.24 to the extent their transactions involve exports, reexports, or transfers to data centers outside of the United States where there is knowledge that a company headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered in, Macau or D:5 will be an intended remote end user. Any entities in violation of this requirement should submit a voluntary self-disclosure (see Section 764.5 of the EAR). Bona fide operators of data centers who are otherwise engaged in activities consistent with the EAR and were providing remote access used for `commercial recommender systems' (as defined in the new § 744.24(d)(1)) may continue providing these services for 90 days, but must obtain a license following expiration of this period to continue these services. Finally, § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(C) is added to create a license requirement for “applicable advanced node integrated circuits” that are subject to the EAR, specified in 3A090.a, designed by a company that is neither an approved IC designer (specified in supplement no. 6 to part 740) or an authorized IC designer (as described in Note 1 to 3A090.a), and exported, exported from abroad, reexported, or transferred (in-country) by a “front end fabricator” or “OSAT” to or within any destination. This addition ensures that a license requirement continues to apply to “front end fabricators” and “OSATs” creating advanced ICs for non-approved, non-authorized IC designers wherever located. Additionally, the presumption created in Note 1 to 3A090.a continues to apply but can be overcome by the “front end fabricator” or “OSAT” if they follow the due diligence steps specified in that note. BIS notes that the inclusion of the phrase “exported from aboard” in this new paragraph is simply descriptive and not intended to indicate that the activity of exporting from destinations other than the United States is not captured in provisions of the EAR where that phrase is omitted.

D. Foreign-Direct Product Rules

BIS is retaining the worldwide destination scope of the advanced computing foreign direct product (FDP) rule in § 734.9(h)(2)(i) if there is “knowledge” that a foreign-produced item is destined worldwide or will be incorporated into any “part,” “component,” “computer,” or “equipment” not designated EAR99 to any location worldwide. Retention of this provision ensures the prohibitions of relevant controls are not otherwise limited.

Though BIS is not making any changes to § 734.9(h), BIS notes that the agency has received questions about the application of that paragraph. Consistent with the robust controls implemented in this IFR, BIS notes that provision's expansive application. If a foreign-produced product is subject to the EAR pursuant to § 734.9(h), BIS retains jurisdiction of that item after its incorporation into another foreign-produced item, as provided by the text of § 734.9(h)(2)(i). For example, if an ECCN 3A090 IC is incorporated into a foreign-produced “part,” “component,” “computer,” or “equipment” (such as a GPU board or server), BIS continues to have jurisdiction over the IC and license requirements may apply to the export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) of the IC (and by extension, the server). Generally, BIS does not retain jurisdiction over items after incorporation into other items, absent the applicability of the EAR's de minimis provisions. But in this context, specifically, in the relevant ECCN entries (ECCN 4A090 and those ECCNs with a .z paragraph), the CCL notes that such items only meet the specifications for those ECCNs if they contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Thus, even third parties who only receive a 4A090 item are on notice that such items contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Furthermore, the retention of BIS's jurisdiction under § 734.9(h) after incorporation is reflected by past BIS guidance to inform parties in the supply chain of the applicability of the FDP rule, including the Model Certification for Purposes of the FDP Rule of supplement no. 1 to part 734 of the EAR.

In addition, consistent with the removal of ECCN 4E091, this IFR removes the AI Model weights FDP rule in § 734.9(l).

E. License Exception Advanced Compute Manufacturing ACM

BIS is retaining License Exception ACM under § 740.28 due to the benefits this license exception plays in supporting the supply chains of export, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of advanced SI items destined to ultimate end users outside of Macau or destinations in Country Group D:5.

Technical Note 2 to 3A090.a and 3A090.b:

1. `Total processing performance' (`TPP') is 2 × `MacTOPS' × `bit length of the operation', aggregated over all processing units on the integrated circuit.

a. For purposes of 3A090, `MacTOPS' is the theoretical peak number of Tera (1012 ) operations per second for multiply-accumulate computation (D = A × B + C).

b. The 2 in the `TPP' formula is based on industry convention of counting one multiply-accumulate computation, D = A × B + C, as 2 operations for purpose of datasheets. Therefore, 2 × MacTOPS may correspond to the reported TOPS or FLOPS on a datasheet.

c. For purposes of 3A090, `bit length of the operation' for a multiply-accumulate computation is the largest bit-length of the inputs to the multiply operation.

d. Aggregate the TPPs for each processing unit on the integrated circuit to arrive at a total. `TPP' = TPP1 + TPP2 + . . . . + TPPn (where n is the number or processing units on the integrated circuit).

2. The rate of `MacTOPS' is to be calculated at its maximum value theoretically possible. The rate of `MacTOPS' is assumed to be the highest value the manufacturer claims in a manual or brochure for the integrated circuit. For example, the `TPP' threshold of 4800 can be met with 600 tera integer operations (or 2 × 300 `MacTOPS') at 8 bits or 300 tera FLOPS (or 2 × 150 `MacTOPS') at 16 bits. If the integrated circuit is designed for MAC computation with multiple bit lengths that achieve different `TPP' values, the highest `TPP' value should be evaluated against parameters in 3A090.

3. For integrated circuits specified by 3A090 that provide processing of both sparse and dense matrices, the `TPP' values are the values for processing of dense matrices (e.g., without sparsity).

4. `Performance density' is `TPP' divided by `applicable die area'. For purposes of 3A090, `applicable die area' is measured in millimeters squared and includes all die area of logic dies manufactured with a process node that uses a non-planar transistor architecture.

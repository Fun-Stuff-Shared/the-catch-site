Q3 data center revenue increased 21% quarter-over-quarter to $108 billion, with balanced growth across hyperscale and AI cloud, industrial, and enterprise subsegments. Hyperscale revenue of $60 billion grew 23% sequentially, driven by the Vera Rubin ramp and sustained strength in Grace Blackwell. Our hyperscale customers' cloud revenue growth accelerated for the sixth consecutive quarter. Cloud industry backlog now sits at $2.5 trillion, up from $2 trillion just three months ago, underscoring the significant multi-year opportunity ahead of our partners and of NVIDIA.

As frontier models become more capable, adoption and revenue are scaling rapidly, with Anthropic's annualized revenue reaching $81 billion at the end of October. We expect the major frontier labs to contribute approximately 15% of our data center revenue this year. Open models are accelerating AI adoption, and weekly token volume on OpenRouter has surged 35x year-over-year and 3.5x in just this past three months. Agentic workloads now account for approximately 80% of tokens. A rapidly expanding ecosystem of inference providers is bringing these models to companies everywhere. At NVIDIA, we are committed to fostering a vibrant open model ecosystem so every country, company, and industry can build its own intelligence.

Together, we will scale its platform and make intelligence more open, more capable, and more accessible. Nemotron is now the most widely used U.S. open model on OpenRouter and is where developers start to build their own intelligence. That foundation is empowering the ecosystem. This past quarter, leading enterprise software and security companies, including Microsoft, Salesforce, Palantir, CrowdStrike, Cadence, and Synopsys announced products that use post-trained open models tailored to domain-specific use cases. Training partners such as Scale, Mercor, Prime Intellect, and Applied Compute are helping enterprises turn open models into their own using their data, tools, and workflows.

And startups across specialized domains, including Lila Sciences, Harvey, Therma, and Abridge, are using post-trained open models to deliver results in science, law, and healthcare. All of this is creating a powerful flywheel. Token prices have fallen nearly 60% since May, accelerating token usage and demand for compute. As a result, GPU rental prices are rising across generations. Hopper GPUs have recontracted at up to twice their original price under the multi-year agreements, while the B300 rates have risen more than 60% since their introduction. NVIDIA Compute remains fully utilized across every hyperscaler and neocloud we serve, underscoring the productivity, durability, and fungibility of our infrastructure. As agents run longer and act with greater autonomy, organizations need stronger controls to ensure safety and security. The NVIDIA Open Agent Safety Platform, which we announced alongside more than 100 partners, enable these controls.

OpenShell contains agents by limiting their access to files and tools, while NVIDIA Sentry, running on BlueField-4, monitors the behavior in silicon and stops agents for overstepping limits in milliseconds. OpenShell is already gaining broad ecosystem support from partners across AI models, enterprise software, cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, and robotics. Companies working with NVIDIA include Anthropic, AWS, CoreWeave, CrowdStrike, Dell, Google Cloud, HPE, IREN, JPMorgan Chase, Lenovo, Microsoft, Nebius, OpenAI, and SpaceXSI . Advances in agent harnesses are accelerating the adoption of agentic AI. Meta's Muse agent, launched on NVIDIA's infrastructure, surpassed 10 million setups in the United States and Canada within the first six weeks. To support this massive scale and future growth, Meta intends to expand its deployment of Vera Rubin hardware, with the goal of maximizing performance and efficiency for its next generation of AI agents. Agentic AI is driving extraordinary demand for CPUs, far outstripping supply.

NVIDIA Vera CPU meets the growing demand, delivering leading performance at data center processing and scientific simulation workloads. CoreWeave, Meta, Nscale, and OpenAI have now adopted Vera, joining AWS, OCI, and SpaceXSI , the leading partners we announced last quarter. We continue to see very strong demand for Vera and expect total CPU revenue to more than double in fiscal year 2028. AI Clouds, Industrial, and Enterprise, or ACIE, revenue of $47 billion increased 18% sequentially and nearly tripled year-over-year. Sequential growth was driven by Vera Rubin shipments to top leading neocloud customers and the start of our frontier labs building their own data centers. Neoclouds remain an increasingly important part of the global AI infrastructure ecosystem, providing additional customers with access to NVIDIA Compute and expanding the reach of our platform.

We have supported the development of this ecosystem, and we will continue to be disciplined and selective in our support. NVIDIA's full-stack AI technology platform uniquely positions us to address opportunities across sovereign, enterprise, and Physical AI. Sovereign AI revenue more than tripled year-over-year in Q3. In September, we announced a collaboration with multiple NVIDIA cloud partners in Australia to develop up a 2 GW of land, power, and shell capacity by the end of calendar 2027, with an opportunity to host multiple generations of NVIDIA DSX AI factories. In enterprise, financial services delivered the strongest growth, with on-prem revenue reaching nearly $5 billion on a trailing 12-month basis and more than doubled year-over-year. As more customers leverage NVIDIA infrastructure to accelerate quantitative trading, we expect enterprise revenue to be more than double in fiscal year 2028.

Physical AI revenue nearly tripled year-over-year in Q3 and is now on track to exceed $25 billion for the full year. At GTC Berlin, we expanded our NVIDIA Isaac robotics platform with a new humanoid reference design built on Isaac GR00T and Jetson Thor. In automotive, NVIDIA Hyperion and Alpamayo are enabling Mercedes-Benz and Bolt with Lucid to build robotaxi fleets, and Waabi to develop level four autonomous trucks, and Volvo Cars to advance safe automated driving. Q3 edge computing revenue of $8.2 billion increased 14% sequentially and 42% year-over-year. Sequential growth was driven by stronger enterprise demand for Blackwell workstations and edge compute for Physical AI applications. Q3 sales of licensed Hopper data center products to China was $2.4 billion.

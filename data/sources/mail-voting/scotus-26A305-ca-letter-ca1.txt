       RE:    United States Postal Service, et al. v. California, et al., No. 26A305

       Dear Mr. Harris:

               Today, the First Circuit issued an order denying defendants’ and intervenors’ requested
       stay of the preliminary injunction. With respect to the likelihood of success on the merits, the
       First Circuit reasoned that Congress did not authorize the challenged USPS rule through “the
       ‘wafer-thin reed’ of a general grant of authority to manage the postal system.” Order at 6
       (quoting Biden v. Nebraska, 600 U.S. 477, 499 (2023)). As to the equities, the First Circuit
       observed that the injunction is narrow, id. at 8 n.4, and emphasized that defendants and
       intervenors “have not even seriously challenged” the district court’s “detailed findings about the
       chaos and widespread disenfranchisement that would occur between now and November 3
       should the Final Rule take immediate effect, including the fact that it would be impossible for
       some States to” comply with the rule in time for the upcoming election, id. at 6; see also Br. of
       Amici Curiae Current and Former Election Officials in Support of Neither Party at 1-2, No.
       26A305 (U.S.) (explaining that “the work necessary” by “state and local election officials” to
       implement the rule “cannot be responsibly accomplished in the time that remains” before the
       November 2026 election).

A+ A- Story Highlights Michael Burry says the market could be nearing “a major top” despite fresh record highs. He continues to bet against Nvidia, Micron, Palantir, Tesla, and other AI-related stocks. The investor believes lower volatility could bring more money into the market before a sharp reversal. Michael Burry , the investor best known for The Big Short , is sticking with his bearish outlook even as the stock market continues to climb. In a new Substack post, Burry warned that it is “possible we are near a major top, and possible a 1987-type fall.” He continued to maintain his bearish positions in the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) and several AI-related stocks, including Nvidia (NVDA) , Micron (MU) , Palantir (PLTR) , Tesla (TSLA) , Applied Materials (AMAT) , and Caterpillar (CAT) . The comments came after the S&P 500 (SPX) closed at another record high on Tuesday , while the Nasdaq 100 (NDX) posted one of its strongest rallies in months. Even so, Burry said the latest gains have not changed his long-term view. Burry Sees Echoes of Earlier Market Peaks Burry pointed to a rare market pattern highlighted by BTIG analyst Jonathan Krinsky . He noted that the S&P 500 has surged 5% over four trading days to a new high only three other times. Two of those periods were closely tied to the dot-com boom. Still, Burry said the broader market is not his main focus. “What really matters is the SOX, and the Momentum trade,” he wrote. He noted that both came under pressure in July and said investors should watch whether semiconductor stocks and momentum trades can regain market leadership. Bearish Bets Stay in Place Despite the market’s rally, Burry said he is not backing away from his bearish positions. “I continue to believe it is possible we are near a major top, and possible a 1987-type fall,” he wrote. Even so, Burry said the rally could continue for a while. He believes record highs could attract more investors, while calmer markets may encourage large funds to take on more risk. That could push stocks even higher before the trend reverses. He also said the Goldman Sachs Momentum Pair Trade remains under pressure after its strong run. He added that the VIX/VIXEQ remains near a 12-year low, suggesting the market appears calmer than it really is. According to Burry, many volatility-focused funds are still “licking wounds” from July. Burry added that he continues to hold his short positions. However, he noted that all of those trades remain profitable except for his bearish bet against Nvidia. “Again, shorting is not for everyone,” Burry wrote. “I must short. Most should not.” Which Stock Does Wall Street Like the Most? According to TipRanks’ Stock Comparison Tool , Applied Materials, Micron, and Nvidia carry Strong Buy consensus ratings. Meanwhile, Palantir and Caterpillar have Moderate Buy ratings, while Tesla is rated Hold. Among the group, Micron offers the highest implied upside based on Wall Street’s average price target at 75.9%. Nvidia follows with 45.7% upside, while Caterpillar has the lowest implied upside at 16.8%.

Photograph: Gary Cameron/Reuters Donald Trump Trump signs new orders targeting birthright citizenship Executive orders come after supreme court rejected president’s first bid to restrict guarantee enshrined in US constitution Robert Mackey and Guardian staff Thu 6 Aug 2026 17.08 EDT Last modified on Thu 6 Aug 2026 17.10 EDT Share Prefer the Guardian on Google Donald Trump on Thursday signed two executive orders that the White House said aims to restrict eligibility for birthright citizenship and ban “birth tourism”. The ‌orders come just over a month after the United States supreme court rejected Trump’s first attempt to restrict the guarantee of birthright citizenship enshrined in the US constitution. Trump has long sought to dismantle the principle of birthright citizenship, making a top priority of his second-term immigration crackdown. What to know about the US supreme court ruling on birthright citizenship Read more The orders signed on Thursday expand the definitions ​of children the White House deems ineligible ⁠for birthright ​citizenship and ​seek to ban “birth ​tourism”, which the president told reporters on Thursday is practiced by hundreds of thousands of people. There According to the Migration Policy Institute, the highest census-based estimate “puts birth tourism at 22,000 to 26,000 babies born annually on US soil, although government data show just 9,600 births to mothers with foreign addresses in 2024”. Trump’s father, Fred Trump, was born a US citizen to a non-citizen mother who arrived in New York five months pregnant.

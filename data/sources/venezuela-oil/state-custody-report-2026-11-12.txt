# 1. Executive Overview

## 1.1 Introduction

This report provides a comprehensive account of the Venezuelan natural resource revenues deposited in, held by, and disbursed from the U.S. Treasury Venezuela Custody Account (also referred to as the Venezuela Custody Account), a deposit fund account established consistent with Executive Order (E.O.) 14373, “Safeguarding Venezuelan Oil Revenue for the Good of the American and Venezuelan People.”$^{1}$ It documents the financial oversight, governance, and control mechanisms implemented by the U.S. Department of State (DoS) and its contracted monitor and advisor, KPMG, to promote transparency and accountability in the management of these funds.

The Venezuela Financial Transparency Program reviews deposit receipts, fund administration, compliance activities, and disbursement processes associated with the Venezuela Custody Account. This report provides visibility into how funds are deposited, monitored, managed, and disbursed in accordance with established authorization, compliance, and governance protocols to support approved macroeconomic, public-sector, humanitarian, and operational purposes. Not all these processes and the safeguards described below have been in place since January 2026; the process has been iterative and DoS and KPMG have adjusted, refined, and added to these processes over time. All funds and natural resource proceeds administered under this program constitute sovereign assets of the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, held and managed exclusively for the benefit of the Venezuelan people.

This report covers January 3, 2026, to September 30, 2026, and may be updated to reflect future data and enhanced monitoring capabilities, including centralized data repositories, automated reconciliation, and advanced analytics. Any subsequent report will integrate newly available transaction-level records, risk assessments, and compliance findings as these data governance and modernization initiatives mature.

For analytical clarity, the report progresses from strategic and financial review to operational oversight and compliance activities. The sections that follow include a financial summary (traceability), review of the Venezuela Custody Account transaction activity, vetting and forensic reviews, institutional oversight and governance descriptions, and a forward outlook. Together, these sections provide an integrated view of the controls, monitoring activities, and governance processes supporting the administration of funds within the Venezuela Custody Account framework.

During the first nine months of operation, the U.S. Government established custodial control over Venezuelan natural resource revenue deposits, implemented multi-layered authorization and compliance controls, and monitored the distribution of funds through approved economic and public-sector channels. More than $12 billion was deposited and disbursed under a controlled governance structure designed to promote transparency, accountability, and protection of sovereign assets.$^{2}$

## 2. Program Framework and Operating Environment

### 2.1 Background

On January 9, 2026, President Donald J. Trump issued E.O. 14373, pursuant to authorities including the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the National Emergencies Act.³ The Order established protections governing specified Venezuelan government revenues held by the United States, defined as “Foreign Government Deposit Funds” (FGDF), which include funds derived from commodity-sale proceeds of Venezuelan natural resources from the Government of Venezuela or its agencies or instrumentalities. Under the Order, covered funds are held by the U.S. Treasury in a designated Venezuela Custody Account. The funds remain sovereign property of Venezuela and are protected from attachment or other judicial process and may not be transferred, withdrawn, or otherwise dealt in except as authorized. The funds are held pending disposition for public, governmental, or diplomatic purposes as determined by the Secretary of State, with the Secretary of the Treasury responsible for their administration and for executing authorized disbursements or transfers.⁴

**Operating Environment & Challenges.** Venezuela’s vast natural resources, particularly its oil reserves, have historically served as the primary engine of the nation’s economy and principal source of U.S. Dollar (USD) income. Over time, sanctions, international legal disputes, governance challenges, and financial-control deficiencies significantly constrained traditional fund flows and Venezuela’s access to international financial markets. These conditions increased risks related to the receipt, management, and distribution of resource-derived funds, creating the need for enhanced oversight mechanisms to increase transparency, preserve economic continuity, safeguard critical revenue streams, and support the delivery of essential public services.

**Program Rationale & Controlled Structure.** Certain OFAC licenses (e.g., General Licenses 46E, 48D, 50C, 51D, 52C, and 54C) direct firms to deposit payments to the Government of Venezuela, or its agencies or instrumentalities, made in exchange for authorized activities involving Venezuela-origin natural resources in the Venezuela Custody Account. The Venezuela Custody Account mechanism serves as a controlled deposit management structure under which payments from authorized activities involving Venezuelan natural resources are used for authorized macroeconomic, public-sector, humanitarian, and operational purposes. Like escrow, trust, and other controlled holding-account arrangements used in financial oversight environments, this approach provides heightened transparency into the management and release of funds by allowing for transaction monitoring and other compliance controls. Authorized funds are subsequently disbursed from the Venezuela Custody Account to designated transaction accounts, or approved third-party accounts, at the direction of the Banco Central de Venezuela (BCV), following transaction review, approval and authorization procedures. The BCV administers allocations and disbursements to approved downstream entities, including commercial banks, and payments on behalf of government entities and public service organizations. This structure is intended to support macroeconomic stability and the restoration of critical governmental functions and key sectors, including energy, infrastructure, healthcare, and public services, while promoting accountability and stewardship of public resources.

**Program Oversight & Governance Controls.** To support the accountable administration of public funds, requests for disbursement are subject to a structured authorization and compliance review process administered under DoS protocols supported by KPMG. The BCV, on behalf of the interim Venezuelan government, submits disbursement requests to the DoS detailing the intended use of funds for approved governmental, operational, and public-interest objectives. Through controlled disbursement, transaction monitoring, and defined oversight mechanisms, the program promotes transparency, guards against unauthorized use of funds, strengthens financial controls, and supports restoration of Venezuela’s institutional capacity and economic sustainability.

This approach is designed to balance immediate operational continuity and the execution of essential and time-sensitive disbursements with the compliance, transparency, and oversight mechanisms necessary to safeguard sovereign resources and support long-term institutional development.

### 2.3 Summary

The Venezuela Custody Account serves as the central custody and oversight mechanism for revenues generated from activities related to Venezuelan natural resources. The oversight structure provides transparency into how funds are collected, managed, authorized, safeguarded, and disbursed to advance approved economic and humanitarian objectives.

During the period covered by this report (January 3 – September 30, 2026), the Venezuela Custody Account received deposits totaling more than $12.26 billion from verified and approved entities engaging in authorized activities related to Venezuelan natural resources. While crude oil and petroleum-related deposits represented the largest source of inflows, the account also received approved deposits associated with gold, mineral, and fertilizer transactions. Of the total amount, $12.11 billion (98.7%) was disbursed from the Venezuela Custody Account, while $168.56 million remained in custody as of September 30, 2026, for future authorized disbursements pursuant to requests by the BCV on behalf of the interim Government of Venezuela.

### 3. Venezuela Custody Account Summary (January – September 2026)

#### 3.1 U.S. Treasury Venezuela Custody Account and Historical Custody Account Overview

To provide verifiable oversight of Venezuelan natural resource revenues, the U.S. government established a centralized custody account to safeguard sovereign assets and enable deposits to be approved, tracked, and protected from unauthorized use prior to their approved disbursement.

Implementation of this approach was executed in two distinct phases. During the initial phase, through January 2026, for safeguarding purposes, deposit payments made by firms in exchange for the sale of Venezuela-origin natural resources were deposited into a temporary custody account of the BCV’s at a commercial offshore banking institution, the Qatar National Bank (QNB), subject to custody controls described in Section 3.1.1 below. By late January 2026, custody account operations transitioned to the U.S. Treasury Venezuela Custody Account.

The following subsections summarize the purpose, activity, and disposition of each of these custody accounts and provide a detailed overview of the transition from the interim arrangement to the active custody account structure. Collectively, they illustrate the volume of natural resource revenues deposited and the amounts disbursed through approved channels.

##### 3.1.1 Phase 1: Interim Custody Account (January 2026)

In January 2026, a temporary custody account of the BCV’s at QNB was established to hold deposit payments from the initial U.S.-facilitated sales of Venezuelan oil. Use of the Qatari bank provided a short-term, exigent solution to facilitate the urgent sale of oil that had accumulated in storage and was at risk of creating permanent shut-in of Venezuelan oil production, and to avoid creditor attachment while holding the initial Venezuelan oil deposit payments until the U.S.-based mechanism was established. Similar to the protection from creditor attachment subsequently established by E.O. 14373, this approach prevented creditors from disrupting access to the revenues and allowed for continued availability of funds to address Venezuela’s critical economic and governmental needs. This interim mechanism provided emergency liquidity and oversight of $500 million in initial deposits.

Following receipt of the initial $500 million in deposits, funds were allocated and disbursed to participating Venezuelan financial institutions through a legacy Foreign Exchange Auction process described in Section 7.2.1. On January 15, 2026, $300 million was disbursed to four participating Venezuelan financial institutions: Mercantil C.A. Banco Universal, Banco Provincial (BBVA), Banesco Banco Universal, and Banco Nacional de Credito (BNC). Each institution received $75 million to facilitate the exchange of VES.$^{7}$

Additional authorized disbursements of $163.95 million and $36 million followed on January 23 and January 28, 2026, respectively, bringing total disbursements to $499.95 million. As of September 30, $50,000 remains in the Interim Qatar Custodial Account.

## 4. The Process

### 4.1 The Flow of Funds

![img-3.jpeg](img-3.jpeg)

Figure 4: The Flow of Funds

To provide financial visibility, the process is structured around a four-stage resource lifecycle as illustrated in the figure above. These sequential operational stages serve as a roadmap for this report, tracing the secure flow of funds from their initial deposit into the Venezuela Custody Account to their circulation into the Venezuelan economy.

Stage 1: Sale of Natural Resources: Pursuant to the conditions set forth in applicable OFAC General Licenses (such as General License 52), any persons purchasing covered Venezuelan natural resources, which include oil, gold, minerals, and fertilizer, must deposit payment into the Venezuela Custody Account. Funds are held in suspense until the funding source verification workflow is complete.

Stage 2: Deposits into Venezuela Custody Account: The DoS receives and reviews deposit requests and supporting transaction documentation from first-time depositors, as established in OFAC FAQ 1239. Upon approval and provision of the deposit account information, the company deposits the funds into the Venezuela Custody Account, where they are held until the BCV, on behalf of the interim Government of Venezuela, requests the funds be disbursed.

Stage 3: Disbursement Requests and Authorization: Any disbursement of funds from the Venezuela Custody Account requires authorization from the DoS and U.S. Treasury. The BCV initiates this process by submitting a formal disbursement request to the DoS and U.S. Treasury, including a package of supporting documentation. The DoS, U.S. Treasury, and KPMG conduct risk-based reviews of the BCV's requests before disbursements are approved by the DoS and Treasury.

Stage 4: Controlled Disbursement: After a disbursement obtains the requisite U.S. government approvals, it is executed for its specifically authorized purpose through one of two closely monitored pathways: FX Interventions and Vendor Invoices.

Under Venezuela Sanctions Regulation, 31 CFR part 591, all transactions, including the purchase of natural resources, involving the Government of Venezuela, PDVSA, or any entity in which the Government of Venezuela owns, directly or indirectly, a 50 percent or greater interest, are prohibited unless licensed by OFAC.10 To enable natural resource sales without misdirecting funds, OFAC, in coordination with the DoS, issued a series of general licenses that conditioned authorized sales upon a designated deposit model, whereby approved purchasers of Venezuelan natural resources are required to deposit payment owed to blocked persons, including PDVSA, into the Venezuela Custody Account rather than disbursing funds directly. Deposits into this account occur exclusively when:

1. The commodity transaction involves:

a. Authorized activities involving oil, petroleum products, fertilizer, or agricultural inputs, as defined by OFAC FAQ 1226 and in the annex of General License 46E, conducted under a valid, explicit, and verified OFAC General or Specific License.
b. Authorized activities involving coal or minerals, including gold, as defined in OFAC General License 51D, conducted under a valid, explicit, and verified OFAC General or Specific License.

2. The financial settlement is executed in USD through established clearing systems requiring a compliant, unblocked depository institution.

The deposit collection process begins when entities involved in authorized activities involving Venezuelan natural resources transfer these transaction proceeds directly into the Venezuela Custody Account. The specific controls, onboarding procedures, and due diligence frameworks applied to accept these incoming funds are detailed in Section 6 (Stage 2: Deposits into the Venezuela Custody Account).

## 5.2 Authorized Deposits & Financial Summary

As of September 30, 2026, cumulative deposits totaled $12.26 billion. Deposit payments into the oil subaccount (inclusive of oil, other petroleum-based products, and one natural gas deposit) made up 98.7% ($12.11 billion) of total deposits. Deposits from the sale of gold accounted for 0.88% ($108.0 million), mineral sales accounted for 0.18% ($22.0 million), and fertilizer sales accounted for 0.20% ($24.6 million). Deposits are tracked through commodity-specific subaccounts within the FGDF custody structure, allowing for more organized tracking and data reconciliation. By isolating deposits into dedicated subaccounts, the structure establishes a robust audit trail and allows compliance checks, including prior DoS approvals, to be executed efficiently at the individual subaccount level. Subaccounts for the deposit of revenue associated with oil, gold, and minerals transactions were established on January 28, 2026. The fertilizer subaccount was established on July 21, 2026; prior to this date, fertilizer-related deposits were reported within the mineral account.

### 6.3 Due Diligence and Deposit Reviews

To safeguard the integrity of the program, KPMG and the DoS evaluate prospective depositors through a risk-based due diligence and deposit review process prior to granting access to the Venezuela Custody Account. The review involves the assessment and reconciliation of information and documentation relating to the depositor, counterparties, and underlying depositor relationship, supplemented by open-source intelligence reviews to validate entity legitimacy and identify potential financial crimes, sanctions, compliance, operational, or reputational risks.

The due diligence framework is designed around five (5) core risk categories:

- Financial Crime and Reputational Risk: Assesses whether the transaction or associated parties present money laundering, fraud, bribery, corruption, illicit-finance, or material reputational concerns.
- Sanctions Risk: Assesses direct or indirect exposure to sanctioned parties, including ownership, transportation assets, intermediaries, and potential sanctions-evasion structures.
- Entity Legitimacy & Transactional Transparency: Assesses whether involved entities are legitimate and whether their ownership, operations, and transaction flows are transparent, logical, and commercially justified.

- Conflicts of Interest: Assesses potential self-dealing, state capture, non-arm’s-length arrangements, or undisclosed relationships that could compromise transaction integrity.

## 7.2 Evolution of FX Mechanism

The FX Market System injects USD liquidity into the Venezuelan economy through controlled and transparent mechanisms. Since the program's inception, the system has evolved in response to operational requirements and the maturation of program controls. Until March 2026, USD were disbursed through a Legacy Auction Mechanism before the BCV transitioned to a FX Intervention Mechanism. In July 2026, following the devastating earthquakes that struck north-central Venezuela in June, Banknote FX Interventions were introduced to further expand access to USD liquidity and provide physical currency for circulation through the economy. The earthquakes caused severe structural damage and widespread power and telecommunication outages, affecting electronic transaction channels and digital payment systems across major states. As a result, the DoS and U.S. Treasury worked with the BCV to implement a program allowing for FX interventions using physical USD banknotes to maintain market transactions, support localized emergency commerce, and facilitate humanitarian relief operations.

Together, the Electronic and Banknote FX Intervention Mechanisms support economic stabilization objectives while maintaining oversight, accountability, and transparency across the disbursement process.

### 7.2.1 Legacy Auction Mechanism (January 2026 – March 2026)

The BCV has historically used USD generated from oil sales to influence exchange rates, manage liquidity, and provide targeted access to USD. Following the establishment of the Venezuela Custody Account, a similar objective remained: introducing USD liquidity into the domestic economy through a controlled and transparent process. During the program’s initial phase, this objective was achieved through a BCV-administered Auction Mechanism.

Under the Legacy Auction Mechanism, individuals and businesses submitted bids through participating commercial banks, specifying both the amount of USD sought and the exchange rate they were willing to pay. The BCV reviewed submitted bids, determined allocations, and disbursed USD through participating Venezuelan financial institutions. Unlike rate-fixing interventions, the BCV’s Auction Mechanism facilitated a market-based float, with the BCV setting only the volume of USD offered, while the market largely determined the price.

This model was used during the program’s stabilization phase to facilitate a total of 101 public auctions between January and March 2026. The following section describes the current Electronic FX Intervention Mechanism, which became the program’s primary Foreign Exchange disbursement channel beginning on March 31, 2026.

### 7.3 Electronic FX Intervention Mechanism

The transition to an Electronic FX Intervention model allows for USD liquidity to be disbursed more efficiently and at a fixed exchange rate throughout the Venezuelan economy. Unlike the volume-managed auctions that allow for a market-based float, the BCV's intervention mechanism sets a fixed exchange rate. Under this mechanism, the BCV submits Foreign Exchange disbursement requests to the DoS for review and approval. Once approved, authorized funds are disbursed across Venezuelan commercial banks as authorized based on allocation factors determined by the BCV such as liquidity needs, macroeconomic targets, banking sector VES account balances, historical intervention participation, and each participating institution's demonstrated capacity to distribute USD to its clients through established banking channels.18 Concurrently, the BCV debits each bank's account for the corresponding VES value using an exchange rate established by the BCV. This debiting process serves a dual-monetary purpose that funds the state while managing the monetary supply. Through this mechanism, the BCV gathers local VES in exchange for USD; these VES are then used for government expenditures, such as to fund public sector payrolls. Monetarily, this allows the BCV to absorb (sterilize) excess local currency from circulation, enabling the state to meet its budget needs without printing unbacked currency.

Participating Venezuelan commercial banks are required to purchase their allocated amount of USD and distribute the funds to eligible businesses and individuals through established banking channels. The intervention model enables the BCV to directly manage the timing, volume, and exchange rate of intervention allocations. Once funded, participating institutions distribute USD through a series of intervention events in which businesses and individuals may purchase USD through approved banking products and services, including USD accounts. This process is supported by the BCV's technology platform, which provides the BCV visibility into allocations, account balances, and intervention activity, and allows it to share this information with the DoS and KPMG. ### 7.3.1 Electronic FX Intervention Control Process

The Electronic FX Intervention process follows a structured cycle to identify, assess, monitor, and document fund flows. The lifecycle begins when the BCV submits bank-level foreign exchange allocation requests to the DoS for review and approval. Following review and approval by the DoS and the U.S. Treasury, authorized funds are transferred from the Venezuela Custody Account to a BCV operational account for subsequent distribution across participating commercial banks. The DoS and U.S. Treasury serve a custodial function and administer requested deposits and disbursements in alignment with E.O. 14373, and the list of approved and prohibited expenditures.

As part of its monetary policy program, the BCV conducts foreign exchange interventions on a weekly basis and determines the amount of USD to be allocated to participating institutions. The allocation date and intervention date are effectively the same, representing the point at which the BCV assigns USD to a participating bank. Following the intervention date, participating institutions are provided five business days to distribute the USD to eligible customers. Banks may utilize an additional five-business-day liquidation period, resulting in a maximum liquidation period of ten business days from the intervention date.$^{20}$

Transactions are denominated in USD, and as a result, participating commercial banks cannot settle or clear these funds locally; they are legally and operationally required to maintain active correspondent and custodial relationships with U.S. financial institutions. Through these correspondent banking arrangements, participating institutions maintain USD settlement accounts used to receive and distribute foreign exchange intervention allocations. Every dollar disbursed through the electronic FX intervention program therefore passes through the U.S. financial system, making an active U.S. correspondent banking relationship a prerequisite for participation in the program.$^{21}$

Participating commercial banks receive and clear intervention funds through USD settlement accounts maintained with U.S. correspondent banking partners, which are required to facilitate USD-denominated transactions. Individual and corporate recipients, however, may receive intervention funds through foreign-currency accounts maintained at participating Venezuelan financial institutions and are subject to customer identification, sanctions screening, due diligence, and transaction-monitoring requirements before and after funds are disbursed. In addition to customer-level controls performed by participating institutions, U.S. correspondent banks conduct due diligence over the foreign banks with which they maintain relationships, including assessments of AML, sanctions compliance, customer due diligence, and transaction monitoring programs. As a result, the control framework incorporates multiple layers of review across the disbursement chain.

Participating commercial banks screen and monitor customers and transactions, while U.S. correspondent banks independently assess and oversee the control environments supporting these activities. These controls provide additional safeguards designed to identify, investigate, restrict, or reject potentially prohibited or high-risk transactions before and during the movement of funds. Consequently, electronic FX intervention funds are not distributed directly to unidentified members of the public. Rather, access to USD is provided through regulated financial institutions operating within established compliance frameworks and subject to

### Institutional Due Diligence and Account Controls

Integrating the customer-facing control procedures of Venezuelan commercial banks with the independent compliance policies of U.S. and international financial institutions establishes consecutive points of verification across the disbursement chain. Participating commercial banks often treat USD activities as higher-risk, subjecting them to greater review than standard domestic currency accounts.

At participating Venezuelan banks, customers seeking to open a USD account must clear onboarding controls that prohibit fictitious or anonymous accounts. These controls require formal identity verification, in-person interviews, source-of-funds declarations, and verification against official public registries. To manage ongoing risk, commercial banks utilize risk-based monitoring, customer risk-scoring methodologies, and EDD workflows. These procedures may include enhanced review of higher-risk customers, politically exposed persons (PEPs), and cross-border activities in accordance with internal policies and regulatory requirements.

In addition to customer-level controls performed by participating commercial banks, correspondent banking relationships are themselves subject to ongoing due diligence and risk assessments. U.S. correspondent banking institutions evaluate the AML, sanctions compliance, customer due diligence, and transaction monitoring frameworks maintained by participating financial institutions as a condition of establishing and maintaining correspondent banking relationships. #### **7.4 Banknote FX Interventions Mechanism**

In July 2026, the program introduced physical USD Banknote Distribution through the Banknote FX Intervention Program as a complementary mechanism for providing liquidity to the Venezuelan economy. This initiative is supported by an arrangement with the U.S. Department of State (DoS), which authorizes the use of custodial funds to facilitate the import and distribution of physical U.S. currency. The cash distribution process, which is governed for Venezuela by the BCV and SUDEBAN regulatory frameworks, involves pre-shipment bank due diligence, secure air cargo delivery, controlled armored transport to vaults located at BCV facilities, and rigorous multi-day counting procedures to capture and record banknote serial numbers. SUDEBAN, as the primary AML and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) supervisor for the Venezuelan banking sector, enforces Resolution N° 010.25, which mandates comprehensive risk management systems for all supervised institutions.$^{25}$ The Banknote Control Process developed by the DoS and KPMG, in conjunction with the U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve, provides the governance and EDD for the implementation of the USD Banknote Distribution.

The Banknote FX Intervention Mechanism is designed to support transactions that rely on cash-based payment methods and to expand access to USD throughout the Venezuelan economy given present limitations to banking access, connectivity to the U.S. financial system, and electronic payment infrastructure. Increasing the volume of physical U.S. currency in circulation allows for a wider diffusion of USD in Venezuela, supporting commercial activity and facilitating the purchase of essential goods and

Physical banknotes also help address practical limitations associated with domestic electronic payments, where inflation, currency volatility, settlement delays, and exchange rate differentials may affect the effective value of VES-denominated transactions. Under this program, only authorized entities (businesses and individuals) are eligible to receive banknotes based on respective BCV and commercial bank policies, with 98.7% of disbursements allocated to business entities. As a result, the circulation of physical U.S. currency provides an additional channel for liquidity disbursement and enables wider access to funds for businesses and individuals. This intervention is especially critical considering prevailing humanitarian conditions, where reliable access to physical currency serves as a vital lifeline for purchasing food, healthcare supplies, and other necessities.

### 7.4.2 Banknote Control Process

DoS collaborated closely with U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve in developing the Banknote Control Process that represents leading oversight practices over the Banknote Distribution lifecycle. The control process establishes a testing framework that mitigates AML, sanctions, and counterfeit risks, aligns key stakeholders across the BCV intervention exchange program, and enables secure, compliant, and expedited downstream distribution of banknotes. DoS partnered with global banknote providers who are licensed non-bank financial intermediaries and specialized international financial entities operating under U.S. regulatory and AML frameworks.

As part of the initial planning, due diligence procedures, and agreements between relevant parties, a variety of controls were agreed to by the BCV. These include, but are not limited to, shipment inspection under Closed Circuit Television (CCTV) or dual control; the BCV warranting that it will not distribute to any party under sanctions by OFAC, EU, UK, or UN; only the transfer to a pre-approved list of licensed Venezuelan banks; sharing of BCV and bank compliance policies; and providing AML and CFT documentation. The BCV must obtain DoS approval prior to distributing banknotes to Venezuelan banks for their customers.

Controls are applied across nine operational stages described and as seen in the figure below. The Banknote Control Process begins with (1) authorization and initiation, which establishes formal approval of the Banknote FX Intervention before any currency disbursement occurs. This is followed by the (2) secure shipment of banknotes using specialized logistics and security escorts to mitigate transit risks. Upon arrival, (3) receipt and secure storage protocols verify the physical intake and prevent unauthorized access at high-security facilities. Once deposited, (4) counting and serial number capture processes verify exact quantities and register unique serial numbers to establish a robust audit trail and detect defective notes. Prospective recipient data are collected from the banks and enable the DoS and KPMG review (5) prior to DoS authorization. Next, the (6) preparation of allocations for commercial banks establishes procedures for the precise packaging and staging of earmarked funds, which are then formally handed over during (7) bank collection and confirmation to legally transfer custody. The serialized currency is then released for (8) distribution to verified end recipients. Finally, the process concludes with (9) financial and performance reporting to consolidate transaction data, evaluate program metrics, and perform final reconciliation.

### 7.4.3 Ongoing Monitoring and Due Diligence

The USD Banknote FX Intervention incorporates transaction-level monitoring and risk-based due diligence procedures designed to promote transparency over the downstream distribution of physical U.S. currency and identify potential sanctions, corruption, fraud, money laundering, reputational, and diversion risks.

Participating commercial banks provide the DoS and KPMG, through mandatory reporting to the BCV, detailed transaction-level data regarding proposed banknote recipients and distributions. This information includes relevant recipient identifiers, such as tax identification numbers, transaction amounts, bank information, and banknote serial-number data, enabling distributed banknotes to be traced through the participating commercial bank to the identified end recipient. This transaction-level traceability strengthens the program's ability to monitor the movement of physical currency, reconcile distributions, and investigate potential discrepancies or other risk indicators.

Physical banknotes allocated for distribution remain in controlled custody and are not released to end recipients until the applicable recipient information has been reviewed and the proposed distributions have been expressly authorized by the DoS. This pre-distribution review provides an opportunity to identify and address potential concerns before physical currency enters broader circulation. ### 7.5 Statistical Sampling Methodology

This statistical sampling methodology operates within an established oversight framework designed to track allocated funds and perform EDD reviews based on transactions ("Transactions") at each participating commercial bank in Venezuela. The primary objective is to calculate a sample size that can be used to select a statistically representative sample of recipients ("Recipients") on a regularized cadence. Additionally, the methodology enables a representative sample of Banknote Distributions to provide EDD and downstream recipient testing. The diagram below illustrates the operational workflow.

![img-22.jpeg](img-22.jpeg)

Figure 19: Sampling Process Overview³³

# Inputs

The sampling framework begins by using various inputs to inform the sampling process. This includes transaction-level data including dates, banks, recipients, and transaction amounts; the statistical parameters for sampling, such as population definitions, stratifications, and precision, based on due diligence needs; and the DoS acceptance of the technical and statistical parameters. Transaction data that is missing key information required for sampling or due diligence, such as a recipient's tax identification or transaction amount, are excluded from the sample population. Transactions with missing recipient tax identification are excluded because they cannot be linked to a specific recipient or classified into a recipient type grouping for sampling purposes. Transactions with missing transaction amounts are excluded because they cannot be placed within an appropriate sampling stratum and it is unknown whether there are any dollars at risk for the transaction. As of September 30, 2026, no transaction data is missing recipient's tax identifications or transaction amounts for these missing data field exclusions to be implemented.

1. 1. **Identify Populations:** To account for inherent variations in transaction frequency, recipient profiles, transfer amounts, and distinct operational characteristics, each transaction type (e.g., banknotes, electronic transfers, etc.) needs to be sampled as a separate population.
2. 2. **Apply Exclusion Criteria:** Recipients previously identified as high risk and recipients with transactions totaling \$0 as there are no funds at risk in the transactions.
   - • **Apply Recipient Type Grouping:** Transaction categories include Venezuelan Individuals, Venezuelan Domestic Legal Entities, and Foreign / Other. This facilitates representation and analysis of each Recipient Type's operational footprint.
3. 3. **Create Census Stratum:** Prior to drawing the statistical sample, “Transactions” amounts are aggregated at the recipient level, and a census review is conducted on Recipients with total transactions above a desired threshold.
4. 4. **Draw Statistical Sample:** In selecting the sample, recipients and their aggregated transactions are sorted by their unique identifier. Random numbers are generated using Stata and a specified random number of seed.$^{36}$ A subset of recipients is selected based on the calculated sample size and these recipients undergo EDD reviews. EDD will not be conducted for recipients of transactions if the recipient has already been reviewed in the prior 12-month period. Previously identified high-risk recipients are tracked for the next 12-month period to prevent them from receiving any Banknote Distributions until they are removed from the high-risk recipient list. This helps isolate new recipients of transactions who have not undergone a due diligence review in the past year. Using transaction-level details alongside a census review of large transaction recipients, the sampling framework generates representative samples tailored to transaction volume and variance, focused on EDD of unreviewed, high-impact recipients, delivering actionable error-rate projections and anomaly detection to safeguard program integrity.

## 7.6 Vendor Payments

In addition to foreign exchange interventions, authorized funds may be disbursed on behalf of the interim Government of Venezuela directly to vendors and service providers supporting governmental purposes, critical goods, services, and operational needs. Between March 6, 2026 and September 30, 2026, $133.11 million has been disbursed through vendor payments.

Venezuelan law requires that any payments denominated in USD be processed by the BCV. Pursuant to Convenio Cambiario N°1, USD transactions between Venezuelan entities and vendors must be settled through the Cámara de Compensación en Moneda Extranjera, a centralized mechanism administered by the BCV used to clear and settle foreign-currency payments.$^{37}$ Because vendor payments involve contractual obligations, identifiable counterparties, cross-border payment flows, and varying levels of commercial and compliance risk, they are subject to a risk-based review and approval process. This process incorporates documentation requirements, compliance screening, appropriate governance approvals, and targeted due diligence measures to assess transaction legitimacy, sanctions compliance, financial integrity, and diversion risks prior to disbursement. The following sections outline the governance framework, review methodology, and oversight processes supporting vendor payments from the Venezuela Custody Account. Disbursements are handled consistent with E.O. 14373 and do not constitute use of funds in the Venezuela Custody Account for commercial activity in the United States.

### 8.3 Downstream Recipients

This section details how program funds were distributed across key sectors of the Venezuelan economy. By analyzing recipient industries and mapping downstream corporate entities, the data shows how USD were used after leaving intermediary banks and the economic sectors represented within the reported recipient population.

Following the primary disbursement of funds from the Venezuela Custody Account into a BCV operational account, USD were distributed into the Venezuelan domestic banking system. To understand the relationship between primary, intermediary, and downstream recipient populations, it is important to distinguish between transfers made to participating commercial banks and the subsequent allocation of USD to individual and corporate customers.

The outflow ledger presented in Section 8.2 reflects transfers to participating commercial banks, vendors, and other registered recipients. The recipient analysis presented in this section reflects a separate stage of the distribution process and is based on downstream transaction data reported by participating commercial banks to the BCV and subsequently provided to the DoS and KPMG for review and analysis. The recipient statistics presented below reflect combined downstream recipient activity across both the Electronic FX Intervention and Banknote FX Intervention mechanisms. Accordingly, the bank-level disbursements discussed in Section 8.2 and the recipient-level information presented below represent different layers of the same USD distribution framework. These activities can be viewed through three related tiers:

1. **Primary Disbursements** (Custody to Operations): Funds are transferred from the Venezuela Custody Account to a BCV operational account for approved foreign exchange activities. These transactions represent the initial release of funds from custody and form the basis of the outflow reporting presented in Section 8.2.
2. **Intermediary Disbursements** (Operations to Commercial Banks): The BCV allocates USD from its operational account to the U.S. correspondent and custodial accounts supporting participating Venezuelan commercial banks. These allocations represent the intermediary stage between fund release and downstream customer activity. Most of the disbursements presented in Section 8.2 reflect transfers at this level.
3. **Downstream Recipient Activity** (Commercial Banks to End Recipients): Participating commercial banks subsequently distribute USD to individuals and corporate entities through regulated USD-denominated account activity and regulated foreign exchange mechanisms. These transactions are executed under customer identification, due diligence, and AML/KYC requirements maintained by participating financial institutions and applicable banking regulations. Recipient information analyzed in this section is derived from data reported by participating commercial banks to the BCV and subsequently provided to the DoS and KPMG for analysis and classification.

## 9. Ongoing Monitoring Plan & Strategy

### 9.1 Ongoing Monitoring Plan

This report supports a broader oversight and monitoring effort meant to bring transparency and accountability to Venezuela's natural resource sector. The work completed during the period covered by this report involved analysis of vast amounts of data provided by the BCV and other stakeholders across a range of financial, operational, and regulatory data sources.

The information presented in this report should be viewed as a point-in-time assessment based on the data and documentation available as of September 30, 2026.

Beyond the reporting activities reflected herein, the oversight team has been actively implementing enhanced monitoring capabilities, data governance processes, and technology solutions designed to enhance financial oversight and further improve transparency. These efforts include developing centralized data repositories, automating reconciliation capabilities, standardizing reporting frameworks, and implementing advanced analytic tools that will enable more efficient tracking, monitoring, and reporting of fund activity across the program lifecycle. Continued monitoring activities may incorporate newly available financial records, transaction-level data, risk assessments, compliance reviews, and monitoring results as data integration efforts mature.

This report therefore serves not only as a record of current observations, but as the foundation of a sustainable oversight framework that will continue to support transparency, accountability, and informed decision-making.

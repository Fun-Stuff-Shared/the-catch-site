1. Executive Overview

1.1 Introduction

This report provides a comprehensive account of the Venezuelan natural resource revenues deposited in,
held by, and disbursed from the U.S. Treasury Venezuela Custody Account (also referred to as the
Venezuela Custody Account), a deposit fund account established consistent with Executive Order (E.O.)
14373, “Safeguarding Venezuelan Oil Revenue for the Good of the American and Venezuelan People.” 1 It
documents the financial oversight, governance, and control mechanisms implemented by the U.S.
Department of State (DoS) and its contracted monitor and advisor, KPMG, to promote transparency and
accountability in the management of these funds.

The Venezuela Financial Transparency Program reviews deposit receipts, fund administration, compliance
activities, and disbursement processes associated with the Venezuela Custody Account. This report
provides visibility into how funds are deposited, monitored, managed, and disbursed in accordance with
established authorization, compliance, and governance protocols to support approved macroeconomic,
public-sector, humanitarian, and operational purposes. Not all these processes and the safeguards described
below have been in place since January 2026; the process has been iterative and DoS and KPMG have
adjusted, refined, and added to these processes over time. All funds and natural resource proceeds
administered under this program constitute sovereign assets of the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, held
and managed exclusively for the benefit of the Venezuelan people.

This report covers January 3, 2026, to September 30, 2026, and may be updated to reflect future data and
enhanced monitoring capabilities, including centralized data repositories, automated reconciliation, and
advanced analytics. Any subsequent report will integrate newly available transaction-level records, risk
assessments, and compliance findings as these data governance and modernization initiatives mature.

For analytical clarity, the report progresses from strategic and financial review to operational oversight and
compliance activities. The sections that follow include a financial summary (traceability), review of the
Venezuela Custody Account transaction activity, vetting and forensic reviews, institutional oversight and
governance descriptions, and a forward outlook. Together, these sections provide an integrated view of the
controls, monitoring activities, and governance processes supporting the administration of funds within the
Venezuela Custody Account framework.

During the first nine months of operation, the U.S. Government established custodial control over
Venezuelan natural resource revenue deposits, implemented multi-layered authorization and compliance
controls, and monitored the distribution of funds through approved economic and public-sector channels.
More than $12 billion was deposited and disbursed under a controlled governance structure designed to
promote transparency, accountability, and protection of sovereign assets. 2

2. Program Framework and Operating Environment

2.1 Background

On January 9, 2026, President Donald J. Trump issued E.O. 14373, pursuant to authorities including the
International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the National Emergencies Act. 3 The Order established
protections governing specified Venezuelan government revenues held by the United States, defined as
“Foreign Government Deposit Funds” (FGDF), which include funds derived from commodity-sale
proceeds of Venezuelan natural resources from the Government of Venezuela or its agencies or
instrumentalities. Under the Order, covered funds are held by the U.S. Treasury in a designated Venezuela
Custody Account. The funds remain sovereign property of Venezuela and are protected from attachment or
other judicial process and may not be transferred, withdrawn, or otherwise dealt in except as authorized.
The funds are held pending disposition for public, governmental, or diplomatic purposes as determined by
the Secretary of State, with the Secretary of the Treasury responsible for their administration and for
executing authorized disbursements or transfers. 4

Operating Environment & Challenges. Venezuela’s vast natural resources, particularly its oil reserves,
have historically served as the primary engine of the nation’s economy and principal source of U.S. Dollar
(USD) income. Over time, sanctions, international legal disputes, governance challenges, and financial-
control deficiencies significantly constrained traditional fund flows and Venezuela’s access to international
financial markets. These conditions increased risks related to the receipt, management, and distribution of
resource-derived funds, creating the need for enhanced oversight mechanisms to increase transparency,
preserve economic continuity, safeguard critical revenue streams, and support the delivery of essential
public services.

Program Rationale & Controlled Structure. Certain OFAC licenses (e.g., General Licenses 46E, 48D,
50C, 51D, 52C, and 54C) direct firms to deposit payments to the Government of Venezuela, or its agencies
or instrumentalities, made in exchange for authorized activities involving Venezuela-origin natural
resources in the Venezuela Custody Account. The Venezuela Custody Account mechanism serves as a
controlled deposit management structure under which payments from authorized activities involving
Venezuelan natural resources are used for authorized macroeconomic, public-sector, humanitarian, and
operational purposes. Like escrow, trust, and other controlled holding-account arrangements used in
financial oversight environments, this approach provides heightened transparency into the management and
release of funds by allowing for transaction monitoring and other compliance controls. Authorized funds
are subsequently disbursed from the Venezuela Custody Account to designated transaction accounts, or
approved third-party accounts, at the direction of the Banco Central de Venezuela (BCV), following
transaction review, approval and authorization procedures. The BCV administers allocations and
disbursements to approved downstream entities, including commercial banks, and payments on behalf of
government entities and public service organizations. This structure is intended to support macroeconomic
stability and the restoration of critical governmental functions and key sectors, including energy,
infrastructure, healthcare, and public services, while promoting accountability and stewardship of public
resources.

Program Oversight & Governance Controls. To support the accountable administration of public funds,
requests for disbursement are subject to a structured authorization and compliance review process
administered under DoS protocols supported by KPMG. The BCV, on behalf of the interim Venezuelan
government, submits disbursement requests to the DoS detailing the intended use of funds for approved
governmental, operational, and public-interest objectives. Through controlled disbursement, transaction
monitoring, and defined oversight mechanisms, the program promotes transparency, guards against
unauthorized use of funds, strengthens financial controls, and supports restoration of Venezuela’s
institutional capacity and economic sustainability.

This approach is designed to balance immediate operational continuity and the execution of essential and
time-sensitive disbursements with the compliance, transparency, and oversight mechanisms necessary to
safeguard sovereign resources and support long-term institutional development.

2.3 Summary

The Venezuela Custody Account serves as the central custody and oversight mechanism for revenues
generated from activities related to Venezuelan natural resources. The oversight structure provides
transparency into how funds are collected, managed, authorized, safeguarded, and disbursed to advance
approved economic and humanitarian objectives.

During the period covered by this report (January 3 – September 30, 2026), the Venezuela Custody Account
received deposits totaling more than $12.26 billion from verified and approved entities engaging in
authorized activities related to Venezuelan natural resources. While crude oil and petroleum-related
deposits represented the largest source of inflows, the account also received approved deposits associated
with gold, mineral, and fertilizer transactions. Of the total amount, $12.11 billion (98.7%) was disbursed
from the Venezuela Custody Account,while $168.56 million remained in custody as of September 30, 2026,
for future authorized disbursements pursuant to requests by the BCV on behalf of the interim Government
of Venezuela.

3.1 U.S. Treasury Venezuela Custody Account and Historical Custody Account Overview

To provide verifiable oversight of Venezuelan natural resource revenues, the U.S. government established
a centralized custody account to safeguard sovereign assets and enable deposits to be approved, tracked,
and protected from unauthorized use prior to their approved disbursement.

Implementation of this approach was executed in two distinct phases. During the initial phase, through
January 2026, for safeguarding purposes, deposit payments made by firms in exchange for the sale of
Venezuela-origin natural resources were deposited into a temporary custody account of the BCV’s at a
commercial offshore banking institution, the Qatar National Bank (QNB), subject to custody controls
described in Section 3.1.1 below. By late January 2026, custody account operations transitioned to the U.S.
Treasury Venezuela Custody Account.

The following subsections summarize the purpose, activity, and disposition of each of these custody
accounts and provide a detailed overview of the transition from the interim arrangement to the active
custody account structure. Collectively, they illustrate the volume of natural resource revenues deposited
and the amounts disbursed through approved channels.

3.1.1 Phase 1: Interim Custody Account (January 2026)

In January 2026, a temporary custody account of the BCV’s at QNB was established to hold deposit
payments from the initial U.S.-facilitated sales of Venezuelan oil. Use of the Qatari bank provided a short-
term, exigent solution to facilitate the urgent sale of oil that had accumulated in storage and was at risk of
creating permanent shut-in of Venezuelan oil production, and to avoid creditor attachment while holding
the initial Venezuelan oil deposit payments until the U.S.-based mechanism was established. Similar to the
protection from creditor attachment subsequently established by E.O. 14373, this approach prevented
creditors from disrupting access to the revenues and allowed for continued availability of funds to address
Venezuela’s critical economic and governmental needs. This interim mechanism provided emergency
liquidity and oversight of $500 million in initial deposits.

Following receipt of the initial $500 million in deposits, funds were allocated and disbursed to participating
Venezuelan financial institutions through a legacy Foreign Exchange Auction process described in Section
7.2.1. On January 15, 2026, $300 million was disbursed to four participating Venezuelan financial
institutions: Mercantil C.A. Banco Universal, Banco Provincial (BBVA), Banesco Banco Universal, and
Banco Nacional de Credito (BNC). Each institution received $75 million to facilitate the exchange of VES. 7

Additional authorized disbursements of $163.95 million and $36 million followed on January 23 and
January 28, 2026, respectively, bringing total disbursements to $499.95 million. As of September 30,
$50,000 remains in the Interim Qatar Custodial Account.

6.1.2 Non-Standard Intake Workflow (Suspense Mitigation)

To maintain the integrity of the Venezuela Custody Account and prevent unauthorized, fraudulent, or non-
compliant deposits, any deposits made into the account without prior approval from the DoS are treated as
compliance risks until their source and purpose can be verified. These funds are isolated and subjected to
additional review. Although this is not a common occurrence, these instances are subjected to established
due diligence processes.

In these instances, the DoS places the funds in suspense. KPMG, in coordination with the DoS, initiates
outreach to the depositor and other relevant parties to obtain the required documentation, which is then
reviewed by KPMG and the DoS. If the information is deficient, the deposit is rejected and the funds return
to the prospective depositor’s bank that sent the funds to the Venezuela Custody Account.



6.2 Deposit Activities

Funds are deposited into the Venezuela Custody Account in accordance with applicable OFAC licenses
(which require depositors engaged in covered activities to deposit funds into the Venezuela Custody
Account) and are subject to DoS approval. The DoS ensures deposit payments are allocated to appropriate
subaccounts based on information about the transaction generating the proceeds for deposit.

7.3.1 Electronic FX Intervention Control Process

The Electronic FX Intervention process follows a structured cycle to identify, assess, monitor, and
document fund flows. The lifecycle begins when the BCV submits bank-level foreign exchange allocation
requests to the DoS for review and approval. Following review and approval by the DoS and the U.S.
Treasury, authorized funds are transferred from the Venezuela Custody Account to a BCV operational
account for subsequent distribution across participating commercial banks. The DoS and U.S. Treasury
serve a custodial function and administer requested deposits and disbursements in alignment with E.O.
14373, and the list of approved and prohibited expenditures.

As part of its monetary policy program, the BCV conducts foreign exchange interventions on a weekly
basis and determines the amount of USD to be allocated to participating institutions. The allocation date
and intervention date are effectively the same, representing the point at which the BCV assigns USD to a
participating bank. Following the intervention date, participating institutions are provided five business
days to distribute the USD to eligible customers. Banks may utilize an additional five-business-day
liquidation period, resulting in a maximum liquidation period of ten business days from the intervention
date. 20

Transactions are denominated in USD, and as a result, participating commercial banks cannot settle or clear
these funds locally; they are legally and operationally required to maintain active correspondent and
custodial relationships with U.S. financial institutions. Through these correspondent banking arrangements,
participating institutions maintain USD settlement accounts used to receive and distribute foreign exchange
intervention allocations. Every dollar disbursed through the electronic FX intervention program therefore
passes through the U.S. financial system, making an active U.S. correspondent banking relationship a
prerequisite for participation in the program. 21

Participating commercial banks receive and clear intervention funds through USD settlement accounts
maintained with U.S. correspondent banking partners, which are required to facilitate USD-denominated
transactions. Individual and corporate recipients, however, may receive intervention funds through foreign-
currency accounts maintained at participating Venezuelan financial institutions and are subject to customer
identification, sanctions screening, due diligence, and transaction-monitoring requirements before and after
funds are disbursed. In addition to customer-level controls performed by participating institutions, U.S.
correspondent banks conduct due diligence over the foreign banks with which they maintain relationships,
including assessments of AML, sanctions compliance, customer due diligence, and transaction monitoring
programs. As a result, the control framework incorporates multiple layers of review across the disbursement
chain.

Participating commercial banks screen and monitor customers and transactions, while U.S. correspondent
banks independently assess and oversee the control environments supporting these activities. These controls
provide additional safeguards designed to identify, investigate, restrict, or reject potentially prohibited or
high-risk transactions before and during the movement of funds. Consequently, electronic FX intervention
funds are not distributed directly to unidentified members of the public. Rather, access to USD is provided
through regulated financial institutions operating within established compliance frameworks and subject to

7.4 Banknote FX Interventions Mechanism

In July 2026, the program introduced physical USD Banknote Distribution through the Banknote FX
Intervention Program as a complementary mechanism for providing liquidity to the Venezuelan economy.
This initiative is supported by an arrangement with the U.S. Department of State (DoS), which authorizes
the use of custodial funds to facilitate the import and distribution of physical U.S. currency. The cash
distribution process, which is governed for Venezuela by the BCV and SUDEBAN regulatory frameworks,
involves pre-shipment bank due diligence, secure air cargo delivery, controlled armored transport to vaults
located at BCV facilities, and rigorous multi-day counting procedures to capture and record banknote serial
numbers. SUDEBAN, as the primary AML and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) supervisor
for the Venezuelan banking sector, enforces Resolution N° 010.25, which mandates comprehensive risk
management systems for all supervised institutions. 25 The Banknote Control Process developed by the DoS
and KPMG, in conjunction with the U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve, provides the governance and
EDD for the implementation of the USD Banknote Distribution.

The Banknote FX Intervention Mechanism is designed to support transactions that rely on cash-based
payment methods and to expand access to USD throughout the Venezuelan economy given present
limitations to banking access, connectivity to the U.S. financial system, and electronic payment
infrastructure. Increasing the volume of physical U.S. currency in circulation allows for a wider diffusion

7.4.2 Banknote Control Process

DoS collaborated closely with U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve in developing the Banknote Control
Process that represents leading oversight practices over the Banknote Distribution lifecycle. The control
process establishes a testing framework that mitigates AML, sanctions, and counterfeit risks, aligns key
stakeholders across the BCV intervention exchange program, and enables secure, compliant, and expedited
downstream distribution of banknotes. DoS partnered with global banknote providers who are licensed non-
bank financial intermediaries and specialized international financial entities operating under U.S. regulatory
and AML frameworks.

As part of the initial planning, due diligence procedures, and agreements between relevant parties, a variety
of controls were agreed to by the BCV. These include, but are not limited to, shipment inspection under
Closed Circuit Television (CCTV) or dual control; the BCV warranting that it will not distribute to any
party under sanctions by OFAC, EU, UK, or UN; only the transfer to a pre-approved list of licensed
Venezuelan banks; sharing of BCV and bank compliance policies; and providing AML and CFT
documentation. The BCV must obtain DoS approval prior to distributing banknotes to Venezuelan banks
for their customers.

Controls are applied across nine operational stages described and as seen in the figure below. The Banknote
Control Process begins with (1) authorization and initiation, which establishes formal approval of the
Banknote FX Intervention before any currency disbursement occurs. This is followed by the (2) secure
shipment of banknotes using specialized logistics and security escorts to mitigate transit risks. Upon arrival,
(3) receipt and secure storage protocols verify the physical intake and prevent unauthorized access at high-
security facilities. Once deposited, (4) counting and serial number capture processes verify exact quantities
and register unique serial numbers to establish a robust audit trail and detect defective notes. Prospective
recipient data are collected from the banks and enable the DoS and KPMG review (5) prior to DoS
authorization. Next, the (6) preparation of allocations for commercial banks establishes procedures for the
precise packaging and staging of earmarked funds, which are then formally handed over during (7) bank
collection and confirmation to legally transfer custody. The serialized currency is then released for (8)
distribution to verified end recipients. Finally, the process concludes with (9) financial and performance
reporting to consolidate transaction data, evaluate program metrics, and perform final reconciliation.

7.6 Vendor Payments

In addition to foreign exchange interventions, authorized funds may be disbursed on behalf of the interim
Government of Venezuela directly to vendors and service providers supporting governmental purposes,
critical goods, services, and operational needs. Between March 6, 2026 and September 30, 2026, $133.11
million has been disbursed through vendor payments.

Venezuelan law requires that any payments denominated in USD be processed by the BCV. Pursuant to
Convenio Cambiario N°1, USD transactions between Venezuelan entities and vendors must be settled
through the Cámara de Compensación en Moneda Extranjera, a centralized mechanism administered by the
BCV used to clear and settle foreign-currency payments. 37 Because vendor payments involve contractual
obligations, identifiable counterparties, cross-border payment flows, and varying levels of commercial and
compliance risk, they are subject to a risk-based review and approval process. This process incorporates
documentation requirements, compliance screening, appropriate governance approvals, and targeted due
diligence measures to assess transaction legitimacy, sanctions compliance, financial integrity, and diversion
risks prior to disbursement. The following sections outline the governance framework, review
methodology, and oversight processes supporting vendor payments from the Venezuela Custody Account.
Disbursements are handled consistent with E.O. 14373 and do not constitute use of funds in the Venezuela
Custody Account for commercial activity in the United States.

7.6.3 Transaction Due Diligence

The transaction due diligence review process serves as a key control designed to establish a comprehensive
understanding of each request, including the requesting entity, relevant counterparties, business activities,
ownership considerations, potential integrity risks, and the purpose of the proposed transaction. As part of
this process, KPMG performs risk-based due diligence to assess whether the payment is consistent with
program objectives, applicable control requirements, and the intended use of funds.

Prior to KPMG’s engagement as technical advisor to undertake transaction due diligence in April 2026,
Citibank reviewed each proposed vendor payment submitted by the BCV and facilitated by Citibank
through Citibank’s applicable sanctions, AML, CFT, and Anti-Bribery and Corruption programs, which are
designed to identify potentially high-risk parties, unusual payment activity, and other financial crime
indicators. After KPMG was engaged as a technical advisor, KPMG reviewed past payments facilitated by
Citibank, maintaining visibility into previously approved disbursements. Open communication between
KPMG and Citibank has continued, supporting continuity of review and access to information concerning
payments processed before KPMG’s involvement.

Building on recognized AML review methodologies, among other risk assessment best practices, KPMG
applies a risk-tiered assessment approach based on factors such as transaction value, industry sector,
geographic exposure, counterparty profile, and supporting documentation. Reviews may include sanctions
screening, reputational and regulatory assessments, beneficial ownership analysis, and other targeted due
diligence procedures designed to identify potential compliance, integrity, or diversion risks. The level of
monitoring and control activities applied to each payment may vary based on the assessed level of risk,
with additional due diligence activities potentially ranging from outreach directly to the transaction
counterparties, in-person diligence and site visits, coordination with OFAC to confirm license compliance,
or performing due diligence on additional parties related to the transacting entity. The results are provided
to the DoS to support approval, rejection, escalation, or implementation of additional risk mitigation
measures.

participating commercial banks. The account is also used to facilitate approved operational and

8.3 Downstream Recipients

This section details how program funds were distributed across key sectors of the Venezuelan economy.

were used after leaving intermediary banks and the economic sectors represented within the reported
recipient population.

Following the primary disbursement of funds from the Venezuela Custody Account into a BCV operational
account, USD were distributed into the Venezuelan domestic banking system. To understand the
relationship between primary, intermediary, and downstream recipient populations, it is important to
distinguish between transfers made to participating commercial banks and the subsequent allocation of
USD to individual and corporate customers.

The outflow ledger presented in Section 8.2 reflects transfers to participating commercial banks, vendors,
and other registered recipients. The recipient analysis presented in this section reflects a separate stage of
the distribution process and is based on downstream transaction data reported by participating commercial
banks to the BCV and subsequently provided to the DoS and KPMG for review and analysis. The recipient
statistics presented below reflect combined downstream recipient activity across both the Electronic FX
Intervention and Banknote FX Intervention mechanisms. Accordingly, the bank-level disbursements
discussed in Section 8.2 and the recipient-level information presented below represent different layers of
the same USD distribution framework. These activities can be viewed through three related tiers:

    1. Primary Disbursements (Custody to Operations): Funds are transferred from the Venezuela
       Custody Account to a BCV operational account for approved foreign exchange activities. These
       transactions represent the initial release of funds from custody and form the basis of the outflow
       reporting presented in Section 8.2.
    2. Intermediary Disbursements (Operations to Commercial Banks): The BCV allocates USD from
       its operational account to the U.S. correspondent and custodial accounts supporting participating
       Venezuelan commercial banks. These allocations represent the intermediary stage between fund
       release and downstream customer activity. Most of the disbursements presented in Section 8.2
       reflect transfers at this level.
    3. Downstream Recipient Activity (Commercial Banks to End Recipients): Participating
       commercial banks subsequently distribute USD to individuals and corporate entities through
       regulated USD-denominated account activity and regulated foreign exchange mechanisms. These
       transactions are executed under customer identification, due diligence, and AML/KYC
       requirements maintained by participating financial institutions and applicable banking regulations.
       Recipient information analyzed in this section is derived from data reported by participating
       commercial banks to the BCV and subsequently provided to the DoS and KPMG for analysis and

9. Ongoing Monitoring Plan & Strategy

9.1 Ongoing Monitoring Plan

This report supports a broader oversight and monitoring effort meant to bring transparency and
accountability to Venezuela’s natural resource sector. The work completed during the period covered by
this report involved analysis of vast amounts of data provided by the BCV and other stakeholders across a
range of financial, operational, and regulatory data sources.

The information presented in this report should be viewed as a point-in-time assessment based on the data
and documentation available as of September 30, 2026.

Beyond the reporting activities reflected herein, the oversight team has been actively implementing
enhanced monitoring capabilities, data governance processes, and technology solutions designed to
enhance financial oversight and further improve transparency. These efforts include developing centralized
data repositories, automating reconciliation capabilities, standardizing reporting frameworks, and
implementing advanced analytic tools that will enable more efficient tracking, monitoring, and reporting of
fund activity across the program lifecycle. Continued monitoring activities may incorporate newly available
financial records, transaction-level data, risk assessments, compliance reviews, and monitoring results as
data integration efforts mature.

This report therefore serves not only as a record of current observations, but as the foundation of a
sustainable oversight framework that will continue to support transparency, accountability, and informed
decision-making.

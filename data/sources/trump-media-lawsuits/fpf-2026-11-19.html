Judge gets First Amendment wrong in greenlighting Trump’s NYT lawsuit

Nov. 19, 2026 / Freedom of the Press Foundation

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE:

New York, Nov. 19, 2026 — A federal judge today allowed parts of President Donald Trump’s defamation lawsuit against The New York Times, its reporters, and publisher Penguin Random House to proceed. Although other claims were thrown out, U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday declined to dismiss claims that the Times defamed Trump by, according to Merryday, suggesting he and his relatives committed tax crimes.

The judge acknowledged the Times had consulted with numerous experts on tax law, but questioned whether the expert opinions they obtained were sufficiently formal and detailed.

Freedom of the Press Foundation (FPF) Chief of Advocacy Seth Stern said:

“Judge Merryday’s ruling completely misconstrues the Supreme Court’s ‘actual malice’ standard for defamation lawsuits by public officials. The whole point of that standard — which requires proof that reporters either knew their reporting was false or recklessly disregarded the truth — is to give the press breathing room to do its job and prevent judges from micromanaging journalists’ reporting processes.

“Second-guessing whether reporters should have met with the legal experts they consulted one more time, asked them a few more questions, or had them write a more formal opinion is precisely the kind of inquiry that courts are supposed to avoid, so that officials can’t use defamation lawsuits to make investigating them prohibitively risky and expensive.

“If the legitimacy of journalists’ editorial judgment is subject to the goalposts being shifted by the whims of individual judges who don’t know the first thing about journalism, the press will be forced to walk on eggshells when reporting on the powerful, and the public will suffer.”

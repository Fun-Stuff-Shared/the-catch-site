Register to Vote
Find out more about registering to vote in the Granite State. Your state and local election officials are here to help.
[NOTICE: On October 22, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court stayed a decision by the district court related to the use of student identification cards. This guidance has been updated to accurately reflect the Supreme Court's decision]
You can register to vote in local, state, and federal elections in New Hampshire if you are:
- A United States citizen;
- At least 18 years old on election day;
- A resident in the town/city ward you seek to register in. There is no minimum period you are required to have lived in your community before registering.
To register to vote, you will be required to complete a Voter Registration Form provided by your local town/city clerk or supervisor of the checklist. You will also need to provide proof of your identity, age, U.S. citizenship and domicile (primary residence address).
You can register as a Democrat, Republican, or Undeclared voter.
Where to register
Town/City Clerk's Office or Supervisors of the Checklist Meeting
Town/City Clerk's Office or Supervisors of the Checklist Meeting
You can register to vote up to 6-13 days before the election with your local town/city clerk or at a local supervisors of the checklist meeting.
Polling Location on Election Day
Polling Location on Election Day
If you miss the deadline to register before the election, you can register at your polling location on election day.
By Absentee
By Absentee
If you meet the state's requirements and qualifications and are unable to register in person, you may register by absentee.
Required documentation
Some examples of documents you can use to prove each qualification include:
- Identity - photo driver’s license issued by any state or the federal government; U.S. passport, passcard, armed services identification, or other photo identification issued by the U.S. government; photo identification issued by local or state government; or any other evidence that reasonably establishes that it is more likely than not that the person is who they claim to be. Note: effective October 22, 2026, registrants cannot use student identification cards to prove identity.
- Age - driver's license, government-issued photo ID, passport/passport card.
- U.S. Citizenship - U.S. birth certificate, U.S. passport/passport card, naturalization papers, other proof of citizenship issued by the United States Department of State, or other reasonable documentation which indicates the applicant is a United States citizen. Your citizenship may also be shown with proof that you were previously or are currently registered to vote in New Hampshire. Your citizenship may also be proven by completing a qualified voter affidavit attesting, subject to the penalties for voter fraud, that you are a United States citizen but do not possess some or all of the documents necessary to prove your citizenship.
- Domicile (primary residence) - NH driver's license, NH non-driver ID, NH resident vehicle registration, federal picture identification, government-issued check, benefit statement, or tax document with the current domicile address listed.
- If you do not possess the Domicile (primary residence) documents as detailed above, you may prove your domicile by completing the “Attestation of Not Possessing Qualified Domicile Proof Documents form AND providing one of the following:
- A rental/lease agreement, utility or other bill with your current domicile, or other qualified document under RSA 654:12; or
- A completed "Confirmation of Domicile" form, which is signed by the property owner/manager/tenant of the address at which the applicant resides; or
- A completed Homeless Confirmation of Domicile form.

Assistance with proving qualifications
If you do not have documentary proof of one or more of your voter qualifications, your municipal clerk or supervisors of the checklist may be able to assist you with proving your qualifications. With your permission, local election officials may search your current or prior voter registration status in New Hampshire and certain information from the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles and Division of Vital Records to help prove your voter qualifications.

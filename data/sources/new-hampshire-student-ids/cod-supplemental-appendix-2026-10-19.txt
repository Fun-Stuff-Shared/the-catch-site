                   Voting in New Hampshire Prior to HB 323

      1.     Since the introduction of New Hampshire’s voter ID law in 2012, up

until the passage of HB 323, many students were able to register to vote and obtain

a ballot using qualifying student identification card to establish identity. RSA

659:13.

      2.     Effective June 2, 2026, HB 323 amended RSA 659:13, II, a provision of

New Hampshire’s election code that is titled, “Obtaining a Ballot.”

      3.     On April 23, 2026, the Secretary of State’s Office sent an

NHVotes@sos.nh.gov email titled, “Subject: Informational Flyers - Voting for

Students in NH.” It stated: “Following the passage of HB 323, effective 6/2, student

IDs can no longer be used to register to vote or obtain a ballot on election day.”

That e-mail included links to a “Student Guide to Registering to Vote in

NH” and a “Student Guide to Voting in NH.” These guides stated that

voters need proof of identity to register to vote and vote, and that student

ID could not be used as proof of identity. The Student Guide to

Registering to Vote included information on how to obtain a NH Driver’s

License or Non-Driver ID Card, with a link tree to New Hampshire

Division of Motor Vehicles’ resources regarding which documents may be

used to obtain a NH Driver’s License or Non-Driver ID Card, how to

schedule an appointment to obtain a license or ID, and the fee schedule for

a license or ID.

      4.     In New Hampshire, voting registration is run locally by cities and towns.

RSA 654:8.

                            DEFENDANTS’ RECORD SUPPLEMENT

          Defendants New Hampshire Secretary of State David M. Scanlan and New Hampshire

Attorney General John M. Formella, each in their official capacities, by and through counsel, the

Office of the Attorney General, submit the following supplement to the record, as ordered by the

Court on September 18, 2026 (ECF No. 59).

          1.      Under New Hampshire law, only government-issued identification may be used to

obtain a ballot. RSA 659:13, II(a) (“Only the following forms of identification bearing a

photograph of the voter shall satisfy the identification requirements of paragraph I[.]”).

          2.      Where a person seeks to obtain a ballot but does not present one of the IDs listed

in RSA 659:13, II(a), the identity verification provisions of RSA 659:13, I(c) and II(b) govern

the supervisors of the checklist’s voter-assistance efforts.

          3.      If a voter does not present an acceptable form of ID, the ballot clerk must direct

the voter to a supervisor of the checklist. RSA 659:13, I(c)(1). There are at least five ways in

which a supervisor may be able to assist a voter’s identity verification.

       4.      First, a supervisor could resolve or assist in resolving a dispute regarding whether

a particular ID qualifies under RSA 659:13, II(a).

       5.      Second, a supervisor must check to see if a moderator, clerk, selectman, or other

complete an Attestation of Identity for a Registered Voter on Election Day. RSA 659:13, I(c)(4).

       6.      Third, a supervisor must review the voter’s qualifications and determine if the

supervisor can verify the voter’s identity. RSA 659:13, I(c)(3). For instance, if a New

Hampshire driver’s license shows a different name than that appearing on the checklist due to a

recent marriage, the supervisor should review the voter’s marriage certificate and allow the voter

to obtain a ballot. See id.

       7.      Fourth, a registered voter whose name has changed can update his or her name by

visiting the supervisors of the checklist and completing a new voter registration form. In certain

circumstances, the supervisors may be able to use the Statewide Voter Registration System to

access DMV or vital records information to verify the name change. See, e.g., Election

Procedure Manual at 83 (Defs.’ Ex. B).

       8.      Fifth, a supervisor is an important resource who can explain to a voter which

photo identification forms meet the requirements of RSA 659:13, and discuss options that suit

the voter’s needs.

  Secretary of State requests a stay of the court order on the use of student IDs

CONCORD, NH (October 6, 2026): New Hampshire Secretary of State David Scanlan releases the
following statement on the U.S. District Court’s order related to the use of student ID cards to
register to vote and obtain a ballot on election day.

Earlier this year, the legislature enacted HB 323, which was clearly intended to prohibit the use
of student identification cards for voting. HB 323 was titled “requiring the presentation of a
government-issued photographic means of identification in order to vote.” HB 323 expressly
removed student identification cards from the list of documents that a voter could use to prove
their identity when voting on election day. HB 323 further removed the criteria that had
previously been used to determine if a student identification card could be used.

The legislature subsequently enacted SB 223 to reinforce and strengthen HB 323’s clear
prohibition on the use of student identification cards. SB 223 changed RSA 659:13 to read “only
the following forms of identification bearing a photograph of the voter shall satisfy the identity
requirements of paragraph I” before the list of acceptable government-issued photo IDs.
(Emphasis added.)

The Secretary of State’s Office has a constitutional and statutory duty to faithfully follow all
election procedure laws that the legislature enacts. In doing so, this office follows the New
Hampshire Supreme Court’s instruction to interpret election procedures in the context of the
overall legislative scheme, consistent with legislative intent and common sense.

As relevant here, the voter ID law must be interpreted consistently with voter registration
statutes—both of which require voters to prove their identity. Before a person can vote in a
New Hampshire election, they must register to vote. To register to vote, a voter must prove
their identity. Additionally, all voters — including both previously registered voters and
election-day registrants — must prove their identity before obtaining a ballot on election day.
This Office has consistently and historically interpreted these two statutes to preclude a
situation where a voter could prove their identity when registering to vote on election day but
not be able to subsequently prove their identity when voting on election day.

RSA 654:12 provides a list of acceptable government-issued photo IDs that a person can use to
prove their identity when registering to vote. That list does not include “student identification
cards,” but it does allow a voter to prove their identity with “Any other evidence that reasonably
establishes that it is more likely than not that the person is who they claim to be.”




                                               17a

---

<!-- page 20 -->
Therefore, to give full effect to the legislative intent of HB 323 and avoid a reading contrary to
common sense, this office interpreted RSA 654:12 after the enactment of HB 323 as prohibiting
the use of student identification cards to prove identity when registering to vote. In enacting
HB 323 and SB 223, the legislature clearly intended that student identification cards not be
“reasonable evidence” of identity. If that were not the case, it would be possible for a person to
use a student identification card to register to vote on election day but not be allowed to obtain
a ballot and vote after registering. That would make no sense, confuse voters, and risk
disenfranchisement.

On October 2, 2026, the U.S. District Court for New Hampshire issued an order in a case
challenging the constitutionality of HB 323. Although the court declined to enjoin HB 323, the
court nevertheless ruled that the Secretary of State exceeded his authority because student
identification cards “may” constitute “other evidence that reasonably establishes that it is more
likely than not that [a voter registrant] is who they claim to be” when registering to vote. RSA
654:12. The court further ruled when a person is checking in to vote on election day, ballot
clerks must direct a voter with a student identification card to the supervisors of the checklist,
who shall “review the voter's qualifications and determine if the voter's identity can be
verified.” RSA 659:13, I(c)(1) & (3).

The court’s interpretation defies common sense and defeats the clear legislative intent of HB
323 and SB 223, when interpreted in the context of the overall legislative scheme, that “only”
government-issued identification be used to vote. The court’s interpretation further creates a
significant risk of unequal treatment of voters across the state because supervisors in each town
and ward across the state will have to decide on a case-by-case basis whether a particular
student identification card “along with other available evidence” reasonably proves the voter’s
identity.

Through the Attorney General’s Office, I will be seeking an emergency stay of the court’s order
because I do not believe the court correctly interpreted New Hampshire law and because
judicially altering election rules a month prior to the State General Election creates a risk of
voter confusion and disenfranchisement.

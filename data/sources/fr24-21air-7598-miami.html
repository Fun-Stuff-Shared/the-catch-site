21 Air 767 overruns runway on landing in Miami

Ian Petchenik
  • Updated: September 6, 2026
Add us as a preferred source
  • 21 Air 767 operating for Amazon overran runway in Miami
  • The 767 came to rest near a parking lot next to the airport
  • Aircraft was traveling at 112 knots as it exited the runway

A 21 Air 767 freighter operating for Amazon Prime Air (registration N1997A) overran Runway 30 upon landing at Miami International Airport on 6 September. The aircraft came to rest near a parking lot that services an Amazon warehouse, amongst other businesses, before catching fire. Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava says at least five people have been killed and at least three more have been taken to the hospital. The conditions of those in hospital are not yet known.

The NTSB has launched a Go Team to investigate the crash and will hold its first briefing on 7 September.

CSB7598 departed San Juan at 15:42 UTC and touched down in Miami at 17:53 UTC (13:53 Local time) with a flight time of 2h 11m. ADS-B data received by Flightradar24 shows the aircraft’s speed at 112 knots as it exited the usable area of the runway. The last received speed was 69 knots.

Aircraft Information

CSB7598 was operated by a 21 Air Boeing 767-33A(ER)(BDSF). The 32 year old aircraft flew with various airlines as a passenger carrier from 1994 to 2015, when it was converted to cargo configuration. This 767 is powered by two CF6-80C2B6F engines.

Weather in Miami

Weather at the time of the accident showed thunderstorms and gusting winds.

Winds at 17 knots, gusting to 26 knots. Visibility 10 Statute Miles. Thunderstorm at the airfield with scattered cumulonimbus clouds at 2,000 feet AGL, broken clouds at 4,800 feet, broken clouds at 15,000 feet.

This is an ongoing incident and further updates will be added as they are known.

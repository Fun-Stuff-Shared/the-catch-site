Judge Allows Trump Libel Suit Against New York Times Reporters

A judge has allowed President Trump to proceed with a $15 billion suit against the New York Times, finding a plausible claim that its reporters acted recklessly in accusing Trump of tax fraud.
Judge Steven Merryday denied the defendants’ motion to dismiss the lawsuit, holding that the book “Lucky Loser” includes a series of claims about Trump’s business dealings that are potentially defamatory.
Though the book cites tax experts who stated that Trump’s conduct was “highly suspicious” and “smells like a crime,” the judge ruled that was not enough to state outright that Trump had committed tax fraud or tax evasion. Such a clear-cut judgment would require “an explicit opinion from a qualified source after a thorough examination of pertinent facts,” the judge ruled.
Absent such a statement, the judge ruled that the reporters were venturing into risky territory.
“They know that they don’t know, but they are undeterred,” he wrote.
The book, published by Penguin Random House, included the allegation that Trump had obtained the equivalent of $400 million from his father, Fred, “much of it through fraudulent tax evasion schemes.” The book also asserted that Fred Trump had engaged in “likely tax fraud” when he gave his son gifts disguised as loans; and that the arrangement was designed to avoid gift and estate taxes.
In his lawsuit, Trump argued that the statements were false because neither he nor his father had ever been charged with a tax crime.
The New York Times published two articles drawn from the book in 2024. In the ruling, Merryday dismissed the claims pertaining to the first article, which focused on Trump’s relationship with TV producer Mark Burnett and the development of “The Apprentice.”
The judge also tossed statements from the second article that focused on Trump’s school record. But the judge allowed Trump to sue over a statement from that article that authorities had investigated his “ties to the Mafia” and had found “violations of money laundering laws.”
In response, a New York Times spokesperson said the paper was “encouraged” that almost all of the claims against the paper were dismissed.
“We continue to believe the remaining claim in the filing has no legal merit,” the spokesperson said. “We look forward to making this argument as we continue to defend ourselves against this baseless attack on The Times and the rights of the free and independent press in this country.”
The same judge dismissed an earlier version of Trump’s lawsuit last year, finding it overlong and full of colorful but irrelevant detail. Trump’s lawyers refiled a more concise version a month later.
The suit can now proceed to discovery and motions for summary judgment. The defendants in the case are the Times, three of its reporters, and Penguin Random House.

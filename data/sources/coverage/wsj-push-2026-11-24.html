      The Frantic White House Push to Get Jensen Huang Back

      Trump made clear he wanted the Nvidia CEO reinstated; on Monday he touted the decision, saying the market proved it was right

      By

      Amrith Ramkumar

      ,

      Josh Dawsey

      ,

      C. Ryan Barber

      and

      Robbie Whelan

      WASHINGTON—The artificial-intelligence boom that had powered the U.S. stock market and economy for months was coming undone.

      Jensen Huang, the longtime CEO of chipmaking giant Nvidia NVDA 31.48%increase; up pointing triangle, had been ousted following revelations of two affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Investors were hitting sell on the entire AI trade.

      President Trump told associates there was only one solution: getting Huang, a friend and ally, his job back.

      Over the weekend, Attorney General Todd Blanche presented an opening. The nation’s top law enforcement officer informed Trump and Vice President JD Vance on Saturday that Huang wasn’t the target or the subject of any Justice Department investigation, adding that the department had no evidence to suggest that he had broken laws or was a national security risk, according to U.S. officials.

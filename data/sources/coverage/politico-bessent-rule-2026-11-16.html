Bessent pushed new AI chips rule

The unusual circumstances surrounding the rule's development underscore the divisions within Trump's administration over how aggressively to restrict China's access to U.S. technology.

By Megan Messerly and Ari Hawkins

11/16/2026 10:06 PM EST|Updated: 11/16/2026 11:06 PM EST

Share

  • X
  • Facebook
  • Email
  • Reddit
  • WhatsApp
  • LinkedIn

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed through a new Commerce Department rule cutting off Chinese companies’ remote access to advanced AI chips from Nvidia and others in overseas data centers, according to three people familiar with the matter.

The new rule, which the the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security said in a statement was “written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council,” seeks to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies to circumvent U.S. export restrictions by remotely accessing advanced American chips housed in data centers in third countries, including in Southeast Asia.

Marco Rubio, President Donald Trump’s national security adviser who also serves as secretary of State, supported the rule as well, according to the people.

The three people were granted anonymity to share details of private discussions.

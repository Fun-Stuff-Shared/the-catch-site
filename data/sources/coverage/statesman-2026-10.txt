Trump blames Cornyn, Rove for Paxton’s Senate race troubles

If we have learned anything about Donald Trump, from his days as a self-promoting New York developer to reality TV star to president of the United States, it's that even acknowledging so much as the possibility of losing is anathema to his soul.
And if forced to confront such a possibility, he will assign the blame for it to someone else.

That's what made Trump's Thursday evening post on his social media platform, Truth Social, both surprising and predictable at the same time. In the post, the president accused Republican U.S. Sen. John Cornyn and veteran GOP strategist Karl Rove of roadblocking the chances of Ken Paxton defeating James Talarico in the U.S. Senate race that Texans will decide Nov. 3.
"If Ken Paxton loses, it will be because of WEAK and INEFFECTIVE Political Hacks like John Cornyn and Karl Rove," Trump said in the extended post.
The reasoning, Trump suggested, is that Cornyn has not come out and directly endorsed Paxton since the Texas attorney general soundly defeated him in the Republican primary runoff in May. And Rove, who was among the masterminds of former President George W. Bush's political rise, has not been shy about highlighting the many scandals and missteps that have dogged Paxton since long before he had set his sights on running for senator.
Polling has consistently shown Paxton trailing Talarico by a handful of percentage points in a state that hasn't elected a Democrat statewide since 1994. And Trump's endorsement of who he calls "the Greatest Attorney General in the History of Texas," along with his many visits to the state on Paxton's behalf, appear to have done little to improve those polling numbers.

So what that means, reading between the lines of the Truth Social post, is that Cornyn's and Rove's antipathy toward Paxton is the real reason that traction has so far eluded the Republican nominee.
Trump has been put in a bit of a bind in the Texas race for Senate — some of it by his own making. Much of the national chatter about the race suggests Paxton was Trump's handpicked nominee. But he wasn't. And, the chatter has continued, Trump's endorsement of Paxton in the runoff against Cornyn is what carried the day. It did not.
Let's review. Early in the cycle, Cornyn let it be known that he wanted a fifth six-year term in the Senate, even though Texas' conservative base had growing misgivings about him because, in part, Trump was never his first pick for president in 2016 or in 2024. Paxton entered the race over the objections of the national Republican establishment pretty much because he and Cornyn flat out don't like each other.

If Trump had, in fact, handpicked Paxton, he could have nudged Cornyn aside perhaps by offering him a prestigious ambassadorship or an influential post in the Cabinet. The president did neither, even though it was clear that a Paxton-Cornyn matchup would be ugly, bloody and very expensive for the Republican Party. And, as it turned out, it was all three.
Recall that Trump stayed neutral in the Senate primary, in which Paxton and Cornyn were joined by U.S. Rep. Wesley Hunt of Houston, who would finish third. After Cornyn finished with the most votes, but short of a majority, Trump said he would make an endorsement ahead of the runoff and added that he expected the non-endorsed candidate to quit the race. However, Paxton and Cornyn both said they'd remain in the race regardless of any presidential endorsement.
Then, for weeks, no endorsement came from Trump. But on the day that early voting started, with polls showing Paxton comfortably ahead, Trump endorsed the attorney general and noted that he had been the more loyal of the two candidates. The endorsement certainly didn't hurt Paxton among runoff voters, but the solid polling lead and the near 2-1 final result suggest he'd have won with or without the president's help.
But that didn't stop Trump from taking credit for Paxton's victory. And that helped cement the narrative that the attorney general who had survived an indictment on fraud charges and a Republican-led impeachment on accusations of bribery and misuse of office was the GOP nominee for Senate because Trump had wanted him to be.

And that brings us back to the Truth Social post and the finger-pointing therein. Chad Wilbanks, an Austin lobbyist and a former executive director of the Texas Republican Party, reposted a screenshot of Trump's words. And it wasn't so much to amplify them as to call them — and the president — out.
"I don’t know why he does this. It turns off so many (people) needed to vote Republican. It’s almost as if he’s seen internal polling numbers and is already starting the blame game," Wilbanks said on X. "Respectfully, this is loser mentality."
In an interview Friday, Wilbanks said that if Trump's goal was to fire up fence-sitting Republicans to fall in line behind Paxton, he should have highlighted the attorney general's positive qualities and pointed out why conservatives should defeat Talarico.
"He ought to be telling our voters — Republican voters, independent voters — to fight all the way through Election Day, run through the finish line until the polls close, so we can come out on top," Wilbanks said.

As if he anticipated comments like those of Wilbanks, Trump ended his post with a winning message. And who was the "winner" in that message? None other than Donald J. Trump himself.
"As your ALL TIME WINNER in the History of Texas, Trump won 96% of the Counties in Texas, 242 of 254 ... and by MILLIONS of Popular Votes, the Highest in the History of Texas," he wrote, "your favorite President (ME!) is asking all of those who supported Senator John Cornyn to UNITE, and immediately switch your support to Ken Paxton."

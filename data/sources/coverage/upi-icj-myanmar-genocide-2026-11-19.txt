Myanmar found guilty of genocide against Rohingya - UPI.com

Nov. 19 (UPI) -- The International Court of Justice ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya Muslim people, marking the first time it's delivered a verdict against an entire country.
In a 9-6 vote, Judge Yuji Iwasawa said, the court "finds that the Republic of the Union of Myanmar has committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine State." In another 9-6 vote, the court found that Myanmar failed to prevent genocide by people alongside the country's security forces. In a 14-1 vote, the court decided that Myanmar "must cease any ongoing violation of its obligations" under the genocide convention and pay reparations to the victims.
The court has no power to enforce its rulings but the decision marks an important legal precedent. It's the first time the court has found a country guilty of violating the 1948 Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide.
The case stems from the 2017 burning of about 400 Rohingya villages in "clearance operations." Ethnic Rakhine groups tore through the towns, set the villages on fire and killed at least 10,000 people, according to United Nations estimates. The attacks were part of a collective punishment after Rohingya militants had attacked a police post and military base.
About 1.2 million Rohingya fled to refugee camps in Bangladesh.
The case against Myanmar was brought by Gambia, a small, mostly Muslim nation on the west coast of Africa. It was chosen by the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, a 57-member group that also paid for the lawyers in the case.
Gambia brought the case to the Hague, the Netherlands in 2019, and 11 other countries officially supported the case.
British Human Rights Lawyer Philippe Sands told BBC Radio 4's the World at One that it's a "historic judgment."
Sands was part of the legal team for Gambia.
It's "right up there with the great moments of international justice: Nuremberg, Pinochet, and others," Sands said.
But it doesn't bring back the victims or allow anyone safely back to their homes, Sands said.
U.N. Human Rights Chief Volker Türk said the ruling "must now translate into real action."
"The court has made clear Myanmar's responsibilities. It sends a strong message to all states around the world that those responsible for genocide will be held to account," he said in a statement.
He added that the "suffering, misery and humiliation" the Rohingya people suffered is a "stain on the conscience of humanity" and urged countries to support reparations and programs for Rohingya survivors.
British foreign secretary Ed Miliband ordered that Myanmar's defense attache be kicked out of the United Kingdom and summoned the charge d'affairs.
The United States decided that the Rohingya people were victims of genocide nine years ago.
Last month, Mike Waltz, the U.S. ambassador to the United Nations, accused Gambia's designated judge, Navi Pillay, of "tipping the scales" to "lower the legal threshold for proving genocide so that [a] weakened and false standard can later be weaponized against American and allied soldiers whenever they raise a finger against terrorists that hide behind civilians," the BBC reported.
Former U.N. lawyer Oliver Windridge said he disagrees with Waltz. He said it "demonstrates how the existing threshold can be satisfied when the evidence is sufficiently compelling."

Trump announces Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program

Americans who report fraud in taxpayer-funded federal programs would earn a commission under a new plan the Trump administration announced Tuesday. The Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program would reward whistleblowers with a 1% bounty for helping the federal government recover the money.
“If it’s $10 million, $200 million, $400 million, we’re giving a 1% commission to that person,” President Donald Trump said at an Oval Office event where he was joined by Vice President and White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud Chairman JD Vance, along with White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy Stephen Miller, Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz and conservative influencer Nick Shirley.
Trump said the 1% commission did not have a cap and was available to all Americans, including journalists.
“When you get 1% of a billion dollars, it’s going to be a tremendous amount of money,” the president said, adding: “People will make millions of dollars by turning others in.”
Whistleblowers are encouraged to report cases of corruption and theft to the fraud.gov website. Clicking a tab that says “click to report fraud” automatically links users to the new America.gov AI chatbot, where they can start a report by typing in what happened so it can be forwarded to the appropriate federal office for review, the site says.
Fraud.gov is home to the Trump administration’s official ledger of the fraud, waste and corruption the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud has claimed to have discovered since January 2025. The tally is currently listed as $260.7 billion, although news outlet investigations say there isn’t enough publicly available information to verify the claim.
Some reviewers of the fraud accounting say figures have been duplicated in some cases or are otherwise unproven.
On Tuesday, Shirley became the first recipient to receive a check through the new whistleblower incentive program, when he was gifted $10,000 for his 2025 investigation into what he said were fake Somali-owned childcare centers in Minnesota that had collected millions of dollars in federal subsidies.
“To Americans across the country, your tax dollars are being stolen, and your tax dollars are being stolen from programs that help feed the needy, that help ensure that a young mom who can’t afford a doctor can get access to a doctor for her kids,” Vance said. “It steals from the very social fabric of the United States of America. The president has said it must stop, and it is stopping.”
Trump did not detail how whistleblower tips will be vetted, but he said he plans to rule on who identified the fraud and can claim the money.
“I will be the arbitrator of all of these decisions,” he said.
“You could have disputes where you have three people come in,” he said, adding that the arbitration will be binding and that whistleblowers will receive their money immediately without the need for a criminal charge or conviction.
“Our plan is not to limit this just to criminal prosecution,” Vance said. “If you find the money and you help us recover it, then you benefit from the program.”
Project on Government Oversight Senior Policy Counsel Joe Spielberger criticized the new program, saying it won't meaningfully curb fraud.
“This is not a serious anti-fraud or whistleblower incentive program," Spielberger said in a statement. "The administration and their allies have continuously cited combating fraud as a cover to persecute political opponents and immigrant communities. There is no evidence that tips generated from the public will be treated similarly and consistently investigated. This only incentivizes political provocateurs to target anyone the administration deems an enemy.”
The Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program isn’t the first to incentivize Americans to report fraud, tax evasion or securities violations. The False Claims Act of 1863 compensates whistleblowers with 15%-30% of the funds collected by the government in cases in which fraud against federal contracts has been discovered.
Vance said 99% of the fraud his White House Task Force is finding in the federal government isn’t covered by the False Claims Act.

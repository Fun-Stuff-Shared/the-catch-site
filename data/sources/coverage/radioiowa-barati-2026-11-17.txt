Planned Friday blast could coat section of eastern Iowa in lead dust - Radio Iowa

Parts of southeast Iowa could be dealing with a toxic cloud of lead-laden dust on Friday.
The U.S. Army plans to detonate nearly five tons of unstable lead azide between 11 AM and noon at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant outside Burlington.
Army spokesperson Justine Barati says the explosion could send fine particles of lead as far north as Maquoketa.
“The good thing is, if people have concerns about it being on their property, all they have to do is hose that off, and it’s gone.”
Lead azide is the primary explosive in most detonators, and it’s being held in an ethanol-and-water solution in two underground concrete storage containers.
Barati says investigations early this year found those tanks were too dry and the lead azide was forming crystals.
“When it crystallizes, then it becomes very volatile,” Barati says. “A nationwide team of experts has been assessing this since then to figure out what was the best way to dispose of this, and they determined that the safest method was to detonate it in place.”
The current weather models show potential lead air dispersion up through Muscatine and through a narrow corridor between Iowa City, Cedar Rapids, Clinton and Davenport.
The Army will be monitoring weather patterns and update its exposure maps regularly before and after the detonation.
(By Eliza Billingham, Iowa Public Radio)

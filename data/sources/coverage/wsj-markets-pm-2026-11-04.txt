What Happened in Markets Today
Stocks rallied following the midterm elections. Investors traditionally like divided government, and that could be all the more true under this president, who has given them headaches on everything from tariffs to Iran. The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,569 points, or 3.0%. The S&P 500 rose 1.6% and the Nasdaq gained 1.4%.
The reaction in tech stocks was split. New Democratic governors in states like Texas and Ohio could slow the construction of new data centers. That hit the more speculative “neoclouds” like CoreWeave and Nebius, whose stocks fell 9.9% and 6.7% respectively. It also brought down suppliers of chips and other hardware to data centers, with Nvidia falling 3.1% and memory chip maker Micron declining 7.0%. But shares of big “hyperscalers” jumped. Their existing data center capacity could become more valuable, and they may have the financial and political heft to keep building. Amazon and Microsoft both gained around 6%.

Oil plummeted. The reaction in oil prices was at first subdued, but accelerated after Vice President JD Vance blamed the Iran war for Republican defeats, and reports emerged that Trump is considering replacing Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, though the president denied this. Brent crude futures settled down 8.7% to $94.62 per barrel.
CVS will break off its namesake pharmacies. The company said it has decided to split in two, reversing one of the signature deals that defined an era of health industry consolidation. One company will house its insurance giant Aetna and pharmacy-benefit manager Caremark, the other its 9,000 retail pharmacies. CVS shares ended the day down 12.5%.

One Big Story
Americans’ long-simmering frustrations over the high cost of living boiled over Tuesday night to rearrange the nation’s political map. Voters decisively swept Democrats to power in the House and Senate after two years of rising prices, a deepening housing crisis and the everyday pain of filling up a gas tank.
Among voters polled by the Associated Press, roughly 39% said the economy was the single most important issue facing the country, and nearly 90% said the cost of living was a crucial or major factor in their vote.

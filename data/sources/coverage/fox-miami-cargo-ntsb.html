NTSB reveals if crew survived Amazon cargo jet disaster as it shares details about victims

The crew survived after the plane struck a cleaning van and civilian vehicle while landing at Miami International Airport, officials said

By Bonny Chu Fox News

Amazon Miami cargo jet runway overrun involved 2 vehicles with 8 occupants: NTSB

NTSB Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy said the Boeing 767 continued about 1,300 feet beyond the paved runway surface.

NEWYou can now listen to Fox News articles!

Federal regulators said the two crew members aboard the Amazon cargo jet that crashed at Miami International Airport on Sunday survived the accident.

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) provided the update during a Sunday press conference after officials said the cargo plane overran the runway and caught fire while landing. The crash killed five people and injured five others, authorities said Sunday. 

The jet collided with two vehicles carrying at least eight occupants, according to NTSB Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy. 

Seven of the victims were in a van that was struck and "torn apart" on airport grounds, Homendy said. The 2012 white Ford Econoline van was owned by Ocean Service Corporation, a contract cleaning company that provides services for airlines, she said.

NTSB investigators examine a wreckage field Monday after a Boeing 767 cargo plane overran the end of Runway 30 while landing Sunday at Miami International Airport. (@NTSB_Newsroom/X)

The NTSB said the aircraft then crashed through the airport perimeter and struck a civilian vehicle, a Toyota Corolla Cross LE, that was traveling on Northwest 67th Avenue. 

Homendy said investigators observed what appeared to be deployed airbags in the vehicles as they surveyed the crash site. 

"You can see components from vehicles, both passenger vehicles and the aircraft itself," Homendy said. 

The jet then "continued into the grass and impacted another fence line, which separated an area where Tesla robotaxis were stationed," Homendy said. 

An Amazon Prime Air Boeing 767 crashes off the runway and crushes a vehicle near the Miami International Airport in Miami on Sunday, Sept. 6, 2026.  (Al Diaz/Miami Herald/Tribune News Service via Getty Images)

In total, the jet reportedly traveled 1,300 feet beyond the paved surface. 

Homendy said the wreckage was "extensive," noting that the crash damaged airport navigational aids and equipment, while fencing was ripped apart throughout the area.

Officials investigate a Prime Air cargo plane at Miami International Airport after it overran the runway at Miami International Airport in Miami, Florida, on September 6, 2026. (CHANDAN KHANNA / AFP via Getty Images)

Officials said the Boeing 767 had completed two other flights before crashing in Miami, where its operator, 21 Air LLC, is based. 

The aircraft traveled from Cincinnati to Miami, then from Miami to San Juan, Puerto Rico, before returning to Miami, Homendy said. 

NTSB investigators have recovered the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder, which they planned to examine. 

Prime Air flight 7598 became disabled after overrunning Miami International Airport’s diagonal runway. (Google Maps)

Homendy described the crash site as "devastating," saying wreckage was scattered throughout the area. 

"I would describe it in one word, devastating, utter devastation," she said.

"I mean, you can see on the paved surface, tire marks that continue through the grass. There's debris everywhere."

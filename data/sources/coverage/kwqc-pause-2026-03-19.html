Iowa Army Ammunitions Plant pause continues

By KWQC Staff
Published: Mar. 19, 2026 at 2:58 PM CDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

MIDDLETOWN, Iowa (KWQC) - The Iowa Army Ammunition Plant remains paused while experts continue a safety review to plan the safe removal of lead azide.

The plant paused operations on March 12 for a safety review.

Lead azide is a chemical compound used to make munitions that must be disposed of safely, according to the Joint Munitions Command.

“The Army will implement a multi-step plan to neutralize and dispose of the lead azide,” said Lt. Col. Daniel Nosse, commander of the plant. “There is no immediate danger to the public. The safety of our people and our neighbors is non-negotiable.”

The plant, the Joint Munitions Command and federal and state partners are working with experts in munitions, engineering and environmental safety to determine the safest way to remove the material.

Officials said the plant will continue working with local emergency management, first responders and environmental regulators and will let the community know about potential impacts.

At this time, there is no estimate for when the plant will resume operations.

Justice Department asks courts to block Alaska Senate race if Democrat wins - UPI.com

Oct. 23 (UPI) -- The U.S. Justice Department has filed a lawsuit preemptively calling on the courts to block the certification of Alaska's upcoming Senate race if the Democratic candidate wins.
Democrat Mary Peltola and incumbent Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan are locked in a tight battle for the seat that could have implications for party control in the upper chamber of Congress, The Washington Post reported.
If Democrats gain four seats in the November mid-term elections, the party would take control of the Senate, weakening the Republican Party's position in Congress.
The suit, filed Thursday in the U.S. District Court for the District of Alaska, said the candidacy of a third party in the race, Dan. J. Sullivan Jr., was meant to mislead voters into voting for the wrong Republican candidate, splitting the vote and giving Peltola an advantage. Dan J. Sullivan Jr. is also a Republican but is listed on ballots without a party affiliation.
The Justice Department said the certification of results of the race should be delayed if the combination of votes for the two men named Dan Sullivan would surpass Peltola's number of votes.
Alaska's Republican primary was held in August. Sen. Dan S. Sullivan received 68,726 votes while Daniel J. Sullivan got 4,107.
President Donald Trump told reporters Friday that he was behind the lawsuit, CNN reported.
"Well, I'm the one that said 'do it,'" he said.
Attorney General Todd Blanche said -- before the lawsuit was filed -- that the Justice Department wouldn't interfere in the race in Alaska.
"It is not DOJ's intention to -- and it's never our intention to -- improperly interfere with elections or the state certification process," he said Thursday. "But, you know, to say there's no circumstances that we would ever do that -- of course, I'm not going to say."
Nate Adams, a spokesperson for Sen. Sullivan, said the senator would "let the DOJ complain run its course."
"Alaskans can tell the difference between a Democrat sideshow and Senator Sullivan's strong record of delivering for Alaska, a record he'll keep building on for the next six years," Adams said.

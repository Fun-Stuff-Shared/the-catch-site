Cantwell calls on Lutnick to stop AI chip sales to China

Sen. Maria Cantwell, the top Democrat on the Senate commerce committee, alleged in a letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Sunday that the department under his leadership failed to prevent China from accessing the U.S.'s most advanced artificial intelligence chips.
In the letter, Cantwell prodded Lutnick to enforce export controls and stop the flow of AI chips to companies barred from receiving the technology. She sent the missive ahead of a planned meeting this week between U.S. officials and Chinese President Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation, in China.
Her urging comes as Washington reels from revelations that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with an alleged Chinese spy named Alice Huang. Nvidia is the leading U.S. company designing advanced AI chips.
Alice Huang was an executive at Singaporean technology company Megaspeed that allegedly smuggled restricted chips to China.
Lutnick, meanwhile, has come under fire for stalling efforts to crack down on chip sales to China and for his close personal relationship with Jensen Huang, who was a vocal opponent of export controls.
"The Department of Commerce has resisted efforts to restrict companies flagged as national security threats, weakened safeguards, and left open pathways for the PRC to train advanced AI models using sensitive American technology," Cantwell wrote to Lutnick. "These failures to carry out the law help give away America's technology advantage in AI."
The Commerce Department did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Monday.
Cantwell called on Lutnick to use his authority to enforce a 2018 law, known as the Export Control Reform Act, which gives the Commerce Department broad latitude to enforce export controls and protect sensitive American technology.
She also cites potential financial conflicts involving Lutnick's sons, who control Cantor Fitzgerald, which she said held $1.2 billion in Nvidia stock at the time of his nomination. And she notes the Trump administration's 25% fee on certain Nvidia chips sold to China, which she said could create "a perverse financial incentive to approve exports of sensitive American technology."
"America's national security and AI leadership cannot be compromised by family business interests or a policy that generates federal revenue from exporting technology critical to our competitive edge," Cantwell wrote.
Both Democratic and Republican members of Congress have ramped up pressure on Lutnick and other Commerce Department officials after a Nov. 9 New York Times report alleging the department may have slow-walked an investigation into Megaspeed.
On Thursday, Senate banking committee Chair Tim Scott, R-S.C., summoned both Huang and Lutnick to testify before his committee in December.
Lutnick has agreed to appear before the panel, Kristen Eichamer, a Commerce Department spokesperson, said in a statement last week.
And Democrats, who will take control of both the House and Senate in January, have signaled they will investigate Jensen Huang and Commerce officials.
On Wednesday, Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., the top Democrat on the Senate banking panel, and Sen. Chris Coons, D-Del., the top Democrat on the Senate Foreign Relations subpanel with jurisdiction over East Asia and the Pacific, sent a letter to Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller instructing them to preserve all documents and communications related to Nvidia's exports of "advanced AI chips to China or to entities that may divert such chips to China."
The letter gives all three officials until Nov. 17 to confirm that preservation directives have been issued.

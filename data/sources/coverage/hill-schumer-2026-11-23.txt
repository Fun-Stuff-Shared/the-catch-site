Chuck Schumer vows investigation into Jensen Huang and Nvidia after CEO's return

Schumer: Democrats will use ‘full power’ to probe Huang, Trump, Nvidia executives
Today's top stories - November 23, 2026
Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) said Monday that Democrats will use their “full power” to investigate Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and other executives at the company after Huang was reinstated.
“The Trump Administration’s rush to reinstate Jensen Huang underscores the need for Congress to act,” Schumer said Monday morning in a post on the social platform X. “Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security, emphasizing that he’s learned nothing from his resounding election defeat.”
“Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,” he added.
The post from Schumer, who is likely the next Senate majority leader in the upcoming Congress, follows Huang being reinstated as Nvidia’s CEO. Huang returned to the role a little more than a week after he stepped down amid an affair scandal reportedly involving an alleged Chinese spy.
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” Harvey Jones, Nvidia’s board chair, said in a statement Monday.
“We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company,” he continued.
Trump, who is an ally of Huang, lauded Nvidia’s board of directors over Huang’s reinstatement, saying it “made the correct and proper decision.”
“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” Trump said on Truth Social.
The president later said he convinced Nvidia’s board to bring Huang back.
The Hill has reached out to the White House and Nvidia for comment.

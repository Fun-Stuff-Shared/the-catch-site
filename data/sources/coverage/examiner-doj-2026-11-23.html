DOJ presses ahead with Megaspeed and Super Micro investigations after Jensen Huang comeback at Nvidia

By Kaelan Deese
Published November 23, 2026 6:07pm ET | Updated November 23, 2026 6:08pm ET
Advertisement
Add Washington Examiner on Google
Advertisement

The Justice Department is pressing ahead with investigations involving Megaspeed and Super Micro Computers as President Donald Trump and tech executives welcomed Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO on Monday, while other lawmakers and critics question the government’s role in his reinstatement.

A law enforcement source told the Washington Examiner on Monday that both investigations were “still going strong” and that the DOJ never opened an investigation into Jensen Huang or Nvidia as part of the Megaspeed case, despite Trump’s reference in a Truth Social post on Monday morning to the attorney general’s role in clearing Huang. Senators, meanwhile, are pressing the administration to explain what federal officials reviewed and what they told Nvidia’s board before it concluded that Huang’s return posed no national security risk.

“The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct,” Trump wrote on Monday.

Advertisement

Megaspeed and Super Micro Computers are two technology companies accused of helping Chinese interests acquire Nvidia chips in defiance of U.S. export controls.

“The AG did speak with the President over the weekend,” a Justice Department official told reporters Monday. “The DOJ has no evidence of any wrongdoing by either Jensen Huang or Nvidia.”

The law enforcement source further said that two active investigations have been underway for more than a year, emphasizing that Jensen Huang and Nvidia have cooperated. Semafor reported Nov. 12 that the inquiries focused on suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang’s involvement and that there was no indication prosecutors would seek criminal charges against Jensen Huang or Nvidia. Alice Huang and Jensen Huang were having an affair, and Alice Huang was an executive at Megaspeed.

Advertisement

Trump had publicly urged Nvidia’s board to rehire Jensen Huang as recently as Sunday night, the Associated Press reported Monday. The board announced a unanimous vote to reinstate him six days after ruling out his return. Trump took credit for persuading directors in a CNBC interview, and Nvidia shares jumped more than 30% at the opening bell.

But lawmakers on both sides of the aisle were skeptical of the Trump administration’s intervention.

Sen. John Curtis (R-UT) pressed for the names of the officials who advised the board and details of their advice. Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) sought to learn whether the FBI had warned Huang in a defensive briefing, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) called the reinstatement “reckless”.

Advertisement

Law enforcement experts weigh in on investigation’s handling

Former law enforcement officials who spoke to the Washington Examiner differed on whether federal investigators should have opened a case into Huang or Nvidia before Trump backed his return. Some said the facts may not have warranted a criminal inquiry, while others said the affair and the reported warning to Trump from Secretary of State Marco Rubio should have invited closer scrutiny before the administration endorsed Jensen Huang’s reinstatement.

Former FBI agent Stuart Kaplan said national security inquiries often remain intelligence-gathering efforts, rather than domestic criminal cases.

“It is possible that at the end of whatever they were doing on the national security side, that it did not rise to the level that there was a need for a concurring investigation on the criminal side,” he said.

Hans von Spakovsky, a former DOJ official now with Advancing American Freedom, said that if Alice Huang were a Chinese agent, the relationship itself warranted an inquiry into whether Jensen Huang compromised national security.

“To say such an affair is not sufficient evidence is a whitewash,” he said. Attorney General Todd Blanche said at a Nov. 12 press conference that the DOJ had yet to determine whether Alice Huang was a Chinese spy.

Meanwhile, former federal prosecutor Neama Rahmani said the way DOJ handled its inquiry into Alice Huang “makes sense” if Nvidia and its CEO are cooperating witnesses in the separate but related Megaspeed investigation.

“It makes sense that Alice Huang, Megaspeed and Super Micro are under investigation for violating federal law related to export of semiconductor technology to China,” Rahmani said. “Jensen Huang and Nvidia could be cooperating witnesses in that investigation, which would mean they wouldn’t necessarily be a subject or target of the investigation.”

Huang back as CEO, but still the subject of internal oversight

Nvidia’s board said Monday that its own inquiry “will proceed to conclusion” and that Jensen Huang has committed to cooperating. Independent directors retained former acting U.S. Attorney Joon H. Kim to assist, the Washington Examiner reported Nov. 10.

The Department of Commerce and DOJ said in an unsigned joint statement last week that Commerce opened the Megaspeed inquiry in 2024 over possible export-control violations and sought Nvidia’s sales records before DOJ took the lead. Bloomberg reported Nov. 12 that federal prosecutors were examining whether entities tied to Alice Huang helped smuggle Super Micro products into China.

Singapore authorities allege her associate Li Ming misled Super Micro about a prospective server purchase. Li also helped make major decisions at Megaspeed, the Wall Street Journal reported Saturday.

The law enforcement source also told the Washington Examiner the DOJ’s separate antitrust review involving Nvidia remains open. Jensen Huang is, meanwhile, scheduled to sit for a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1, potentially giving Congress its first opening to press further as scrutiny of his reinstatement continued on Monday.

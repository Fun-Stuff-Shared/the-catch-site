Chutzpah Department: After Federal District Court Failed to Act within Hours on DOJ "Request" to Expedite TRO Hearing in California Ballot Case, DOJ Already Appealing to the Ninth Circuit - Election Law Blog

I predicted this morning that the DOJ’s request for the federal court to rule on its TRO motion by noon today (rather than to wait for the already set hearing on October 27) would lead it to attempt to leapfrog over the judge, likely to the Ninth Circuit, and then to SCOTUS. And now, just 5 hours after the DOJ chutzpah-imposed deadline for the district court, DOJ filed this notice of appeal.
To say this is irregular is an understatement. This is a complex case with two government defendants and a number of potential intervenors. The court’s scheduling order was a reasonable one given the complexity (and I’d say, given the very weak case of DOJ on the merits to force California to segregate ballots).
I’m not aware of any case where a party, much less the DOJ, sought to appeal a district court’s failure to quickly enough schedule a hearing on a TRO when a hearing had already been set. (I’m not even sure that DOJ’s “request” is a motion that’s appealable as an interlocutory order.) I expect this will get denied by the Ninth Circuit, or, maybe the 9th Circuit will take too long for the impatient DOJ and it will try to go directly to SCOTUS.
This is a Hail Mary procedurally on a Hail Mary suit on the merits.
Today AG Todd Blanche said that DOJ was not trying to cause “chaos” in the elections. That’s exactly what this move is.
UPDATE: It strikes me that this is a form of stealth “mandamus,” which is a court order to a lower court or other government official to do something. That’s a very tough thing to get, especially when there is zero reason to believe that the district court judge has done anything improper with timing or anything else.

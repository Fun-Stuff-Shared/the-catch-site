Extreme flooding sets records in Florida, with more rain to come

Parts of Florida remained underwater Thursday, with schools closed, residents stranded and little relief in sight as forecasters warned of more downpours.

Along the Space Coast, the National Weather Service warned of life-threatening flash flooding in central Brevard County as bands of heavy rain continued to move ashore. Videos showed cars stranded in floodwaters and neighborhoods submerged. In Palm Bay, emergency crews used boats to evacuate residents.

After days of heavy rain, the St. Johns River, Florida’s longest river, rose to a record 18.1 feet at a gauge west of Cocoa on Thursday morning.

Farther south, Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport in Broward County had recorded nearly 17 inches of rain over two days by Thursday morning, according to preliminary National Weather Service totals.

The flooding kept public schools in Broward, Miami-Dade and Brevard counties closed Thursday.

A flood watch remained in effect for parts of southeast Florida through Thursday evening, while most of east-central Florida was under a watch through Friday evening.

Forecasters warned that some areas from Indian River County north to Volusia County could see an additional 4 to 8 inches of rain through Friday night, with totals potentially topping 15 inches near the coast.

Proposed change to SNAP threatens free school meals for 1.7 million children

The Trump administration’s changes to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, or SNAP, cut more than 5 million people off from federal food benefits and eliminated free school meals for some students.

Now, a newly proposed rule threatens free school meals for nearly 2 million children.

The Agriculture Department wants to limit a provision called “broad-based categorical eligibility,” which allows families that receive certain public benefits to automatically qualify for SNAP, commonly known as food stamps.

The department, which runs SNAP, estimates the change could drop 6.3 million people from the food assistance program because they exceed income limits. Because SNAP recipients are automatically eligible for free school meals, the families of more than 1.7 million children affected by the change would have to fill out paperwork to apply for the free meals — or lose access to them.

Most of those children — 1.66 million — would still be living in households that qualify for free meals, according to USDA. School districts have said automatic enrollment has been helpful because many families don’t complete the paperwork. Districts end up footing the bill for those children’s meals.

USDA said the rule change would ensure SNAP can be accessed only by the households that need it most. Nutrition advocates argue it would exacerbate food insecurity.

The public can comment on the proposal until Dec. 10. If adopted, the rule will take effect in fall 2028.

Van Gogh painting nets record $190.4M after dispute over its history

A Vincent van Gogh painting fetched a record-breaking $190.4 million at auction after a last-minute agreement between the sellers and the heirs of the painting’s former Jewish owners allowed the sale to proceed.

“Châtaigniers en fleurs” (“Blossoming Chestnut Trees”) went under the hammer at Sotheby’s on Wednesday evening, and secured the highest price ever paid for a Van Gogh artwork.

The 1890 painting “dates from the extraordinary period of creative intensity that marked the final two months of the artist’s life,” the auction house said.

The sale appeared uncertain last week, as descendants of the painting’s former owners, Samuel and Hedwig Fischer, said the family sold the painting in the 1930s due to Nazi persecution.

On the day of the auction, Sotheby’s confirmed that a deal between the Fischer heirs and the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which has owned the painting for more than 50 years, would allow the sale to proceed. “In recognition of the painting’s complex Nazi-era history … the two families have reached a mutually satisfactory resolution,” Sotheby’s said in a statement.

Gunnar Schnabel, an attorney for the Fischer heirs, said the family hopes the resolution “may offer some encouragement to other families who have faced persecution during the Nazi era.”

Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya minority, top U.N. court rules

The International Court of Justice ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in a landmark judgment. It is the first time the top court of the United Nations has ruled that a state violated the 1948 Genocide Convention.

Judges at the court in The Hague voted 9 to 6 that Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya Muslims in its 2017 military operations in the northern Rakhine state.

“The court finds that Myanmar’s security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians, perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence, as well as acts of extreme violence against children, and attacked fleeing Rohingya,” presiding Judge Yuji Iwasawa said.

In the case brought by Gambia in 2019, Myanmar argued that its operations were a legitimate defense against militants.

The ICJ said evidence shows about 10,000 people were killed.

The court has ordered Myanmar to provide reparations including safe return for Rohingya refugees, nearly a million of whom were driven to camps in Bangladesh. It said Myanmar should prosecute perpetrators and pay compensation that will be decided in later proceedings.

ICJ rulings are legally binding, but the only way to enforce them is through a vote of the U.N. Security Council.

Thursday’s ruling could weigh on other cases. South Africa has brought a case accusing Israel of genocide in Gaza. Israel rejects the allegations, which have been echoed by Israeli and international rights groups. An ICJ verdict can take years.

Trump displays an all-male lineup of top aides on Asia trip

The U.S. delegation that traveled to China with President Donald Trump was overwhelmingly male, highlighting the recent departures of high-profile women from the president’s inner circle.

The cohort that met the Chinese delegation for a meeting Thursday included Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, Ambassador to China David Perdue and Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino. The Chinese delegation, too, was made up entirely of men.

Trump’s first administration was also heavily male but had some women in senior positions, including press secretary Sarah Sanders. His second term began with Susie Wiles as chief of staff.

But with Wiles having been replaced by Miller, the delegation that traveled with Trump was overwhelmingly male to an extent that hasn’t been true in decades.

Of the 17 people who traveled on Air Force One with Trump on his trip to the Philippines and China, four are women: Special Assistant Alina Bulazel, Executive Assistant Natalie Harp, Communications Advisor Margo Martin and Chief of Protocol Monica Crowley, according to lists provided to reporters by the White House.

In addition to Wiles, former press secretary Karoline Leavitt departed her post in August, and Trump has yet to find a permanent replacement. Katie Zacharia briefly filled the role but left after only five days, the shortest tenure for a press secretary in U.S. history.

All cabinet members who have left the administration since the beginning of Trump’s term have been women.

Attorney General Pam Bondi, Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi L. Noem and Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard have resigned or been fired and were replaced by a man.

China’s leadership has also steadily regressed in gender representation. Women have never held a spot on the Politburo Standing Committee, the country’s top decision-making body, and the current 22-person Politburo is the first in two decades not to include a woman.

Joshua Yang and Cleve Wootson contributed to this report.

A paramedic responded to a deadly accident. She discovered the victim was her son.

On Monday evening, first responders in Sigurd, Utah, received an emergency call: A 28-year-old man was trapped after a vehicle he was helping to fix fell on him.

Deputies reached the scene and were able to pull Logan Mumford free, but he was “unresponsive with obvious significant trauma to the head,” Sevier County Sheriff’s Office said in a statement this week. Then paramedics arrived — among them Mumford’s mother.

“It was not until she heard his name that it was realized it was her own son,” the sheriff’s office said. The man’s wife looked on as his mother tried to save his life, the statement said.

Mumford, a father of two young children, was airlifted to the hospital but died of his injuries.

“One of my fellow paramedics, a friend, and a fellow first responder, experienced every parent’s worst nightmare,” a Sevier County paramedic, who was not named, said in the same statement.

“Behind every emergency call is a family, a story, and a community forever changed,” the paramedic continued. “Last night, this tragedy became far too personal for one of our own.”

The NIH is releasing the last chimpanzees used for lab testing

After more than a decade, the call to free the last of the government’s lab chimpanzees has been answered.

This week, the National Institutes of Health announced that it is releasing its final group of research chimps to a 200-acre forested federal sanctuary in Keithville, Louisiana. Next year, the 33 primates will relocate from the Keeling Center for Comparative Medicine and Research in Texas to Chimp Haven, home of more than 250 chimps owned or supported by the NIH.

“We believe that chimpanzees should have the opportunity to engage in natural behaviors like you would see in the wild, so that includes climbing trees, foraging for different food items and interacting with other chimpanzees,” said Rana Smith, the sanctuary’s president and chief executive officer, whose residents range in age from 9 to almost 67 years old.

Chimp Haven, a retirement home with five-star amenities, last received 21 chimps from the Alamogordo Primate Facility in New Mexico in 2025, a decade after the government banned invasive testing on the endangered species. Before the ruling, Kathleen Conlee, vice president of animal research issues for Humane World for Animals, said the agency used the great apes in biomedical research, such as studies on AIDS and other infectious diseases.

The NIH has not yet disclosed the names, genders or ages of the chimps, but Smith said her staff has a full roster of activities planned for the newest members of the tribe, such as violin concerts, food puzzles and film screenings.

“We have chimps that enjoy watching movies,” she said. “They love Bollywood movies and musicals.”

U.S. Supreme Court says New Hampshire can limit student IDs for voting - UPI.com

Oct. 22 (UPI) -- The U.S. Supreme Court ruled Thursday student IDs in New Hampshire cannot be used for next month's midterm election.
In a brief court order, the justices granted an emergency request by the state, which was backed by the Republican National Committee.
The unsigned order said a federal judge should not have ordered voting rule changes so close to the Nov. 3 election, citing precedent.
"Here, the District Court did not point to anything that would justify deviating from that principle," the order said.
Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the sole dissenter. "I would not disturb a clearly justifiable federal court order issued to ensure that the public receives accurate information about a State's electoral processes," she wrote.
A state law was already on the books that restricted the use of student IDs as a primary form of identification. The ruling means voters cannot use them as backups to establish their identities.
The dispute arose from a state law enacted in the spring that removed student IDs from a list of forms of identification which election officials would accept when a ballot is requested.
The law was challenged by nonprofit voting rights group Coalition for Open Democracy, which argued it unlawfully burdened the right to vote and discriminated against young voters
U.S. District Judge Joseph Laplante declined to block the law, but he earlier this month reconsidered the case and put guidance from Republican New Hampshire Secretary of State David Scanlan on hold.
The guidance from Scanlan said student IDs could be used when people seek ballots, which Laplante ruled went further than the law, which barred them as a primary source of identification.
A separate provision of New Hampshire law allows people to establish identities using other unofficial forms of ID. It says people can use "any other evidence that reasonably establishes that it is more likely than not that the person is who they claim to be."
Under the ruling by Laplante, election officials could still consider student ID in trying to determine if someone is a valid voter.
The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals in Boston declined to stay Laplante's ruling, prompting the state to ask the Supreme Court to intervene.

How many votes did winner Scott Colom get in MS US Senate election?

See new vote count for winner Scott Colom in MS US Senate election
- Democratic candidate Scott Colom officially won the Mississippi U.S. Senate midterm election.
- Incumbent Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded the race on Nov. 11.
- Colom's win was supported by high youth turnout and a strong grassroots movement.
Official results from the Mississippi Secretary of State's office released on Nov. 17 confirmed that Democratic candidate Scott Colom won the U.S. Senate midterm election.
Colom claimed victory on Nov. 11, the same day that Republican incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded. His campaign said in a statement ahead of a victory rally that it was mathematically impossible for Hyde-Smith to recover from her vote deficit.
Colom won by 8,031 votes, according to the results provided by the Secretary of State. He received 480,153 votes in total, and Hyde-Smith received 472,122 votes. The Secretary of State's office published results for the last of Mississippi's 82 counties on Tuesday but had not released a statewide total, noting it had until Dec. 3 to "declare" all results because of runoff elections in down-ballot races.
The official count took so long to finalize in large part because of the thousands of absentee and affidavit ballots that took election workers days to count. The final total was not available until two weeks after Election Day, despite the Secretary of State's office requiring counties to submit their certified results days earlier on Nov. 13.
At the end of the night on Nov. 3, after all but two Senate races nationwide had been called, Colom was ahead by just 43 votes. The tight margin was not entirely unprecedented in the modern era of GOP control in Mississippi. In Hyde-Smith's first bid for the Senate seat, a 2018 special election, she won the first round by just over 3,200 votes.
She had a more decisive victory as the lone Republican candidate in the runoff, beating Democratic challenger Mike Espy by nearly 66,000 votes.
Colom's advantage in the 2026 election grew over time, bolstered by impressive youth turnout and a grassroots movement dissatisfied with the economic climate and impending threat of partisan gerrymandering. The district attorney from northeastern Mississippi made significant gains in previously Republican areas through campaign events across the state.
Colom has fewer than two months to transition away from his current judicial responsibilities and prepare for the congressional job that awaits him in Washington. He will join a mostly Democratic class of 16 freshmen senators, seven of whom flipped their seats. Democrats will hold commanding control over both chambers in January.

Trump awards $10,000 to Nick Shirley for viral video on Minnesota daycares

The president awarded the money as he announced a new whistleblower program on federal fraud.

By Elliot Hughes

The Minnesota Star Tribune
October 20, 2026 at 8:01PM
Advertisement

Right-wing YouTuber Nick Shirley was set to receive a $10,000 award from President Donald Trump on Tuesday as he announced the creation of a $25 million whistleblower program focused on government fraud.

The Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program promises tipsters money faster than other whistleblower rewards programs.

However, conflicting statements between Trump and the Department of Justice, which said it will administer the program, have clouded how it is supposed to function.

The White House said Trump would present a $10,000 check to Shirley, who posted a viral video last year about alleged fraud in Minnesota, but a check was not given to Shirley in the presence of journalists Tuesday.

Advertisement

How it works: The Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program will focus on fraud within federal programs and benefits, with a particular emphasis on hospice care, home health care, day care and special services.

The $25 million program is administered by the U.S. Department of Justice.

The department said in a news release that whistleblowers can receive $10,000 to $30,000 for information leading to criminal charges. In a news conference at the White House on Tuesday, Trump said whistleblowers could also receive 1% commission on recovered stolen funds.

However, the Department of Justice said payouts can be made “at a much earlier stage of a prosecution and does not require the matter to result in large fines or forfeiture.” The department said the early-stage rewards are designed to encourage prompt disclosure from whistleblowers.

“We’re going to make it very easy and very quick and people can make a lot of money,” Trump said.

In a news release, the Department of Justice said, “awards will be made in the Justice Department’s sole discretion following the filing of criminal charges.”

However, Trump said, “I will be the arbitrator of all these decisions.”

We went to the day cares Nick Shirley visited. Here’s what we found.

Our reporting shows: Last winter, Shirley posted a 43-minute video on YouTube, which has been viewed millions of times, showing his visits to 10 day care centers in Minnesota.

He accused Somali American-owned daycares of defrauding the state of $111 million, an unsubstantiated claim. Somali American businesses experienced a surge in harassment after the video.

Following the video, state regulators visited nine centers highlighted by Shirley. The agency said it found children present at all except one, which had not opened for the day when inspectors arrived. The 10th center highlighted in the video closed in 2022.

In July, Fahima Egeh Mahamud, the owner of a day care that Shirley visited, Future Leaders Early Learning Center, pleaded guilty to wire fraud and conspiring to defraud the U.S. by collecting $4.6 million from false claims for her child care center and receiving about $850,000 by turning in false or inflated invoices to the federal child nutrition program.

Future Leaders Early Learning Center operated in south Minneapolis as a meal site under Feeding Our Future, the nonprofit at the center of the largest pandemic-era fraud scheme in the U.S.

Republican legislators have taken credit for providing information for Shirley’s video.

The conversation: On Tuesday, Trump and members of his cabinet praised Shirley. In brief remarks, Shirley said, “I was concerned about the wellbeing of our tax dollars and right now I’m standing next to the president of the United States.

“I hope after today, millions of people are empowered in the United States to go out and make a change inside your own community, and you’ll actually be rewarded for saving America money. When you expose fraud, you help save America.”

Advertisement

During the news conference, Trump didn’t miss an opportunity to take a jab at several elected Democrats in Minnesota, including U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar (D-Minn.), who is currently being investigated by federal immigration authorities.

Advertisement
Advertisement

“I think she’s a sick, very dishonest person, who hates our country,” he said.

Trump said Democratic Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan, who is running in Minnesota’s U.S. Senate race, “knew all about it,” referring to fraud issues in the state.

In a social media post, Flanagan pushed back saying, “The most corrupt president of all time just called me a liar from the Oval Office. Give me a freakin’ break.”

The Associated Press contributed to this story.

Ottawa removes seafood from counter-tariffs list, adds copper wire and charcoal | CBC News

Reversal follows feedback from industry, Finance Canada says
The federal government says it has removed seafood and fish products from its list of counter-tariffs and added copper wire and charcoal to the list to maintain "dollar for dollar" counter measures on the U.S.
Finance Canada issued a statement saying it had dropped seafood products from the list late Wednesday evening, saying the decision was based on "feedback."
"We are continually working with Canadian industries to assess the effectiveness of these measures, with a primary focus on industries that have been targeted by U.S. tariffs," the government said in the statement.
Ottawa's initial response to the latest tariffs imposed by U.S. President Donald Trump, which it spelled out on Tuesday, include raising duties on American steel and aluminum products from 25 to 50 per cent, as well as many seafood items at 25 per cent rates.
A spokesperson for the Ministry of Finance also confirmed to CBC News on Thursday that copper wire, charcoal and a handful of other products, such as plaster sheets and tiles and printed photographs, were being added to the list. Those items will be tariffed at 50 per cent when the countermeasures go into effect on Sept. 8.
Minister of Finance François-Philippe Champagne reiterated that the government listened to Canadians when deciding to make the change, though he didn't directly answer a question about what message the move might send to the White House.
"We made the update because I think that was the right thing to do in the interest of Canada," Champagne told reporters.

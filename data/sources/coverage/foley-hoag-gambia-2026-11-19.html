Foley Hoag Leads The Gambia to Landmark Victory in Genocide Case Against Myanmar at the International Court of Justice

November 19, 2026

Foley Hoag today secured a landmark victory before the International Court of Justice (ICJ) on behalf of its client, The Gambia, in a case alleging that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya people.

In its judgment, the Court found that Myanmar is responsible for committing genocide against the Rohingya and ordered that Myanmar take several measures as a form of reparations. The ruling marks a significant step toward accountability for atrocities committed against the Rohingya and follows nearly seven years of litigation before the Court.

Foley Hoag has served as counsel to The Gambia throughout the case, which The Gambia filed in November 2019 with the support of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation. The suit sought to hold Myanmar accountable for violations of the 1948 Genocide Convention arising from atrocities committed by the Myanmar military against the Rohingya, a predominantly Muslim minority group in Myanmar’s Rakhine State.

“This judgment is a landmark victory for the Rohingya people and for countless individuals in Myanmar who have endured violence at the hands of the military,” said Arsalan Suleman, partner at Foley Hoag and lead counsel for The Gambia. “It represents an important step toward accountability and honors the courage and perseverance of the victims and survivors who have spent years seeking justice.”

Dawda Jallow, Attorney General and Minister of Justice of The Gambia, welcomed the Court’s decision.

“Today the International Court of Justice confirmed what the Rohingya have said since 2017: what was done to them was genocide, and Myanmar is responsible," AG Jallow said. "This victory belongs to the Rohingya. They survived, they testified, and today they were believed."

Today’s judgment follows years of written and oral proceedings before the Court, including hearings on Myanmar’s preliminary objections and on the merits of The Gambia’s claims. The decision is a major milestone in efforts to secure accountability under international law for atrocities committed against the Rohingya people.

The legal team is led by Suleman and includes Partners Andrew Loewenstein, Tafadzwa Pasipanodya, and Peter Tzeng; Special Counsel Professor Pierre d’Argent, Associates Yasmin Al Ameen and Sun Young Hwang; former associate Diem Huong Ho; co-counsel Professor Philippe Sands and Paul Reichler of 11KBW, Jessica Jones of Matrix Chambers, Alejandra Torres Camprubi of Torres Iuris; and Gambian State Counsel Chalis Njai and Mariama Ngum. The team is also supported by Legal Assistant Nancy Lopez.

UPDATE: Army says it will proceed with lead azide detonation

By KCRG Staff
Published: Nov. 21, 2026 at 7:52 PM CST|Updated: 2 hours ago
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

MIDDLETOWN, Iowa (KCRG) - The U.S. Army said it plans to carry out a controlled detonation of nearly five tons of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant by November 24, warning that dropping temperatures have created an “immediate threat” of an uncontrolled blast.

It’s unclear exactly what day and what time the detonation will happen.

In a federal court filing Saturday, lawyers for the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) notified judges that the Army intends to invoke an emergency exemption in federal hazardous waste rules to destroy lead azide stored at the plant in Middletown, Iowa.

With temperatures set to dip below 30 degrees this week, Army officials said the changing weather conditions have increased the risk that the lead azide could “spontaneously explode.”

The Army said the bunker where the lead azide is located is now more vulnerable to temperature changes because the blast doors were removed in preparation for the controlled detonation and cannot be replaced.

The lawsuit from The Iowa Environmental Council and the Iowa chapter of the Sierra Club challenged an emergency permit the EPA granted to the U.S. Army for the detonation.

The groups allege in their lawsuit that many Iowans did not receive information about potential lead exposure.

The U.S. Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals ordered an administrative stay Thursday, blocking the EPA permit that allowed the Army to conduct the controlled detonation of lead azide.

In a post on Facebook Saturday, the City of Fort Madison said it had been notified that the detonation will take place either Monday or Tuesday.

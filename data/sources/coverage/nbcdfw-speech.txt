Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton Tuesday in Texas' closely watched U.S. Senate race, becoming the state's first Democratic Senator in more than three decades.

"We just took back Texas. We just proved that all the money in the world is no match for the people of Texas. We just showed that politics of division is no match for a politics of love. And we just sent a message to every billionaire mega donor, every trillionaire, mega donor. Every corrupt defender of this broken political system in this state, in this country. Power belongs to the people." Talarico said in his acceptance speech.
The projected victory marks a historic breakthrough for Texas Democrats, ending a decades-long losing streak in U.S. Senate elections.
Talarico will be the state's first Democratic Senator since Bob Kreuger, who spent six months in the U.S. Senate in 1993 after being appointed to finish Sen. Lloyd Bentsen's term when he resigned to become U.S. Secretary of the Treasury.
"We were the underdogs in this race. We went up against nearly 40 years of doubt and disillusionment. We went up against more than $300 million, more than any Senate campaign in American history. We went up against all the negative ads, smear campaigns, foreign bots, and AI deepfakes that money can buy. Those attacks were the final howls of a dying establishment. The death rattle of a status quo that knows its days are numbered," said Talarico. "They played dirty because they were desperate. But we never lost hope. We never lost faith. And against big money and long odds, we took our power back. Always, always bet on the underdog."
Talarico drew scrutiny for missing the campaign trail while he was sick with the flu in early October. Paxton’s campaign asked for “proof of life” and called on Talarico to produce a photo of himself with a newspaper showing the current date. Talarico responded by renewing his challenge to debate Paxton, but Paxton did not respond.
Talarico has served in the Texas House of Representatives since 2018, representing parts of North Austin. He is a former public school teacher and Presbyterian seminarian. He previously taught at Rhodes Middle School in the San Antonio Independent School District. Talarico also worked at Reasoning Mind, an education nonprofit, and MAYA Consulting, an education consulting firm.

Ken Paxton, the Republican nominee for U.S. Senate, is a politician and lawyer who has served as Texas's 51st Attorney General since 2015. As attorney general, he led conservative legal challenges to the Obama and Biden administrations on immigration, healthcare, environmental regulations, and LGBTQ+ rights. Before becoming Attorney General, Paxton served in the Texas House of Representatives from 2003 to 2013 and in the Texas Senate from 2013 to 2015, representing North Texas's McKinney area.
Throughout his political career, Paxton has faced a long history of legal controversies, corruption accusations, and ethical scandal. His 2026 campaign for U.S. Senate has been endorsed by President Donald Trump, Sen. Ted Cruz, Senate Majority Leader John Thune, the Texas Farm Bureau, the National Border Patrol Council, the National Federation of Independent Businesses, and 18 U.S. representatives from Texas.
Paxton defeated incumbent senior U.S. Sen. John Cornyn by more than 25 points in the 2026 Republican primary runoff in March.
The editorial boards of The Dallas Morning News and the Houston Chronicle endorsed Talarico's campaign along with the AFL-CIO Texas, End Citizens United, the Congressional Black Caucus, the Congressional Hispanic Caucus, OAG whistleblower David Maxwell, and county legend and liberal activist Willie Nelson.
The race between Paxton and Talarico has become one of the country's most expensive and closely watched midterm battles. Talarico posted historic fundraising numbers, raising more than $64 million in the third quarter of 2026, prompting tech billionaire Elon Musk to back Paxton with multi-million-dollar super PAC donations.
Each U.S. state elects two senators regardless of population size, and U.S. senators serve six-year terms. The Constitution sets no term limits for U.S. senators, and they can be re-elected an unlimited number of times. The Senate is divided into three classes so that roughly one-third of the seats are up for election every two years.

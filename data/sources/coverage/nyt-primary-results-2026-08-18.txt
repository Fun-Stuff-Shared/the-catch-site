Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
At an aviation museum in Anchorage, Senator Dan Sullivan celebrated advancing to the general election and portrayed himself as a grass-roots candidate focused on local issues, while accusing “D.C. Democrats” and “Lower 48 liberals” of funding his opponent, Mary Peltola. Sullivan is also benefiting from significant spending by Republicans outside Alaska. He criticized Peltola’s recent staff shake-up, saying “My campaign’s not melting down like some other campaigns.”
Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
Peltola spoke briefly to supporters at a brewery in Anchorage, thanking them for their work and taking aim at Senator Dan Sullivan as she turned toward the general election. She did not take questions from reporters, even as she shook hands with volunteers before leaving the event.
Christine Zhang Election Data Reporter
The first batches of results from Alaska included about half of the expected vote in all three statewide races. The results include ballots cast on Election Day and early votes cast through Thursday. Most mail ballots, along with early votes cast after Thursday, will be counted in the coming days.
Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
As expected, Mary Peltola and Senator Dan Sullivan easily advanced. It’s still early, but Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher whom Republicans have accused of entering the race as a spoiler, is leading the lesser-known candidates competing for the remaining two spots on the four-person ballot.
William P. Davis
Alaska has reported only about a quarter of its votes, but it’s already clear that Mary Peltola and Senator Dan Sullivan will advance to the general election. The other two spots in the state’s top-four primary remain up for grabs, with Dan J. Sullivan — the candidate who shares the incumbent’s name — leading a field of more than a dozen others.
Jennifer Medina Reporting on California politics
The two candidates in California’s special election also faced each other in the June primary, when Aisha Wahab received 43 percent of the vote, compared with 17 percent for Melissa Hernandez. But AIPAC and other outside groups have recently spent millions of dollars supporting Hernandez and attacking Wahab, and the race is now expected to be much closer.
William P. Davis
In the special election in California’s 14th District, Alameda County has reported some ballots returned early but no Election Day votes. The county has historically taken several hours to count and report Election Day votes, which are expected to come in batches. Those votes make up a relatively small share of the outstanding vote. Most of the remaining ballots are mail ballots that will be counted over the next week or so.
Christine Zhang Election Data Reporter
The first results in Alaska are expected between 12:45 a.m. and 1 a.m. Eastern, according to the state’s Division of Elections. Most of the state is four hours behind Eastern time, while the western Aleutian Islands are five hours behind.
Christine Zhang Election Data Reporter
The first votes out of Alaska will consist of results from those who cast ballots on Election Day or through Thursday as part of the state’s early voting period. The bulk of the mail-in ballots, as well as early vote ballots cast after Thursday, will be counted in the coming days. Mail-in ballots that are postmarked by Election Day will still be counted as long as they reach the Division of Election within 10 days.
Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
Alaska also features competitive races for governor and its sole House seat. A host of Republicans and two prominent Democrats are vying to succeed Gov. Mike Dunleavy, a Republican who is term limited. In the House race, the matchup is likely to be set between Representative Nick Begich, a Republican, an d Bill Hill, a commercial fisherman and educator running as an independent.
Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
Polls are now closed in most of Alaska, where Senator Dan Sullivan, a Republican, and former Representative Mary Peltola, a Democrat, are expected to easily advance to the four-person general election race for U.S. Senate. The intrigue revolves around who will claim the third and fourth spots — those candidates could be potential spoilers who split votes with the front-runners in November. One of the lesser-known candidates is also named Dan Sullivan; Republicans fear his presence on the ballot in November could confuse voters and harm the sitting senator’s re-election chances.
Jennifer Medina Reporting on California politics
The polls have just closed in the special election for California’s 14th congressional district. The winner of tonight’s race will fill out the rest of term for Eric Swalwell, who resigned amid sexual misconduct allegations earlier this year. The race became far more contentious in the final weeks of the campaign as AIPAC spent more than $2 million in the race.
Susan Cooper Eastman Reporting from Florida
State Representative Angie Nixon, who begins the general election as a clear underdog to Senator Ashley Moody in the Florida Senate race, said a brief interview with The New York Times tonight that she would defy expectations in November the same way she won the Democratic primary. “By going out and having conversations with people,” she said.
Tim Balk Politics reporter
Angie Nixon, the democratic socialist who won the Democratic Senate primary in Florida despite being vastly outspent, said in an interview Tuesday afternoon that she had defeated an incumbent Democratic state lawmaker in 2020 despite being outspent by 5 to 1. She downplayed her affiliation with the Democratic Socialists of America.
Evan Reynolds Election Data Reporter
With almost all of the vote reported, the Ninth Congressional District in Florida has proved to be the most competitive in the state. The top two Republican candidates — Dan Green, a Navy reserve officer, and Ben Butler, a cattle rancher — are separated by a few hundred votes.
Susan Cooper Eastman Reporting from Florida
Natasha Sutherland, a friend of Angie Nixon and her family, came to Jacksonville from Tallahassee for her primary night party. “Democrats need a Black person at the top of the ticket to inspire Black voters to turn out in a state that is well represented by Black voters,” she said. “People need to see someone like us in office.”
David Ovalle Reporting from Florida
Byron Donalds has been criticized for his embrace of artificial intelligence, although he’s softened his stance on data centers more recently. “We will lead in innovation but we will not put the cost of A.I. on the backs of Floridians and their electric bills.” Communities across Florida, as across the nation, have raised concerns about data centers.
David Ovalle Reporting from Florida
Byron Donalds is promising that if he is elected as Florida’s governor, all state contracts and expenditures will be posted online. Florida public records are famously open but those records — ask any Florida journalist — have become harder to obtain under Gov. DeSantis.
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
Byron Donalds, now the Republican nominee for Florida governor, dominated in most of the state but is trailing to Jay Collins in a number of largely white, rural counties in North Florida. Mr. Donalds is Black, and some Republicans will be looking at his underperformance in those counties with concerns about whether white Republicans are unwilling to vote for a Black nominee and might stay home in November. If elected, Donalds would become Florida’s first Black governor.
David Ovalle Reporting from Florida
Byron Donalds took the stage at his primary night event to celebrate his win. “We have won a massive victory.” He said Republican rivals Paul Renner and James Fishback called to congratulate him and urged him to beat David Jolly. “Tonight we are one united Republican Party,” Donalds said.
Andrea Zarate Reporting from Florida
The energy at Debbie Wasserman Schultz’s watch party peaked as she walked in to loud chants of “Debbie! Debbie!” Surrounded by her team and Black community leaders, she wiped away tears. She also embraced her father — who served as her campaign treasurer for 30 years — and took time to honor the memory of her late mother.
Susan Cooper Eastman Reporting from Florida
At Angie Nixon’s victory party, some attendees say they’re not surprised their candidate won. “She’s for the people,” said Kaydene Williams, a Nixon supporter. “She makes herself available to the people.” The crowd is continuing to chant and sing, waiting for Nixon to come out.
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
The ideological spread in Florida’s Democratic statewide ticket reminiscent of 2018, the last big, contested election in the state. Andrew Gillum, a progressive, was the nominee for governor, and Senator Bill Nelson, a moderate, was seeking re-election. Both men lost after recounts, and a debate has raged among Florida Democrats since about whether the way to win is by moving to the left or moving to the center.
David Ovalle Reporting from Florida
Erika Donalds, the wife of newly minted Republican nominee for governor, Byron Donalds, is now up speaking to a jubilant crowd at the watch party in Orlando. Dipping into the culture battles, she says to applause that her husband will keep Florida free of “radical ideology that threatens our way of life.”
Shane Goldmacher National politics reporter
The statewide ticket for Democrats in Florida represents about as big an ideological spread within the party as possible. On one end, there is David Jolly, a former Republican who is now the Democratic nominee for governor, and the other is Angie Nixon, a democratic socialist who won the Senate nomination.
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
Alex Vindman, in conceding the Democratic primary for Senate in Florida to Angie Nixon, told supporters near Fort Lauderdale that “this is not the result that we were hoping for.” He said Nixon “ran a strong campaign” and “challenged the status quo.”
Shane Goldmacher National politics reporter
The defeat of Representative Cory Mills in Florida represents the third Republican House member to exit before the general election in recent weeks who had been seen as a weak candidate jeopardizing an otherwise more solid Republican seat. The others are Representative Andy Ogles of Tennessee, who lost his primary, and Representative Chuck Edwards of North Carolina, who said he would not seek re-election.
Susan Cooper Eastman Reporting from Florida
At Angie Nixon’s watch party on the St. Johns River in Jacksonville, a big television screen is broadcasting news of Nixon’s win in the Democratic Senate primary. The crowd is jumping, participating in a call-and-response chant of “this is what democracy looks like.”
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
Some Republicans had mused whether Byron Donalds would hit 50 percent in the Republican primary for governor. He appears under that threshold for now, which has led to private concerns already about under-performance by a Trump-backed candidate — as is happening to other Trump-backed Republicans in Florida tonight.
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
Florida has gotten the governor’s race it expected, between Byron Donalds, the Republican, and David Jolly, the Democrat. It has gotten a hugely unexpected Senate race between Ashley Moody, the incumbent Republican, and Angie Nixon, a progressive state lawmaker.
Kellen Browning Reporting from Alaska
It’s midafternoon here in Anchorage and Mary Peltola, the Democratic candidate for U.S. Senate, just spoke with supporters and volunteers. In a sign of her campaign’s efforts to court Republican voters in a conservative state, Peltola noted how she went “toe to toe with Biden” while in Congress, and said “there have been a few good things, here and there,” in the Trump administration.
Patricia Mazzei Reporting from Broward County, Florida
At Alex Vindman’s Election Night event near Fort Lauderdale, Fla., his supporters are having tacos for dinner and appear a little nervous. The event is being held inside an American Legion hall, in a nod to Vindman’s military background. It is so hot inside that they brought extra fans in.
Christine Zhang Election Data Reporter
In Florida, votes cast on Election Day are starting to be reported, and they appear to favor Angie Nixon in the Democratic primary for Senate.
Luke Vrotsos Election Analyst

Jay Collins is currently leading in several rural, historically Republican counties in the northern part of the state which tend to have significantly lower percentages of residents with a college degree than the state as a whole.

Navy Identifies Helicopter Squadron Commander As Missing Sailor in Arabian Sea - USNI News

Cmdr. Gabriel Edwards, commanding officer of Helicopter Sea Squadron 5, embarked on USS George H.W. Bush (CVN-77), died July 1 after his helicopter ditched into the Arabian Sea, the Navy announced late Tuesday.
Edwards was one of four sailors aboard the MH-60S helicopter when it landed in the water. The other three sailors were rescued. The Navy called off the search for Edwards after four days. Bush is currently deployed in the Middle East as part of the Navy’s participation in the war with Iran.
“The thoughts and prayers of every member of the Carrier Strike Group 10 team are with the Edwards family. While we are far from home, our heartfelt support remains at your side,” Rear Adm. Todd Cimicata, commander of Carrier Strike Group 10, said in the Tuesday Navy release. “Gabe’s legacy as a husband, father, friend and fearless leader will never be forgotten. We are thankful to each who carry a unique and indelible part of Gabe’s memory with us as we continue this important mission.”
Edwards commissioned into the Navy through the Reserve Officers’ Training Corps in 2006 after graduating from Norfolk State University that same year, reads the Navy release. He began as a helicopter pilot in 2008 and previously served with the “Sea Knights” of Helicopter Sea Combat Squadron 22, the “Red Wolves” of Helicopter Sea Combat Squadron 84 and the “Firehawks” of Helicopter Sea Combat Squadron 85. He also served as part of Naval Special Warfare Group 4.
Edwards assumed command of the “Nightdippers” of Helicopter Sea Squadron 5 in July 2025. The Fiscal Year 2027 O-6 Line Officer Promotion Board recently selected him for promotion to captain, according to the release.
In a post on social media, Acting Secretary of the Navy Hung Cao ordered Edwards to be posthumously promoted to captain.
“Freedom comes at a price. There is always a cost,” Cao wrote. “We mourn the loss of Commander Gabe Edwards – a naval aviator commanding officer of Helicopter Sea Combat Squadron (HSC) 5, and leader who strengthened our fleet and inspired those he led.”
He is survived by his wife, Rebecca, and two children.
“We are profoundly grateful to every sailor, aviator, and airman who devoted countless hours, extraordinary skill, and unwavering determination in the effort to bring Gabe home,” Rebecca Edwards said in the Navy release. “Gabe has dedicated his life to serving his country with honor, courage and commitment. He led with humility, integrity and compassion, always putting his people before himself. To those who served alongside him, he was a respected leader and mentor. To our family, he is the love of my life, an extraordinary father to his children and the foundation of our home. His greatest joy was always his family.”
The Navy is investigating the helicopter’s emergency landing. Initial investigation does not suggest it was a result of hostile action, USNI News previously reported.

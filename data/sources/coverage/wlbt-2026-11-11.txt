24% of affidavit ballots were rejected statewide, including some in Hinds County

Most were not registered to vote, commission says; commissioner points to registration deadline
JACKSON, Miss. (WLBT) - As of Tuesday night, 76% of affidavit ballots statewide have been accepted, and 24% have been rejected. The rejected ballots include 212 cast by Jackson State University students.
According to the Hinds County Election Commission, most of those students were not registered to vote.
Reasons ballots were rejected
Of the 212 Jackson State students’ ballots rejected:
- Six were registered in another county.
- 18 were cast at the wrong precinct.
- 188 were not registered to vote.
Student voter registration efforts
Jakari Holland, a Jackson State senior who took part in voter registration drives to help classmates become eligible to vote for the Election Day held on November 3, said, “Even with the Jackson community and students on campus, we registered over 1,500 people to vote.”
The student said he feels frustration over the rejected ballots.
“Frustration in a sense because you spend so much time registering students to vote, making sure that you’re actually educating them because we can talk to people about, okay, you can go vote, but actually educating them about who’s on the ballot,” Holland said.
Election commissioner comments
Hinds County Election Commissioner Felicia McClinton said different organizations helped students register. She said she believes those groups could bear some responsibility.
“The organization probably turned in their application after the deadline that’s set by the circuit clerk, which was October the 5th,” McClinton said.
McClinton said students were not the only voters whose ballots were rejected for the same reasons. She said she dealt with the issue throughout the county.
McClinton said she had more than 600 affidavit ballots to process, which she believes is more than any other election commissioner had. She also said she probably had to reject more than half of them.

Trump Media stock plunges after midterms

The stock price for President Donald Trump’s media company plunged to an all-time low Wednesday after a voter surge delivered Democrats control of Congress.
Shares in Trump Media & Technology Group fell more than 13 percent in early trading, to about $5.10. The stock price is down more than 60 percent this year. The company runs the Truth Social website, on which Trump frequently posts.
Trump is the sole beneficiary of a trust that owns 41 percent of the company, or roughly 114 million shares, and is managed by his eldest son, financial filings show.
At current prices, that stake is worth roughly $600 million, down from nearly $4 billion in 2024.
The drop caps a years-long slide in stock price for the company, which announced in August that it had recorded $1.7 million in revenue and $238 million in losses for the second quarter of the year.
In 2021, investors in the special purpose acquisition company that helped take Trump’s company public paid $175 a share.
The company has announced in recent months that it would push to merge with a fusion-power company, expand further into cryptocurrency, and charge businesses for faster access to Trump’s posts.
Counties across the country have backed Democrats by significantly higher margins than they did in 2024, according to a Washington Post analysis of live election results.
The shifts are especially stark in majority-Hispanic counties, where voters have shifted nearly 30 points to the left compared with their 2024 presidential vote. But voters moved at least 15 points left in every sort of county: rural areas, suburban hubs, racially diverse and even majority-White counties.

Midterm voters delivered sharp rebuke of Trump’s handpicked candidates
President Donald Trump with Sen. Darline Graham (R-South Carolina) in Rock Hill during a campaign rally last week. (Alex Brandon/AP)

President Donald Trump handpicked several of the GOP candidates who lost Tuesday, adding insult to injury in a dismal election cycle for the president and his Republican allies.
In South Carolina, Trump had personally persuaded Sen. Darline Graham to run for a full term after throwing his weight behind her to succeed her brother, Lindsey Graham, after his death in July. She lost to Democratic physician Annie Andrews by more than four percentage points, in a state Trump won by nearly 18 points in 2024.
During the GOP primary for the Senate race in Texas, Trump endorsed Ken Paxton over Sen. John Cornyn (R), despite the numerous scandals dogging Paxton. On Tuesday, James Talarico upset Paxton, becoming the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Texas since 1988.
Other candidates Trump boosted — all but clearing the field for them during the Republican primaries — included former Republican National Committee chairman Michael Whatley for Senate in North Carolina and Vivek Ramaswamy for governor in Ohio. Both lost their races as well.
A defiant Trump blamed others for the overwhelming blue wave Tuesday night.
“I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE! Thank you for your attention to this matter,” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

Texas’s next U.S. senator and governor — Democratic colleagues in the state House of Representatives — met up after their victory parties at (where else?) Whataburger, the place where Texas teams celebrate.
Scores of supporters filled a location in Austin as James Talarico and Gina Hinojosa hugged and were joined by Texas Comptroller-elect Sarah Eckhardt (D) to cheers and applause.
Outside, car horns honked and drivers cheered a blue wave that swept Republicans from power in the state for the first time in decades. Their supporters stayed to celebrate with jalapeño burgers, fancy ketchup and onion rings.

What Democrats’ win could mean for Trump’s monument makeover projects
President Donald Trump walks from the podium after speaking at the Reflecting Pool on Monday. (Alex Brandon/AP)
1 min
Loading...
President Donald Trump this week touted his Reflecting Pool renovations, contending that its deep-blue basin (and algae-free water) showed what his administration could accomplish. Now Democrats’ blue wave threatens to swamp Trump’s plan to keep remaking Washington.
The new Democratic-led House and Senate could file suit to block Trump’s most controversial building projects, such as his in-progress White House ballroom, arguing that they have not been authorized by Congress and are illegal. That could slow or even stop the projects after the Supreme Court in August ruled that a historic preservationist likely could not prove that she would be harmed by the ballroom, clearing the way for Trump to keep building.
The new liberal majorities may also rein in Trump’s spending, after the president diverted taxpayer money to his Washington projects with little complaint from GOP leaders.
There’s also a more immediate practical effect from Tuesday night’s rout. Two congressional Democrats will replace Republicans on a 12-person federal review panel that Trump packed with allies who have swiftly approved his construction projects.
Trump is set to get some more short-term wins, though. That panel, with its current membership, is expected to approve Trump’s planned triumphal arch as soon as Thursday.

Google to turn off search for schools that don’t want students using AI
The Google logo at a store in Manhattan. (Andrew Kelly/Reuters)
1 min
Loading...
Amid outcry from educators about tech in classrooms, Google said Tuesday that artificial-intelligence tools, including the chatbot Gemini, will be disabled by default on K-12 student accounts, unless administrators choose to turn them on.
But there’s a catch: Google search also will be disabled.
Google search typically includes an AI overview that, if expanded, has a chat window for further questions. Google said Tuesday it was building educational tools into AI overviews to help students work through problems without skipping straight to answers.
Google says the move responds to feedback from parents and teachers who say that AI products have been disastrous for the ability of children to learn and to think critically.
The policy shift triggered outrage Tuesday as people argued Google was trying to force schools to opt in to AI.
“Google is trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools,” New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani (D) said on X. “No corporation should have the power to dictate how we teach our children.”
The New York Times earlier reported that schools that turned off AI access entirely would not have access to Google search.
Google did not immediately return a request for comment.

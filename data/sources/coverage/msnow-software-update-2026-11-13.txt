Software update triggered rare nationwide FAA ground stop, official says

A software update caused the rare nationwide ground stop ordered by the Federal Aviation Administration on Friday afternoon, a federal official with direct knowledge of the investigation told MS NOW. The official was granted anonymity to discuss internal information.
The FAA said earlier Friday that the ground stop, which began around 4 p.m. ET and remains in effect, was issued due to “extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety” related to “communication issues.”
The FAA’s alert said international flights arriving in the U.S. “will be managed on a case by case basis.” The agency did not indicate how long the ground stop would last, only stating that planes should not depart “until further notice.”
In a statement to MS NOW, the agency said communication issues were “impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers” and that FAA technicians were investigating. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy shared the same statement on his X account.
A Justice Department source told MS NOW that officials were not currently tracking any potential connection to terrorism and described the problem as a “frequency issue.”
Two people at the FAA confirmed that Friday’s ground stop was only the second nationwide ground stop since the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks. The previous nationwide ground stop was in 2023. More than 4,000 flights were already in the air en route to U.S. airports nationwide when Friday’s ground stop was issued, according to the flight tracking company Flightradar24.
Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin described the situation as “unprecedented” while speaking to reporters at the White House, but said there was no threat to Americans’ safety. Mullin insisted it “isn’t another 9/11,” adding, “There’s no reason for the public to be afraid or scared for your loved ones.”
Mullin also said it was “too early to tell” how long the ground stop would last.
“We haven’t discussed any time frame,” he said.
Mullin said that President Donald Trump had been briefed on the situation. The president had not yet publicly addressed the ground stop as of shortly before 6 p.m. ET, when he posted a lengthy message on Truth Social about working with a bipartisan group of senators on legislation to support tax credits for film and television production.
Rep. Jim Himes, the ranking Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, said he had not been told what caused the FAA ground stop. As a member of the Gang of Eight, Himes would typically be briefed if the incident involved a national security threat.
Sen. Maria Cantwell, the top Democrat on the Senate Commerce, Science and Transportation Committee, said the committee plans to review “technology issues related to how planes communicate through primary frequencies, backup frequencies, and other issues.”
The FBI does not believe the air traffic control communication issue was caused by a cyberattack or criminality, Bloomberg reported, citing one person familiar with the matter.

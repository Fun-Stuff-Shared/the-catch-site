Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is set to testify before Senate Banking Committee

Senate Banking panel sets Huang testimony for Dec. 3 after Nvidia CEO’s return
Sen. Tim Scott (R-S.C.), chair of the Senate Banking Committee, set the date for Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang to provide testimony on Dec. 3 after the tech leader’s high-profile exit earlier this month.
The hearing — called “Protecting America’s AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls” — is scheduled to start at 10:00 a.m. EST, with Huang the only witness, according to a notice sent out by Scott.
This comes after Nvidia announced Monday that Huang would be returning to the company. He was ousted after admitting to having an extramarital affair, with reports alleging he had a relationship with an accused Chinese spy and another executive at Nvidia who reported to him.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.), ranking member of the panel, characterized Huang’s rehire as “reckless,” adding that it didn’t “change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company’s board still owe Congress answers about the company’s compliance with U.S. export controls.”
Warren and Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.), another member of the Banking Committee, signaled interest in investigating the role that certain Trump administration officials had in Huang’s reinstatement.
The Hill reported Monday that Vice President Vance was personally involved in President Trump’s push to reinstate Huang over the weekend, a source familiar with the matter said.
The source cited Vance’s long-standing relationships in Silicon Valley and standing in the tech industry as putting him in a good position to influence Nvidia’s board.
Trump told CNBC in an extensive interview that he had been personally “speaking to the board directly and indirectly” because he “felt very strongly about it.”
“And I felt more strongly as I saw what was happening with the company, because that was to me a very important part of our country,” the president continued.
Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.), another member of the banking panel, wrote that Huang “had multiple affairs including with subordinates and an alleged Chinese spy.”
“His own board described his behavior as lacking ‘candor.’ I’ve served on a public company board. His behavior does not support reinstatement,” the senator continued.
He warned that this recent decision didn’t excuse Huang from appearing before Congress or Nvidia from “answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips.”
This hearing would mark the tech CEO’s first public hearing before Congress. Huang has also agreed to testify at a closed-door hearing before three House committees, which have been conducting their own investigation into Nvidia’s part in helping Chinese companies evade U.S. export controls.

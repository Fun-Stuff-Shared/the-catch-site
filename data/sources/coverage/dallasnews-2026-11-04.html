Talarico pledges unity, bipartisanship as he prepares for Washington

The senator-elect says Texans want results, not political division, as he pledges cooperation and accountability in Washington.

By Joseph Morton, Washington BureauNov 4, 2026

WASHINGTON – For James Talarico, Texans voted for a smile instead of a snarl.

A promise of unity over political warfare. A candidate who talked about bringing people together rather than tearing his opponents down.

Talarico, a day after defeating Republican Ken Paxton in Texas’ bitterly fought Senate race, said Wednesday in a series of national interviews that his victory was not so much a partisan triumph as a demand for something different from Washington.

“It wasn't about the Democratic Party or the Republican Party. It was working people across our state, coming together, first-time voters, three-time Trump voters,” a somewhat hoarse and sleep-deprived Talarico said Wednesday on NBC’s "Today” show.

The Austin state representative, who ended nearly four decades of Republican dominance in Texas Senate elections, cast the results as a triumph of working families over wealthy elites.

“The real fight in this country is not left versus right. It's top versus bottom, because all of us are getting screwed,” he said.

His remarks offered an early look at how he intends to turn a campaign built around affordability, political reform and unity into a governing agenda in Washington’s highly partisan scene.

In the interviews, he pledged to work with President Donald Trump while also holding his administration accountable,

Advertisement

Article continues below this ad

“We can and must do both of those things. It is not an either-or choice,” he said.

Beyond party lines

Talarico said his victory Tuesday reflected dissatisfaction that crossed traditional political lines, including among Texans who had previously supported Trump.

He described a coalition united by economic frustration and a desire for change.

“Whether you're a little more progressive or a little more conservative, none of us can afford gas or groceries or healthcare because the system has been rigged against us by the people at the top who run this economy, who run this government,” he said.

Article continues below this ad

During the race, Paxton and his Republican allies portrayed Talarico as a dangerous liberal, attacking his religious beliefs, views on gender and support for transgender rights. They mocked his masculinity and warned that his election would threaten conservative Texas values.

Talarico questioned Paxton’s character and cited corruption allegations, saying the Republican was unfit to hold a Senate seat once held by Lyndon B. Johnson and Sam Houston.

Talarico made what he called a “politics of love” central to his campaign, saying Texans were tired of being divided by politicians. He returned to that message in his victory speech Tuesday night.

“People across the political spectrum are hungry for a different kind of politics. Not a politics of hate, not a politics of fear, not a politics of division, but a politics of love,” he said.

Talarico said on NBC that voters expect him to deliver sweeping changes, from reducing the influence of wealthy political donors to expanding access to healthcare and investing in public education.

“We’ve got to unrig this economic system that only benefits the top 1%. We’ve got to pass universal health care so that no one in our state, no one in our country, goes bankrupt just because they get sick,” he said.

Talarico, a former public school teacher in San Antonio, also emphasized education as a central part of his agenda.

“So we must invest in our teachers and our students so that every underdog in this country has a fighting chance,” he said.

On foreign policy, he renewed his opposition to prolonged military conflicts that he said have increased costs for Americans.

“And, of course, we have to end these corrupt forever wars that are driving up prices and killing our brave men and women in uniform,” he said.

Advertisement

Talarico insisted his priorities represent changes Trump and both parties should pursue together.

ABC’s George Stephanopoulos pressed Talarico on how he would persuade Trump to change on Iran and tariffs.

“The people of this country may have convinced him last night,” Talarico said. “They delivered a resounding message that they want the president, they want both parties in Congress to focus on them, to focus on making their lives easier and better and less stressful.”

Working with Trump

Talarico said his call for cooperation does not mean he intends to avoid confrontation with the Trump administration.

Advertisement

Article continues below this ad

Asked whether Democrats should focus on legislative priorities or investigating the administration, he rejected the premise that they must choose.

“We have to hold this administration accountable, but we should also work with President Trump to deliver for working people across Texas and across this country,” Talarico said.

Asked about Democrats impeaching Trump, Talarico said it wouldn’t be up to him as a senator to bring articles of impeachment but that he would review the evidence diligently if called upon to do so.

“Our top priority has to be delivering for the people of Texas, for the people of this country,” Talarico said. “They didn't send us to Washington for political retribution.”

Democrats flipped enough seats Tuesday night to take control of the Senate, but Talarico declined to back Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York for majority leader.

Talarico said he would meet with every senator seeking a leadership position to hear their vision before making any decisions on whom to support.

He said he hadn’t spoken with Paxton, but appreciated the attorney general acknowledging the validity of the election results and offering kind words for Talarico in his concession speech.

Talarico said even he was surprised by his double-digit victory, but sensed something special in a campaign that drew first-time voters and three-time Trump supporters to his message of love over division.

“That can be a model for our politics as a nation going forward,” he said.

A lasting shift?

Even as the counting continued, the question is whether Texas Democrats have found a lasting path to victory or merely benefited from a national Democratic wave and voter concerns about Paxton’s character.

Brandon Rottinghaus, a political science professor at the University of Houston, suggested the scope and breadth of Texas Democrats’ wins reflects a deeper realignment.

“This is like the tornado that took Dorothy and Toto to Oz,” Rottinghaus said. “It changes the entire nature of Texas politics.”

He said Democrats were able to put together a solid coalition of racial minorities, independents, young people, college-educated voters and women.

Talarico also made inroads in traditionally Republican suburbs and fast-growing communities, expanding Democrats’ reach beyond their urban strongholds.

“Democrats have been saying for decades that demographics are destiny,” Rottinghaus said. “We saw that on election night.”

Texas also could become competitive in the 2028 presidential election, he said.

“The fact that you've got so many offices that Democrats hold, it’s going to give them lots of opportunities to expand the brand and to dive into more coalition building,” he said.

Nov 4, 2026
Joseph Morton
Washington Bureau

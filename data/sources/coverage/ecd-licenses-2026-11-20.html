Nvidia Told BIS its Chip Exports Were 'Low' Risk, House Panel Says in Release of License Data

November 20, 2026 by Marc Selinger|Top News

In what it called “the most detailed public disclosure in the history of the Export Control Reform Act” of 2018, the House Foreign Affairs Committee on Nov. 19 released Commerce Department data showing that the Bureau of Industry and Security has approved the export of more than 879,000 Nvdia H200 and AMD MI325X AI chips to China out of 3.4 million in requested sales.

The committee noted that some of those approvals were for entities that the Defense Department recently added to its 1260H list of Chinese military companies, including e-commerce company Alibaba, electric carmaker BYD, Internet search provider Baidu and technology firm Tencent (see 2606080044).

In addition, the committee said some of the entities that could have access to the chip exports are chip-smuggling front companies, including Maginfra and Beijing New Matrix Information Technologies.

Maginfra and Beijing New Matrix are “fake companies,” committee Chairman Brian Mast, R-Fla., said at a brief meeting of his panel. “If BIS approved the sale of these chips to these firms, obviously, that is of tremendous concern to us, especially given that President [Donald] Trump made clear last year that the sale of AI chips to China will have very real national security conditions" (see 2601130073 and 2512080059).

According to the committee, Nvidia and AMD maintained that all of the license applications posed a “low” risk of diversion. Lawmakers suggested that BIS seems to have failed to question the chipmakers' assertions.

“The license information we are disclosing today will help inform the American public about how BIS has handled licenses for Nvidia H200 and equivalent AMD chips to China,” committee ranking member Gregory Meeks, D-N.Y., said during the panel's meeting. "The record shows a troubling contradiction. Even as there were massive delays in license reviews at BIS, the agency seemed to approve licenses for advanced chips to China with little scrutiny.  I have raised questions about this for over a year, and we need to know why."

In an emailed statement, a BIS spokesperson said that Commerce's "approach to H200 licensing is based on a rule published in the Federal Register in January 2026, which imposes significant additional national security-related requirements on license applicants in order to qualify for case-by-case consideration rather than a presumption of denial. Licensing decisions are the product of deliberation and consultation among multiple U.S. government agencies."

A spokesperson for Nvidia said in an emailed statement that the bureau's "review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes. Despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China.”

AMD said in an emailed statement that it "complies with applicable U.S. export control laws and regulations and maintains rigorous processes to ensure compliance across our global operations. We remain focused on serving our customers and upholding the highest standards of integrity.”

The data release came as multiple congressional committees seek to hear from Commerce officials and current and former Nvidia leaders next month about allegations that Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed International illegally diverted sensitive Nvidia chips to China (see 2611180033). The Commerce Department and DOJ are also investigating the matter (see 2611170015)

Late in October, a spokesperson for Nvidia said that the diversion allegations against Megaspeed "were thoroughly investigated and debunked last year" (see 2610290036). The company’s board of directors announced in early November that it has tapped a committee of independent directors to investigate reports that former CEO Jensen Huang “has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort” (see 2611090036).

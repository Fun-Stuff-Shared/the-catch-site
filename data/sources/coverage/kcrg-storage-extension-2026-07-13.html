Iowa Army Ammunition Plant gets extension to store hazardous waste

By KCRG Staff
Published: Jul. 13, 2026 at 7:47 AM CDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

MIDDLETOWN, Iowa (KCRG) - The Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown says the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has granted an extension for the site to store explosive materials until it finds a safe disposal option.

The site was was using lead azide for production, but is now no longer needed. The chemical is used in munitions production.

IAAAP paused operations for nearly two weeks in March to conduct a safety review and find a safe storage option for the materials.

Since the materials were no longer used in production, they were reclassified as hazardous waste. IAAAP previously filed an emergency Resource Conservation and Recovery Act permit with the EPA to comply with federal regulations to manage the material.

IAAAP says the lead azide has been stored in earth-covered, explosion-proof magazines to mitigate any potential risk.

The extension on the EPA permit allows the site to form a permanent safe disposal plan, says IAAAP commander Lt. Col. Daniel V. Nosse.

“Securing this permit extension allows us to maintain the highest safety and regulatory standards while we actively finalize our long-term disposal plans,” Nosse said. “The U.S. Army is committed to following the environmental regulations that will ensure that the treatment and disposal of the lead azide is completed in a manner that is safe, practical, and done with minimal disruption to the community.”

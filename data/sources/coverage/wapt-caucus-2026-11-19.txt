Mississippi Legislative Black Caucus demands investigation into voting irregularities - WAPT

Black Caucus calling on lawmakers to investigate number of inactive voters following general election
The Mississippi Legislative Black Caucus is demanding an independent investigation into voting irregularities during the Nov. 3 general election, citing concerns about discarded ballots and voter classifications.
"The Secretary of State is the chief election official in the state of Mississippi, and it’s his responsibility be training all local elected officials so that they can then train the poll workers effectively and efficiently," said State Rep. Zayika Summers, a Democrat from Hinds County.
The caucus is seeking an independent examination of allegations that Secretary of State Michael Watson used data from the credit reporting company Experian, which listed 230,000 registered Mississippi voters as inactive.
"We need to find out how many were wrongly marked inactive. Who approved the Experian data? What were counties told to do it?" said State Rep. Kabir Karreim, chair of the legislative black caucus.
Kameisha Smith, a civic engagement consultant, said she organized the registration of nearly 300 voters in Holmes County, only to discover her own name had been purged when she tried to vote.
"It felt like mine in that moment, I felt powerless in that moment," Smith said.
Members of the caucus said any investigation into voting irregularities should be thorough, transparent, and timely.
Watson responded to the call for an investigation in a statement.
"Our team welcomes an investigation because we have continuously followed the law. Mississippi's voter rolls are maintained by local election officials under state and federal law. Our job is to give them the needed resources to better do their jobs, and that's exactly what we did," Watson said.
Watson also had previously said that to ensure accurate voter rolls, Mississippi followed Electronic Assistance Commission guidance and repeatedly provided data to local officials.
The data distributed to them in 2025, which included U.S. Postal Service change-of-address information and commercial data, flagged approximately 113,000 individuals for their review, according to Watson. He said not a single voter was made inactive or removed from the voter rolls without review by a local election official.

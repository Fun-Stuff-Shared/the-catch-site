The voting groups that powered the Democratic sweep - Washington Post

Trump’s winning 2024 coalition, including Hispanics and younger voters, swung back hard toward Democrats.
Voters across the country from a range of demographic groups backed Democrats by overwhelming numbers on Tuesday, giving the party control of both chambers of Congress in a sharp rebuke to President Donald Trump and the GOP agenda.
Rural areas, suburbs and major cities all moved toward Democratic candidates by double digits compared with the 2024 election, according to a Washington Post analysis of data as of 8:30 a.m. Wednesday.
Midterm turnout is lower than in presidential election years and almost always swings against the party of the sitting president. But these results included new high-water marks for Democrats, who won in multiple states for the first time in decades — and in nearly a century in the case of Kansas.
Democrats successfully defended competitive seats in Michigan and Georgia while flipping seven Senate seats — in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas — that were held by Republicans, some of which Trump won by double-digits in 2024.
The groups that shifted the most
Majority-Hispanic counties
Counties where the majority of the citizens of voting age are Hispanic. Most are in Texas and New Mexico.
Lower-income counties
Counties where the median household income is under $60,000.
Younger counties
Counties where the majority of the citizens of voting age are 18 to 44.
Rural counties
Counties where most adults live in small towns and rural areas.
Here’s a look at some of the most telling shifts in major Senate races:
Michigan
Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, a former public health official, improved Democratic margins substantially in Michigan’s lowest-income counties in his victory over Republican Mike Rogers, a former congressman.
Lower-income counties in Michigan
Michigan counties where the median household income is under $70,000.
Kansas
Democrat Adam Hamilton, a Methodist megachurch pastor, defeated Sen. Roger Marshall (R) thanks to strong support in the Kansas City suburbs where his church is located. But the state’s mostly rural western and central regions — which are traditionally conservative — also moved away from Republicans.
Rural counties in Kansas
Kansas counties where most adults live in small cities and rural areas.
Texas
Voters in the overwhelmingly Hispanic areas around San Antonio and along the border helped power Democratic state Rep. James Talarico’s victory in Texas against Ken Paxton, the state’s scandal-plagued GOP attorney general. That shift also translated to the governor’s race, where Gov. Greg Abbott (R) was unseated by state Rep. Gina Hinojosa (D).
Majority-Hispanic counties in Texas
Texas counties where the majority of the citizens of voting age are Hispanic.
South Carolina
Black voters are key for Southern Democrats. But pediatrician Annie Andrews also made significant improvements among South Carolina’s White voters with lower education levels in unseating Sen. Darline Graham (R), who had been appointed to fill the seat held by her brother, Lindsey Graham.
White and non-college-educated residents
South Carolina counties where the majority of residents above the age of 25 are White and do not have a bachelor’s degree.
About this story
Live election data is from the Associated Press as of 8:30 a.m. Nov. 4. House data includes only districts with at least 90% of expected vote reporting, which means that districts in the West, including California, Oregon and Colorado, are sparsely represented in this analysis because their votes are counted after those in Eastern and Midwestern states. Past election data is from Dave Leip’s Atlas of U.S. Presidential Elections.
To calculate the demographic rates for counties and districts in the story, The Washington Post used 2024 five-year American Community Survey data from the Census Bureau. Estimates for education and race are based solely on the adult citizen population of a county. Populations are identified as urban, suburban, small city and rural based on the 2023 National Center for Health Statistics county-level classification system. Medium metros are metropolitan areas with 250,000 to 1 million people.
These figures represent aggregated population demographics, not individual voter preferences.
For congressional districts, The Post aggregated this block-group, tract and county data to congressional districts. For areas that are part of multiple congressional districts, The Post weighted those population estimates based on the relevant 2020 block-level unit for each district. For example, to calculate the number of college-educated adult citizens in each district, The Post used estimates of residents over 25 as the denominator.
Independent candidates who caucus with a party are counted as representing that party. In addition, in Senate races in Nebraska, Idaho and South Dakota where state Democrats have coalesced behind the independent candidate, those independent candidates are counted as Democrats to calculate shifts. Montana, which has three major candidates running, is not represented.

New York Times must face Trump’s $15 million defamation lawsuit | Courthouse News Service

TAMPA, Fla. (CN) — A federal judge declined to throw out President Donald Trump’s $15 million defamation lawsuit against The New York Times and Penguin Random House publishing company on Thursday.
In a 12-page order, U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday dismissed two of Trump’s claims, but determined the remaining statements accusing him of tax evasion and possible ties to the Mafia during his New York real estate career were based on “incomplete, minimal information.”
“In other words, the facts alleged in the complaint create at least a plausible inference that the authors and the publishers knew that no reliable conclusion about violations of the tax laws, including especially the estate and gift tax laws, is possible without reliance on qualified opinion formulated after the necessary factual and legal research and analysis, which they knowingly failed to obtain,” wrote Merryday, a George H.W. Bush appointee.
Trump filed his lawsuit in September 2025 in Tampa federal court, accusing the Times of “spreading false and defamatory content” and acting as a “full-throated mouthpiece of the Democratic Party.”
He pointed to three articles published by the Times in the months leading up to the 2024 presidential election that questioned his business practices and rise to fame with “The Apprentice.” Another article dealt with former Chief of Staff John Kelly’s assertions that Trump made admiring statements about Adolf Hitler.
In addition to the Times, Trump is targeting reporters Susanne Craig, Russ Buettner, Peter Baker and Michael S. Schmidt, and Penguin Random House, which published “Lucky Loser: How Donald Trump Squandered His Father’s Fortune and Created the Illusion of Success.” That book was written by Craig and Buettner.
The Times and Penguin Random House did not immediately respond to a request for comment. In their motion to dismiss, the companies repeatedly cited the landmark 1964 case New York Times Co. v. Sullivan that requires the president, as a public figure, to prove the false statements were published with actual malice — meaning they knew the statement was false or acted with reckless disregard for the truth.
In his order, Merryday criticized the publications’ failure to obtain qualified sources to back up statements on Trump’s tax practices.
“Knowing of the necessity for that opinion but failing to procure that opinion is a fine example of the purposeful avoidance of the truth, a knowing failure that remains actionable, even after Times v. Sullivan,” the judge wrote.
Trump’s legal team did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
Trump has increasingly used the courts to attack media outlets he deems as political enemies. Last week, another federal judge in Florida allowed his $10 billion lawsuit against the British Broadcasting Corporation to proceed. Earlier this year, a federal judge dismissed Trump’s lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal over an article about a bawdy letter Trump supposedly sent to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

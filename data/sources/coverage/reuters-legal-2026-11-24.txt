Explainer-Nvidia and board still face legal risks after Jensen Huang’s return By Reuters

By Tom Hals
WILMINGTON, Delaware, Nov 24 (Reuters) - Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO at the urging of US President Donald Trump, less than two weeks after the co-founder of the AI chip powerhouse resigned following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged spy for China.
The reported ties between Huang and a suspected spy led to his resignation as CEO and sparked investigations by the US securities regulator and FBI, congressional hearings, a review by the company’s independent directors and a proposed investor class action lawsuit.
Below is a look at how the rehiring, which sparked a massive rally in Nvidia’s stock, could impact the legal exposure for the company and its directors.
WHAT ARE THE ALLEGATIONS?
Huang allegedly had intimate relations with Alice Huang, an unrelated Chinese businesswoman who is accused of having ties with Beijing. Huang is also accused of having had intimate relationships with at least two Nvidia employees. He resigned on November 12.
Huang has acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and apologized, but has not publicly addressed the individual alleged relationships in detail. China has denied the espionage allegations. Attempts by Reuters to reach Alice Huang have been unsuccessful.
Nvidia said in a statement that based on US government input the board was confident that "Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk," a claim backed by Trump in an interview with CNBC. Trump said that Huang did nothing wrong and that his intervention led to Huang’s return.
Nvidia sits at the center of the technological rivalry between the United States and China. Washington has imposed restrictions on exports of advanced AI chips to China since 2022, including those made by Nvidia.
COULD THE BOARD BE ON THE HOOK DESPITE TRUMP’S ASSURANCES?
An investor filed a so-called derivative lawsuit in federal court on November 11 seeking to hold the board liable for failing to make a good-faith effort to oversee the business and Huang. The lawsuit said the board allowed Nvidia to make repeated assurances about Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang and her former company, Megaspeed, until the allegations were public.
Nvidia formed a committee of independent directors that hired law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to assist in an internal investigation. The committee is still investigating, Nvidia said on Monday.
Trump’s statement that Huang did not pose a national security risk could strengthen the directors’ legal defenses, according to legal experts. The directors could argue that Trump’s assurances mean the company will not be prosecuted for export control violations, removing the threat that rehiring Huang could damage the company.
However, Lee Squitieri, one of the attorneys representing the shareholder in the derivative lawsuit, told Reuters in an email that rehiring Huang does not change the liability scenario very much and that the problems extend beyond possible export control violations. Squitieri said Huang dominated the board and harmed Nvidia by leaving it without a succession plan and with poor compliance oversight and weak corporate governance.
Derivative lawsuit settlements are generally paid from insurance, and Nvidia has coverage for its officers and directors of up to $950 million.
WHAT HAPPENS TO ANY FEDERAL CRIMINAL PROBE?
Trump has said the FBI is investigating Huang’s affair with Alice Huang, but has been vague about the focus of the probe.
Attorney General Todd Blanche said on November 12 the FBI is investigating Alice Huang. The Department of Justice has said it had no evidence of wrongdoing by Nvidia or its co-founder.
Reuters could not immediately determine the status of federal criminal probes, and the Justice Department declined to comment.
Legal experts said Trump’s remarks on Monday make it unlikely the Justice Department would continue an investigation during his administration, particularly given how much influence the president wields over the department.
But the statute of limitations for export control violations extends beyond January 2029, when Trump leaves office, and a new attorney general could reopen an investigation. Trump also could preemptively pardon Huang to avoid future prosecution for violating federal law.
CAN STATES STILL PURSUE CASES AGAINST HUANG AND THE COMPANY?
Attorneys general in Missouri and California said on Monday their investigations will continue.
The office of California Attorney General Rob Bonta, a Democrat, said on November 19 it had served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia to determine if China obtained advanced chips or confidential information and whether Nvidia misled the public. Bonta has not said whether the investigation is criminal or civil in nature and a probe does not mean a case will be brought.
The office of Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway, a Republican, said it would proceed with its investigation into whether Huang’s actions harmed state pension funds. Her office previously said it was viewing the investigation through a consumer protection lens focused on potential misrepresentations.
WHAT HAPPENS TO THE SEC PROBE?
The US Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia for potential accounting issues, Reuters reported on Friday. Reuters could not immediately ascertain on Monday whether Trump’s intervention had changed the status of that federal probe.
The SEC did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
However, the independence of the SEC, which is structured as a bipartisan commission and is chaired by Trump appointee Paul Atkins, has eroded under Trump’s second administration, according to legal experts.
In March, Reuters reported that the SEC’s enforcement chief quit after just six months on the job after clashing with the agency’s leaders over the direction of its enforcement program, including the handling of cases with ties to Trump and his family.
DO INVESTORS HAVE LEGAL RECOURSE?
A proposed class action was filed in San Jose, California, federal court on November 9 alleging the company and Huang defrauded shareholders by concealing the alleged relationship with Alice Huang and Megaspeed, which Jensen Huang described in an October 30 Bloomberg interview as "normal and not unusual."
The lawsuit did not specify damages but the stock lost around 20% from its peak to the end of the class period, a decline of around $1 trillion in market value.
The stock rally sparked by rehiring Huang could undermine the value of the lawsuit if the shares continue to rise. US securities law caps shareholder damages if the stock rebounds in 90 days after an initial drop.

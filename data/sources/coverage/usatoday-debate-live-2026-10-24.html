Ossoff, Collins clash on AI, immigration, more in debate: Live updates

Irene Wright
USA TODAY
Updated Oct. 13, 2026, 9:13 p.m. ET

On the first day of early voting in Georgia, the two candidates at the top of the ballot are hitting the debate stage for the second time.

Incumbent Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff and Republican House Rep. Mike Collins are in a battle for the U.S. Senate seat, one that could help determine control of Congress going into the second half of Trump's second term.

Ossoff is the only incumbent Democratic senator running in a state Trump won in 2024, but he has outperformed Collins in polls throughout the summer and has earned endorsements from across the political spectrum.

But, Collins has the Trump endorsement and has been running on supporting the president in his war in Iran, his strong push on immigration (Collins authored the Laken Riley Act) and has said the high prices across the country are a result of Democratic initiatives and the Green New Deal.

Collins and Ossoff took the debate stage for the first time last week with Atlanta News First, fielding questions on topics like affordability, healthcare, congressional experience and more.

The debate quickly spiraled into attacks between the candidates — and now they are going in for round two.

9:10 pm ET October 13, 2026

Both candidates decline to take questions following debate

Irene Wright

Despite the tradition of candidates taking questions from the press following the Atlanta Press Club debates, both Ossoff and Collins declined Tuesday night.

Both campaigns did not provide a reason for skipping questions.

8:55 pm ET October 13, 2026

AI is 'most complex and important legislative question of our time,' Ossoff says

Irene Wright

When asked about the role of AI in Americans' future, Ossoff said it was the "most complex and important legislative question of our time."

Ossoff called for federal inspectors, transparency from the federal government, and the fortification of the federal system in defense from bioterrorism. He also attacked the Trump Administration's lack of support for artificial intelligence regulation.

"We're in a race with China for AI. It's coming. It's already here... The problem is there's a lot of bad actors out there in anything that you have," Collins said. "But before we even get there, we need to make sure that these data centers are not harming the local infrastructure, like the water and electricity."

8:41 pm ET October 13, 2026

'Voting should always be a state's right,' Collins says of DOJ interference in midterms

Irene Wright

Collins was asked whether Donald Trump won the 2020 election point blank multiple times during the debate. At no point did he answer yes or no, but instead said Biden became the president.

When asked whether Collins would support federal intervention in Georgia's midterm elections this year, Collins said "voting should always be a state's right."

"In Georgia, if (voters) don't have confidence in our elections, then we need to make sure that we do and that needs to go for any state," Collins said. "You know the problem is that we have voter ID here in Georgia. We do, but when offered the opportunity at least five times, Jon Ossoff voted against the SAVE Act to get voter ID for all across this country. Y'all, we live in the best country in the world. There's no reason we shouldn't have the most secure elections in the world."

Collins has said Trump won Georgia in 2020 during his campaign, including shortly before his Republican primary race.

8:31 pm ET October 13, 2026

'Your campaign is in collapse,' Ossoff says

Irene Wright

Collins used his opportunity to ask Ossoff a question to talk about the Laken Riley Act, an immigration bill authored by Collins and named after a Georgia nursing student who was killed by an undocumented immigrant on the University of Georgia campus in 2024.

Collins accused Ossoff of failing to work on the bill and said his policies on immigration led to her death. Ossoff did vote in support of the bill when it passed as the first bill of Trump's second term.

Ossoff responded by saying Collins was "lying" about Ossoff's record because "your campaign is in collapse."

"You're lying about a young woman's death because your campaign is in collapse, and your campaign is in collapse because the whole state knows that you're currently under federal invesigation for the illegal misuse of tax dollars," Ossoff said.

Collins remains under a House ethics investigation from his time in Congress, in which he is accused of hiring a staffer for a no-show job and misusing taxpayer funds.

8:23 pm ET October 13, 2026

Collins denounces white nationalism as debate gets heated

Irene Wright

Collins has been followed throughout the campaign by reports of ties to white nationalism, including a CNN report that found Collins' son-in-law was sharing white supremacist messages on social media.

"I condemn any hate speech, anti-Semitism, hate of any kind. I fire people when things happen, fire them," Collins said.

In response, Collins attempted to tie Ossoff to the Democratic Socialists of America and Democratic figures like Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed and social media commentator Hasan Piker.

The congressman then said Ossoff's rhetoric is the reason a man was recently arrested for threatening the life of Collins and his son-in-law.

Ossoff continued to push Collins, quickly turning the debate into an arguing match in which both candidates spoke over one another while the moderator tried to get the debate back on track.

8:11 pm ET October 13, 2026

'I have zero interest in running for president,' Ossoff says in first question

Irene Wright

Jon Ossoff, as his name has been circulated as a possible 2028 presidential candidate, again said he has "zero interest in running for president" during the first question of the debate.

Ossoff committed to serving his full six-year term if elected, and said his focus was on Georgia.

The senator has been asked about the rumors repeatedly, answering "no" to any presidential ambitions each time. Early polls, however, show he may have a chance should his decision to run change between now and 2028.

8:06 pm ET October 13, 2026

'Globetrotting trust fund kid,' Collins opens on offense

Irene Wright

Rep. Mike Collins came out swinging in the debate by using his opening statement to talk about Senator Jon Ossoff.

The congressman called Ossoff a "globetrotting trust fund kid" and said this election was a choice between "Georgia and Georgetown."

"Behind his highly-skilled, scripted online clips is a coward, a coward who lies abut his accomplishments ad views his success not as what he does for Georgia, but the number of clicks that he gets on social media and the praise that he gets from his radical friends," Collins said.

Collins promised to question Ossoff about his record in the debate.

7:04 pm ET October 13, 2026

First day of early voting in GA shows high numbers

Irene Wright

Early voting kicked off in Georgia on Tuesday, with tens of thousands of voters across the state casting ballots.

Preliminary data from the Georgia Secretary of State's Office shows at least 178,000 people voted on the first day.

Four of the Atlanta metro counties made up the highest ballot counts, with Fulton submitting more than 21,000 ballots, DeKalb submitting nearly 15,000 ballots, Cobb submitting 14,500 ballots and Gwinnett submitting just over 11,000 ballots.

The in-person ballots build on 9,927 absentee ballots that have been returned and accepted by election officials. A total of 131,200 absentee ballots were requested for the midterm elections.

On the first day of early voting in the primary election, April 27, more than 35,000 people voted, a number Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger said was a record. Raffensperger also said the turnout proved "Georgians trust our elections."

6:56 pm ET October 13, 2026

Ossoff releases statement ahead of debate

Irene Wright

Ossoff released a statement ahead of the debate with Collins Tuesday night following a fiery debate appearance last week:

“Georgia voters deserve answers from a sitting member of Congress, Mike Collins, who wants to represent this state in the U.S. Senate, and Georgians did not get answers from Mike Collins last week," the senator said in the statement. "On Tuesday, when I see him again on the debate stage, we will continue to press him to explain what he cannot explain, and to answer questions that he refuses to answer.”

What happened in last Ossoff, Collins debate?

Emily R. Condon

Sen. Jon Ossoff, D-Georgia, faced off against his Republican challenger, Rep. Mike Collins, in their contentious Oct. 8 debate that included topics on affordability, immigration enforcement, data centers − and sharp personal attacks that drew widespread attention online.

The candidates traded insults such as Ossoff calling Collins a "bigot" and Collins calling Ossoff a "trust-fund kid."

Georgia's race is one of the races that will determine whether Democrats take control of the Senate, as it is one of just two Senate seats Democrats are defending in states carried by President Donald Trump in 2024, along with Michigan.

Ossoff leads Collins by 8 to 11 points in polls of likely Georgia voters by the Public Polling Project (Sept. 21-31), Insider Advantage (Sept. 22-23) and Wedgewood Polls (Oct. 2-3).

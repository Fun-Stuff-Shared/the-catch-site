Judge lets Trump's defamation suit against The New York Times and Penguin Random House proceed

Nov 19, 2026 · 6:45 PM · By MLQ.ai Newsroom · Edited by Peter Foy · 2 min read
Key points
  • Merryday granted the motion to dismiss Counts III and IV but denied it for Counts I, II, V and VI of Trump's second amended complaint [1].
  • Seven statements survive: six from the book Lucky Loser on Fred Trump's transfers of wealth and taxes, and one from a Times article about Mafia ties and money-laundering findings [1].
  • The judge found 27 other challenged statements not actionable, denied Trump another amendment and gave the defendants 28 days to answer [1].

A federal judge in Tampa on Thursday allowed President Donald Trump's defamation lawsuit against The New York Times and Penguin Random House to move forward, though only on a narrow set of statements. In a 12-page order, U.S. District Judge Steven D. Merryday granted the defendants' motion to dismiss Counts III and IV of Trump's second amended complaint and denied it for Counts I, II, V and VI [1].

Trump filed the suit in September 2025 seeking $15 billion, plus punitive damages, against the New York Times Co., reporters Peter Baker, Susanne Craig, Michael Schmidt and Russ Buettner, and Penguin Random House, which published Craig and Buettner's book [2]. Merryday struck the first complaint that month, and in July 2026 he deferred a ruling on the Times' motion to dismiss and ordered Trump to amend again [2].

What survives

The order lists 27 challenged statements that "are not actionable" and will not proceed as claims of defamation published with "actual malice," the standard set by the Supreme Court in New York Times Co. v. Sullivan for public officials [1].

Seven statements remain. Six come from Lucky Loser, including that Trump "had received the equivalent of more than $400 million from his father, much of it through fraudulent tax evasion schemes" and that Fred Trump had given his son "taxable gifts masquerading as loans, a likely tax fraud that went unnoticed." The seventh, from a Times article, says authorities "looked into his ties with the Mafia, found violations of money laundering rules and penalized him for skirting stock trade rules" [1].

Merryday wrote that the complaint plausibly alleges the authors and publishers knew those accusations of complex crimes were "based on incomplete, minimal information" because they did not obtain a formal opinion from a qualified tax expert. "They know that they don't know, but they are undeterred," he wrote, calling that "a fine example of the purposeful avoidance of the truth" [1]. The defendants had argued that Craig and Buettner analyzed about 100,000 pages of records and relied on tax experts quoted in their 2018 reporting [1].

The judge denied Trump's request to amend again, ordered the defendants to answer within 28 days and said the ruling does not decide whether evidence about the dismissed statements is admissible at trial [1]. The Times and Penguin have denied defaming Trump and argued in court that his case is legally flawed; they did not immediately respond to Reuters' requests for comment on the ruling [3]. On Nov. 10, U.S. District Judge Jeffrey T. Kuntz in Florida allowed Trump's $10 billion suit against the BBC to proceed (our report).

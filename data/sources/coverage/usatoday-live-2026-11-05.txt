Midterm results live updates: Alaska, Mississippi Senate races still neck-and-neck
Republicans are still licking their political wounds after Democrats' resounding midterm election wins on Tuesday.
The seismic blue wave left the GOP scrambling and eyeing a path forward for a post-Trump era, even as the president remains at the helm of the party — and White House — for the next two years.
"If we don’t change, we’ll take more beatings," retiring Rep. Don Bacon, R-Nebraska, said.
Democrats, meanwhile, are continuing to celebrate resounding midterm election wins over President Trump, while hoping to add to their congressional majorities as the final races are decided.
"The biggest takeaway from last night is that millions of voters put their faith in Democrats to fight for them," Democratic Party Chairman Ken Martin said Wednesday in the wake of the stunning cross-country electoral rout. "Now, we’re going to get to work and fight for a more affordable America."
A previously listless Democratic Party flipped the House, Senate and pulled off several down-ballot races, from protecting abortion rights in some states to winning the governor's office in Texas. As of early Thursday, Democrats had flipped seven Senate seats and more than 30 House seats. Votes are still being counted in the U.S. Senate races in Alaska and Mississippi, as well as dozens of House contests.
Trump said he will accept the midterm results and work with Democrats, but he also pledged to veto Democratic bills that come across his desk.
He called the veto the "ultimate power," though Congress can override a presidential veto with a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Maine CD2 to be determined by ranked choice voting
Margie Cullen
Maine’s 2nd Congressional District House race will be determined by ranked choice voting after neither former Republican Gov. Paul LePage nor Democratic State Auditor Matt Dunlap received over 50% of first place votes, the Secretary of State’s office announced Nov. 5.
Maine uses ranked choice voting in the general election only in federal races with more than two candidates. Under this system, voters rank candidates in order of preference. If no one candidate gets over 50% of the total first-choice votes, the last place candidate gets eliminated and their second-choice votes get distributed. The process continues until one candidate cracks 50%.
Initial results received by the Secretary of State's office showed LePage with a slight lead of 49.89% to Dunlap’s 48.31% out of 341,248 total first choice ballots cast. A little under 6,000 votes separated the two candidates. There were 123 votes (0.04%) for declared write-in candidates and 6,017 (1.8%) blank votes.
The Secretary of State’s office said the ranked choice tabulation will begin Nov. 9.
The announcement comes after LePage had initially declared victory on the evening of Nov. 4.
This will be the fourth time in the past five elections that Maine’s 2nd District has gone to ranked choice tabulation. The last time this race went to a ranked choice runoff, in 2024, it took four days to complete.
In an election that saw Democrats flip over 30 House seats, Maine 2nd District is an opportunity for a Republican pick-up. It covers the more rural and conservative northern part of the state, and has given Trump his only Electoral College vote in New England in the past three presidential elections, due to Maine's system of apportioning Electoral College votes by district. But it has been represented for the last eight years by Democratic Rep. Jared Golden, who was able to win re-election four times by running as a blue dog Democrat who has historically been a strong supporter of gun rights and said he would be "OK" with Trump winning reelection in 2024.

5 Black governors make US history with record wins
Mary Walrath-Holdridge
A record five Black governors will serve at the same time beginning in January, ahistoric milestoneafter the Nov. 3 midterm elections nearly doubled the number of Black Americans ever elected governor in the United States.
Before this election, only three Black Americans had ever been elected governor: Wes Moore in Maryland, Deval Patrick in Massachusetts and Doug Wilder in Virginia. Never had two served simultaneously. On Election Day, four Black gubernatorial candidates broke barriers in their states, joining Moore, who won reelection.
Democrat and former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms made history as Georgia’s first Black and first woman governor, defeating GOP pick Rick Jackson to end Republicans' 24-year gubernatorial reign in the state.
Democrat David Crowley defeated Republican Tom Tiffany to become Wisconsin’s first Black governor, months after briefly suspending his campaign.
Democrat, state attorney general and former state senator Aaron Ford defeated incumbent Republican Joe Lombardo to become Nevada's first Black governor and U.S. Rep.Byron Donalds, the only GOP candidate on the list, dodged Election Day's blue wave to become Florida's first Black governor.
“The story of this election is not just about who won,” Dominik Whitehead, chief of field for the NAACP, said in a Nov. 4 statement. “It’s about the Black wave of voters who understood what was at stake and made their voices heard.”

Mississippi Senate race outcome might take days
Michael Loria
It remains unclear who Mississippi will send to the U.S. Senate, and the state's top elections official is warning the final result might not be known for days.
"Counties have 10 days from the date of the election to submit their certified results to our office, which is November 13," Secretary of State Michael Watson said in a statement Wednesday. "Additionally, there are approximately 3,600 outstanding absentee-by-mail ballots that must be received within five business days and an unknown number of affidavit ballots that will be processed within the 10-day window counties have to certify their results."
On the ballot in the Magnolia State are Republican incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith and Democratic challenger Scott Colom. With 80% of ballots counted, Colom held a slight edge, with 353,440 votes to Hyde-Smith's 352,203. Independent Ty Pinkins had 15,696 votes, USA TODAY previously reported.
The tight race could deliver Democrats an eighth flipped Senate seat and make Colom Mississippi's first Democratic U.S. senator since Sen. John C. Stennis left office in 1989. Hyde-Smith has held the seat since 2018.
Where does the Alaska Senate race stand?
James Powel and Aysha Bagchi
In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola leads incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan by 637 votes with 74% of ballots counted, according to the Associated Press. Peltola and Sullivan are both below the 50% threshold needed to avoid sending the race to Alaska's ranked-choice voting system.
Alaska's voting system can make results take longer to determine in close races. According to David Becker, a former Justice Department voting rights lawyer who runs the Center for Election Innovation & Research, election officials will have to wait "for some time" for votes to come in from remote areas.
The state also has a significant population of military voters, whose ballots can arrive up to 15 days after Election Day, as long as they were postmarked by Election Day.
If no candidate gets more than 50% of the vote, the state's ranked choice voting system comes into play, which could mean further delays. Under state law, the ranked choice tally wouldn't start until Nov. 18.
"So it might be the second half of November where we get a really good sense of that," Becker said.

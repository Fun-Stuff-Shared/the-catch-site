Trump’s $15 Billion Lawsuit Against “The” “New York Times” After They Accused Him of Tax Fraud Moves Forward

The lawsuit was initially filed in September 2025, before Trump’s legal team was permitted refile in August 2026
NEED TO KNOW
- U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday ruled that President Donald Trump can sue The New York Times and Penguin Random House for alleged defamation
- The lawsuit claims the defendants knowingly published unverified allegations of Trump's tax crimes and financial misconduct
- Despite the Thursday, Nov. 19 ruling, the defendants maintain the claims lack merit and plan to defend their reporting
President Donald Trump has been permitted to sue The New York Times in a $15 billion libel lawsuit.
The 80-year-old president filed a defamation lawsuit against the newspaper, publishing house Penguin Random House, and reporters Susanne Craig, Russ Buettner and Pete Baker on Aug. 27. U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday denied the defendants' motion to dismiss the lawsuit on Thursday, Nov. 19, according to the ruling viewed by PEOPLE. (The recent ruling follows Merryday's decision to throw out Trump's initial September 2025 version of the 85-page complaint and allowed his attorneys to refile, per Politico.)
Merryday's recent ruling found that the defendants did make potentially defamatory claims in their pieces, including Buettner and Craig's book Lucky Loser, which was included in two articles, "The Star-Making Machine That Created 'Donald Trump' " and "For Trump, a Lifetime of Scandals Heads Toward a Moment of Judgment" in 2024.
The judge ruled that the statements suggesting Trump had likely committed "complex" tax crimes were based on insufficient input from tax experts. "The facts alleged in the complaint create at least a plausible inference that the authors and the publishers knew that no reliable conclusion about violations of the tax laws," the ruling reads.
He wrote, "In other words, the [defendants] know that they don't know, but they are undeterred. Knowing of the necessity for that opinion but failing to procure that opinion is a fine example of the purposeful avoidance of the truth, a knowing failure that remains actionable."
Merryday permitted Trump to sue over the claims in the second Times article that federal, state and local authorities had investigated his alleged "ties to the Mafia," and authorities finding "violations of money laundering laws," as well as penalization "for skirting stock trade rules."
"The facts alleged in the complaint create at least a plausible inference that the authors and the publishers knew that no reliable conclusion about violations of the tax laws, including especially the estate and gift tax laws, is possible without reliance on qualified opinion formulated after the necessary factual and legal research and analysis, which they knowingly failed to obtain," the ruling states.
While the defamation per se and defamation per quod claims about the book and the second article were allowed to proceed, Merryday dismissed the same counts for the first article.
Merryday ruled that the statements in the first article "are not actionable as, and will not proceed further in this action as claims of, defamation published with 'actual malice.' "
The Thursday, Nov. 19 ruling agrees that the claims are plausible and specific enough to be considered defamation and pursued in court, but Merryday did not explicitly state that Lucky Loser defamed the president.
In response to the ruling, Trump's legal team issued a statement obtained by PEOPLE, expressing their satisfaction with the ruling.
"President Trump continues holding the Fake News accountable for deceiving the American people," the statement read. "The New York Times has a long pattern of defaming President Trump in service of its leftist political agenda, and today's momentous ruling is a victory for honesty in America."
"The Times is encouraged to see Judge Merryday's dismissal of almost all claims against us," Times spokesperson Charlie Stadtlander said, Politico reported. "We continue to believe the remaining claim in the filing has no legal merit. We look forward to making this argument as we continue to defend ourselves against this baseless attack on The Times and the rights of the free and independent press in this country."
"The president has been given three opportunities to amend his claims, and the lawsuit remains obviously meritless," Stadtlander said, according to The Times.

The 2026 lawsuit follows the president's 2020 and 2021 lawsuits against the newspaper. The first case included a lawsuit over a 2019 Opinion essay and was dismissed in 2020. The second concerned an investigation into the politician's finances and was dismissed in 2023.
The president also filed lawsuits against 60 Minutes in 2025, alleging it deceptively edited a campaign trail interview with then-Vice President Kamala Harris on the CBS program. The lawsuit initially sought $10 billion in damages. CBS' parent company Paramount eventually agreed to pay $16 million to Trump's future presidential library, raising concerns about the precedent Paramount could set for media companies by giving in to the president's demands.

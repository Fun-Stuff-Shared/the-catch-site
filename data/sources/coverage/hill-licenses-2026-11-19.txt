Trump administration approved shipment of nearly 900,000 AI chips to China
The Hill's Headlines | PM - November 19, 2026
The Trump administration approved the shipment of nearly 900,000 AI chips to Defense Department-designated Chinese military companies, according to newly released documents from the House Foreign Affairs Committee.
The documents revealed the U.S. signed off on the sale of 879,868 AI semiconductor chips from Nvidia and AMD. The specific chips sold were Nvidia’s sensitive H200 chips along with their AMD equivalent.
The order was fulfilled through 21 license applications as of May 11 — including sending 75,000 to Alibaba, 150,000 to Tencent and its subsidiaries, 43,000 to Baidu and 4,000 to BYD. The four companies were all identified on the Defense Department’s 2026 list of alleged Chinese military companies operating in the U.S.
The committee wrote that under the Export Control Reform Act, Rep. Brian Mast (R-Fla.), chair of the panel, was able to “publicly reveal export control license application information” related to Nvidia and AMD’s AI chip licenses for China.
Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-N.Y.), ranking member on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, accused the Trump administration of mishandling chip export controls, saying it “has put our national security and technology edge at risk.”
He also bashed the Bureau of Industry and Security for failing “to enforce the rules,” adding that “Congress must now conduct rigorous oversight of the administration’s policies.”
An Nvidia spokesperson told The Hill, “the review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes.”
“Despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China,” the spokesperson added.
“AMD complies with applicable U.S. export control laws and regulations and maintains rigorous processes to ensure compliance across our global operations,” AMD told The Hill in a statement.
Mast has led probes in the House into the Trump administration’s approval of Nvidia’s H200 chips last year. Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang previously argued to federal government officials that the sale of its third or fourth best chips would make China dependent on the U.S.-made infrastructure.

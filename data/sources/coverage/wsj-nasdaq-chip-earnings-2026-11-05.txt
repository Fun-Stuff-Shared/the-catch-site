Nasdaq Composite Plunges 5.5% as Chip Earnings Spark Tech Stock Rout - WSJ

U.S. stocks plunged as disappointment in two major chip designers’ earnings and post-election fears about curbed data-center development caused a rout in artificial-intelligence companies.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1833.06 points, or 3.44%, to 51523.06, its biggest loss since April 4, 2025. The broad S&P 500 fell 334.89 points, or 4.36% to 7353.73. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite plunged 1421.52 points, or 5.52%, to 24315, entering correction territory and closing 11.9% below its October high. It was the biggest drop for the Nasdaq in more than a year.
“It’s a reconsideration of yesterday,” said JJ Kinahan, senior vice president at Cboe Global Markets. “Many in the market looked beyond national headlines and said ‘a lot of municipalities went and voted against the ability for data centers to be built in their backyard.’”
Expectations for AI profits have risen to almost unattainable heights after two years of rapid gains in tech stocks. Late Wednesday, Qualcomm, a longtime chip supplier to Apple and other smart-phone makers, revealed the growing pains caused by the AI boom. Higher prices for memory components and other materials weighed on its handset profit margins, while the torrid growth of its data-center business was not quite torrid enough to compensate.
“Qualcomm’s earnings touched it off,” said Kinahan. “It furthers the narrative that happened in the last earnings season: ‘alright, I see the potential [of AI] and we’re spending a lot of money on it, but what are they actually generating?’”
Similarly, British chip maker Arm Holdings fell sharply despite posting 27% growth in quarterly revenue as its outlook was not quite as rosy as some investors had anticipated.

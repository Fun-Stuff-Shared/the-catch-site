FAA issues rare nationwide ground stop; no indication of 'nefarious attack' - ABC News

Homeland Security said there is no indication yet this was a "nefarious" act.
The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop Friday for all airports due to a communication issue affecting air traffic control.
The agency's alert said the stop was "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety."
Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters Friday evening that the federal government is investigating, there was no indication yet that the incident was a "nefarious attack" and that everyone is safe.
"It's just a telecommunications outage as of now," he told reporters.
Mullin said the government will look into the root of the problem.
"This isn't another 9/11. I cannot repeat that enough," he added.
Shortly after the stop was issued, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in an X statement that it was "due a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers."
"FAA technicians are investigating the issue," he said in an X post.
This is the second time since Sept. 11, 2001, that the FAA has ordered a nationwide ground stop. The last time was in 2023, when the FAA NOTAM system went down and pilots could not get their pre-departure paperwork.
Air Route Traffic Control Centers are responsible for all flights above 18,000 feet. There are 22 of those centers in the U.S.
Canada's civil air navigation service, NAV Canada, told ABC News it is monitoring the situation with the FAA ground stop and there is no impact to Canadian systems.
NAV Canada said it "continues to support the safe movement of air traffic within our airspace, including any aircraft that may be diverted."
Pilots who are in the air have flight plans and are prepared to continue with their flight path in case of such an emergency, according to ABC News contributor John Nance.
"All pilots in that environment know what to do," Nance told ABC News on Friday.
"It is a matter of predictability. Air Traffic Control knows that airplane is going to come out of the sky at some point of time looking for a pathway," he later added.
Mullin told reporters that the federal government will be looking into all possibilities behind the communications issue. When asked by a reporter if it could be a cyber attack, he said they were not ruling anything out.
"As we investigate what caused this, you always got to assume that, as many cyber attacks that take place, that you make that assumption," Mullin said. "But as of right now, that's not known."
According to Flightradar24, there were 3,100 airborne aircraft bound for U.S. airports earlier amid nationwide ground stop. No additional aircraft are taking off at this time. Flights that are currently in the air will be diverted to other airports.
Earlier in the afternoon, the FAA issued a ground stop at both Chicago's O'Hare and Midway airports due to equipment outage.
-ABC News' Victoria Beaule contributed to this report.

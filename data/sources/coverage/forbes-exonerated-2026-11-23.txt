Trump Says Government Investigation Found Huang ‘Did Nothing Wrong’—Says Nvidia CEO Wouldn’t Be Back Without Him

Topline
President Donald Trump praised Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the company’s board for reinstating him Monday, telling CNBC, that “a very thorough investigation” by his administration found Huang “had bad judgement,” but “did nothing wrong.”
Key Facts
Trump said Huang had been “exonerated” when asked about the possibility Huang could face criminal liability for his relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset, Alice Huang.
“We believe he’s fine, he’s absolutely fine,” Trump said, adding that he “convinced” the board and “made the deal” to reinstate Huang.
The president said he spoke to the board “directly and indirectly” since Huang resigned from the company amid revelations of his affair with Alice Huang, and that he spoke to Huang Sunday night because he “knew what was going on.”
“I said, ‘Jensen just promise me one thing, you’re going to work hard’ . . . he’s so excited [to be returning],” Trump said.
Calling Huang a “national treasure,” Trump said, “I’m very, very happy that the company had the courage and the board had the courage to do the right thing” to allow Huang to return to his job.
Tangent
Trump’s comments come shortly after he wrote on Truth Social that the Nvidia board “made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO.” Trump said the “United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security” and the “Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess any evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.” Trump urged the company to continue its own internal investigation of compliance with U.S. export controls. Trump also posted a bizarre meme on Truth Social Monday morning that mirrors the infamous image of him raising his fist in the air shortly after he was shot in the ear in an assassination attempt against him. In the meme, Trump is replaced by Huang holding a red bra in the air with the Nvidia flag flying in the backdrop.
Key Background
Nvidia said it was reinstating Huang based on “input from senior levels of the United States government,” less than two weeks after Huang resigned. The board said it decided “unanimously” to reinstate Huang after “a series of discussions and meetings” with him, along with input from the U.S. government that made the board “confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.” Huang said in a statement Monday he was “honored” to return to the company and “take[s] this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.” Along with Trump, prominent members of the Silicon Valley tech community advocated for Huang to be reinstated. Hours before Nvidia announced the decision, Trump reiterated his push for Huang to be rehired, writing on Truth Social, “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang. It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.” In addition to Alice Huang, the CEO of a Singaporean data company, Megaspeed, that was accused of funneling restricted Nvidia chips to China, Huang was also accused of having extramarital affairs with two Nvidia employees. While he admitted to the affair with Alice Huang, he did not comment publicly on the other two affair allegations. Trump was reportedly made aware of U.S. intelligence that found Alice Huang was in contact with China’s spy agency during the time she was having an affair with Huang and when Megaspeed was doing business with the company, The New York Times reported Sunday. Trump reportedly downplayed the intelligence and has claimed publicly that he doesn’t believe Alice Huang is “a spy.”

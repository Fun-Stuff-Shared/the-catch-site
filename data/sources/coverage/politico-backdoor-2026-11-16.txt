Commerce closes China’s backdoor to Nvidia chips after White House steps in - POLITICO

A press release announcing the new rule said it was written at "the direction of Department of Treasury and the National Security Council."
11/16/2026 11:56 AM EST
The Commerce Department took steps Monday to prevent companies in restricted countries from renting advanced AI computing power from data centers abroad — a blow to tech giant Nvidia, whose chips fill those data centers.
In an interim final rule expected for publication in the Federal Register, the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security said it would require companies to apply for licenses before selling or shipping advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil — countries the rule identifies as posing a higher-than-normal risk that chips will be diverted, or rerouted, to China. Public comments on the rule are due 60 days after its publication in the Federal Register.
The rule follows a scandal over Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company accused of arranging to funnel hundreds of thousands of advanced processors into China through subsidiaries. The scandal reached the top of Nvidia last week, when CEO Jensen Huang resigned after admitting a relationship with a former Megaspeed executive, whom U.S. officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset.
The rule was written at the direction of Department of Treasury and the National Security Council, according to the press release issued by BIS. That’s a signal that it bypassed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, who’s under fire for allegedly failing to act against illegal shipments of Nvidia chips to China.

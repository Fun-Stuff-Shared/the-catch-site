Understanding the big Democratic midterms win: November 4-6, 2026 Economist / YouGov Poll

In this week’s Economist/YouGov poll of 3,166 registered voters:
Plus polling on election reactions, Trump rallies, voting wait times, views of the parties, liking or disliking Trump, and the war in Iran.
All of these items are among registered voters unless otherwise stated.
- 23% say the outcome of the election surprised them a great deal, 24% some, 25% a little, and 24% not at all. 38% of MAGA Republicans — Republicans who say they are MAGA supporters — were surprised a great deal, compared with 20% of non-MAGA Republicans
- 50% think Donald Trump’s campaign rallies hurt the Republican candidates he campaigned for a little or a lot, 12% that they helped a lot or a little, and 25% that they made no difference. In late October, 62% of Republicans said the rallies were helping; now 24% of Republicans say they helped. 73% of Democrats say the rallies hurt, up from 56% who said they were hurting
- Among people who say they voted in person, 41% say there was no wait at their polling place, up from 30% of in-person voters after the 2024 presidential election. 18% waited more than 15 minutes, down from 29% in 2024
- 44% have a very or somewhat favorable opinion of the Democratic Party and 51% an unfavorable one, for a net favorability — the share with a favorable opinion minus the share who are unfavorable — of -7. The Republican Party's net favorability is -20 (38% favorable vs. 58% unfavorable)
- 50% think the Republican Party is too extreme and 43% think the Democratic Party is. 42% say only the Republican Party is too extreme, 35% only the Democratic Party, 8% both, and 6% neither
- 28% like Donald Trump as a person a lot or somewhat, the lowest share in the 22 Economist/YouGov polls that have asked since his second term began; 55% dislike him somewhat or a lot. 33% of Republicans like him a lot, down from 40% in October and 55% in late January 2025. 2% of Democrats like him a lot or somewhat, compared with 3% in October and 8% in late January 2025
- 23% think, in hindsight, that the decision to go to war in Iran was the right decision, the smallest share in the eight polls that have asked since April, and 60% the wrong decision. 47% of Republicans say it was the right decision, down from 69% in April and 60% in October; 4% of Democrats say so, the same as in April

Methodology: The poll was conducted among 3,166 registered voters. Respondents were selected from YouGov's opt-in panel to be representative of U.S. adult citizens. A random sample (stratified by gender, age, race, education, geographic region, and voter registration) was selected from the 2019 American Community Survey. The sample was weighted according to gender, age, race, education, U.S. region, 2024 presidential vote, 2020 election turnout and presidential vote, baseline party identification, and current voter registration status. 2024 presidential vote, at time of weighting, was estimated to be 48% Harris and 50% Trump. Demographic weighting targets come from the 2019 American Community Survey. Baseline party identification is the respondent's most recent answer given around November 8, 2024, and is weighted to the estimated distribution at that time (31% Democratic, 33% Republican). The margin of error is approximately 2.2%.

DOJ tells California registrars to quarantine ballots within 48 hours

In the latest escalation of the Department of Justice’s efforts to block California from counting some ballots, prosecutors sent individual letters to numerous county registrars Friday demanding that they sequester ballots returned by voters flagged as possibly lacking U.S. citizenship.
Officials in four California counties confirmed to the Chronicle that they received a letter.
In two letters obtained by the Chronicle, the Justice Department’s top Northern California prosecutor on Friday gave the registrars in Contra Costa County and Santa Clara counties 48 hours to pore through lists totaling more than 1,300 voters and confirm their citizenship.

In an email to members of California Association of Clerks and Election Officials Friday, Los Angeles County Registrar Dean Logan said the letters were sent to “some or all” counties.
The demand letter sent to Contra Costa Clerk-Recorder Kristin B. Connelly and Santa Clara County Registrar of Voters Matt Morales from U.S. Attorney Craig Missakian threatens legal action if the election officials do not immediately sequester ballots returned by voters that the feds said might not be U.S. citizens.
Andrea L. Russi, deputy county counsel for Contra Costa, said they have not yet determined how they will respond to the request.
“We aren’t doing anything wrong and are conducting this election in accordance with state and federal election law, as well as the Constitution of the U.S. and the Constitution of the State of California,” Connelley told the Chronicle. “Voters need to vote and not be intimidated.”

Merced County Registrar Melvin Levey said his office also got a letter from a U.S. Attorney Eric Grant on Friday that he is currently reviewing with county counsel. The letter required the review of 177 names. In a text message, Levey said his team is “moving forward” and will have “a great election conducted fairly and lawfully.” Fresno County Clerk-Registrar of Voters James A. Kus said his office also received a letter Friday.
“We are reviewing the information in coordination with the California Secretary of State and Attorney General,” Kus said in a statement.
A spokesperson for Secretary of State Shirley Weber told the Chronicle Friday the office had “no information to share.”
The demands come amid a bitter feud over federal election interference between California and the Trump administration that has escalated throughout the week. Last week, the DOJ sued California, and has asked a court to block the state from counting about 20,000 ballots it says it has identified as possibly fraudulent. A hearing on that request is scheduled for next week. Election law experts have called the lawsuit baffling and unlikely to succeed. On the heels of the lawsuit, the Department of Justice also threatened to criminally prosecute election officials who knowingly count ballots from fraudulent voters.
On Monday, the DOJ announced it had charged 28 people with voter fraud. Of the 28 charged, 23 people have been accused of voting illegally, allegedly casting 34 illegal ballots in elections dating back to 2020, including one provisional ballot.

On Wednesday, in a separate case, an Oakland-based federal judge blocked the Department of Homeland Security from carrying out operations focused on uncovering voter fraud in a number of blue states across the country. The order noted that the government’s methodology for identifying possibly fraudulent voters has been known to include errors and to wrongly ensnare U.S. citizens.
“Today’s Department of Justice letter comes just hours after the Trump Administration preemptively sued to block the election certification in Alaska if the Republican candidate for Senate loses the race,” Rep. Mark DeSaulnier, D-Antioch, whose district includes portions of Contra Costa County, said in a statement. “I am deeply concerned by the escalating weaponization of the Justice Department to interfere with the November 3rd election.”
Logan, in his email to local election officials, said that the DOJ letters to registrars include a “posture of intimidation.”
He also said it “misrepresents the facts” of the Trump administration’s own actions, “attempts to tarnish the Secretary of State and Los Angeles County” and “threatens local election officials twelve days before the election.”

Logan, who is a named defendant in the DOJ’s lawsuit against the state, noted that the letter sent to local officials notes that the secretary of state’s office was presented with a list of 20,000 suspected noncitizen voters. But Logan said only his office — not the secretary of state — received the list. Contra Costa’s letter was accompanied by a list of 407 names the U.S. attorney said represented the county’s portions of that 20,000 figure. Santa Clara’s letter included a list of 902 names.

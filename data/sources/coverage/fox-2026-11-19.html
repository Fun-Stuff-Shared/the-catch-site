Trump clears hurdle in $15 billion lawsuit against New York Times

Judge denies motion to dismiss the president’s lawsuit, which Times says has 'no merit'

By Brian Flood Fox News
Published November 19, 2026 7:02pm EST
  • Comments

President Donald Trump’s legal team is celebrating Thursday after a judge denied The New York Times’ motion to dismiss the president’s $15 billion defamation lawsuit. (Getty Images/iStock)

President Donald Trump’s legal team is celebrating Thursday after a judge denied The New York Times’ motion to dismiss the president’s $15 billion defamation lawsuit against the paper.

U.S. District Judge Steven D. Merryday allowed four counts centered on seven specific accusations of criminal conduct to move forward after Trump’s legal team argued that the Times and publisher Penguin Random House showed reckless disregard, alleging "purposeful avoidance of the truth" in a book authored by Times journalists.

"President Trump continues holding the Fake News accountable for deceiving the American people. Judge Merryday has correctly denied the motion to dismiss filed by The New York Times, Penguin Publishing and their writers as to President Trump’s powerhouse defamation lawsuit," a spokesman for Trump’s legal team told Fox News Digital.

NEW YORK TIMES LOSES DEFAMATION SUIT, ORDERED TO PAY $9.25M TO FORMER ALABAMA BASKETBALL PLAYER

A spokesman for Trump’s legal team told Fox News Digital, "President Trump continues holding the Fake News accountable for deceiving the American people." (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

"These defendants will now have to answer for intentionally and maliciously defaming President Trump," the spokesman continued. "The New York Times has a long pattern of defaming President Trump in service of its leftist political agenda, and today’s momentous ruling is a victory for honesty in America."

Judge Merryday issued a pointed critique of Susanne Craig and Russ Buettner, the authors of "Lucky Loser: How Donald Trump Squandered His Father’s Fortune and Created the Illusion of Success," for publishing definitive claims of complex tax fraud and money laundering without a formal legal opinion or forensic auditing.

Merryday observed that accusations were made against Trump and his father without charges or convictions.

"In other words, the facts alleged in the complaint create at least a plausible inference that the authors and the publishers knew that no reliable conclusion about violations of the tax laws, including especially the estate and gift tax laws, is possible without reliance on qualified opinion formulated after the necessary factual and legal research and analysis, which they knowingly failed to obtain," Merryday wrote.

TRUMP TOUTS LEGAL VICTORY OVER BBC AS JUDGE SAYS HIS LAWSUIT AGAINST BRITISH BROADCASTER CAN PROCEED

President Donald Trump’s legal team argued The New York Times and Penguin Publishing showed reckless disregard. (Kent Nishimura/AFP via Getty Images)

The court dismissed the Times’ reliance on outside commentators, such as a former prosecutor saying a Trump transaction "smells like a crime" and a professor who called the action "suspicious."

"Obviously, even a qualified expert offering a superficial characterization such as a transaction is ‘suspicious’ or ‘could’ violate the law or attributing to a transaction the ‘smell’ of crime or noting that a gift tax return can evade IRS scrutiny or generously giving his immensely valuable time to aid laypersons ‘lost in the weeds’ is far from a formal legal opinion sufficient to remove serious doubt about the truthfulness and accuracy of a statement accusing a specific person of fraud or tax evasion or some related crime arising from a specific transaction," Merryday wrote.

While Trump’s lawsuit can move forward, Merryday also dismissed two counts and threw out 27 other challenged statements as non-actionable under the strict First Amendment protections of New York Times Co. v. Sullivan and denied Trump's request to amend his complaint a third time.

TRUMP DOJ WITHDRAWS SUBPOENAS TARGETING NEW YORK TIMES JOURNALISTS AMID LEAK PROBE

The New York Times told Fox News Digital the lawsuit has "no legal merit."  (Mario Tama/Getty Images)

A New York Times spokesperson provided the following statement to Fox News Digital: "The Times is encouraged to see Judge Merryday’s dismissal of almost all claims against us. We continue to believe the remaining claim in the filing has no legal merit. We look forward to making this argument as we continue to defend ourselves against this baseless attack on The Times and the rights of the free and independent press in this country."

The judge ordered The New York Times to file a formal answer within 28 days.

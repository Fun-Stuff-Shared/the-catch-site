Preliminary data show the spoiled absentee ballot rate rises in North Carolina

ByMichael Perchick
Wednesday, October 21, 2026 9:52PM
Preliminary data from the State Board of Elections show that the percentage of absentee ballots being spoiled has increased compared with the same point in 2022.

RALEIGH, N.C. (WTVD) -- As early voting turnout surges across North Carolina, preliminary data from the State Board of Elections show that the percentage of absentee ballots being spoiled has increased compared with the same point in 2022.

Through Tuesday, 924,160 votes had been cast statewide, with the total turnout up more than 38% compared with the same period four years ago. Wake County continues to lead the state in early voting turnout.

However, the number of voters casting absentee ballots has decreased compared with the same period in 2022. For those using absentee voting, preliminary data shared by the State Board of Elections shows 3.1% of absentee ballots have been spoiled, compared with 2.6% at the same point in 2022.

"That is a statistically significant increase. And to really hammer home for voters, this is a state where statewide elections can be decided by hundreds of votes out of millions," said Brooks Fuller, Policy director for Common Cause North Carolina, a Raleigh-based nonprofit.

The increase comes as voters face new requirements surrounding how absentee ballots must be submitted and who can return a ballot on behalf of a voter.

The State Board of Elections approved the new restrictions along a 3-2 party-line vote last month, Republicans in support and Democrats opposed.

"We sounded the alarm about this when the State Board (of Elections) put forth the proposed rules that got held up in the Rules Review process earlier in the summer. We told them that there needed to be more notice and opportunity for voters to correct these issues," Fuller said.

A federal judge upheld the restrictions earlier this week.

"The biggest thing is the internal envelope, the sealing of the internal envelope. It must be sealed, and it must be in the proper container envelope, the second envelope," said Jason Tyson, Director of External Affairs for the North Carolina State Board of Elections.

In previous years, issues such as improperly sealed envelopes could have been cured. Now, those ballots are spoiled.

"These are eligible voters whose voices are being hindered at the ballot box," said Shruti Parikh, the Director of Education and Political Engagement with North Carolina Asian Americans Together.

The Raleigh-based advocacy group joined a lawsuit challenging the new limitations, which a federal judge upheld earlier this week.

"There's different reasons why a ballot can be spoiled. They're worried about chain of custody issue like this ballot was tampered with or this ballot looks like there was something that happened to it on the way from the absentee voter to the Board of Elections," Tyson said.

Another change this year: the list of people who can drop off an absentee ballot for most voters is also limited to family members, though voters with disabilities have greater latitude.

"My parents mistakenly thought that their neighbor could drop it off since they were coming to vote early. And their ballot was subsequently spoiled. In the past, their ballot would have been curable. But this year, with the new rules, it's spoiled," Parikh said.

County boards of elections are tasked with notifying voters when their ballots are spoiled and explaining how they can receive a new ballot or outlining other options to vote.

"A family member of mine was going to put in an absentee ballot in the mail and then go off to Nepal for a vacation. There is no way he's going to receive word if his absentee ballot is not counted correctly," Fuller said.

The State Board of Elections is encouraging absentee voters to use BallotTrax to monitor the status of their ballots and has posted instructions online for voters with ballot issues.

"If (a voter) feels like they didn't do something properly after they mailed it in, they can contact their county board and say, 'Hey, I don't feel like I did this right. What are the steps I need to take,'" Tyson said.

Tyson further flagged detailed guidance sent to county boards of elections, including Numbered Memo 2020-23.

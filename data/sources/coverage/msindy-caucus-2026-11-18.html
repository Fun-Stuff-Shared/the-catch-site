Mississippi Black Caucus calls for investigation into ‘voter roll errors’

Derrion Arrington
Nov 18, 2026

The Mississippi Legislative Black Caucus asked Thursday for an independent investigation of the Secretary of State’s office over the more than 230,000 voter registrations listed as inactive before the Nov. 3 election, saying nobody has accounted for how many were marked that way in error.

Every legislative seat and every statewide office is on the ballot in 2027. The caucus wants public hearings, preserved records and answers from Secretary of State Michael Watson and county officials before voters go back to the polls.

“Mississippians are still asking, did my vote count,” said Rep. Kabir Karriem of Columbus, the caucus chairman, at a news conference in the second floor rotunda of the Capitol. “They deserve an answer on the record.”

Kayesha Smith said she trained a team of canvassers in Holmes County and registered close to 300 people. On Election Day she drove voters to the polls in Durant, then drove to Lexington to vote herself with ten minutes left. Her name was not on the roll. She went to a second building and her name was not on the roll there either, she said, so she cast an affidavit ballot and followed up afterward.

Election commissioners told her the ballot would not count, Smith said, because an address in San Antonio had come up on her record. She said she pulled her Experian report and found the Texas address listed, an entry she had disputed and had removed.

“I’ve never lived in any part of Texas a day in my life,” Smith said. “But because of this inaccuracy, my right to vote was taken away.”

Watson announced a partnership with Experian in 2025 that gave county officials access to credit bureau and Social Security data to check voter addresses. The caucus wants to know who approved the use of that data, what counties were told to do with it, and what the office did after civil rights groups warned that eligible voters were being marked inactive.

“We need to see the contracts involving our voter data, the instructions that were sent to our counties, and the warnings received by the Secretary of State’s office,” said Sen. Bradford Blackmon of District 21. “More importantly, we need to know what actions were taken in response to those warnings.”

Blackmon also asked whether citizenship checks under the SHIELD Act flagged eligible Mississippians and whether anyone was kept from voting because of them.

Watson has defended the work. In a Nov. 16 statement from his office, he said federal and state law require voter roll maintenance and that “not a single voter was made inactive or removed from the voter rolls without review by a local election official.” He said the 230,000 figure is “the total number of people, many of whom have died or moved, marked inactive by local elections officials during more than five years of voter roll maintenance,” and that a voter moved to inactive status “can cast an affidavit ballot that then gets processed.” He put the statewide numbers at 21,484 affidavit ballots cast in 2026, close to the 21,321 cast in 2024, with about 75 percent approved and counted.

The caucus does not have its own count of rejected affidavit ballots. Rep. Zakiya Summers of Jackson said advocacy groups in the coalition are filing public records requests to get the county figures, and that the lack of a number is part of the reason for the investigation.

The request goes to the legislature’s Investigate State Offices committees, and Summers said it takes a joint decision by the committees in both chambers to open one. Asked whether hearings could happen before the session convenes in January, she said it was unlikely.

“Do I think that it’s likely that it will happen prior to session? Probably not, because we are going into the holiday season,” Summers said. “But the chair would have the ability to make that decision.”

Summers said the caucus holds Watson responsible for how the election was administered at the county level.

“It all starts and ends with leadership. The Secretary of State is the chief elections official in the state of Mississippi,” she said. “It’s his responsibility to be training all local elected officials so that they can then train the poll workers effectively and efficiently.”

Rep. Timaka James-Jones, whose District 51 takes in Holmes County along with Humphreys, Sharkey, Yazoo and Leflore, pointed to House Bill 788, which she authored and which passed the House this year. The bill amends Section 23-15-152 to return a voter on inactive status to active status when that voter casts an affidavit ballot, and it lets a valid affidavit ballot serve as a written request to transfer a registration to a new precinct.

“No eligible Mississippi voter should have to fight for the opportunity to cast a ballot, stand in a polling place confused about their registration status, or even leave without knowing what steps that they can take to ensure that their vote counts,” James-Jones said.

The caucus said it is preparing an election package for the session that includes online voter registration, early voting, hearings for absentee ballots before they are rejected, changes to the roll maintenance laws that allow third party data to be used against registrations, and the Robert G. Clark, Jr., Voting Rights Act of Mississippi, a state level voting rights bill that went to the House Apportionment and Elections Committee in the last session.

The Rev. C.J. Rhodes, who worked with the caucus on its Operation Turnout voter drive, told faith leaders and students to “educate, agitate, and activate” ahead of 2027.

Voters who had trouble at the polls can file an account at msblackcaucus.com under “Share Your Voting Experience” or leave a message on the caucus hotline at 769-289-0992.

“Your experience is evidence,” Summers said.

Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO after Trump pushes board

President Donald Trump called the technology executive a “national treasure” and argued that the U.S. economy needs him.

Nov. 23, 2026, 8:59 AM EST / Updated Nov. 23, 2026, 4:34 PM EST
By Steve Kopack, Daniel Arkin and Jared Perlo

Jensen Huang will return as Nvidia’s president and CEO, the company’s board announced Monday, in a stunning reversal for an executive who resigned two weeks ago following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence believes to be a Chinese spy.

The move is effective immediately, the chip giant said in a statement. The company said the board’s unanimous decision to reinstate Huang came after “input from senior levels of the United States government.”

President Donald Trump cheered news of Huang’s return in an interview with CNBC and confirmed that he personally intervened to make it happen, speaking “directly and indirectly” with the company’s board — a highly unusual step for an American president. He said he believed the executive “did nothing wrong” and “there’s nothing wrong with a comeback.”

In a previous social media post, Trump said the federal government had “no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.”

Nvidia’s stock soared 31% on the news, buoying the market after a technology sell-off Friday that seemed to confirm anxieties about a bubble in the AI sector. Nvidia’s advanced computer chips power key AI firms’ models, and the company has helped finance the country’s massive investment in new data centers.

Huang said in a statement of his own that he was “honored to return to NVIDIA.”

Huang has established himself in recent years as the unofficial ambassador for the AI boom, the companies backing it and the trillions of dollars being poured into the sector. Nvidia employees greeted him with loud cheers at the company’s corporate headquarters in Santa Clara, California, on Monday, videos posted online showed.

“I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support,” Huang said in his statement.

The reinstatement was all the more dramatic given that Nvidia’s newly installed chairman, Harvey Jones, effectively dismissed this possibility last week. “The Nvidia Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Jones said in a statement on Nov. 17.

In an interview the same day, Nvidia’s interim chief executive Collette Kress also said that the company would not reinstall Huang.

Huang’s removal from Nvidia came after The New York Times reported that Huang had a close relationship with Alice Huang, a woman who U.S. intelligence agencies believed was a Chinese spy. The Financial Times followed that report, saying that Huang had an affair with the woman.

In a memo to Nvidia’s workforce Nov. 8, Huang said, “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.”

The New York Times then reported that Huang had an extramarital relationship with a senior leader at Nvidia who reported directly to him.

The company’s board launched a search for a new leader, reportedly seeking out Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas. However, Haas earlier Monday announced that he was remaining at Arm, with a contract extension through 2033.

Nvidia also set up a new “national security and export control oversight” panel in light of the revelations about Huang.

Trump, in his social media post, said Attorney General Todd Blanche “has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

Blanche spoke with Trump over the weekend and told him that the Justice Department had no evidence of any wrongdoing by either Huang or Nvidia, a Justice Department official told NBC News.

Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, who serves on the Senate Foreign Relations and Intelligence committees, expressed skepticism about Trump’s assurances. “No national security risk? Based on what?” Cornyn wrote in a post on X.

The company could still face legal exposure on other fronts despite Trump’s statement. The Wall Street Journal and Reuters both reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is probing the company. The Journal said the inquiry was focused on the firm’s accounting practices.

The SEC declined to comment to NBC News on Friday. Nvidia did not return a request for comment on the reports.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta’s office confirmed Monday it would continue probing Nvidia.

“The reinstatement of Jensen Huang does not change the fact that the American people deserve to know what happened here — whether Nvidia misled investors and the public,” Bonta’s office said.

Huang’s return likewise “changes nothing” for the three House committees that are set to conduct a closed-door interview with Huang on Dec. 1, a spokesperson for the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence told NBC News.

Huang is scheduled to appear before the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, the House Foreign Affairs Committee and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party. Nvidia’s general counsel is also set to appear Dec. 2.

The following day, Huang is scheduled to appear at a public hearing before the Senate Banking Committee on “Protecting America’s AI Advantage.”

Huang can expect to face tough questions there from some of his public detractors, and the hearing will be closely watched. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had initially been scheduled to testify alongside Huang, but a Monday notice from the committee listed Huang as the only witness.

Lutnick, reached by phone Monday, told NBC News: “Nice to speak to you. See you.” He then hung up.

The ranking Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee, Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., said in a statement: “The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company’s board still owe Congress answers about the company’s compliance with U.S. export controls.”

Warren also called for more transparency into how Trump’s input may have played into Nvidia’s decision to bring Huang back.

Huang had received an outpouring of support from prominent Silicon Valley tech leaders and investors, many of whom clamored for his return.

Brad Gerstner, CEO of Altimeter Capital Management, which has a significant stake in Nvidia, wrote in a letter last week co-authored with Atreides Management managing partner Gavin Baker that Nvidia’s board should keep all options on the table, including potentially asking Huang to return.

Yale School of Management professor Jeff Sonnenfeld said the White House’s involvement in Huang’s reinstatement raises serious questions about corporate governance and conflicts of interest, creating “total turmoil and confusion over integrity.”

“It looks like a serious erosion of capitalism,” Sonnenfeld said.

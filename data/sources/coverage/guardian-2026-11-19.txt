US ⁠judge allows Trump’s $15bn lawsuit against New York Times to proceed | Donald Trump | The Guardian

A federal judge in Florida on Thursday said Donald Trump’s $15bn defamation lawsuit against the New York Times and book publisher Penguin Random House may proceed, a victory for the US president in his legal campaign against media companies he accuses of treating him unfairly.
US district judge Steven Merryday in Tampa dismissed two claims in the suit while allowing core defamation claims to move forward.
The president’s claim against the publishers alleges defamation by three New York Times reporters – Susanne Craig, Russ Buettner and Peter Baker – in two articles published by the outlet and a 2024 book Lucky Loser: How Donald Trump Squandered His Father’s Fortune and Created the Illusion of Success, which was co-authored by Craig and Buettner.
Trump claims the articles and book contain false statements that damaged his businesses and personal reputation. An earlier, 80-page version of the complaint was thrown out because it did not contain a concise summary and was filled with “vituperation and invective”, the court said.
An amended, 40-page complaint was filed in October last year that removed references to the 2024 presidential election. Mediation talks failed, along with a request by the defendants to move the action to federal court in New York.
The Times and Penguin have denied defaming Trump and said in a court filing that his case is legally flawed and aimed at scoring political points.
In dismissing two of Trump’s claims, Merryday, an appointee of George HW Bush, said the rest of his complaint plausibly alleged that statements accusing Trump of “planning and perpetrating complex crimes” were “based on incomplete, minimal information”.
In his claim, Trump cited 22 statements from a book by Baker and 12 from the articles by Craig and Buettner. They include assertions about Trump’s role in creating his hit reality TV show The Apprentice, his inheritance from his father, his business ventures, and his upbringing in New York.
Representatives for the New York Times and Penguin Random House did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
Charlie Stadtlander, a spokesperson for the Times, has previously said “the lawsuit remains obviously meritless”. A spokesperson for Penguin Random House has also described the action as “meritless” and said it “stands by the book and its authors”.

Trump defends Commerce boss Lutnick over Nvidia-Megaspeed controversy: ‘Could have had a blowup with China’

WASHINGTON — President Trump said Monday that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick held off on blacklisting a suspected Nvidia chip smuggler to keep good relations with Beijing.
“He might have delayed it, but he was doing that because if you didn’t delay it, if you didn’t do something, you could have had a blowup with China,” Trump told reporters on the South Lawn before leaving for Asia.
“We’re having a very good relationship with China right now, and I understand both sides of it,” he added.

Trump also shrugged off an ongoing feud between Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent as “two different views” over allowing Beijing access to sensitive AI technology and said he has no plans to replace the former Cantor Fitzgerald boss.
Bessent publicly blasted Commerce and Lutnick Friday for the delay in blacklisting Singapore-based Megaspeed despite fears it was smuggling advanced AI chips to China.
“Commerce … has the pen. You would have to ask them about their internal processes,” Bessent said during an interview on CNBC’s “Squawk Box.”

Lutnick’s department has not placed a single company on its so-called “Entity List” in more than a year — the longest period without such action in 18 years, according to the Financial Times.
Trump approved action against chip diversion three times this year, according to Axios, including after a Nov. 5 Oval Office meeting, only for Lutnick to stall each time.
A Commerce probe into Megaspeed was handed to the DOJ late last year, while Nvidia said it cut off chip sales to the firm in June 2025.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang stepped down Friday after reports emerged that he’d had an affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom US intelligence believes to be a Chinese spy.
Trump questioned that assessment Monday morning, saying “I don’t know that she’s a Chinese spy” and adding that he “would not mind” Jensen Huang returning as CEO.

Lutnick and Bessent are both traveling with Trump to the Philippines and China, where Trump is scheduled to meet Thursday with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

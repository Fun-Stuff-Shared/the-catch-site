Trump administration sues to block Senate race certification in Alaska if Republican loses

By Jan Wolfe
October 23, 20262:58 PM UTCUpdated ago
Item 1 of 3 Republican US Senator Dan Sullivan and Democratic US Senate candidate Mary Peltola participate in a debate in Anchorage, Alaska, US, October 15, 2026. REUTERS/Kerry Tasker/File Photo

Oct 23 (Reuters) - President Donald Trump's administration on Thursday sued to ​block Alaska's US Senate race certification if the Republican ‌party loses, citing candidate name confusion.
The lawsuit, filed in federal court in Anchorage, said Alaska "will fail in its obligation to protect ​the integrity of its election" if it certifies "any electoral ​results tainted by the inclusion on the ballot ⁠of Daniel J. Sullivan," a Senate candidate with the ​same name as Republican incumbent Senator Dan Sullivan.

The unelected ​Sullivan, a former US Forest Service worker and elementary school teacher, filed to run as a candidate on May 29 seeking to unseat ​Senator Dan S. Sullivan and "put Alaska first," according to ​his campaign website, which advertised "Dan Sullivan Challenges Dan Sullivan for US ‌Senate ⁠Seat."
Dan J. Sullivan has denied in interviews with US news outlets that his aim is to cause confusion or divert votes from Dan S. Sullivan.
Polling has shown ​Democratic candidate Mary ​Peltola tied ⁠or slightly ahead of incumbent Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in a state the president carried ​by some 14 percentage points in 2024. ​In Alaska, ⁠as in much of the US, Trump's approval rating is underwater, hit by rising diesel and gasoline prices and ⁠a conflict ​in the Middle East that ​has proven deeply unpopular with voters.

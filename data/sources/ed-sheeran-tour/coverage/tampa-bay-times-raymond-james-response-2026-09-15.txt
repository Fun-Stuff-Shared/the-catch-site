Tampa didn't seek to kick Macklemore off RayJay show, board member says

Tampa Sports Authority board members said they knew little of the decision to remove the rapper Macklemore from Ed Sheeran's U.S. tour, slated to end at Raymond James Stadium on Nov. 7.

Several members of the Tampa Sports Authority, the public agency that manages Raymond James, on Tuesday told the Tampa Bay Times that they knew little of the decision. One member, Tampa City Council chairperson Alan Clendenin, said the change "doesn't have anything to do with the Tampa Sports Authority."

"The Tampa Sports Authority's position hasn't changed: We don't get involved in freedom of speech issues," Clendenin said. "We are a public facility and we're doing business as the Tampa Sports Authority. This had to do with certain owners that are not in the city of Tampa taking a stance, and they are discussing with the artists involved. This is between other stadiums, the artists, and the artists' promoters."

Board members Patrick Manteiga and Joe Robinson directed questions about artists and their contracts to the authority's staff. Eric Hart, the chief executive officer, did not immediately respond to a call seeking comment. The Times asked the authority for Sheeran's contract, but the agency said no records are available.

Robinson referenced Robert Kraft, whose company owns the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium in Massachusetts, who confirmed in a statement that he barred Macklemore from performing at his venue. Gillette Stadium is privately owned, Robinson said, "so Kraft can tell you what he wants." As a public stadium, he said, Raymond James must "follow the laws on the books."

Application of the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide
(The Gambia v. Myanmar: 11 States intervening)
The Court delivers its Judgment on the merits
THE HAGUE, 19 November 2026. The International Court of Justice today delivered its
Judgment on the merits in the case concerning Application of the Convention on the Prevention and
Punishment of the Crime of Genocide (The Gambia v. Myanmar: 11 States intervening). The Court
found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine State
during the 2017 “clearance operations”, in violation of its obligations under the Convention on the
Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide (the “Convention”).
The Court also found that Myanmar violated its obligations under the Convention to prevent
the commission of genocide by individuals acting alongside its security forces, to punish genocide
and other acts enumerated in the Convention, and to enact the necessary legislation to give effect to
the Convention. However, the Court found that Myanmar did not directly and publicly incite the
commission of genocide by private persons against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine State.
The Court further found that Myanmar violated its obligation under the Court’s Order of
23 January 2020 to take effective measures to prevent the destruction and ensure the preservation of
evidence related to allegations of acts of genocide.
As a consequence of these breaches of the Convention, the Court ordered Myanmar: to cease
any ongoing violations by enacting legislation giving full effect to its obligations under the
Convention; to take immediate and effective steps to investigate all persons reasonably suspected of
having committed acts contrary to the Convention and, if warranted, to prosecute and punish them;
to provide assurances and guarantees of non-repetition and prevent similar violations from occurring
in the future; to adopt measures of restitution in the interest of the Rohingya, including allowing the
safe, voluntary and dignified return to their place of residence and reunification of families for the
Rohingya who were displaced as a result of the Respondent’s wrongful acts, whether they were
displaced within Myanmar or abroad; and to compensate the Rohingya group and its members for
the damage caused.
Finally, the Court decided that the question of the amount and the modalities of compensation
or other reparation should be determined in a subsequent phase of the proceedings, and that, to that
end, Myanmar should transmit to it, within two years of today’s Judgment, a report on the measures
of restitution it has adopted and the amount and modalities of compensation.

-2History of the proceedings
On 11 November 2019, The Gambia filed an Application instituting proceedings against
Myanmar concerning alleged violations of the Genocide Convention.
On 23 January 2020, the Court made an Order indicating certain provisional measures
addressed to Myanmar.
On 22 July 2022, the Court issued its Judgment on preliminary objections raised by Myanmar,
in which it found that it had jurisdiction, on the basis of Article IX of the Genocide Convention, to
entertain The Gambia’s Application and that the Application was admissible.
By two Orders dated 3 July 2024 and 25 July 2025 respectively, the Court decided that the
Declarations of intervention under Article 63 of the Statute submitted by 11 States were admissible
in so far as they concerned the construction of provisions of the Genocide Convention.
The written proceedings in the case, including the written observations of the intervening
States, concluded on 25 September 2025. Public hearings on the merits were held from 12 to
29 January 2026. The final submissions of the Parties can be found on the website of the Court in
verbatim records 2026/20 and 2026/23, respectively.

-6The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations.
It was established by the United Nations Charter in June 1945 and began its activities in April 1946.
The Court is composed of 15 judges elected for a nine-year term by the General Assembly and the
Security Council of the United Nations. The seat of the Court is at the Peace Palace in The Hague
(Kingdom of the Netherlands). The Court has a twofold role: first, to settle, in accordance with
international law, legal disputes submitted to it by States; and, second, to give advisory opinions on
legal questions referred to it by duly authorized United Nations organs and specialized agencies.

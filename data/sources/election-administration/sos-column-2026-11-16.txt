Mississippi's Election Integrity Held Strong | MS SOS

Mississippi just conducted its midterm election, and I’m proud to say our election system held strong. Our climb from 26th to 11th in the country regarding election integrity policies, earned through stronger laws and cleaner rolls, is exactly why I have confidence in Mississippi's process, the local officials, and the poll workers who run elections across our 82 counties.
Maintaining accurate voter lists ensures the integrity of our elections and prevents voter fraud. Voter list maintenance, one piece of the process that has received attention, is not optional. Both federal and state laws require voter roll maintenance, and Mississippi law assigns that work to our election commissioners. My office is responsible for providing those officials with the resources needed to do their jobs well.
The U.S. Election Assistance Commission (EAC) was also created to help election officials. Under the Biden administration, the EAC ran a pilot study with Experian, and due to its success, the Trump administration has continued to encourage jurisdictions to use credit bureau information as one data point.
Experian pitched itself for voter roll maintenance, published successful case studies, and partnered with the EAC. Their success with the EAC and Mississippi led to a contract with Experian for their statewide voter roll maintenance data to provide additional resources to help local election officials.
Beginning in January 2021, we encouraged our local election officials to better maintain their voter rolls. At the time, seven counties had more than 100 percent of the voting-eligible population on their voter rolls. That means these counties' voter rolls included more eligible voters than actually lived in the county! This is a recipe for potential voter fraud.
To ensure accurate voter rolls, Mississippi followed EAC guidance and repeatedly provided data to local officials. The data distributed to them in 2025, which included U.S. Postal Service change of address information and commercial data, flagged approximately 113,000 individuals for their review. To be clear, not a single voter was made inactive or removed from the voter rolls without review by a local election official.
There have been some false reports alleging that 230,000 voters were taken off the voter rolls at one time. This claim, repeated by Mississippi Today and others, is an egregious lie. That was the total number of people — many of whom have died or moved — marked inactive by local elections officials during more than five years of voter roll maintenance.
Thankfully, Mississippi law built in a safety net to address errors. A voter moved to inactive status can cast an affidavit ballot that then gets processed and, if the voter is a qualified elector of Mississippi, it will be counted. Additionally, the voter's registration status is automatically updated to active.
The affidavit ballot numbers tell the real story. In 2026, Mississippi voters cast 21,484 affidavit ballots, nearly identical to the 21,321 cast in 2024. The process worked exactly as it has in prior elections, and the volume reflects a normal, high-engagement election, not a breakdown in the system.
It’s important to emphasize that after all 2026 affidavit ballots were reviewed by local election officials, approximately 75% of those votes were approved and counted. That also means several thousand improper votes were caught by the system and not counted—and as a result, the integrity of legitimate votes was protected.
As to this election, I am confident our Circuit Clerks, Election Commissioners, Poll Workers, Resolution Boards and the team here at the Mississippi Secretary of State’s office did their jobs to the best of their ability, which brings an election outcome the voters of Mississippi can trust. The process worked, and the law was followed. Mississippians elected a new Senator and re-elected all four Congressmen. May we all pray for them, and all of our elected officials, as they serve our state and country.
MICHAEL WATSON
Secretary of State
State of Mississippi

 Secretary of State David Scanlan and Attorney General John Formella Provide
Voters with Important Update on the Use of Student ID Cards Ahead of General
                                   Election

Following an October 22 stay issued by the U.S. Supreme Court, student Ids cannot be used to
prove identity when registering to vote, voting by absentee, or checking in to vote on election
day.

CONCORD, NH (October 23, 2026): On October 22, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court stayed the
District Court’s October 2 injunction in a case challenging HB 323, which the legislature enacted
to prohibit the use of student ID cards when registering to vote and voting.

The Supreme Court’s decision means that registration and identity requirements for the
November 3 State General Election revert to the same as those that were in effect during the
September 8 State Primary Election.

Accordingly, effective immediately, student ID cards cannot be used to prove identity when
registering to vote, voting by absentee ballot, or when checking in to vote on election day.

To assist student voters with navigating voter registration and voter identification requirements,
the Secretary of State's Office has produced a "Student Guide to Voting in NH" and a "Student
Guide to Registering to Vote". The Attorney General's and Secretary of State's Offices encourage
people to share these guides with any eligible voters they know who are students.

Additionally, the Secretary of State's Office will immediately resume radio public service
announcements and begin running television PSAs informing the public that student ID cards
cannot be used to prove identity.

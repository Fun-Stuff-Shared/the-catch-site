                                  16   III.      MOTION FOR PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

                                  17             A.      Legal Standard

                                  18             A “preliminary injunction is an extraordinary and drastic remedy” which should not be

                                  19   granted unless the movant shows “substantial proof” and, “by a clear showing carries the burden

                                  20   of persuasion.” Mazurek v. Armstrong, 520 U.S. 968, 972 (1997) (emphasis in original; quotation

                                  21   marks and citation omitted). The scope of “injunctive relief should be no more burdensome to the

                                  22   defendant than necessary to provide complete relief to the plaintiffs.” Califano v. Yamasaki, 442

                                  23   U.S. 682, 702 (1979); see also Trump v. CASA, Inc., 606 U.S. 831, 847 (2025).

                                  24             To obtain preliminary injunctive relief, the moving party must show: (1) a likelihood of

                                  25   success on the merits, (2) a likelihood of irreparable harm to the moving party in the absence of

                                  26   preliminary relief, (3) the balance of equities tips in the favor of the moving party, and (4) an

                                  27   injunction is in the public interest. Winter v. Natural Res. Def. Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7, 20

                                  28   (2008). The Winter factors may be evaluated on a sliding scale such that preliminary relief may be

                                   3   Cottrell, 632 F.3d 1127, 1134-35 (9th Cir. 2011) (citation omitted). To grant preliminary

                                   6          B.      Likelihood of Success on the Merits

                                   7          Because it “ ‘is a threshold inquiry’ and the most important factor,” Baird v. Bonta, 81

                                   8   F.4th 1036, 1040 (9th Cir. 2023), the Court begins by examining Plaintiffs’ likelihood of success

                                   9   on the merits. In addition to a bevy of arguments on the legal merits, Defendants also raise

                                  12                  1.      Standing



 United States District Court
                                  13          “Article III confines the federal judicial power to the resolution of ‘Cases’ and

                                  14   ‘Controversies.’ For there to be a case or controversy under Article III, the plaintiff must have a

                                  15   ‘personal stake’ in the case – in other words, standing.” TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez, 594 U.S.



Northern District of California
                                  16   413, 423 (2021) (citation omitted). To have standing, Article III requires that “[t]he plaintiff must

                                  17   have (1) suffered an injury in fact, (2) that is fairly traceable to the challenged conduct of the

                                  18   defendant, and (3) that is likely to be redressed by a favorable judicial decision.” Spokeo, Inc. v.

                                  19   Robins, 578 U.S. 330, 330 (2016) (citing Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555, 560

                                  20   (1992)). “To establish an injury in fact, a plaintiff must show that [they] suffered ‘an invasion of a

                                  21   legally protected interest’ that is ‘concrete and particularized.’ ” In re Facebook, Inc. Internet

                                  22   Tracking Litig., 956 F.3d 589, 597 (9th Cir. 2020) (quoting Spokeo, 578 U.S. at 339). “The party

                                  23   invoking federal jurisdiction bears the burden of establishing these elements.” Lujan, 504 U.S. at

                                  24   561. Because “standing is not dispensed in gross, . . . plaintiffs must demonstrate standing for

                                  25   each claim that they press and for each form of relief that they seek.” TransUnion, 594 U.S. at

                                  26   431. At the preliminary-injunction stage, a plaintiff must make a “clear showing” that it is likely

                                  27   to establish each element of standing. Ariz. All. for Retired Ams. v. Mayes, 186 F.4th 622, 631

                                  28   (9th Cir. 2026) (en banc).

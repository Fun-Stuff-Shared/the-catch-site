Federal Court Sets October 30 Hearing in CHIRLA and Trujillo v. DHS, a Challenge to Federal Actions Targeting California Voters

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
UCLA VRP: Alberto Lammers, alberto@uclavrp.org

Federal Court Sets October 30 Hearing in CHIRLA and Trujillo v. DHS, a Challenge to Federal Actions Targeting California Voters

WASHINGTON (October 21, 2026) — A federal judge today scheduled an October 30 hearing on a request for emergency relief in a lawsuit brought by the Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights (CHIRLA) and California voter Pedro Trujillo, represented by the CHIRLA, UCLA Voting Rights Project (VRP), and VRP’s Senior Advisor Xaiver Becerra, challenging federal actions targeting California voters identified as alleged noncitizens.

Chief Judge James E. Boasberg of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ordered the plaintiffs to file a superseding motion for a temporary restraining order by October 22. The government’s opposition is due October 26, followed by the plaintiffs’ reply on October 28.

The hearing is scheduled for Friday, October 30, at 10:30 a.m. ET / 7:30 a.m. PT and the counsels will be appearing in person.

The UCLA VRP plaintiffs have sought to stop federal demands and threats aimed at requiring California election officials to remove registered voters, withhold ballots, or segregate and ultimately not count ballots based on federal lists of alleged noncitizens. The superseding motion will set out the request the court will consider under the new schedule.

In a supplemental brief filed today, the plaintiffs described recent federal actions, including the DOJ’s separate lawsuit against California, threats of criminal prosecution against election officials, and Monday’s announcement of criminal charges against 28 lawful permanent residents.

The brief also raises concerns about the accuracy of the federal voter list, citing a senior DOJ official’s acknowledgment that some people listed may ultimately be citizens and reporting that some appear to have been born in the United States.

“Individual allegations must be evaluated on their evidence and through due process,” said Chad Dunn, legal director of the UCLA Voting Rights Project. “They do not justify treating thousands of registered voters as ineligible. We are asking the court to protect eligible voters from being wrongly targeted while voting is already underway.”

The lawsuit, filed September 2, challenges federal database-matching efforts and demands that state election officials act on the resulting lists. The plaintiffs argue that these actions exceed federal authority and violate constitutional protections and federal law.

Today’s order sets a briefing schedule and hearing. The court has not granted or denied the requested temporary restraining order.

“The events of the last few days, from threats to state and local officials, increased general immigration operations, the random knocking on doors by federal agents and the recent arrests are causing a chill in the community. People, including naturalized citizens, are making their plans to vote and the Federal Government is doing its utmost to quash their attempts to exercise this fundamental right that they have lawfully acquired. We look forward to bringing all this to the court’s attention next week,” stated Carl Bergquist, General Counsel, Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights (CHIRLA).

Background

The lawsuit, filed September 2 by CHIRLA and California voter Pedro Trujillo, represented by the CHIRLA and UCLA Voting Rights Project attorney, challenges federal database-matching efforts that plaintiffs argue risk wrongly identifying naturalized citizens as noncitizens and interfering with California’s elections. 

The dispute escalated last week after the DOJ gave California officials 48 hours to review a list of more than 20,000 alleged noncitizen registrants and demanded that officials withhold or segregate ballots from people whose citizenship they could not independently confirm. 

On October 15, the plaintiffs filed an emergency application for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction. That same day, DOJ filed a separate lawsuit against California, Secretary of State Shirley Weber, and Los Angeles County elections chief Dean Logan seeking to prevent ballots from listed voters from being counted until their citizenship was verified. Federal officials subsequently warned of potential criminal consequences for election officials who knowingly processed ballots from noncitizens. The plaintiffs argue that these demands threaten eligible voters and violate federal authority limits and restrictions on systematic voter removals within 90 days of an election.

ABOUT the UCLA Voting Rights Project

The UCLA Voting Rights Project was established to protect equal voting rights for all Americans through strategic litigation, social science research and policy advocacy. The VRP seeks to ensure that all individuals are afforded equal access to the electoral process and representation in governance. For more information about the UCLA Voting Rights Project, please visit vrp.ucla.edu.

The emergency relief the United States seeks (see Dkts. 22, 62) is compelled by
the Defendants’ own admissions. In their oppositions, Defendants State of California,
Secretary of State Shirley Weber, and Los Angeles County Registrar Dean Logan
contend they lack both the mechanisms and the legal authority to prevent non‑citizens
from casting ballots in the November 2026 federal election, and that their current
ballot‑processing practices render votes anonymous and permanently untraceable once
opened. See, Dkts. 31, 32. These concessions establish not only the immediacy of the
threat to the integrity of lawful votes, but also the irreversibility of the harm unless this
Court intervenes at once. As Defendants continue processing ballots that cannot later be
segregated or verified for eligibility, the dilution of lawful votes accelerates in real time.
This is an emergency. Every minute of processing that occurs without segregating
the ballots of suspected non-eligible voters causes untraceable disenfranchisement and
dilution of the votes of United States citizens.
Defendant Dean Logan admits in his opposition to the United States’ application
for temporary restraining order, “[s]ince the County began its [Vote By Mail (“VBM”)]
ballot processing operations on October 11, 2026, the County has processed more than
331,000 VBM ballots to date and anticipates much higher volumes of VBM ballots to
be received as Election Day approaches (citation omitted). Regardless of voting method,
voting is fully anonymous—even to the County itself, in compliance with California and
federal law. (citation omitted). Once a vote-by-mail ballot has been removed from its
return envelope, or an in-person ballot has been cast, there is no way to associate
the voter with that ballot. (citation omitted).” Dkt. 32-1 at 11. (Emphasis added).
Assistant Registrar-Recorder Monica Flores admits in her declaration that “[t]he
[Los Angeles County] Registrar is not equipped or authorized to verify voter
citizenship because it does not independently house citizenship-related information
beyond the voter attestation as identified in federal and state law (such as, among other
things, immigration records, travel records, naturalization records, etc.) and certainly is
not equipped to do so within the election period.” Dkt. 32-4, p. 15. (Emphasis added).

The United States does not seek to remove voters from the rolls. It simply wants to
work with State and County officials to segregate the ballots of suspected non-citizens to
ensure that only eligible votes are counted, and that United States citizens are not
disenfranchised by illegal votes.
Once an unlawful vote is processed, it cannot be undone if it is later discovered
that the voter was ineligible. See Dkt 32-1. There is no un-ringing the bell, which is
ringing as we speak. Absent a temporary restraining order, lawful votes of United States
citizens are permanently diluted. The United States respectfully requests this Court rule
on its emergency ex parte application for temporary restraining order to preserve the
status quo and prevent dilution of votes by noon on October 22, 2026.

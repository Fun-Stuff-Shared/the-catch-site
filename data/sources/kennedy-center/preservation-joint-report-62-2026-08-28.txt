                              JOINT MEET-AND-CONFER REPORT

          Pursuant to the Court’s August 7 and August 14, 2026 Orders, the parties submit this report

regarding their Local Rule 16.3(a) conference.

          As the Court is aware, the Kennedy Center’s Board of Trustees (the “Board”) voted on

August 13, 2026, to adopt a proposal that contemplates a full closure of the Center’s main building;

the maintenance of fine- and performing-arts programming activities at the REACH; support for

offsite performances by the National Symphony Orchestra; the continuation of educational

programming; the continuation of certain signature productions put on by the Center at offsite

locations; and the relocation of the memorial experience for President Kennedy to the REACH

building. See ECF 60 at 2.1 Defendants plan to move next month to dissolve the injunction in the

closely related Beatty matter insofar as it prohibits them from closing the Center, and the parties

expect that Representative Beatty will oppose that motion. See Joint Status Report, Beatty v.

Trump, No. 25-cv-04480 (D.D.C. Aug. 18, 2026), ECF 64.

        Given the centrality of the closure decision to the Board’s project plans, see, e.g., id. at 1-

2, the parties propose that the Court stay discovery and the parties’ Rule 26 and Local Rule 16.3

submissions until the Court rules on Defendants’ forthcoming motion to dissolve the injunction

entered in the Beatty case. As the Court recognized in its order denying Plaintiffs’ preliminary-

injunction motion, without a stable target—i.e., a final closure decision and settled project plans—

it is difficult, if not impossible, for the parties to litigate this case. See ECF 45 at 42. While a stable

target may come into focus if the Court dissolves the injunction and permits Defendants to go

forward with their closure of the Center, commencing discovery now into the legality of a project

plan that may never go into effect as constituted and has not yet been fully fleshed out does not

promote the efficient and economical resolution of this matter. The fact that several of Plaintiffs’

claims may turn on specific details of the ultimate project plan—for example, whether and when

permits will be required for work that might affect the NPS-administered Rock Creek Parkway,

see id. at 13—further favors commencing discovery (should any be necessary) only when

Defendants’ plans are reasonably final and certain.

        Plaintiffs note that their agreement to this proposal is based on their understanding that

(1) apart from ongoing facilities maintenance and capital improvement efforts described by Mr.

Floca in his July 14, 2026 declaration, see ECF 56 ¶¶ 4-5, no work at the Center will begin unless

and until the injunction in the Beatty matter is dissolved; and (2) if the Court grants Defendants’

forthcoming motion to dissolve the injunction, Defendants will carry out work substantially in

accordance with the plan proposed to and approved by the Center’s Board (and which is being

filed with the Court). Defendants have pledged to inform Plaintiffs and the Court if there is any

change in the Board’s plans that would constitute a reversal of the Center’s position that the

building will not be demolished and that no new structure will be constructed. Plaintiffs reserve

       PLAINTIFFS’ ADDENDUM TO JOINT MEET-AND-CONFER REPORT

       Plaintiffs fully endorse the plan proposed in the parties’ Meet-and-Confer Report (“the

Report”). Plaintiffs submit this addendum to explain that, because Defendants declared in the

Beatty proceeding earlier this week that demolition of the Kennedy Center was a possibility,

Plaintiffs must reserve all rights to seek emergency relief. Defendants would not agree to include

that information in this Report.

       As the Court is aware, Plaintiffs brought this case, in part, because of their credible concern

that the Kennedy Center faced the same threat of demolition as the White House’s East Wing. Mr.

Trump (the Chair of the Center’s Board) stated on social media that he intended to close the Center

for a “[c]omplete [r]ebuilding” of a “new and spectacular Entertainment Complex.” ECF 24-4. Mr.

Trump later stated that while he planned to “us[e] the steel” and “some of” the historic original

marble—a gift from the Italian government, see ECF 45 at 36-37—the building would be “brand

new.” ECF 24-33. The Kennedy Center’s Executive Director, Matt Floca, subsequently disavowed

plans to demolish the Kennedy Center. Crediting those representations over Mr. Trump’s

statements, the Court denied Plaintiffs’ preliminary-injunction motion.

       It thus came as a surprise earlier this week when the Department of Justice—opposing

Representative Beatty’s motion for emergency relief in the related case against many of the same

defendants—stated that if Mr. Trump is not allowed to put his name on the Kennedy Center, “the

Center will deteriorate further into an unsafe, decrepit structure that will be required to be taken

down, with a determination to follow on what to build on the site, such as a large outdoor

amphitheater overlooking the Potomac River that has been proposed, by some, for many years.”

Defs.’ Response to Mot. for Emergency Inj. Relief at 2, Beatty v. Trump, No. 25-cv-4480 (D.D.C.

Aug. 24, 2026), ECF 68 (emphasis added).

       Defendants’ claims in the Beatty filing were deeply troubling to Plaintiffs because they

represent a fundamental change in Defendants’ posture vis-à-vis the future of the Kennedy Center

building. Plaintiffs therefore proposed that the following modest eleven-word provision be

included in the Report: “Plaintiffs reserve all rights in the event that Defendants’ plans change or

work commences at the Center without the Beatty injunction having been dissolved, including

work on ‘a large outdoor amphitheater overlooking the Potomac River’” (emphasis added), with

a citation to Defendants’ Beatty filing. Even though the quoted language is taken directly from

Defendants’ papers in Beatty, counsel for Defendants informed Plaintiffs that Defendants would

not join any report that included it.

Center down. But given Defendants’ written intimation only a few days ago that they might do

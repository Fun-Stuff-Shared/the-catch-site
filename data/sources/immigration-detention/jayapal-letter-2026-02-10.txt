Dear Secretary Noem, Attorney General Bondi, and Acting Director Lyons,
        We write to address the Trump administration’s continued reliance on an absurd policy
that has been rejected by over 300 judges.1 In July, Acting Director Lyons issued a memo to
Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) employees declaring that immigrants, who at one
point entered the country between ports of entry, must be held in immigration detention without
receiving a bond hearing for the duration of their cases.2 ICE created a legal fiction on which to
rely: that all immigrants who entered without inspection, regardless of the timing of their entry,
are to be treated as if they are still seeking admission at the border, and therefore afforded fewer
protections under the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA). This ignores that the standard
detention provision for persons in removal proceedings, INA 236, expressly encompasses
persons who entered without inspection and thus are inadmissible.3
        The memo declares that now, instead of processing individuals under the authorities of
Section 236 of the INA, ICE will now be relying on the far more restrictive Section 235. This is
despite the fact that Section 235 very clearly lays out to whom it applies: an inadmissible
noncitizen “who is arriving in the United States” or a noncitizen “who has not been admitted or
paroled into the United States” and who cannot prove two years of continuous physical presence
in the United States.4 ICE conveniently ignores this so that it can indiscriminately throw more
people into immigration detention to juice the administration’s detention statistics in the hope
that the heinous conditions in immigration detention centers will force people to stop fighting
their cases and accept deportation.
        This policy flies in the face of the Due Process Clause of the Constitution, decades of
precedent, and common sense. Unsurprisingly, this has led to many challenges in federal court,
resulting in courts becoming clogged with these cases. This policy has been rejected by judges

        On November 25, California District Judge Sunshine Sykes certified a nationwide class
of individuals living in the United States upon being detained, ruling that such individuals are

immigrants across the country continue to be denied bond hearings due to ICE’s policy.8
       This must end. As executive branch appointees, your job is to enforce the law. Over 300
judges have told you 1,600 times exactly what the law is.9 Now you must follow it. Rescind this
absurd memo and allow detained immigrants the opportunity to obtain bond hearings.

1.2 Summary of the Action
This Action constitutes the first operational component of the EU–Greenland Investment Package announced by the
President of the European Commission and contributes to the implementation of the renewed EU–Greenland
partnership under the current Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF).

The Action supports Greenland’s green and digital transitions and economic diversification and resilience by
combining targeted infrastructure investments, investment-enabling support and institutional capacity
strengthening. It focuses on four complementary areas: satellite connectivity, renewable hybrid energy solutions in
remote settlements, support to sustainable raw materials value chains, and the strengthening of Greenland’s capacity
to prepare, coordinate and implement EU-supported programmes and investments and advance key policy areas of
cooperation.

The Action contributes directly to the EU’s global objectives on climate mitigation, digital inclusion, resilience and
secure value chains, in line with the Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument


                                                                                                          Page 3 of 27
(NDICI-Global Europe) Global Challenges Multiannual Indicative Programme (2021-2027) and the Global Gateway
strategy. It complements existing EU instruments deployed in Greenland, including the Green Growth Programme
under the Decision on the Overseas Association, including Greenland (DOAG)3, the Connecting Europe Facility
(CEF) and Horizon Europe.

In the field of digital connectivity, the Action supports the reinforcement of geostationary (GEO) and low-earth orbit
(LEO) satellite capacities in Greenland, with a focus on remote settlements. This will improve service quality,
resilience and redundancy, while supporting the uptake of high-quality, secure and sustainable connectivity solutions
in compliance with applicable regulatory frameworks and improve human security in remote areas where reliable
connectivity is essential, for example for search and rescue support.

In the energy sector, the Action supports the deployment of Greenland’s Energy Sector Plan focused on transitioning
from a heavy reliance on imported oil, by supporting the deployment of hybrid diesel-photovoltaic-battery solutions
in remote settlements currently dependent on diesel-based micro-grids. These solutions provide a cost-effective and
technically mature pathway to reduce fuel consumption, greenhouse gas emissions and operational costs, while
strengthening energy security and resilience. In line with Global Gateway principles, the Action promotes high
environmental, social and cybersecurity standards and encourages diversified and resilient supply chains. To ensure
full alignment with the Commission risks assessment, the financial support to the energy sector will be conditional
on full alignment with the Commission’s guidance on EU funding for inverters, as approved by the Commissioners’
Project Group on Economic Security.

In the field of raw materials, through technical assistance, the Action supports the implementation of the EU-
Greenland Partnership on raw materials, including by strengthening the enabling environment for sustainable raw
materials value chains and their integration with EU industrial ecosystems, by facilitating exchanges between project
promoters and relevant EU industries.

Finally, the Action aims at supporting the strengthening of implementation and coordination functions within
the Greenlandic administration as an enabling mechanism to ensure effective delivery of relevant policy priorities
of the EU–Greenland Partnership. This support mainly focuses on improving the preparation, coordination and
monitoring of interventions across priority sectors of EU–Greenland cooperation, thereby enhancing delivery quality,
coherence and institutional continuity as cooperation further expands.

The Action will be implemented in line with the Team Europe approach, in close coordination with the Governments
of Greenland and Denmark, EU Member States, European International and Development Financial Institutions
(IFIs/DFIs), Export Credit Agencies (ECA), and the EU private sector, as appropriate.

1.3 Zone benefitting from the Action
The Action shall be carried out in Greenland.

2    RATIONALE

2.1 Context
The President of the European Union announced, during her intervention at the 2026 World Economic Forum in
Davos, the establishment of a new investment package for Greenland under the current Multiannual Financial
Framework (2021-2027). In line with the spirit of the EU-Greenland Partnership, these investments will be focused
on areas of mutual interest and align with existing policies and strategies.

industrial and raw material value chains offer key opportunities to foster innovation, growth and resilience, while
contributing to global climate objectives.

In this context, the EU has expanded its engagement with strategic and like-minded partners through bilateral and
multilateral cooperation. Greenland represents a key partner with which the EU is seeking to deepen cooperation
beyond traditional areas such as fisheries and education, in line with the Global Gateway strategy and the objectives
of the NDICI–Global Europe instrument. New areas of cooperation include renewable energy, digital connectivity,
raw materials, sustainable tourism, research, biodiversity and environmental protection. These areas have been
identified throughout regular exchanges of the Commission’s services with Greenlandic authorities and Greenland-
based stakeholders on green growth and connectivity-related topics. These exchanges have accelerated since the
opening of the Commission’s Office in Nuuk in March 2024, and have been bolstered by the biannual policy
dialogues and the organisation of business-oriented events, such as the EU-Greenland Week in November 2025 in
Brussels.

Greenland is pursuing strategies to enhance climate resilience including through an ambitious agenda to increase the
share of renewable energy in its energy mix, building of additional economic sectors that can help increase resilience
in an economy where exports are almost exclusively from the fisheries sector. Tourism, raw materials, and renewable
energy are sectors that have so far been identified. It is equally important for Greenland to provide comparable access
to services throughout the country, including in remote and isolated settlements, for instance in terms of connectivity.
Reliable connectivity is an essential precondition for human security and socio-economic development (e.g. search
and rescue, telemedicine, remote education). It is also an important tool for developing new economic activities, such
as tourism, for local communities that have seen their traditional livelihood of hunting and fishing drastically reduced
as a consequence of climate change.

As Greenland faces many challenges, the need to enhance its small administration has been identified as a priority
by Greenland to ensure the efficient use and deployment of domestic and external resources. In an enhanced EU-
Greenland Partnership the need for coordination and planning is a pre-requisite for effective implementation and
durable impact.

2.2 Lessons learnt
To support priorities areas including renewable energy, digital connectivity, raw materials, sustainable tourism,
research, biodiversity and environmental protection in Greenland, the Commission has mobilised a range of
instruments, including the Green Growth Programme under the DOAG, the CEF and Horizon Europe. These
instruments have laid important foundations for expanded cooperation and investment-oriented engagement.

Through the Green Growth Programme, the EU has allocated EUR 22.5 million to support green growth objectives
in Greenland, including project preparation in renewable energy, environmental protection initiatives and research
activities.

In addition, the Connecting Europe Facility has supported the development of Greenland’s digital infrastructure
through four grants for a total EUR 54.7 million for the Tusass Connect subsea cable project along Greenland’s west
coast. Horizon Europe supports several research projects in Greenland, contributing to scientific cooperation and
innovation capacity.

Experience from the Green Growth Programme, the CEF and Horizon Europe confirms the effectiveness of
combining targeted infrastructure investments with technical assistance and capacity-building measures. These
instruments have supported digital inclusion, climate mitigation, environmental protection, economic diversification
and resilience in Greenland.

technical assistance can support the maturation of projects, including by connecting them with European off takers,
strengthening the enabling environment and facilitating structured engagement with markets under high
environmental and social standards.

These lessons highlight the value of complementing existing instruments with targeted investment-enabling support
and reinforced coordination capacity, as proposed under this Action.

2.3 Problem Analysis
Short problem analysis

Engagement with Greenlandic and European public and private stakeholders indicates that infrastructure investments
in remote and Arctic contexts would benefit from additional risk-mitigation, project preparation and coordination
support, particularly where projects are characterised by complex logistics, limited scale or innovative technical
solutions. In Greenland, geographic remoteness, climatic conditions and market size are inherent contextual
characteristics that shape investment decision-making.

In the energy and digital sectors, the combination of high upfront costs, specialised technical requirements and
challenging operating environments calls for well-sequenced and coordinated approaches to project development and
deployment, especially in remote settlements.

In the field of raw materials, while interest in Greenland’s potential is growing, the development of sustainable value
chains benefits from continued strengthening of regulatory, technical and coordination frameworks, in line with
Greenlandic priorities and high Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) standards.

timeliness of programme implementation, while fully preserving Greenland’s ownership of policy and investment

         Public Policy

In the field of green growth, the Government of Greenland has established a robust policy framework and continues
to advance the implementation of key strategic initiatives relevant to the EU–Greenland partnership. These include,
notably, the Sustainability and Growth Plan II (adopted in November 2023) and the Energy and Water Sector Plan
(2017),6 alongside ongoing work on an updated sector plan for energy and green transition.

The Government of Greenland reports regularly on progress in the implementation of these initiatives through
biannual EU–Greenland policy dialogue meetings and ad hoc technical exchanges with the Commission’s Office in
Nuuk. These exchanges provide a structured forum to assess progress, discuss priorities and ensure alignment
between Greenlandic strategies and EU-supported interventions.

The Sustainability and Growth Plan II sets out a comprehensive reform agenda aimed at strengthening long-term
economic resilience and sustainability, with a focus on education and skills, economic diversification, future-proofing
of the public sector, and reforms in taxation, welfare, housing and elderly care. The plan provides a credible strategic
framework for addressing structural trends and supporting inclusive growth over the medium to long term across the
country, including in remote and isolated settlements.

In the area of renewable energy, Greenland continues to pursue an ambitious transition agenda. The existing energy
and water sector plan establishes clear objectives to increase the share of renewable energy, modernise energy
systems and ensure affordable and reliable access to electricity and water across the territory. Nukissiorfiit, the
publicly owned utility responsible for electricity, water and heat supply, plays a central role in implementing this
strategy. Currently, approximately 70% of electricity supplied is based on renewable energy, primarily hydropower,
and further progress is expected through ongoing and planned investments.

The effective and timely implementation of these policies is supported by a strong institutional framework and
sustained political commitment. Targeted EU support under this Action is designed to accompany the rollout of
national strategies, reinforce implementation capacity where appropriate and maximise the impact of existing policy
efforts.

In conclusion, the policy is sufficiently relevant and credible for budget support contract objectives to be largely
achieved. Therefore, the policy can be supported by the Commission with the proposed budget support contract.

         Macroeconomic Policy

This assessment draws on the Government of Greenland’s Political Economic Report for 20257 and the Greenland
Economic Council’s Report for the second half of 2025,8 as well as key indicators related to the implementation of
the Sustainability and Growth Plan II.

Greenland’s economy continues to demonstrate resilience, supported by sound macroeconomic management and
prudent fiscal policies. Following several years of favourable performance driven by strong fisheries revenues and
major infrastructure investments, economic growth has moderated, reflecting the natural completion of large
construction projects and normalisation of external conditions. GDP growth has remained positive, with projections
indicating moderate growth over the medium term.

Recent macroeconomic assessments indicate that Greenland is entering a period of weaker growth, with the 2025
forecast revised down to 0.2% amid declining fishing revenues and reduced shrimp quotas. Unemployment, after a
prolonged low, has begun to rise, while inflation remains close to target and aligned with European trends.

The Government of Greenland continues to pursue a stability-oriented macroeconomic policy, underpinned by the
objectives set out in the Sustainability and Growth Plan II. The plan aims to strengthen the foundations of public

Inflationary pressures have eased following adjustments observed in recent years, and price developments remain
closely monitored. Investment activity remains significant, particularly in infrastructure, including airport
development and renewable energy projects, which are expected to support economic activity and connectivity in the
coming years.

Fisheries remain a cornerstone of the economy and exports, with recent legislative reforms strengthening
sustainability and resource management. At the same time, continued efforts are underway to foster economic
diversification, including in tourism, renewable energy and raw materials, supported by evolving regulatory
frameworks and investment initiatives.

The public sector plays an important role in Greenland’s economic model, providing stability and continuity while
supporting strategic investments. State-owned enterprises operate in key sectors such as transport, energy,
telecommunications and logistics, contributing to service provision across the territory.

In conclusion, the authorities are pursuing a stability-oriented macroeconomic policy and the eligibility criterion is
met.

        Public Financial Management

Greenland’s public financial management system continues to provide a solid framework for fiscal discipline,
transparency and accountability. The Finance Act is prepared and implemented in accordance with the Budget Act,
which establishes clear rules to ensure balanced budgets over the medium term and prudent expenditure growth.

The Budget Act requires a balance between income and expenditure over a four-year period and sets limits on the
growth of operating and capital expenditure. These rules support fiscal sustainability and encourage efficiency in
public spending. The Government of Greenland has demonstrated consistent commitment to these principles,
supported by sound liquidity management and a strong net financial position.

Revenue performance has remained robust, supported by stable tax receipts and revenues from fisheries-related
activities. Ongoing efforts to broaden the revenue base and strengthen domestic revenue mobilisation contribute to
the resilience of public finances and support long-term sustainability.

The public financial management framework is further strengthened by regular reporting, internal controls and audit
mechanisms. These arrangements provide a reliable basis for the use of budget support instruments and ensure that
EU funds can be managed in line with the principles of sound financial management.

In conclusion, the public finance management reform strategy is sufficiently relevant and credible, including on
domestic revenue mobilisation, and the eligibility criterion is met.

        Transparency and Oversight of the Budget

The Government of Greenland ensures a high level of transparency and public access to budgetary information.
Annual budgets are published in a timely manner and are accompanied by explanatory documents, forecasts and
background analyses, enabling informed public and parliamentary scrutiny.

Budget proposals and supporting documentation are made publicly available in parallel with parliamentary
discussions, and clear summaries are provided in both Danish and Greenlandic. This approach enhances transparency,
supports public understanding of fiscal developments and strengthens democratic accountability.

accordingly. These practices provide a solid basis for transparency and oversight and are fully consistent with the

3    DESCRIPTION OF THE ACTION

3.1 Objectives and Expected Outputs
The Overall Objective of this Action is to address global challenges in Greenland such as climate change and limited
integration into the digital economy and global value chains, through economic diversification, green and digital
transitions and strengthened resilience.

The Specific Objectives of the Action are to:
1. Support access of the Greenlandic population to reliable and resilient digital connectivity, particularly in remote
   areas.
2. Support the implementation of Greenland’s Energy Sector Plan by increasing renewable energy use, reducing
   greenhouse gas emissions and enhancing energy security and resilience in remote settlements.
3. Support the development of sustainable raw materials value chains and integration with EU markets while
   fully respecting Greenlandic ownership, environmental and social safeguards and decision-making (ESG).
4. Support the effective implementation of EU–Greenland cooperation priorities across sectors through
   strengthened preparation, coordination and monitoring of interventions within the Greenlandic administration,
   including EU-supported actions, thereby enhancing delivery quality, coherence and continuity as cooperation
   expands.

The Outputs to be delivered by this action contributing to the corresponding Specific Objectives are:

Outputs contributing to Outcome 1 (or Specific Objective 1):
1.1. Increase in GEO satellite capacities in isolated areas of Greenland’s west coast.
1.2. Increase in LEO satellite capacities in isolated areas of Greenland’s west and east coasts.

These outputs will improve service quality, redundancy and resilience of satellite connectivity services,
complementing existing subsea cable infrastructure and supporting digital inclusion, service delivery and socio-
economic opportunities in remote communities.

Outputs contributing to Outcome 2 (or Specific Objective 2):
Induced output:
2.1. Measurable reduction in diesel fuel consumption and emissions.
Direct outputs:
2.2. Deployment of hybrid diesel-PV-battery solutions in remote settlements.
2.3. Promotion of high-quality, sustainable technologies consistent with EU environmental, social and cybersecurity
standards.

Under this outcome, the Action supports the implementation of Greenland’s Energy Sector Plan, which seeks to
reduce reliance on imported fossil fuels while strengthening energy security and affordability. Hybrid diesel-PV-
battery systems are particularly well-suited to the geographic and climatic conditions of remote settlements and
enable an accelerated, cost-effective transition while maintaining grid stability.

Outputs contributing to Outcome 3 (or Specific Objective 3):
3.1. Identification and advisory support on raw materials value chain opportunities that can be developed in
cooperation with EU private sector actors and financial institutions.
3.2. Facilitation of engagement between project promoters, financial stakeholders and potential offtakers through
technical assistance and business facilitation activities.


                                                                                                            Page 9 of 27
Under this outcome, the Action focuses on strengthening the enabling environment, project preparation and market
integration. The Action does not pre-select projects and fully respects Greenlandic ownership, regulatory frameworks
and high environmental, social and governance standards.

Outputs contributing to Outcome 4 (or Specific Objective 4):
4.1. Support the capacity of the Government of Greenland to implement EU funding.
4.2. Strengthening of monitoring, reporting and performance tracking across EU-funded portfolios.

Under this outcome, the Action supports capacity building, with a view to enhance the Greenlandic administration’s
capacity to plan and coordinate international cooperation, including from the EU. Policy delivery and implementation
support is conceived as a horizontal delivery instrument, supporting the implementation of relevant policy priorities
of the EU–Greenland partnership across sectors such as energy, digital connectivity, raw materials, tourism and
environment.

By reinforcing preparation, coordination and monitoring functions, capacity building contributes to more timely,
coherent and effective delivery of EU-supported interventions. It is not an end in itself and does not substitute line
ministries or national decision-making structures, but serves as an enabling mechanism to support policy
implementation as cooperation expands.

Environmental Protection & Climate Change


                                                                                                         Page 10 of 27
Outcome of the Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA) screening
The SEA screening concluded that environmental and climate-related aspects of this Action should be addressed
primarily at the level of individual interventions. This approach reflects the diversified nature of the Action, which
combines infrastructure investments, technical assistance and coordination support across different sectors.
Environmental considerations will therefore be integrated at project design and implementation stages, ensuring
proportionality and alignment with Greenlandic and EU environmental requirements. The Action contributes
positively to climate mitigation objectives, notably through the deployment of hybrid renewable energy solutions and
the reduction of fossil fuel use in remote settlements.

Outcome of the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) screening
The EIA screening classified this Action as Category B, not requiring a full EIA, but for which environmental aspects
will be systematically addressed during the design and implementation of individual projects. For infrastructure-
related activities, procurement specifications and contractual arrangements will integrate environmental safeguards,
lifecycle considerations and waste management requirements, including for photovoltaic panels and battery systems.
In the field of raw materials, any support provided will be conditional on compliance with high environmental
standards, including robust environmental assessments at project level, in line with Greenlandic legislation and
international best practices applicable to Arctic ecosystems.

Outcome of the Climate Risk Assessment (CRA) screening
The CRA concluded that this Action presents no or low climate risk and does not require further detailed assessment.
On the contrary, the Action contributes positively to climate adaptation and resilience. The deployment of hybrid
diesel-photovoltaic-battery solutions reduces exposure to fuel supply disruptions and price volatility, while enhanced
satellite connectivity supports crisis response, emergency communications and service continuity in remote and

Overall, the Action makes a direct and measurable contribution to climate mitigation and adaptation objectives, while
ensuring that environmental risks are appropriately managed at project level.

Gender equality and empowerment of women and girls
As per the OECD Gender DAC codes identified in section 1.1., this action is labelled as G1. This implies that this
Action contributes to gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls. By improving access to energy,
digital connectivity and economic opportunities, the Action supports more inclusive participation in social and
economic life, particularly in remote communities.

Enhanced digital connectivity facilitates access to education, health services, employment opportunities and support
networks, including for women and girls. Implementing partners will be required to ensure equal opportunities, non-
discrimination and gender-sensitive approaches in the design and implementation of activities supported under this
Action.

Human Rights
The Action applies a human rights-based approach across all its components. Particular attention will be paid to
inclusive consultation processes, respect for indigenous peoples’ rights and meaningful engagement with local
communities.

Activities supported under this Action will be designed and implemented in a manner that respects fundamental rights
and promotes social inclusion. Enhanced access to digital services and reliable energy supply is expected to support
access to information, public services and participation in economic and social activities.

Disability
As per OECD Disability DAC codes identified in section 1.1. this Action is labelled D0. This implies that this Action
contributes indirectly to the inclusion of persons with disabilities. Improved digital connectivity enables access to
telemedicine, online education and remote work opportunities, which are particularly relevant in remote settlements.
Wherever feasible, implementing partners will promote accessibility and inclusive participation, including in
consultation processes and employment opportunities linked to supported activities.

This Action is labelled as making a significant contribution to the reduction of inequalities (I-1). By improving access
to energy, connectivity and investment opportunities in remote and sparsely populated areas, the Action directly

Support to sustainable economic activities, including renewable energy deployment and raw materials value chains,
is expected to contribute to job creation and fiscal revenues over the medium to long term, supporting broader socio-
economic development across Greenland.

Democracy
Through its support to effective policy implementation and coordination, the Action contributes to sound governance
and institutional resilience. The resort to horizontal capacity building supports transparent, coordinated and
accountable delivery of EU-supported interventions, without substituting national decision-making structures.

Implementing partners will be required to adhere to the principles of sound financial management, transparency and
accountability, including proportionate anti-fraud measures.

Conflict sensitivity, peace and resilience
The Action contributes positively to resilience by strengthening critical infrastructure, enhancing service continuity
and supporting inclusive socio-economic development in remote areas.

Disaster Risk Reduction
The Action contributes to disaster risk reduction objectives, notably through enhanced satellite connectivity, which
supports early warning systems, emergency communications and coordination of response efforts in case of natural
disasters or infrastructure disruptions. Improved energy resilience in remote settlements further strengthens
preparedness and continuity of essential services under adverse conditions.

Other considerations if relevant
No additional cross-cutting issues have been identified at this stage.

3.5 The Intervention Logic



                                                                                      Page 13 of 27
The intervention logic of this Action reflects the evolution of EU–Greenland cooperation towards a more investment-
oriented and delivery-focused partnership.

Targeted infrastructure investments in digital connectivity and renewable energy directly support the implementation
of Greenlandic policies and strategies, delivering tangible benefits in terms of service quality, resilience and
sustainability. In parallel, investment-enabling technical assistance and facilitation mechanisms support the
maturation of sustainable raw materials value chains and structured engagement with markets.

Effective delivery of these interventions is supported by horizontal capacity building. By strengthening preparation,
sequencing, monitoring and coordination functions, this implementation support enhances the Government of
Greenland’s administrative capacities, and therefore policy implementation across sectors.

Together, these elements contribute to reducing implementation risks, improving coherence and maximising the
impact of EU support, while fully respecting Greenlandic ownership and decision-making.

4.3 Implementation of the Budget Support Component

          Rationale for the Amounts Allocated to Budget Support
The amount allocated for the budget support component is EUR 8 000 000, and for complementary support is
EUR 13 150 000.

This amount is based on the need to support the fulfilment of Outcome 2, namely increasing renewable energy
use, reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and enhancing energy security and resilience in remote settlements
through the implementation of Greenland’s Energy Sector Plan, while promoting high environmental, social and
supply-chain standards consistent with EU priorities.

Budget support allows EU contributions to be linked to clearly defined and verifiable milestones and supports
the effective implementation of agreed policy priorities. Through direct financial support to partner authorities
in Greenland, it contributes to the timely delivery of projects of public interest and facilitates the roll-out of
agreed interventions. As an established instrument within the EU–Greenland Partnership, budget support is well
understood by the Greenlandic authorities and provides a predictable and efficient framework for cooperation.
Its use supports coherence with existing cooperation modalities and contributes to the effective and sustainable
delivery of EU-supported actions, in line with both Greenlandic and EU priorities.

          Criteria for Disbursement of Budget Support
    a)    Conditions

The general conditions for disbursement of all tranches are as follows:
   - Satisfactory progress in the implementation of Greenland’s energy and water sector policy framework,
       including the Energy and Water Sector Plan and, where applicable, its future updates, and continued
       credibility and relevance thereof or of the subsequent policy.
   - Maintenance of a credible and relevant stability-oriented macroeconomic policy or progress made
       towards restoring key balances.
   - Satisfactory progress in the implementation of reforms to improve public financial management,
       including domestic revenue mobilisation, and continued relevance and credibility of the reform
       programme.
   - Satisfactory progress with regard to the public availability of accessible, timely, comprehensive and
       sound budgetary information.

       policy priorities:
    - The hybrid dieel-photovoltaic-battery programme has reached an advanced implementation stage at
       portfolio level.
    - A substantial share of the planned hybrid diesel-photovoltaic-battery systems has been physically

The chosen performance indicators and targets to be used for the disbursement of variable tranches will be

upward/downward revisions of targets. Such changes shall be authorised in writing ex-ante, at the latest at the
beginning of the period under review applicable to the indicators and targets.

In exceptional and/or duly justified cases, for instance where unexpected events, external shocks or changing
circumstances have made the indicator or the target irrelevant and could not be anticipated, a variable tranche

In case of a significant deterioration of fundamental values, budget support disbursements may be suspended,
reduced or cancelled, in accordance with the relevant provisions of the financing agreement.

            Direct Management (Grants)
      a) Purpose of the grant(s)

A part of this Action may be implemented through an operating grant, to exceptionally provide support to the
Government of Greenland to facilitate policy delivery and implementation. This entails the implementation of
activities relative to Outcome 4.

      b) Type of applicants targeted

Applicants must be ministries or public bodies falling under the responsibility of the Government of Greenland.

      c) Justification of a direct grant

Under the responsibility of the Commission’s authorising officer responsible, the grant may be awarded without
a call for proposals to ministries or public bodies falling under the responsibility of the Government of
Greenland, selected using the following criteria: Capacity to take on an interservice role, to strengthen
preparation, coordination and monitoring of interventions within the Greenlandic administration, including EU-
supported actions.

Under the responsibility of the Commission’s authorising officer responsible, the recourse to an award of a grant
without a call for proposals is justified on the basis of Article 198c of the Financial Regulation, bodies with a de
jure monopoly. Indeed, the Government of Greenland is legally responsible of its administration and of the
public policies covered by the EU-Greenland cooperation agenda, in line with the 2009 Act on Greenland’s Self-
Government.10 As regards the specific entity within or attached to the Government of Greenland which will
receive the funding, it will be defined by the latter in close coordination with the Commission, based on the
Government of Greenland’s own rules and administrative arrangements, and on the capacity of the selected
entity to comply with the criteria listed in this section.

The part of the action under the budgetary envelope reserved for grants may, partially or totally and including
where an entity is designated for receiving a grant without a call for proposals, be implemented in indirect
management with an entity, which will be selected by the Commission’s services using the criteria defined in
section 4.4.1.c above.

4.7 Organisational Set-up and Responsibilities
A steering committee involving concerned Greenlandic ministries and the European Commission and other
interested entities will be set up to oversee the implementation of the Action, to improve coordination, to
exchange on arising challenges and ensure close monitoring and coherence. Those are to be defined in the
financing agreement but could include international financing institutions to improve access to finance, as well
as, on an ad-hoc basis and for consultations, the private sector and civil society organisations, including those
representing women in all their diversity and persons with disabilities). The composition of the steering
committee shall strive for equal representation of women and men in the committee.

In addition to the Steering Committee, the biannual EU-Greenland Policy Dialogues will be used to take stock
of implementation. During these dialogue meetings, the Government of Greenland presents updates on
performance of the sectors supported, Greenland's macroeconomic situation and reform of the public finance
management system. Following these meetings, an Aide Memoire is prepared and mutually agreed. Continuous
exchanges and consultations are being reinforced by the European Commission Office in Nuuk.

As part of its prerogative of budget implementation and to safeguard the financial interests of the Union, the
Commission may participate in the above governance structures for governing the implementation of the Action
and may sign or enter into joint declarations or statements, for the purpose of enhancing the visibility of this
Action and ensuring effective coordination.

5    PERFORMANCE MEASUREMENT

5.1 Monitoring and Reporting
The day-to-day technical and financial monitoring of the implementation of this Action will be a continuous
process, and part of the implementing partner’s responsibilities. To this aim, the implementing partner shall
establish a permanent internal, technical and financial monitoring system for the Action and elaborate regular
progress reports (not less than annual) and final reports. Every report shall provide an accurate account of
implementation of the Action, difficulties encountered, changes introduced, as well as the degree of achievement
of its results (Outputs and direct Outcomes) as measured by corresponding indicators, using as reference the
logframe matrix (for project modality) and the partner’s strategy, policy or reform action plan list (for budget
support).

The Commission may undertake additional project monitoring visits both through its own staff and through
independent consultants recruited directly by the Commission for independent monitoring reviews (or recruited
by the responsible agent contracted by the Commission for implementing such reviews).

Roles and responsibilities for data collection, analysis and monitoring:

Implementing partners will be responsible for the collection, analysis and reporting of data related to the
indicators defined in the logical framework. Data sources may include administrative data, reports from national
authorities, utility companies, project promoters and other relevant stakeholders.

5.2 Evaluation
Having regard to the nature of the Action, a final evaluation may be carried out for this Action or its components
via independent consultants or via an implementing partner.

It will be carried out for accountability and learning purposes at various levels (including for policy revision),
taking into account in particular the external dimension of EU policies, including the EU’s cooperation agenda
under the Global Gateway strategy.

The evaluation of the budget support component of this Action may be performed individually or through a joint
strategic evaluation of budget support operations carried out with the partner country, other budget support
providers and relevant stakeholders.

As for the evaluation of the other components of this Action, the Commission shall inform the implementing
partner at least two months in advance of the dates envisaged for the evaluation missions. The implementing
partner shall collaborate efficiently and effectively with the evaluation experts and inter alia provide them with
all necessary information and documentation, as well as access to the project premises and activities.

The evaluation reports may be shared with the partners and other key stakeholders following the best practice
of evaluation dissemination11. The implementing partner and the Commission shall analyse the conclusions and
recommendations of the evaluations and, where appropriate, apply the necessary adjustments. The financing of
the evaluation may be covered by another measure constituting a Financing Decision.

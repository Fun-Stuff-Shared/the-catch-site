FACT SHEET
PLANNED LEAD AZIDE DETONATION FACT SHEET — November 20, 2026


Situation Overview
The U.S. Army is planning two controlled detonations of degraded lead azide at the
Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Friday, November 20, 2026, at
approximately noon.
The detonation is expected to produce a dust plume containing lead particles that may
travel beyond the installation and settle on surrounding surfaces. The U.S.
Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has issued an emergency permit authorizing
the operation. Iowa HHS was recently made aware of the planned detonation and is
coordinating with other state agencies and federal partners to assess potential public
health concerns and support local communities.
Potential Public Health Concerns
The primary potential exposure pathways include:
   •   Airborne exposure: Breathing in lead-containing particles following the
       detonation.
   •   Surface exposure: Contact with or ingestion of lead dust deposited on soil,
       playgrounds, vehicles, buildings, and other surfaces.
Lead is a toxic metal that can cause serious health effects. No safe level of lead
exposure has been identified. Young children and pregnant women are at particular
risk.
Monitoring and Potentially Affected Areas
The U.S. Army has released two maps identifying areas of potential airborne lead
exposure and lead dust deposition. These are modeled projections, not confirmed
measurements, and may change based on weather conditions.
The Army has indicated it will update its maps:
   •   Six hours before the detonation and hourly thereafter until detonation.
   •   Following the event, with a final estimate of the impacted area.
The Army will also communicate when the airborne exposure period has ended.
Residents should follow the latest official instructions.
Current maps and updates: U.S. Army Installation Community Notifications

Recommended Public Health Precautions
Local public health agencies are encouraged to share the following recommendations
with residents in potentially impacted areas:
   •   Stay indoors: Keep windows and doors closed for 24–48 hours after the
       detonation as a precaution, unless updated official guidance indicates otherwise.
   •   Use indoor air recirculation: Residential furnaces may continue operating.
       Facilities with outside air intakes, including schools and commercial buildings,
       should consider temporarily switching to recirculation when safe.
   •   Improve indoor filtration: Consider using a portable air cleaner equipped with a
       HEPA filter.
   •   Monitor official updates: Follow local news and the U.S. Army's website for
       plume updates and safety instructions.
   •   Clean surfaces safely: Once the airborne exposure period has ended, wet-
       clean potentially affected surfaces, including playground equipment, toys, and
       windowsills, using soap and water. Avoid dry sweeping that may redistribute
       dust. Consider washing your vehicle and replacing the cabin air filter if needed.
   •   Limit unnecessary travel: Avoid potentially affected areas when possible. Use
       vehicle air recirculation if driving through impacted areas.
   •   Practice good hygiene: Wash hands before eating or drinking and remove
       shoes when entering homes to limit tracking dust indoors.
   •   Seek medical guidance: Contact a healthcare provider if concerned about lead
       exposure or related health effects.


Blood Lead Testing and Health Concerns
Lead exposure may not cause immediate symptoms. Blood lead testing is the only
recommended method for evaluating potential exposure.
   •   Individuals concerned about exposure should contact their healthcare provider to
       discuss whether testing is appropriate.
   •   Children younger than six and pregnant women warrant particular attention
       because of their increased vulnerability.
Healthcare providers can help determine appropriate testing and follow-up based on an
individual's exposure history. Refer to Reportable Environmental and Occupational
Conditions for instructions on reporting all blood lead tests.

Recommended Actions for Local Public Health Agencies
Local public health agencies should consider the following actions:
   1. Maintain situational awareness. Monitor updated Army plume projections and
      official post-detonation announcements.
   2. Coordinate with local partners. Share information with county emergency
      management, schools, childcare providers, hospitals, long-term care facilities,
      and EMS agencies.
   3. Support community preparedness. Encourage organizations to review HVAC
      operations, outdoor activities, and procedures for minimizing lead dust exposure.
   4. Respond to public inquiries. Provide consistent information about exposure
      prevention, environmental precautions, and access to blood lead testing.
   5. Communicate emerging concerns. Coordinate with Iowa HHS and emergency
      management partners regarding significant community health concerns or
      suspected exposures.

Important: Lead Azide Detonation Complete
Updated November 25, 2026, 12:30 p.m.
Based on information shared by the U.S. Army and air monitoring data reviewed to date, Iowa HHS believes the risk of breathing in harmful amounts of lead from this event is low. We will continue to review environmental monitoring information, including soil sampling results as they become available, to determine whether any additional public health recommendations are needed.
Iowa HHS encourages Iowans to follow their health care provider's recommendations for routine lead testing and to contact their provider with any health concerns or questions about potential lead exposure. We will continue to work with local, state, and federal partners and share updates as additional information becomes available.
Lead is a toxic metal that is harmful if inhaled or swallowed and can pose serious health risks. There is no safe level of lead that has been identified, particularly for young children and pregnant women. If you are concerned about possible lead exposure, contact your health care provider to discuss whether blood lead testing is appropriate.
Remember to wash your hands before eating and drinking and take your shoes off at the entrance of homes to minimize tracking.
Information about lead exposure, testing, and prevention is available through Iowa HHS'
Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program
and through the
reportable environmental & occupational diseases, poisonings, and conditions
webpage. Lead exposure may not cause immediate symptoms, which is why blood lead testing is used to identify exposure. Individuals with questions or concerns should consult their health care provider and review available Iowa HHS resources.

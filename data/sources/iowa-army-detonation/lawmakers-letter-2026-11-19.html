Duckworth, Durbin Lead Illinois, Iowa Bipartisan Lawmakers in Calling on Army, EPA to Consider Delaying Planned Detonation of Lead Azide, Request Detailed Plans

In their letter, lawmakers express concern about public health and lack of adequate communication from the Army and EPA with local officials and communities

 

[WASHINGTON, D.C.] – U.S. Senator Tammy Duckworth (D-IL); U.S. Senate Democratic Whip Dick Durbin (D-IL); U.S. Representatives Darin LaHood (R-IL-16), Mary Miller (R-IL-15), and Eric Sorensen (D-IL-17); Illinois Governor JB Pritzker; and Senators-elect Julianna Stratton (D-IL) and Josh Turek (D-IA) today sent a letter to Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Administrator Lee Zeldin expressing the lawmakers’ grave concerns with Friday’s planned detonation of 9,700 pounds of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP) in Middletown, Iowa, and called on the Army and EPA to consider delaying this detonation until there is an opportunity to explore safer alternatives. Lead dust is particularly harmful to children and pregnant women, and it can contaminate soil and critical farmland indefinitely. Further, communities across both Iowa and Illinois were informed of the planned detonation only this week.

“While we understand the urgent need to address these volatile materials, their detonation has the potential to spread lead contamination in and around the region. We call on you to consider delaying this detonation until there is an opportunity to explore safer alternatives,” the lawmakers wrote.

At a minimum, the lawmakers—recognizing the risks associated with transporting lead azide—requested an immediate update before the Army proceeds on the detailed detonation plans; mitigation efforts; likely range and location of the possible toxic plume, including as modeled for weather conditions; pre- and post-detonation monitoring and sampling; possibilities of alternative disposal procedures; and plan to remain in timely communication with state and local officials and communities both before and after the detonation.

“We encourage you to remain in timely communication with state and local officials and communities across both Iowa and Illinois, which were informed of the planned detonation only this week,” the lawmakers wrote. “Our offices have received reports of poor communication from the Army and EPA about the detonation plans and anticipated monitoring after the detonation. In addition, there are significant concerns about appropriate Army and EPA oversight of this contractor-operated facility, the site of which remains on the Superfund list. We urge you to examine the contractor’s handling of this and other hazardous material to prevent any future issues.”

The lawmakers’ letter concludes, “Given the urgency of this scheduled detonation, we request an immediate reply to this letter before any planned detonation and look forward to working with you to ensure that the health and safety of families across Iowa and Illinois remains the highest priority.”

Full text of the letter is available on Senator Duckworth’s website and below:

November 19, 2026

Dear Acting Secretary Telle and Administrator Zeldin:

We write to express our grave concerns with this Friday’s planned detonation of 9,700 pounds of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP) in Middletown, Iowa. While we understand the urgent need to address these volatile materials, their detonation has the potential to spread lead contamination in and around the region. We call on you to consider delaying this detonation until there is an opportunity to explore safer alternatives. At a minimum, we request an immediate update before the Army proceeds on the detailed detonation plans; mitigation efforts; likely range and location of the possible toxic plume, including as modeled for weather conditions; pre- and post-detonation monitoring and sampling; possibilities of alternative disposal procedures; and plan to remain in timely communication with state and local officials and communities both before and after the detonation.

We appreciate that the Army and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) have studied the need to address the degraded lead azide at the IAAAP, including the risks associated with transporting the material. However, Iowa and Illinois residents understandably are concerned about the potential health impacts of the scheduled detonation. Lead dust is particularly harmful to children and pregnant women. It can contaminate soil and critical farmland indefinitely.

We encourage you to remain in timely communication with state and local officials and communities across both Iowa and Illinois, which were informed of the planned detonation only this week. Our offices have received reports of poor communication from the Army and EPA about the detonation plans and anticipated monitoring after the detonation. In addition, there are significant concerns about appropriate Army and EPA oversight of this contractor-operated facility, the site of which remains on the Superfund list. We urge you to examine the contractor’s handling of this and other hazardous material to prevent any future issues.

Given the urgency of this scheduled detonation, we request an immediate reply to this letter before any planned detonation and look forward to working with you to ensure that the health and safety of families across Iowa and Illinois remains the highest priority.

Sincerely,

Public Information Update: Planned Lead Azide Detonation at Iowa Army Ammunition Plant Postponed - News & Notices - Linn County Emergency Management

The U.S. Army has announced that the two controlled detonations of degraded explosive material at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown, Iowa, originally scheduled for Friday, November 20, have been postponed until Monday, November 23.
The operation is being conducted by the U.S. Army under an emergency permit issued by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), which establishes requirements for atmospheric modeling, environmental monitoring, public notification and any necessary cleanup.
Linn County Emergency Management and Linn County Public Health continue to receive information through the Iowa Department of Homeland Security and Emergency Management and the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services (Iowa HHS) regarding potential impacts to Linn County.
While the U.S. Army's most recent atmospheric modeling for Friday's planned detonations placed Linn County outside the projected plume area, updated modeling will be necessary to assess potential impacts associated with the rescheduled operation.
At a Glance
- The planned detonations will no longer take place Friday, November 20.
- Atmospheric modeling previously released by the U.S. Army was based on anticipated weather conditions for the original date of the operation.
- Updated forecasts will help inform whether any protective actions are warranted for Linn County residents.
- Iowa HHS has not recommended specific protective actions for individuals outside the projected plume area. The department has issued general precautions for individuals within areas identified by the Army's models.
- Linn County Emergency Management and Linn County Public Health will continue reviewing information provided by state and federal authorities throughout the weekend and as the rescheduled operation approaches.
What does this mean for Linn County residents?
The direction and extent of any potential plume will depend on atmospheric conditions at the time of the detonations. Previous forecast maps should not be relied upon to determine potential impacts associated with the rescheduled operation.
At this time, Linn County officials have not received new recommendations from state or federal authorities for specific protective actions for Linn County residents.
Should updated information indicate potential impacts to Linn County, local emergency management and public health officials will coordinate with state and federal partners to evaluate local response needs and communicate any recommended protective actions.
What can residents do in the meantime?
Residents are encouraged to stay informed through official sources and maintain general emergency preparedness.
- Register for emergency notifications through Alert Iowa.
- Familiarize yourself with your home's heating and ventilation controls.
- Review your household emergency plan.
- Review general emergency preparedness resources.
- Follow the U.S. Army's website for updated forecasts and information regarding the planned operation.
Iowa HHS has made public health guidance available for individuals in areas identified as potentially impacted. The Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship has also issued guidance for livestock producers near the planned detonation site.
Event-specific recommendations regarding blood lead testing are anticipated to follow the operation and associated environmental testing. Linn County Public Health will share additional guidance as it becomes available from Iowa HHS.

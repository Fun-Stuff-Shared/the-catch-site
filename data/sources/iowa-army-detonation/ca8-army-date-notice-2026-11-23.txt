THE GOVERNMENT’S NOTICE ON BEHALF OF THE DEPARTMENT
OF THE ARMY OF CHANGE TO CONTROLLED DETONATION DATE
On Saturday, November 21, 2026, the Government notified the Court that the
Army intended to perform a controlled detonation by Tuesday, November 24, 2026,
to treat unstable lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant to protect the public
from the risks of an uncontrolled explosion. Notice, Dkt. No. 5708596. The
Government now notifies the Court that the Army has moved the intended date to
Wednesday, November 25, 2026. 1 As set forth below, Wednesday is expected to
have favorable conditions for a controlled detonation that will reduce the risk to the
public and allow the Army to implement additional safety measures.

The Army has moved the controlled detonation to Wednesday instead of
Tuesday because of more favorable weather, which is more protective of the public.
See Declaration of Maj. Gen. Eric P. Shirley ¶ 9. Notably, the wind direction is
reasonably expected to be East to West/Southwest, and away from high population
areas and larger water bodies. Id. The weather after Wednesday is predicted to be
less favorable, and thus less protective of the public for a controlled detonation. Id.
¶ 11.
The additional day also is favorable because the Army is applying additional
blast barriers around the bunkers to help reduce lead dispersion caused by the
detonation. Id. ¶ 8. Additionally, the Army will apply thousands of gallons of water
during the detonation to help absorb the explosion and reduce lead dust. Id. And
the Army has secured from the Department of Energy highly advanced ballistic gels
and foams designed to reduce particulate matter from high explosive detonations and
will apply them before the controlled detonation. Id. ¶ 7.
These additional protections and the protections under EPA’s Emergency
Permit detailed in the Government’s stay opposition brief are designed to limit the
risk to the public from the controlled detonation. Thus, Wednesday is currently
expected to be the most favorable day to conduct the controlled detonation. See id.
¶ 10.

EPA Provides Update on Sampling Following Yesterday’s Controlled Detonation at Iowa Army Ammunition Plant
Zero Detect Thus Far for Airborne Lead, 1,591 Pre-Detonation Samples Collected, Over 1,000 Post-Detonation Samples Collected
November 26, 2026
Contact Information
EPA Press Office
(
press@epa.gov
)
WASHINGTON –
Today, U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) provided a
public update
following yesterday’s controlled detonation of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. The topline facts at the time of this release are:
Zero Detect Thus Far for Airborne Lead
: All real-time Xact air monitors continue to report, as they have since detonation, zero detection of airborne lead throughout the region.
1,591 Pre-Detonation Samples Collected:
EPA intake now shows agency personnel and contractors collected 1,591 air, water, soil, and dust samples before the controlled detonation.
Over 1,000 Post-Detonation Samples Collected:
300 EPA personnel and contractors were pre-positioned and able to deploy after the controlled detonation to begin post detonation sampling and collected over 1,000 samples since yesterday at about 1:00pm CT.
Sampling Facts:
Over 130 structures that were sampled pre-detonation were sampled again yesterday.
Following the controlled detonation, EPA sampled homes, public parks with playgrounds, and every drinking water intake in the pre-detonation plume model. Today, EPA personnel and contractors will be specifically sampling schools, playgrounds, daycares, churches, and more in the region.
Partnering with the U.S. Department of Agriculture, EPA has collected samples from nearby farms both before and after the controlled detonation.
Extensive sampling of water in the Mississippi River watershed has been completed both before and after the controlled detonation.
EPA Will Complete Air and Water Sampling Today:
Agency staff completed their first round of drinking water sampling on the day of detonation and will conduct a second round today. Further sampling in agricultural ponds in Missouri will continue.
Summa cans
for volatile organic compounds were deployed at 4am CT on the morning of detonation and were collected at 4:00am CT this morning.
Air cassettes that sample for lead will be replaced to continue sampling today, while EPA’s continuous air monitoring systems continue to run. Live information from these systems can be found at
epa.gov/I
owaData
.
Soil and dust sampling is expected to conclude today.
Sampling Results:
The agency is expecting to start receiving lab results on post-detonation sampling as early as tomorrow. Samples have already been shipped to EPA’s Region 7 lab in Kansas City, Kansas, our mobile labs on-site, and private labs under contract to support our response efforts.
170 pieces of equipment have been mobilized for this effort to track and report timely data, including a mobile command center, three mobile lands, and one air toxics lab.
Additional Testing Outside of Lead:
While monitoring for lead exposure, EPA has also been testing volatile organic compounds and particulate matter. The agency is testing for everything we were anticipating could potentially be released as a result of the controlled detonation.
The Trump EPA will continue its presence on the ground for as long as needed to ensure the proper environmental monitoring is taking place following the successful controlled detonation. The agency will continue communicating with community members and the media as we have more information to provide.
You can stay informed by visiting
http://epa.gov/IowaData
or calling 833-649-9624. We request that use of the EPA hotline number remain limited to individuals in the local area.

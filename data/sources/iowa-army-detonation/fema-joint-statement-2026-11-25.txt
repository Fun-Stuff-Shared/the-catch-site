Joint Statement on the Controlled Detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant
English
Release Date
Release Number
November 25, 2026
Release Date:
November 25, 2026
Today, the U.S. Army, HHS, EPA, ATF, FEMA, and USDA successfully carried out a controlled, mitigated detonation of the unstable lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. In one of the most complex mitigation efforts in history, our agencies coordinated at an unprecedented level and eliminated the threat posed by this decaying material. All ongoing air monitoring results available at the time of this statement show no detectable lead.
The shelter-in-place order has been lifted, and Iowans in the affected area can safely resume normal activities.
Our foremost priority was to protect the public from a spontaneous, uncontrolled explosion. Under the steady leadership of President Trump and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, experts from our agencies jointly determined that a controlled, mitigated detonation during optimal weather conditions was the safest way to address the threat. We worked together seamlessly to plan, carry out, and monitor the situation.
Our teams and resources remain on the ground. We will continue to work with state and local officials, share sampling results as they become available, and support the community and region as needed.

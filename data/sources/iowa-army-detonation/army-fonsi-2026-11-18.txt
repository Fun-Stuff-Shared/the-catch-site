                 MITIGATED FINDING OF NO SIGNIFICANT IMPACT
                       for the Lead Azide Risk Mitigation at
                           Iowa Army Ammunition Plant
                                  Middleton, Iowa

1. Title of the Proposed Action

Controlled Detonation and Environmental Risk Mitigation of Unserviceable Lead Azide at
the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP), Middletown, Iowa.

2. Purpose and Need for Action
The purpose of the Proposed Action is to safely, permanently, and definitively eliminate
the immediate explosive safety threat posed by a degraded, unserviceable stockpile of
lead azide at the IAAAP while utilizing engineered mitigations and operational controls to
prevent significant impacts to human health, safety, and the surrounding environment.
The Proposed Action is urgently needed because the stockpile, consisting of drums
containing bulk lead azide, has experienced severe chemical degradation and
crystallization over time while submerged in an ethanol/water storage solution.
Temperature fluctuations have caused the lead azide to dissolve and recrystallize into its
pure form as needles and platelets, which are acutely sensitive to shock, friction, static
electricity, and heat. Due to this instability, the material was legally determined in March
2026 to be an unserviceable hazardous waste exhibiting characteristics of reactivity
(D003) and toxicity (D008).

Department of Defense Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) technical personnel
inspected the drums and issued a formal safety directive to not move the material.
Movement, physical handling, or off-site transit over public transportation routes presents
an unacceptable risk of accidental, catastrophic detonation. Leaving the stockpile in place
without destruction presents an ongoing, escalating threat of uncontrolled explosion,
prolonged regulatory non-compliance under the Resource Conservation and Recovery
Act (RCRA), persistent facility-wide stop-work disruptions, and potential loss of human
life. Therefore, permanent in-place disposal under controlled conditions is necessary.

3. Description of the Proposed Action (Alternative 1 - Selected Alternative)
Under the Proposed Action (Alternative 1 - Mitigated Controlled Detonation), the U.S.
Army will execute a single, coordinated operational mission to destroy the drums of lead
azide in place within the two concrete earth-covered magazines (ECMs) where they
currently reside:
   •   Execution and Phasing: Execution is structured through an integrated multiorganizational command structure:
          o   Phase 1 (Pre-Treatment): Finalization of safety buffers, public outreach,
              utility isolation, baseline environmental sampling, pre-treatment vegetation
              clearing outside active ecological windows, and barrier construction.

          o   Phase 2 (Active Treatment): Military EOD personnel will configure donor
              charges on each drum and execute a simultaneous controlled detonation of
              both magazines.
          o   Phase 3 (Initial Cleanup / Short-Term Closure): Qualified environmental and
              UXO contractors will enter following a 24-hour safety hold to assess site
              conditions, clear potential energetic anomalies, delineate debris fallout
              zones, and excavate heavily impacted crater soils.
          o   Phase 4 (Long-Term Site Remediation & Closure): Systematic debris
              recovery, soil stabilization (if required for D008 waste), transport to licensed
              RCRA disposal facilities, backfilling, grading, and final closure certification.

   •   Integrated Engineering Blast Controls: The ECMs are enclosed within
       engineered blast containment barriers comprising approximately 19,000 cubic
       yards (23,688 tons) of lime-fill foundation and 300 sand-filled HESCO barriers
       (2,390 cubic yards / 3,225 tons of washed sand). The barriers incorporate an
       engineered 28-foot venting aperture oriented to direct blast overpressure and
       ejecta toward the south and southwest, away from primary installation
       infrastructure and populated areas.
4. Alternatives Considered

The U.S. Army evaluated multiple Courses of Action (COAs) against five screening
criteria: (1) Personnel Safety (Handling Minimization); (2) Environmental Protection and
Regulatory Compliance; (3) Public Health and Off-Installation Impacts; (4) Mission
Compatibility and Timeline; and (5) Technical Feasibility.
   a. Alternative 1: Mitigated Controlled Detonation (Proposed Action - Selected):
      Satisfies all screening criteria by eliminating personnel handling of crystallized
      drums, employing engineered containment, strictly enforcing weather criteria, and
      binding the Army to comprehensive post-blast RCRA cleanup.
   b. No Action Alternative: The unserviceable lead azide would remain indefinitely in
      storage. This alternative was carried forward as an analytical baseline pursuant to
      CEQ regulations. It fails the Purpose and Need because it allows the explosion
      hazard to escalate, guarantees persistent RCRA violation, and risks catastrophic
      accidental detonation.

   c. Chemical Neutralization (Eliminated from Detailed Study): Evaluated adding
      caustic chemical reagents to dissolve and decompose the azide over 7 to 22
      operational days. Eliminated because achieving chemical contact requires
      opening drums and physically agitating shock-sensitive crystals, creating an
      extreme and unacceptable risk of fatal accidental detonation.
   d. Transport to On-Site Open Detonation (OD) Unit (Eliminated from Detailed
      Study): Evaluated moving the drums across installation roads to the permitted OD

       grounds. Eliminated because loading, transporting, and burying degraded barrels

   e. Unmitigated Controlled Detonation (Eliminated from Detailed Study):

5. Summary of Environmental Consequences
The Environmental Assessment (EA) evaluated the potential impacts of Alternative 1
across all relevant environmental resources:
   •   Land Use: Temporary operational restriction of the storage yard during setup and
       detonation. Following two-phase remediation and site grading, the footprint will be
       restored to safe, usable condition. Long-term impacts are beneficial.

   •   Air Quality: Detonation will generate a transient, localized plume containing lead
       particulates and combustion byproducts. Unmitigated modeling demonstrates
       concentrations will remain below life-threatening (PAC-2/PAC-3) thresholds. With
       water misting suppression, optimal atmospheric mixing heights, and strict
       meteorological go/no-go limits (winds >15 mph, no inversions, no precipitation),
       impacts will be minor and short-term. Long-term regional attainment of National
       Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) will not be impacted.
   •   Noise (Acoustics): A single, instantaneous impulse noise event will occur. Peak
       impulse sound levels (PK15[met]) will be substantially attenuated by the massive
       sand/lime blast containment barriers, existing earth cover, and surrounding
       topography. The event will not alter the annual C-Weighted Day-Night Level
       (CDNL) or modify installation noise zones defined in the IAAAP Environmental
       Noise Management Program (ENMP).

   •   Hazardous Materials and Waste: Permanently destroys existing quantity of
       reactive (D003) hazardous waste, abating an imminent threat. Secondary
       Demolition Debris (DD) will be sampled via Toxicity Characteristic Leaching
       Procedure (TCLP). Non-hazardous debris will be managed as solid waste;
       hazardous debris will be stabilized on-site or manifested to a permitted RCRA
       Subtitle C facility. Long-term impacts are highly beneficial.

   •   Water Resources: No direct discharges to surface waters. Concrete foundation
       slabs and the lime-fill foundation (which elevates soil pH and binds lead)
       significantly impede vertical migration to the surficial overburden and bedrock
       aquifers. Runoff will be controlled via an Iowa NPDES General Permit No. 2
       Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP). Pre- and post-treatment sampling
       of surface water, sediment, and existing groundwater wells will verify protection of
       local water resources.

   •   Utilities and Infrastructure: Non-essential overhead power lines will be deenergized prior to detonation. Primary blast overpressure is directed away from
       internal rail lines by the barrier venting design. Damaged localized gravel access
       roads will be rebuilt during Phase 4 closure.

   •   Cultural Resources: Demolition of the two 1940 ECMs is programmatically
       cleared and previously mitigated under Section 4.2.6 of the Program Comment for
       Army Warfighting Readiness and Associated Infrastructure (Tier 4 properties).
       Standard inadvertent discovery protocols protect unanticipated archaeological
       resources.
   •   Biological Resources: No forest canopy or tree removal will occur during active
       operations. Pre-treatment brush and tree clearance within the fence line was
       executed and documented under a separate Record of Environmental
       Consideration (REC) in compliance with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS)
       time-of-year restrictions (prohibiting cutting during the April 1 to October 31 bat
       active season). Pre-staged fire crews and rapid post-blast soil removal ensure no
       adverse impacts to the Indiana Bat, Northern Long-eared Bat, or other protected
       species.
   •   Socioeconomics: A temporary restriction of installation access and execution of
       an outer non-essential personnel safety exclusion zone (6,269 feet) will protect
       the workforce and adjacent residents. Agricultural outlease holders will be prenotified, and post-blast soil testing in nearby fields will ensure no loss of crop
       productivity. Eliminating the catastrophic hazard preserves the long-term defense
       mission and local employment base.

6. Enforceable Mitigation Commitments and Best Management Practices
To ensure that environmental and public health impacts remain below significant levels,
the U.S. Army commits to implementing the following mandatory mitigation measures and
Best Management Practices (BMPs):

Environmental and Air Quality Protection
A. Engineered Blast Containment: The Army will utilize constructed protective
   earthworks surrounding both magazines consisting of approximately 19,000 cubic
   yards of lime-fill foundation and 300 sand-filled HESCO units, configured with a 28foot venting gap oriented toward the southwest to deflect blast overpressure away
   from infrastructure and receptors.

B. Fugitive Dust Suppression: Water cannons or misters will thoroughly wet the
   magazine surfaces, containment berms, and adjacent soil prior to detonation to
   suppress particulate kickout, and will be utilized during post-blast excavation and
   debris handling.

C. Meteorological "No-Go" Mandate: The detonation will occur only during daylight
   hours under verified atmospheric conditions: surface wind speeds strictly <15mph,
   zero precipitation forecasted, no electrical storms, cloud ceilings > 2,000 feet, and
   verified absence of temperature inversions. Real-time conditions will be validated via
   on-site meteorological stations.
D. Stormwater Runoff Control (SWPPP): An Iowa NPDES General Permit No. 2
   compliant SWPPP will be implemented, incorporating sediment basins, silt fencing,
   and earthen check dams down-gradient of the treatment area prior to detonation.
   Runoff controls will be inspected and maintained throughout site reclamation.
Hazardous Waste and Site Closure

E. Execution of Two-Phase Closure Plan: Remediation of Demolition Debris (DD) and
   soils will proceed systematically from the outer assessment areas inward. Soils
   exceeding the project cleanup goal, the EPA Regional Screening Level (RSL) for
   industrial soil of 800mg/kg for lead, will be excavated to clean native soil.

F. TCLP Waste Characterization and Stabilization: Excavated materials will undergo
   TCLP testing. Waste exceeding 5.0mg leachable lead will be classified as D008
   hazardous waste and will either undergo bench-tested chemical stabilization to
   eliminate leachability prior to non-hazardous disposal, or will be manifested for direct
   disposal at a licensed RCRA Subtitle C facility.
G. Comprehensive Environmental Sampling (SAP): The Army will execute multiincremental soil sampling across 44 Decision Units extending up to 3,000 feet, preand post-treatment sampling at seven co-located surface water/sediment stations,
   and sampling of up to six existing groundwater monitoring wells to confirm
   containment.
H. Closure Certification Report (CCR): A formal CCR documenting all analytical
   verifications, manifests, excavation depths, and restoration records will be prepared
   within 60 days of demobilization and submitted to EPA Region 7.
Safety, Noise, and Community Protections
I. Explosive Safety Exclusion Zones: Strict perimeter security will be enforced
   establishing a 956-foot inner perimeter restricted exclusively to EOD personnel and a
   6,269-foot outer exclusion zone restricting all non-essential military and civilian
   personnel. Nearby homeowners will be notified and recommended for temporary
   evacuation.
J. Three-Phase Community Engagement: The Public Affairs Office will conduct (1)
   pre-detonation briefings for local elected officials and a public open house; (2) timely
   pre-blast alerts via broadcast media and notification systems; and (3) a post-blast
   public confirmation release and official after-action review.

K. Noise Complaint Management: Detonation timing will adhere to ENMP favorable
   atmospheric guidelines to prevent sound focusing, and any received inquiries or noise
   concerns will be addressed through the formal ENMP complaint process.
L. Site Safety, Health, and Training: All field personnel will hold current 40-hour
   HAZWOPER certification and maintain qualifications under DDESB TP-18.
   Operations will adhere strictly to an approved Site Safety and Health Plan (SSHP) and
   Project Contingency Plan, including 24-hour post-blast hold periods, pre-staged fire
   suppression, and emergency medical routing.

7. Finding of No Significant Impact and Recommendation
Based on the comprehensive environmental analysis presented in the accompanying
Environmental Assessment, and in accordance with the Council on Environmental Quality
(CEQ) Regulations for Implementing the Procedural Provisions of NEPA (40 CFR Parts
1500–1508); the Department of War (DoW) NEPA Implementing Procedures (September
2026); and Army Regulation (AR) 200-1, Environmental Protection and Enhancement,
the U.S. Army concludes that the Proposed Action (Alternative 1 – Mitigated Controlled
Detonation) does not constitute a major federal action significantly affecting the quality of
the human or natural environment under the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969
(42 U.S.C. § 4321 et seq.).

The implementation of the specified engineered blast controls, strict meteorological
constraints, comprehensive post-detonation soil and water remediation, and enforceable
mitigation commitments detailed in this document avoids, minimizes, and reduces all
potential environmental, acoustic, and public health impacts to a less-than-significant
level. Furthermore, the action permanently resolves an imminent and catastrophic
explosive safety hazard and restores vital military-industrial readiness to the installation.
Therefore, the preparation of an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) is not required.

It is recommended that the Commander, Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, approve the
Proposed Action and sign this Mitigated Finding of No Significant Impact.

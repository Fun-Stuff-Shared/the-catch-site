Important: Planned Lead Azide Detonation
Updated November 24, 2026, 4:24 p.m.
Lead Azide Callout
Important: Information regarding the detonation, potential exposure areas, and recommended precautions may change. Local public health agencies should refer to the most current U.S. Army notifications and Iowa HHS guidance when communicating with residents and community partners.
Situation Overview
The Army announced during a press conference the morning of Tuesday, November 24, that the planned detonation of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown will take place at 9 a.m. on Wednesday, November 24.
FEMA has shared that some residents within 5 miles SW of the plant may be evacuated, and residents 5 to 20 miles SW should stay indoors with windows closed to avoid airborne lead dust, noise and vibration. For evacuation details call FEMA: 1-800-621-3362.
The Iowa Department of Health and Human Services continues to evaluate potential public health impacts and is coordinating with federal partners, including the CDC. Our priority remains protecting the health and safety of Iowans and ensuring timely access to accurate information and coordination.
To help Iowans prepare for the detonation and impact, Iowa HHS is providing and updating precautionary guidance to communities, monitoring available information in coordination with other state agencies, and keeping local public health partners informed. Public health guidance will continue to be updated as more information is available.
A dedicated website is available for Iowans seeking more information about the U.S. Army’s planned detonation. The site directs citizens to information from a variety of sources, including the U.S. Army, EPA, USDA, state agencies, and other resources.
Information about the planned detonation is available through: ASC Installation Community Notifications.
Current Iowa HHS Guidance and Safety Tips
The Iowa Department of Health and Human Services (Iowa HHS) has provided the following safety tips:
Stay indoors and keep windows and doors closed for 24 to 48 hours after the detonation if you are in a potentially impacted area.
Turn off or place heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems in recirculation mode, minimize outdoor air intake, and maximize indoor air filtration during the potential exposure period.
Consider using a portable air cleaner with a HEPA filter to help reduce airborne particles indoors.
Monitor local news and official updates for information about the plume and potentially affected areas. The U.S. Army will provide updates at Army Installation Community Notifications.
Wash outdoor surfaces when you see visible dust using soap and water.
If you need to drive through an area impacted by dust, set your car’s AC/heater to recirculate. Afterward, you can take your car to the car wash and replace the cabin filter, if needed. A high efficiency cabin filter will capture fine particulate matter.
Visit your healthcare provider if you have health concerns.
Lead is a toxic metal that is harmful if inhaled or swallowed and can pose serious health risks. There is no safe level of lead that has been identified, particularly for young children and pregnant women. If you are concerned about possible lead exposure, contact your health care provider to discuss whether blood lead testing is appropriate.
Remember to wash your hands before eating and drinking and take your shoes off at the entrance of homes to minimize tracking.
Information about lead exposure, testing, and prevention is available through Iowa HHS' Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program and through the reportable environmental & occupational diseases, poisonings, and conditions webpage. Lead exposure may not cause immediate symptoms, which is why blood lead testing is used to identify exposure. Individuals with questions or concerns should consult their health care provider and review available Iowa HHS resources.

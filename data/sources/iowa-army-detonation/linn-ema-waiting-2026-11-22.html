NEWS RELEASE: State, local officials still waiting on detonation details from U.S. Army

Sunday, November 22, 2026 at 5:16 PM

LINN COUNTY, IOWA – November 22, 2026 – Officials with the Iowa Homeland Security and Emergency Management (HSEM) and Iowa Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) report they still do not have details on a detonation date from the U.S. Army. Governor Reynolds’ Office announced this afternoon that the date and time of the detonation is pending an announcement by the Army. However, in a court filing by the Army on Saturday, November 21, the Army states they intend to act by November 24, 2026, “to protect the public from the more significant threat from an uncontrolled explosion."

The U.S. Army has not released updated plume modeling since an advisory dated November 18. Models are subject to change based on actual weather conditions on the day of the detonation. Weather forecasts for both November 23 and November 24 place Linn County outside any likely plume area.

Iowa HHS has not recommended specific protective actions for individuals or animals outside the projected plume area. HHS has issued general precautions for those within areas identified by the Army's models.

The Governor’s Office today also announced a dedicated website has been created for Iowans seeking more information about the U.S. Army’s planned detonation. The site directs the public to information from a variety of sources, including the U.S. Army, EPA, USDA, state agencies, and other resources. 

The U.S. Army originally planned two controlled detonations of degraded explosive material at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown, Iowa, for Friday, November 20. A federal court of appeals put a temporary pause on the detonation. State officials report the U.S. Army does have the legal ability to sidestep the court stay in the case of imminent danger.

The Linn County Emergency Management Agency and Linn County Public Health will continue coordinating with state and federal partners and communicate relevant developments and any recommended actions for Linn County residents.


What can residents do in the meantime?

As with any developing situation, residents are encouraged to stay informed through official sources and maintain general emergency preparedness.

  • Register for emergency notifications through Alert Iowa.
  • Familiarize yourself with your home's heating and ventilation controls.
  • Review your household emergency plan.
  • Review general emergency preparedness resources.
  • Follow official sources for information and updates, including the U.S. Army's Iowa Army Ammunition Plant updates.

 

Emergencies that require residents to temporarily remain indoors are a good example of why it may be helpful to keep basic emergency supplies readily available. These include:

  • Drinking water and nonperishable food
  • Essential medications and medical supplies
  • Pet food and supplies
  • Phone chargers and backup batteries
  • Plastic sheeting, scissors, and duct tape, should officials recommend temporarily sealing openings

For more information about preparing for emergencies, including a comprehensive emergency-supply checklist, visit Ready.gov/kit.

Iowa HHS has made public health guidance available for individuals in areas identified as potentially impacted. The Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship has also issued guidance for livestock producers near the planned detonation site.

 

What if conditions change?

The U.S. Army is responsible for evaluating potential impacts associated with the operation and notifying potentially affected communities in accordance with EPA permit requirements.

Should new information indicate potential impacts to Linn County, Linn County Emergency Management and Linn County Public Health will coordinate with state and federal partners to evaluate local response needs and communicate any recommended protective actions to residents.

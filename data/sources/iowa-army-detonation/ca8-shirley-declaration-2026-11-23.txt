I, Major General Eric P. Shirley, declares as follows:
1.

The facts set forth in this declaration are based on my personal knowledge and, if

called to testify as a witness, I could and would competently testify thereto under oath.
2.

I am the Commander of Army Sustainment Command (ASC), which I assumed

in July 2025. In June 2026, through an Army reorganization, I assumed responsibility for the
now former Joint Munitions Command and the entities under that command, including all Army
ammunition plants.
3.

As the ASC Commander, I have been closely following the situation at the Iowa

Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP).
4.

The lead azide has been stored at IAAAP since 2009 in two Earth Covered

Magazines (ECMs). Lead azide is stored in a mixture of water and ethanol to keep it stable, but
over the decades of storage, due to seasonal temperature changes, the lead azide has degraded,
causing the formation of needle-shaped crystals that are highly unstable due to a substantial
increase in the lead azide’s sensitivity. Because of this crystallization, the barrel lids are no
longer safe to remove, which prevents the water/ethanol solution in the barrels from being
refilled. This has caused the solution to be more susceptible to freezing within the barrels, which

small amount of friction can break the crystal formation, causing a chain reaction, which

there is an imminent risk of an unintentional detonation and immediate action is needed.
5.

This declaration provides clarifying information as to why the date of the

controlled detonation of the lead azide explosives was changed from Tuesday, November 24,
2026, until Wednesday, November 25, 2026.
6.

The change in detonation timing allowed for increased mitigation measures to be

put in place to better protect human health, public safety, property, and the environment from the
controlled detonation. Based on scientific judgment, it is predicted that these additional measures
should decrease the lead emission fraction to .26 or lower. The emission fraction is the measure
of lead put into the atmosphere during the detonation.
7.

The adjusted time also allowed the Army to secure highly advanced materials

from the Department of Energy, to include ballistic gels and foam, which are designed to reduce
the particulate matter caused from high explosive detonations. This material will be applied
before the controlled detonation.
8.

Further, the extra time allowed the Army to construct additional barriers around

the ECMs, reducing the lead dispersion caused by the detonation. These barriers include a water
wall consisting of thousands of gallons of water that help absorb the explosion and minimize any
lead dust created by the detonation.
9.

The change in date also allows the Army to conduct the detonation during

favorable weather conditions. The Army has worked closely with the Defense Threat Reduction
Agency (DTRA) and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) to model

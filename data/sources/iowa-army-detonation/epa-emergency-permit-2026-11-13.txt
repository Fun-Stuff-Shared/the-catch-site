The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) issues this Emergency Permit for Storage and
Treatment (“Emergency Permit”) to the U.S. Department of the Army (Permittee) to store and treat
lead azide—a hazardous waste that exhibits the characteristic of reactivity (energetic) and the
characteristic of toxicity for lead—at the lowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAAP). IAAAP encompasses
approximately 19,000 acres located at 17571 Des Moines County Highway 79, Middletown, lowa, and
all contiguous property at this location under the control of the Permittee (hereinafter “Permitted
Facility”). The recrystallization of the lead azide during long-term storage has increased the material’s
sensitivity to friction and shock, creating a highly unstable and dangerous situation. This Emergency
Permit is issued upon the EPA’s finding of an imminent and substantial endangerment to human health
and the environment pursuant to the Solid Waste Disposal Act (Sections 3004(a), (u), (v), and 3005 of
the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, or RCRA (42 U.S.C. §§ 6924(a), (u), (v); 6925)) and
regulations promulgated thereunder (Title 40 of the Code of Federal Regulations (C.F.R) Sections 264 —
Subpart X, 270.61). Section 3005(c)(3) of RCRA requires that each Permit issued under Section 3005
shall contain such terms and conditions as the EPA determines necessary to protect human health and
the environment.

This Emergency Permit is required to protect human health and the environment and to authorize the
continued storage of this hazardous waste in place until the operating conditions, as outlined in this
Emergency Permit, allow for the implementation of the treatment activity to safely manage the
explosive hazard. To maximize protection of public health, the Emergency Permit requires the
Permittee to model depositional impacts and airborne plume migration prior to any targeted
detonation date. The required modeling must utilize a conservative coding input to ensure that the
Permittee’s modeling does not under-predict the extent of potential impact. Technical experts from
both the EPA and the Army agree that the modeling on which public notifications will be based is not
necessarily representative of actual conditions that may result from the treatment activity. The EPA
acknowledges the Army’s real-world experience and expert judgment, presented in its permit
application, that the realistic airborne release of lead will, in fact, be less than 100 percent due to
considerable measures the Permittee will implement to mitigate blast effects. However, considering
technical uncertainty regarding a quantifiable reduction of the lead release produced by planned
mitigation measures, the EPA has chosen a conservative Emission Factor to ensure that the prior
notifications are maximally protective of the public.

Appendix B
Air Dispersion and Dust Deposition Modeling Parameters

A. To develop the plume impact estimates required in Emergency Permit Condition IV.G., the
Permittee shall utilize either the Specialized Hazard Assessment Response Capability (SHARC)
model, the Hazard Prediction and Assessment Capability (HPAC) model, or another model
available through the National Atmospheric Release Advisory Center (NARAC), with an Emission
Factor of 1.00 to generate air dispersion and dust deposition modeling as follows:

1. Air Dispersion — Airborne lead shall be modeled to an outer concentration of 0.18 pg/m?.
The model shall be produced to present hourly estimations of the plume’s location and
extent, as calculated from the time of detonation. The hourly estimations shall also depict
relative concentrations within the plume boundary.

2. Dust-Lead Deposition — Two dust-lead deposition isopleths shall be modeled to predicted
deposition concentrations of 2 5 ug/ft? and 2 100 yg/ft?. The dust-lead deposition isopleths
shall be layered on a single figure to identify the relative extent of areas that will potentially
be impacted by the treatment activity.

The isoconcentration maps generated through this modeling shall be appropriately labeled and
of a resolution sufficient to provide members of the public prior knowledge of their potential
for exposure to dust-lead surface deposition resulting from the treatment activity.

B. The isoconcentration map developed in accordance with paragraph A of this Appendix B shall
be publicly distributed in conjunction with narrative advisory messaging consistent with
paragraph B.2. of Appendix C and on the schedule required in Emergency Permit Condition
IV.G.1.

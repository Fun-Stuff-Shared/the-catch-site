   Illinois State Agencies Issue Updates Regarding the Iowa Ammunition
                          Plant Planned Detonation
  Gov. Pritzker Urged Federal Officials for More Transparency as 8th Circuit Court Issues
                       Stay for Monday’s Rescheduled Detonation
Springfield – Today, the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency (IEPA), Illinois Department of Public
Health (IDPH), and Illinois Emergency Management Office of Homeland Security (IEMA-OHS) are issuing
another update to provide as much information and clarity as possible to the public as it becomes
available.

The U.S. Army is planning two controlled detonations of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army
Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. The detonations were planned for Monday, November 23, 2026.
The Eighth Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals issued a stay of the permit for the detonations. As legal
proceedings continue and the Court issues further orders, it is possible that the detonations could
happen early next week.

Earlier today, Governor JB Pritzker today spoke with Acting U.S Secretary of the Army, Adam Telle,
seeking further information and urging transparency related to the planned controlled detonations and
their potential impact on Illinois.

Governor Pritzker is ensuring Illinois officials are closely monitoring court rulings and expect the U.S.
Department of Defense and U.S. Army to comply with court orders. Illinois agencies continue to seek
additional information from federal counterparts to keep Illinois residents informed.

The Illinois EPA and IEMA-OHS are still seeking underlying data for the modeling from the U.S. Army as
they have the expertise to understand the material and better inform the public. Current meteorological
information indicates the plume could have impacts on Western and Central Illinois. Illinois EPA has
requested updated plume modeling. An updated modeling release schedule should be provided,
including for Monday the 23rd. Governor Pritzker also requested this modeling in his call with Acting
Secretary Telles.

Illinois officials previously shared that the detonation of the degraded lead azide would result in lead
dust becoming airborne, potentially impacting residents, farmland, and businesses in Iowa and Illinois.
Illinois is still seeking specifics related to the locations and duration of the air monitoring that is to be
conducted by U.S. agencies. Governor Pritzker was informed by Acting Secretary Telle that the U.S. EPA,

Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and
Explosives (ATF) are prepared to assist state agencies after the planned detonation.

Residents are encouraged to continue monitoring the U.S. Army’s Iowa Army Ammunition Plan
Notification webpage. A FAQ document is also available from the U.S. Army. Residents can file a claim
with the U.S. Army for any alleged damage done because of the detonation. See FAQ document for
details on submitting a claim.

Lead is toxic to humans and animals. Accordingly, members of the public are encouraged to follow all
relevant public health guidance to limit potential exposure. The Illinois Department of Public Health has
shared the following recommendations with local health departments to share with residents:

   •   Stay indoors: Avoid outdoor air by staying indoors on the day of the detonation. Keep windows
       and doors closed for 24–48 hours after the detonation as a precaution, unless updated official
       guidance indicates otherwise.

   •   Use indoor air recirculation: Residential furnaces may continue operating. Facilities with outside
       air intakes, including schools and commercial buildings, should consider temporarily switching to
       recirculation when safe.

   •   Improve indoor filtration: Consider using a portable air cleaner equipped with a HEPA filter.

   •   Monitor official updates: Follow local news and the U.S. Army's website for plume updates and
       safety instructions.

   •   Clean surfaces safely: Once the U.S. Army determines the airborne exposure period has ended,
       wet-clean potentially affected surfaces, including playground equipment, toys, and windowsills,
       using soap and water. Avoid dry sweeping that may redistribute dust. Consider washing your
       vehicle and replacing the cabin air filter if needed.

   •   Limit unnecessary travel: Avoid potentially affected areas when possible. Use vehicle air
       recirculation if driving through impacted areas.

   •   Practice good hygiene: Wash hands before eating or drinking and remove shoes when entering
       homes to limit tracking dust indoors.

   •   Seek medical guidance: Contact a healthcare provider if you have concerns about health-
       related effects from lead exposure.

   •   Livestock and agricultural exposure: Illinois Dept. Agriculture has issued guidance for Livestock
       Producers

   •   Exposure through fishing: Follow IDPH guidance on lead safety for Fishermen.

Blood Lead Testing and Health Concerns

Lead exposure may not cause immediate symptoms. Blood lead testing is the only recommended
method for evaluating potential exposure.

   •   Individuals concerned about exposure should contact their healthcare provider to discuss
       whether testing is appropriate.

   •   Children younger than six and pregnant women warrant particular attention because of their
       increased vulnerability.

Healthcare providers can help determine appropriate testing and follow-up based on an individual's
exposure history. Refer to the Illinois Childhood Lead Risk Questionnaire. IDPH will provide additional
testing guidance as needed following the detonation.

Pet owners should contact their local veterinarian to determine if testing is warranted.

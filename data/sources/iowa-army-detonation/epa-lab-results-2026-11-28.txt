EPA Releases Initial Water Source Lab Results Indicating Wednesday’s Controlled Detonation in Iowa Did Not Result in An Elevation of Lead in Water Bodies that Serve as Sources of Drinking Water
Agency Also Provides Update on Sampling Collection and Methodology
November 28, 2026
Contact Information
EPA Press Office
(
press@epa.gov
)
WASHINGTON
– Today, U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) released initial drinking water lab results and provided the latest public update on agency efforts to sample air, water, soil, and dust before and after the controlled detonation of lead azide by the U.S. Army on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa.
Initial Results
At the time of this press release, initial lab results from EPA’s post-detonation sampling show that the controlled detonation did not result in an elevation of lead in water bodies that serve as sources of drinking water. EPA found no lead in the 38 water samples taken post-detonation at public water supplies, which included samples of finished treated drinking water and untreated water at the intake. This builds on EPA’s air monitoring operations which reported 0.0 µg/m3 readings (otherwise referred to as zero detect or non-detect) of airborne lead for 30 consecutive hours after the controlled detonation using the Xact air monitoring systems.
EPA sampled 35 drinking water sources across Iowa, Illinois, and Missouri before the controlled detonation to set a baseline. Though none of these locations were within the area that the plume actually traveled, and all samples were from untreated source water, EPA is sharing the data publicly to ensure transparency.
As part of this sampling, EPA found four locations pre-detonation that had low levels of lead:
Mark Twain Lake, Missouri – 1.62 parts per billion (ppb)
Illinois River, Illinois – 1.13 ppb
Mississippi River, Illinois – 1.36 ppb
Mississippi River, Illinois – 2.12 ppb
These levels are all significantly below EPA’s lead action level of 15ppb for drinking water, and EPA expects water treatment to further reduce lead levels prior to being distributed to people’s taps. The lead was present in the samples prior to Wednesday’s controlled detonation, and, therefore, not caused by the explosion.
While EPA cannot determine the original source of lead in those waters, the agency notes that lead and lead compounds have been historically used in many applications, including in paint, leaded gasoline, fishing tackle, ammunition, mining, industry, and consumer products.
EPA will work with the states to determine if additional sampling is needed at any of those sites.
As of the time of this release, the agency is preparing to receive, validate, and release post-detonation data on water from taps, soil from structures and non-structures, and dust both inside and on the exterior of structures, and air samples. As these sample results return from the labs and are validated over the coming days, they will be transparently posted at
epa.gov/IowaData
.
Sampling Collection Statistics:
EPA personnel and assets collected a total of over 3,200 samples of air, water, soil, and dust in the surrounding areas. 1,632 samples were collected pre-detonation. 1,607 post-detonation samples have been collected as of today at 5:45pm CT. As of this latest update, 187 structures that were sampled pre-detonation have also been sampled post-detonation. Teams on the ground have also sampled a combination of 27 public parks with playgrounds, schools, daycares, and churches. EPA staff have also been collecting data on farms, drinking water systems, and across the Mississippi watershed.
At this point, EPA has completed post-detonation sampling at 88% of homes that were sampled pre-detonation. Some sampling teams have not yet been able to access certain private properties due to residents not being at home.
As samples have been collected, agency teams have been
quickly preparing and shipping
them off to EPA’s Region 7 lab in Kansas City, Kansas, our mobile labs on-site, and private contract labs being utilized for this effort.
Sampling Strategy:
EPA deployed a robust sample collection strategy to assess potential impacts from the controlled detonation of the 68 barrels of degraded lead azide, focusing on lead as the primary contaminant of concern, while including selected volatile and semivolatile organic compounds, after soliciting input from experts throughout the agency. This strategy was developed in close coordination with what the U.S. Army indicated were the primary concerns, as well as other information provided to EPA.
Air:
Based on the controlled detonation method selected by the U.S. Army, EPA identified the target compound in air as lead, with volatile organic compounds (VOCs) as additional compounds of interest that needed to be tested for before, during, and after detonation. EPA has done so accordingly.
For 30 consecutive hours after the controlled detonation, EPA’s air monitoring operations reported 0.0 µg/m3 readings (otherwise referred to as zero detect or non-detect) of airborne lead using the Xact air monitoring systems. Since the time of the controlled detonation, EPA has reported this data to the public in practically real time at
epa.gov/IowaData
.
On Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (the day of the controlled detonation), these instruments were collecting data on a five-minute average, set from 7:00am CT until 2:00pm CT. Outside of this time frame, Xact air monitoring systems were collecting data on hourly averages. These Xact units were deactivated at 3:00pm CT on Thursday, November 26, due to the consistent 0.0 µg/m3 readings.
The Xact instruments were utilizing Total Suspended Particulate (TSP) inlets to capture any and all suspended particles. While Particulate Matter (PM) 10 and PM 2.5 inlets exist, TSP inlets were used in this sampling event to ensure larger particles would be captured if detected.
Additionally, the agency used Summa cans for VOCs.
As part of the AreaRAE instrument’s standard sensors, which was continuously monitoring the air, hydrogen sulfide and carbon monoxide were also being monitored. Lead air samples were analyzed in a certified lab part of the National Environmental Laboratory Accreditation Program (NELAP). NELAP is a program that sets uniform quality and performance standards for environmental testing laboratories. VOC air samples will be analyzed on-site by EPA PHILIS mobile lab using method TO-15.
Soil:
EPA has identified the target compound in soil as lead, with additional compounds of interest based on process knowledge related to the disposal method selected by the Army. These compounds, for example, include:
2,6-Dinitrotoluene (CAS 606-20-2)
2,4-Dinitrotoluene (CAS 121-14-2)
Di-n-butyl phthalate (CAS 84-74-2)
Diphenylamine
Compounds of interest will be analyzed in soil samples using method 8270 on-site by the EPA PHILIS mobile lab.
Water and Dust Wipe Sampling:
All water and dust wipe sampling will be analyzed for total lead only.
EPA is working diligently to expeditiously return post-detonation sample results from the laboratories. We will continue to communicate transparently with the public as more data is received and validated.

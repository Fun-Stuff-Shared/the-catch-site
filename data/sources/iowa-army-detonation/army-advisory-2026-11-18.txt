   Public and Media Advisory Regarding the Detonation of Lead Azide at Iowa Army
                      Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa



On Nov. 20 at approximately noon CST, the U.S. Army will conduct two controlled
detonations of degraded lead azide (Pb(N3)2) at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant
located at 17571 DMC Highway 79, Middletown, IA 52638. This detonation has been
planned and approved in coordination with the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency
under the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act. In-place planned detonation by
Explosive Ordnance Disposal experts is the safest way to eliminate the explosive safety
hazard. The degraded lead azide has been deemed unsafe for relocation.

The lead azide barrels will be detonated inside earth-covered magazines made of
concrete, and surrounded with Hesco barriers full of sand which are approximately two
stories high. Before and during the detonation, the soil above the earth-covered
magazines will be thoroughly saturated with water, and water misters will be spraying.
These measures should reduce the amount of lead that migrates from the detonation
through the air off the installation.

Extensive modeling efforts predict that the potential level of lead particles in the air may
be elevated but well below the level at which EPA would require protective actions be
taken. The detonation will result in an air plume with some lead-containing particles
which will disperse in the air and deposit on the ground.

Due to the potential for localized window damage residents near Middletown City Park
and Butterfly Park in Middletown, Iowa, have been identified and asked to vacate their
houses from 8 a.m. CST when the EOD team begins setting up the detonations, to 2
p.m. CST.

While safety measures will be fully enforced, the controlled detonation will cause
temporary noise, ground vibrations, and a dust plume that will contain some lead.
Residents in Middletown will hear a loud noise; feel vibrations from two consecutive,
rapid detonations; and see a plume of dust which is expected to linger for no more than
one hour after the detonation, depending on wind speed.

If Middletown, Iowa, residents need a place to stay from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. CST, they are
invited to come to the Armed Forces Reserve Center at 16815 DMC HWY 34
Middletown, IA 52638. All family members are welcome, including pets. Please ensure
pets are appropriately secured in carrying containers or on leashes. Access to the
Armed Forces Reserve Center is strictly controlled and use of the Armed Forces
Reserve Center for this purpose is limited to identified residents of Middletown, Iowa.

The goal of this advisory is to provide the public with actionable information out of an
abundance of caution to minimize lead exposure. There are two primary pathways of
concern:

      1. Lead exposure by breathing in air after the detonation.
      2. Lead exposure from dust on surfaces.

This release contains two maps; one for airborne exposure to lead dust through
respiration, and one for exposure from lead dust on surfaces. The maps are subject to
change due to the weather.

Updated maps are available at this website: https://www.army.mil/asc#org-iowa-army-
ammunition-plant. Members of the public should check for updates:

          •   6 hours in advance of the detonation and every hour thereafter until the
              detonation.
          •   After the event, the US Army will issue a final estimate of the impacted
              area.

The EPA plays a pivotal role in lead poisoning prevention through a variety of
regulatory measures and enforcement actions aimed at reducing exposure to
various environmental media contaminated with lead, including soil. This detonation
has been planned and approved in coordination with the EPA under the Resource
Conservation and Recovery Act. The Army has taken extraordinary measures to
mitigate the plume through earthworks, soil wetting, and water spraying. Pre-
controlled detonation sampling has taken place; air monitors have been established
for perimeter air monitoring following the event; and environmental sampling of the
blast radius will take place. You can learn more about lead in soil here:
https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-soil

To minimize potential exposure, members of the public who live or work in the affected
areas should consider taking the following actions on the day of the detonation:
   • Consider avoiding the affected area.
   • If you are inside of the affected areas, avoid outdoor air by staying indoors.
   • Keep windows and doors closed.
   • If it is safe to do so, set heating and cooling systems to “recirculate” to minimize
      outdoor air exposure and maximize indoor air filtration until the plume passes by
      your community.
   • Avoid unnecessary travel through impacted areas.
   • After the US Army determines the air exposure period has ended, consider
      cleaning indoor and outdoor surfaces such as play surfaces for children and
      windowsills.
   • If your car was parked outside, consider washing your car at a car wash to
      minimize your contact with potentially exposed surfaces.
   • Learn more about actions you can take to protect you and your family from
      potential exposures to lead at https://www.epa.gov/lead/actions-reduce-potential-
      lead-exposure and https://www.epa.gov/lead/learn-about-lead.

Deposition of lead-containing dust may occur after the detonation on surfaces within
certain areas affected by the detonation plume. All humans, but especially children,
pregnant individuals, older adults, and other sensitive populations should avoid outdoor
activities within affected areas.
   All members of the public who reside or work within this affected area should:

   •   Remain indoors during the potential exposure period if they cannot evacuate
       beforehand;
   •   Close all windows, exterior doors, and other openings during the exposure
       period;
   •   Turn off or place heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems in
       recirculation mode, minimize outdoor air intake, and maximize indoor air
       filtration during the potential exposure period; and
   •   Avoid travelling through the affected area during the potential exposure period.
   •   Wet-clean surfaces that may be contacted by children, including outdoor play
       equipment, toys, tables and chairs, handrails, patios, decks, exterior windowsills,
       and other high-contact surfaces. Such instructions should include specific
       cleaning and dust mitigation procedures to address deposited and airborne lead
       particles which minimizes overall human exposure to lead.

Updated plume models will be released the morning of Nov. 20.

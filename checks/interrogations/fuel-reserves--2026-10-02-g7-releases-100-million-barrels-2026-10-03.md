This is a completeness audit of a finished page, so I'll follow the story-completeness method: reconstruct what a reader should have been given, then check the page against primary sources rather than rewriting it.The page’s frame is the October 2 G7 wording versus Trump’s “massive diesel” post. I’ll check that record, then look for the actors, numbers, and same-week releases it leaves out.The joint statement is only part of the record. The pressure campaign, the price move on the day, and what officials said outside the communiqué are the gaps I still need to pin down.The page is a close reading of the communiqué's wording. It is materially incomplete on who was in the meeting, what Washington had demanded before that wording, why diesel is scarce, and what prices and the US reserve did the same week. The arithmetic on the cited FRED rows checks out. Those rows are the wrong prices for October 2.

1. The meeting was not a full leaders' call, which the page's "G7 leaders said" never qualifies. The IEA's own account of the October 2 call lists Donald Trump, Friedrich Merz, Mark Carney, Giorgia Meloni, and Ursula von der Leyen, but the UK as Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband and Japan as Vice Minister Kazuya Endo. Miliband was standing in because Prime Minister Andy Burnham was at his father's funeral. A reader needs that to know whose word the statement is.

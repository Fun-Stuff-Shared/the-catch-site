## The review as returned

I'll audit the September jobs page against the record: what it leaves out, what it asserts without support, and which primary sources it never used. Loading the completeness-audit method first so the gaps are checked against the sources, not guessed.The page is framed as a significance-and-survey check of the September payroll print. I'll reconstruct the week from the BLS release, official reactions, and the market and economist record, then list only what that record adds.The release sits inside a midterm, a rate decision, and a forecast miss. I'm reading the bureau's own tables and the official reactions next, so the gaps are the ones a reader cannot get from the page.The page is right about the headline counts and the bureau’s “changed little” language. It leaves out the week those counts sat in: a forecast miss below the published range, an October rate decision the markets repriced, and the last jobs report before the November 3 midterms.

1. The 29,000 gain was below the bottom of the range the page already quotes, not merely short of the consensus. Reuters’ pre-release survey ran from 35,000 to 180,000, and Bloomberg said the print missed every estimate in its survey. A reader needs that because “short of the surveys” still sounds like a normal miss. Reuters, October 2, 2026, https://www.reuters.com/business/us-job-growth-slows-sharply-september-unemployment-rate-rises-42-2026-10-02/ ; Bloomberg, October 2, 2026, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/us-firms-add-just-29-000-jobs-unemployment-rate-ticks-up

2. Prediction markets were positioned for a beat. On September 29, Kalshi traders put almost 60 percent odds on payrolls above 90,000 and about even odds on above 100,000; Polymarket was about even on a six-figure print. Both settle to the BLS number. CNBC, September 29, 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/prediction-market-traders-september-jobs-report-will-beat-estimates.html

3. Mohamed El-Erian’s own pre-release post expected about 85,000 jobs, a 4.1 percent unemployment rate, and 0.3 percent monthly earnings growth. The release was 29,000, 4.2 percent, and 0.1 percent. His follow-up is the clean public record of that miss and of the participation rise. Posts, October 2, 2026, https://x.com/elerianm/status/2105986502068941125 and https://x.com/elerianm/status/2105999941118136339

4. The market reaction is absent. Stocks rose (Reuters’ close: Dow 51,176.96, up 0.49 percent; S&P 500 7,722.72, up 0.73 percent; Nasdaq 27,190.86, up 1.19 percent). October hike odds fell as low as about 12 percent, then settled near 23 percent, against about 64 percent a week earlier, after cooler inflation had already cut them from about 70 percent. Yields dipped and then resumed a selloff that has the 10-year near multi-year highs. Without that path, the jobs number has no consequence. Reuters, October 2, 2026, https://www.reuters.com/business/wall-st-futures-gain-yields-oil-prices-ease-ahead-jobs-report-2026-10-02/ and https://www.reuters.com/business/view-soft-september-jobs-report-sends-markets-higher-2026-10-02/ ; El-Erian on the two-year, October 2, 2026, https://x.com/elerianm/status/2106008429718364258

5. The September 16 decision is only a quarter-point sentence. It was unanimous, 12–0, the first hike in three years, and Chair Kevin Warsh told the press conference the jobless rate near 4.1 percent and claims were “consistent with full employment,” while inflation risk was to the upside. The Summary of Economic Projections median still had unemployment at 4.1 percent and the funds rate at 4.1 percent at year-end, which is one more quarter-point hike. He did not submit a projection of his own. That is the baseline this report challenged. Statement, September 16, 2026, https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm ; projections, https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm ; press conference transcript, https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260916.pdf

6. This is the last Employment Situation before the November 3 midterms. The Associated Press led with that, and the page’s chronology skips the election and ends on the November 6 jobs report, which comes after the vote. AP, October 2, 2026, https://apnews.com/article/jobs-unemployment-economy-ai-hiring-44b99f9ccbff863888af99402ddf535e

7. There is no official reaction. Kevin Hassett, director of the National Economic Council, said on Fox Business he was “not even a little bit” disappointed, called the report lukewarm, and said about 12,000 of the jobs were factory construction. The release’s matching line is nonresidential specialty trade contractors, up 12,000, which the bureau does not call factory construction. Senator Elizabeth Warren’s statement the same day said the opposite: a failing agenda, 33,000 jobs a month on average since January 2025 against 120,000 in 2024, and real wages down 0.7 percent since the war. The Washington Post, carried in the Detroit News, reported that the White House had not immediately reacted and that the president had told reporters this week, “We have the greatest economy in the history of our country by far.” No Treasury secretary statement turned up in the coverage of those reactions. Breitbart transcript of the Fox Business interview, posted October 3 from the October 2 broadcast, https://www.breitbart.com/clips/2026/10/02/white-house-not-even-a-little-bit-disappointed-in-jobs-report/ ; Warren, October 2, 2026, https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/senator-warren-statement-on-september-2026-jobs-report ; New York Times live, October 2, 2026, https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/10/02/business/jobs-report-economy ; Detroit News / Washington Post, October 2, 2026, https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/10/02/job-growth-down-in-september-with-unemployment-up/92054017007/

8. “The release gives no reason” overstates what is unknown. The bureau’s text gives no cause, but economists the same morning gave one: September payrolls tend to come in weak when Labor Day falls late, and Labor Day was September 7. Reuters also said there is no sign of a broad layoff wave. Reuters, October 2, 2026, https://www.reuters.com/business/us-job-growth-slows-sharply-september-unemployment-rate-rises-42-2026-10-02/

9. The same-week claims release is the check on that layoff claim. Initial claims were 197,000 for the week ending September 26, the lowest since July and below a 200,000 forecast; continuing claims were 1.701 million for the week ending September 19. FRED series ICSA, updated October 1, 2026, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ICSA ; Bloomberg, October 1, 2026, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/us-initial-jobless-claims-slip-to-197-000-lowest-since-july

10. The unemployment increase was not a rise in job loss. In the bureau’s own summary table, job losers fell by 45,000 and job leavers by 173,000, while reentrants rose by 152,000 and new entrants by 116,000. The St. Louis Fed note the page already uses for the unrounded rate says the same thing in flows and the page does not use that part. Table A-11, October 2, 2026, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.t11.htm ; St. Louis Fed, October 2, 2026, https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2026/oct/flash-report-unemployment-rises-slightly-job-growth-slows-september

11. The page’s significance argument is applied to the payroll number and not to the household numbers it treats as the real story. The bureau’s own FAQ, in the release the page cites, says about 122,000 is the establishment threshold and about 650,000 is the household-employment threshold. Household employment rose 406,000, under that bar, and the bureau said participation and the employment-population ratio changed little. The technical note’s illustrated 90 percent band for the unemployment rate, stated at a 6 percent rate, is about plus or minus 0.3 point, and for the number unemployed about plus or minus 425,000. A 0.1 point rate move and a 78,000 rise in unemployment sit inside those illustrations. The FAQ and technical note are in the October 2 release, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm

12. “The household survey counted 8,986 people working more than one job” is wrong as a count of people. The table is in thousands. The next sentence says so, but the sentence itself does not.

13. Nick Timiraos posted the unrounded unemployment rate as 4.19 percent in June, 4.09 in July, 4.14 in August, and 4.18 in September, and said it has stayed between 4.0 and 4.5 since June 2024. September is not a breakout from the prior four months, and on his figures it is still under June. The page’s 4.141 to 4.175 comparison is only August to September. Post, October 2, 2026, https://x.com/NickTimiraos/status/2106003429017710911

14. The Black unemployment rate’s one-point rise is named, but not the path: 6.3 percent in July, 6.0 in August, 7.0 in September, while the Asian rate fell from 3.2 to 2.9. The bureau’s choice of “increased” rather than “little change” means it treated the Black rate as significant. The two-month swing is the part a reader needs before treating 7.0 as a new level. Summary table A in the October 2 release, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm

15. Year-ago levels from that same table are missing. Unemployment was 4.4 percent in September 2025, not a series that only lives between 4.1 and 4.3. Participation was 62.5 percent, against 61.8 percent now, so the monthly rise of 0.2 point does not retrace the year. The bureau also says participation and the employment-population ratio have shown little net change since January. Summary table A, same release.

16. Wages are reported and not compared with prices. The 3.0 percent yearly gain is below the 3.4 percent PCE figure the page already has, so real hourly earnings fell over the year. The Associated Press, a source the page cites, calls 3.0 percent the smallest yearly gain since May 2021. Earnings also missed the forecasts El-Erian posted (0.3 percent on the month, 3.1 percent on the year). The release separately says production and nonsupervisory earnings rose 7 cents, or 0.2 percent, to $32.60. The page’s earnings series is the check on the “slowest since” claim, and the page does not run it. AP, October 2, 2026, same URL as item 6; release text, same URL as item 11.

17. The August revision is not one number. Fox Business, reading the establishment tables, says private payrolls for August were revised from 127,000 to 89,000, while government was revised from a gain of 35,000 to a gain of 44,000. The page’s “down 29,000” is the net. September’s government decline of 17,000 was federal −1,000, state −3,000, and local −13,000, mostly education, not a federal layoff month. Fox Business, October 2, 2026, https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/us-jobs-report-september-2026

18. The release text the page relies on also says manufacturing is up 72,000 since a recent low in December 2025, and that most of the 129,000 financial-activities loss since May 2025 is insurance carriers and related activities, down 90,000. The Economic Policy Institute’s same-day note says manufacturing is still down 21,000 since January 2025, and federal employment is down 328,000 since January 2025. Those two windows are the dispute over whether manufacturing and the federal workforce have turned. Release, October 2, 2026; EPI, October 2, 2026, https://www.epi.org/indicators/unemployment/

19. The health-care slowdown the page reports has an explanation in the AP story the page already cites and does not use. Bradley Saunders of Capital Economics wrote that the slower health-care hiring might reflect the administration’s revocation of work authorization for about 350,000 Haitians after Temporary Protected Status ended in July. That is a claim to check, not a fact the release states. AP, October 2, 2026; the underlying status change was reported through the summer, including Reuters, August 19, 2026, https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-families-healthcare-providers-under-strain-after-trump-crackdown-haitian-2026-08-19/

20. Table A-7, foreign-born and native-born, is not used. It is not seasonally adjusted, so it cannot be lined up with the 485,000 labor-force increase. On that table the foreign-born labor force went from 31.860 million in August to 32.279 million in September, and from 32.073 million in September 2025, and the foreign-born unemployment rate was 3.2 percent against 4.1 percent a year earlier. The release’s FAQ says the surveys do not identify legal status. Anyone reading the immigration argument needs this table and that limit. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.t07.htm , October 2, 2026.

21. The birth-death adjustment, which is part of how the 29,000 is built, is missing. The bureau’s table shows a not-seasonally-adjusted total nonfarm birth-death forecast of −190,000 for September 2026. That figure is not the payroll change and should not be subtracted from 29,000. The model’s ARIMA component was also changed for the January 2026 preliminary estimates. https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesbd.htm , current with the October 2 release.

22. The prior two days already reset the story the jobs report then joined. On September 30 the BEA revised second-quarter real GDP growth to 2.2 percent from 1.5 percent, and first-quarter growth to 2.5 percent from 2.1 percent, led by consumer spending and investment including data centers. The same morning’s personal-income release is the 3.4 percent PCE figure. ADP the same day said private employment rose 90,000, above a Reuters forecast of 70,000, which is why Friday’s private payroll gain of 46,000 was a surprise rather than a second soft print. BEA, September 30, 2026, https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-third-estimate-industries-corporate-profits-state-gdp-and-state-personal-income-2nd ; ADP via Reuters, September 30, 2026, https://www.reuters.com/business/us-private-payrolls-growth-picks-up-september-adp-says-2026-09-30/

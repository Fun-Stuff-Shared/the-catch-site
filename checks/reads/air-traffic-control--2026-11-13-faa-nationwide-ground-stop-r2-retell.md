**1. Five sentences**
- On Friday afternoon the FAA stopped every departing flight in the U.S. for 4 hours 23 minutes. It was a communications failure, and the stop started at 3:53 p.m. after Chicago's airports had already been halted.
- Thousands of flights were diverted or cancelled, and Europe and Mexico held flights headed here.
- Transportation Secretary Duffy says the FAA's old analog switches and routers got overloaded with data and began sending inconsistent data, so the FAA shut the system down. The newer digital equipment held.
- Homeland Security Secretary Mullin says it appears new equipment and patches going in overwhelmed the old system. Unnamed sources say a software update caused it.
- Nobody has said what the change was or why it hit on Friday. Duffy uses the outage to ask Congress for $50 billion.

**2. What it's really about**
Officials say old equipment broke, but the only trigger anyone has named is new equipment going in, and "old hardware" doesn't explain why it failed on Friday.

**3. Questions I still had**
- What was the update or patch, who pushed it, and to what?
- Why did overloaded old switches fail on this day and not earlier?
- Did the O'Hare problem at 3:10 and the national failure share a cause?
- What does "reduced the load" mean, and is the fix permanent? Could it recur Saturday?
- Is the 1980s network failing, or the rebuild around it? The story doesn't say which, though Duffy's pitch needs one answer.
- Who is accountable: the FAA, or L3Harris, which runs the network?
- Why is Homeland Security leading the investigation?
- Is SMART actually ruled out, or only denied by interested parties?
- What are STARS, ERAM and TRACON? They show up unexplained.
- Did the 4,042 airborne flights all land safely? The story doesn't say.

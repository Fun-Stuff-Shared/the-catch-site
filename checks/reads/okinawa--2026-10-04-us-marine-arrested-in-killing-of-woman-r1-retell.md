**1. Five sentences**
- Japanese police arrested a 20-year-old U.S. Marine, Devin Ballard, on suspicion of strangling a 39-year-old woman, Anna Yagi, in a Naha hotel room and taking her wallet and backpack. He denies it.
- Police found him in a bar district about 25 km away the night after the killing, and arrested him there before dawn.
- Because Japanese police caught him off base, Japan holds him. Prosecutors have 23 days from the arrest to decide whether to charge him.
- The Okinawa governor says 30 years of U.S. discipline measures have failed and wants the status-of-forces agreement reviewed. The U.S. ordered a pause and a curfew but did not apologize, and Tokyo says it will keep fixing problems one at a time.
- Press coverage got several facts wrong, including where he was arrested, the arrest counts, and whether he is charged.

**2. What it's really about**
Okinawa wants the rules governing U.S. forces rewritten after another killing, while Tokyo and Washington offer only incremental fixes.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "Police said that question never arose this time." I didn't know what question. The sentence before it covers the custody rule, a 1995 rape case, and "renegotiated", so I couldn't tell what changed.
- "so Japan holds him." The headline and the opening paragraph already said Japan holds him, so the explanation arrives before I knew why custody mattered.
- "Under the agreement that governs U.S. forces in Japan … (SOFA)". The text then switches between "the agreement" and "status of forces agreement". I wasn't sure at first that they were the same thing.
- "Futenma" first appears as the Marine's base. It returns much later as "the Futenma air base" that the governor accepts moving. I wasn't told they were the same place or why that matters.
- "elected in September with the backing of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's party." I couldn't tell why this matters. The party is not named until much later.
- "she and a man appear to have taken a taxi", then "He was wearing different clothes from the man on the footage." The story never says Ballard is the man or what ties him to the hotel. I only inferred it from the tattoos.
- "took him voluntarily to the Okinawa police station and arrested him there". "Voluntarily" followed by "arrested" is a jump.
- "Naha", "Okinawa City", "Kokuba neighborhood", "Wakasa district". I wasn't sure which names I needed to keep.
- The family statement is followed straight by "many posts on X … were blaming her". I didn't see how they connect until the second sentence.
- "Thirty years of measures" jumps from 1995 to 2012 to 2016 to 2017 to 2024 to 2025. The 2024 line ("neither the national government nor the police had reported to the prefecture") came out of nowhere. "Each case since has brought new rules" doesn't say what the reader should take from the list.
- "Japan has made 6 such requests since, and 5 were granted." This sits against "the U.S. keeps a suspect until charged". If the U.S. usually hands suspects over, the custody point seems smaller than the story makes it. It is never reconciled.
- The 2012 item says "alleged assault" but doesn't say where, by whom, or what became of it.
- "He took office on September 30, four days before the arrest." After the history section this jumps back to the present.
- Two Koja quotes say nearly the same thing ("have not worked at all" and "completely ineffective"). I couldn't tell whether they are one statement.
- "The training includes alcohol awareness". Which training? It lands in the U.S. section and I wasn't sure what it referred to.
- "48-hour pause" with "ends at noon on October 9". The order is dated October 6, so I couldn't make the 48 hours add up.
- "Okinawa Area Coordinating Committee" and "Okinawa Policy Council" are named with no explanation of who sits on them.
- "Cases are not arrests, and Okinawa is not Japan". The "Okinawa is not Japan" half isn't explained until the table, and the table is dense (a "Where" column, a "police count" column, three outlets).

**4. Questions I still had**
- How did police connect Ballard to the hotel and to the man on the footage?
- Was Ballard the man in the taxi? What was his relationship to Yagi?
- Why was he free in a bar district the next night, and was he off duty?
- Would the U.S. have given him up if it had held him first? The story says it could keep him, but also says 5 of 6 handover requests were granted.
- Why does Glass say the agreement "is working", and why does Okinawa disagree?
- Is there an alternative to a full SOFA revision that anyone is actually proposing? What would a revision change?
- Does Ballard have a lawyer or consular access?
- Does the curfew apply to everyone in Japan or only Okinawa?
- What was the murder weapon? (Strangled with what?)

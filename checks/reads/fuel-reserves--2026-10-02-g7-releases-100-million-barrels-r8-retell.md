**1. Five sentences**
- In March, IEA members pledged 400 million barrels of emergency oil. About 325 million had gone out by October 2.
- On October 2 the G7 announced a 100 million barrel release over four months, with diesel first.
- The White House says Trump won 100 million barrels of European diesel by threatening a U.S. diesel export ban.
- Germany, the IEA and a leaked EU document say the 100 million is mostly the unfinished March pledge, so it is not new oil. Meanwhile diesel is short because of Hormuz, Houthi attacks, Russia and China.
- Trump says a ban was never on the table. The IEA board meets the week of October 12 to decide who releases what.

**2. What it's really about**
Whether the G7's "100 million barrels" is new oil or the March promise relabeled, and who gets credit for it.

**3. Where I got lost**
- **Lede arithmetic.** "400 million", "about 325 million" and "about 100 million" don't add up (75 ≠ 100). I had to wait until the "Whose barrels" section for the reason ("some countries released more than they pledged"). Even there it's compressed.
- **Stat strip.** "172 million barrels the U.S. share of the March pledge" shows up before I knew what a share or an SPR was.
- **"It sets the release 'taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled,' the commitments being a release governments promised in March."** I read this three times. "Fulfilled" and "promised" pull against each other.
- **Table row "Country pledges listed by the IEA, March 19 | 426.0 million".** Why is this above 400? I never get a direct answer, only "add up to more than the 400 million headline".
- **"The Energy Department does not sell the oil. It lends it to companies, which return it later with extra barrels on top."** I didn't see why this matters to the question.
- **SPR is crude, but the White House says "diesel".** The story never connects the two.
- **U.S. numbers.** 133.5 awarded + 40 offered = 173.5, against 172. "Bids due October 6" has already passed and no result is given.
- **"The agency" and "the energy agency".** Several different "agencies" and "energy agency" appear, and I wasn't always sure it was the IEA.
- **"2.65 million tonnes... about 19.5 million barrels... 600,000 tonnes".** A unit switch mid-sentence.
- **"Why diesel is short".** This jumped from G7 politics to the Persian Gulf, then to a 2023 EU ban, Ukrainian refinery strikes, and China. Only the last sentence ties back.
- **"The European Union banned seaborne imports... from February 5, 2023".** The date felt unconnected to this year's shortage.
- **Brent at 113.96 on September 29, then "settled at $104.28 a barrel, up 4.1 percent" on October 8.** The drop in between isn't explained.
- **"midterm elections on November 3".** It doesn't say these are U.S. elections.
- **The export ban first appears under "Washington and Europe".** The top never says a ban was part of the story. "Pledged not to restrict energy exports to one another" meant nothing to me until then.
- **"EU officials agreed to hold any release beyond March".** I couldn't tell whether "hold" means hold back.
- **"The catch" repeats earlier material.** Germany's "no additional quantities" and Japan's "no plans" are already in the opening. The White House "refined diesel" quote appears three times. Italy "has met its pledge" earlier, then "open to providing more".
- **JPMorgan quote.** It lands in the middle of the Germany/Japan/France list with no setup.
- **Reuters' three accounts of the demand.** 120 million over six months versus 100 million within 20 days. Then Macron says there was no threat, and nothing tells me which to believe.
- **"Tenders 'coming out from different bodies'".** "Tenders" is never explained.
- **"United States lifts its blockade of Iranian ports".** The blockade was never introduced.
- **Ending.** It drifts to hurricanes, ship counts, and Iran talks, away from the G7 question.
- **"Middle East crude still left the region at its pre-war rate that week".** This contradicts the earlier "45 percent below". They cover different periods, but I didn't catch that until a re-read.
- **Source-attributed names.** "Politico", "Euronews" and "Reuters" in prose, plus a run of names (Itkonen, Šefčovič, Hardy, Bessent), made me skim.

**4. Questions I still had**
- Is the 100 million new oil or not? The story leans one way (the headline) but never says it settled the question.
- Where does the 100 − 75 = 25 million gap come from, and why 426 versus 400?
- Do the last 40 million U.S. barrels count inside the 100 million?
- What happened to the October 6 bids?
- How much of the 100 million is actually diesel, and where does it come from?
- Did Trump threaten a ban? The sources conflict and the story leaves it there.
- What does "before 20 days" count from?
- Will any of this lower diesel prices? France says 12 to 18 cents, but nothing is said about the U.S.
- What will the IEA board decide on the 12th?

**5. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "The IEA's figures fit that reading." (announcing and hedging)
- "The G7 statement does not define 'commitments that have already been fulfilled.'" and similar. "Names no amount", "gave no split", "does not say whether", "did not say whether", "without saying". The "X doesn't say" pattern is everywhere and reads like a template.

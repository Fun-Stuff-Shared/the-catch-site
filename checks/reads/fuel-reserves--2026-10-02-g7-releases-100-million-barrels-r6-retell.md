**1. Five sentences**
- On October 2 the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil over four months, with diesel coming first.
- That number is mostly the 400 million barrels that 32 countries pledged in March. About 325 million have been released, and the energy agency counts about 100 million still owed.
- Germany says nothing extra will be released, and the White House calls it 100 million barrels of European diesel.
- Diesel is scarce because Hormuz is choked and Russian and Gulf diesel exports are down. Reuters sources say Washington threatened a U.S. diesel export ban to force Europe's hand, which Trump and Macron deny.
- The agency's board meets the week of October 12 to decide who releases what, and France has already said it will sell 10 million barrels of diesel.

**2. What it's really about**
A headline number sold as new supply is mostly an old, unfinished promise, and each government describes it to suit its own politics.

**3. Where I got lost**
- Lede: "The energy agency counts what is left at about 100 million barrels." "Left" could mean in reserves or still unreleased.
- Stat tile: "20 days named for the diesel release". It isn't a quantity like the others.
- The "agency" is never named in the prose. "IEA" first shows up in quotes much later ("pending a clearer IEA assessment").
- The first table: "Scheduled in the agency's country table… 426.0 million" against the 400 million pledged. Nothing explains the gap. "Fall in crude held… 132.5 million" against "awarded 133.5 million" is also unexplained.
- "Whose barrels" is mostly about U.S. loan mechanics, when I expected Europe.
- "Germany had promised 2.65 million tonnes… released 600,000 tonnes". The units switch from barrels to tonnes. "Its government had said in its own guidance that companies had taken up a first batch" leaves "its" and "first batch" unclear.
- "although 400 minus 325 is 75, the agency counts… about 100 million". The over-delivery explanation arrives in half a clause.
- "Gulf crude exports in August were just under 45 percent below their pre-war level" against the last section's "ports outside the strait kept the region's crude exports at their pre-war level". These look contradictory.
- "Prices show the gap." Which gap? The table gives dollars, while the percentages sit in the paragraph above it.
- The price table mixes dates (Oct 5, Sep 29, Mar 2). Later Brent is "$104.28… up 4.1 percent" on October 8, which is a different date again.
- "The meeting was France's idea." The story jumps here from prices to the origin of the meeting.
- "Both sides signed the G7 pledge not to restrict energy exports". This is the first mention of such a pledge.
- "the only ban texts are the two House bills". The bills were never introduced.
- "A European diplomat called the demand, 'against the threat of a U.S. export ban,' 'blackmail,' according to an official briefed on an EU call." That is a chain of three people.
- France: "still had 8 million barrels to release", then "signaled 2 million barrels of diesel on top", then "sell 10 million barrels". I couldn't tell how these fit together or whether the 10 million is new.
- "Russell Hardy… tenders 'over the next two weeks'". Which tenders?
- Last paragraph: Hurricane Isaias and "talks with Iran were 'productive'" feel unconnected to the rest.
- "Bids on the last U.S. offer were due October 6." The story never says what happened to them.
- The "Sources" list is huge. Entries like "Miliband to stand in for Burnham" and the "Source history" lines ("133 million barrels") mean nothing to a reader.

**4. Questions I still had**
- Did the board decide anything, and how much of the 100 million is actually diesel?
- Does any of it arrive in the U.S., or lower U.S. prices?
- Why would Europe have spare diesel when diesel is short everywhere?
- If the U.S. supplies about half of the EU's diesel, is that the leverage behind the ban threat? The story never says so.
- Who threatened whom, and who is right? Does the story leave that open on purpose?
- What happened to the 40 million-barrel U.S. offer?
- What are the 426.0 and 132.5 figures?
- Who is Burnham?
- Is Hormuz getting better or worse?

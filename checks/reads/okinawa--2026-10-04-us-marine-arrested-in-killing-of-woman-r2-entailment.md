1. **Off-duty jurisdiction — page lines 65 and 120; chronology row for 1960.**
   
   **Page:** “It gives Japan the first right to try U.S. service members for crimes committed off duty”; “Off-duty status decides who tries the case”; “Japan tries off-duty crimes.”
   
   **Record (sofa-article-17):** “offenses solely against the property or security of the United States, or offenses solely against the person or property of another member of the United States armed forces or the civilian component or of a dependent”. The agreement assigns these offenses to U.S. primary jurisdiction, alongside offenses committed during official duties.
   
   **Correction:** Japan generally has primary jurisdiction over off-duty crimes against Japanese victims. The U.S. retains primary jurisdiction over certain offenses against U.S. personnel, dependents, property or security, even when committed off duty. Correct all three occurrences.

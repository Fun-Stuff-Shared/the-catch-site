**1. Five sentences**

North Carolina's elections board told counties on Sept. 4 to throw out any mail ballot whose inner envelope arrives unsealed, even if the outer envelope is sealed. The board voted 3 to 2 along party lines, and it used to count those ballots. The board says 3.1% of mail ballots have been rejected this fall, against 2.6% at this point in 2022. Gov. Stein said the rate had doubled, but his number came from volunteer counts in 27 counties, and PolitiFact rated his claim Mostly False. A federal judge declined to block the rule because voting had already started, and the lawsuit continues.

**2. What it's really about**

The new rule is rejecting some ballots that would have counted before, but nobody can say how many statewide, and the governor's "twice as high" figure doesn't hold up.

**3. Questions I still had**

- How many ballots statewide did the new rule reject? The story says the Board doesn't separate rejections by reason. Why not, and can anyone get that number before Nov. 3?
- Is 3.1% vs 2.6% a real comparison when mail voting is down 38%? The raw count is lower than 2022 (1,349 vs 1,829), and Fuller calls the rise "statistically significant." Who is right, and does it matter?
- Is there any known case of tampering with a ballot in a sealed outer envelope? The Board's answer is "in theory."
- "Ballots arriving later no longer count, even if postmarked in time." What was the old deadline, and when did it change? This is the only mention.
- Who is Gerry Cohen? He's "board member" at Wake's first meeting, which reads like the State Board. What is his party and role? Dave Boliek and Siobhan O'Duffy Millen also appear with little setup.
- Wake has 147 unsealed-inner rejections. Of those, how many would have counted under the old rule? Cohen says all 42 at the first meeting would have. Mecklenburg and Guilford give no such split.
- Guilford's total is "about where it was," with no number.
- "Spoiled" and "rejected" seem to be used interchangeably. Are they the same thing?
- Of the voters who haven't voted again, how many still can? Is anyone known to have lost a vote?
- What is "rulemaking," and why does the Board's formal rule need the legislature's approval?
- Mecklenburg and Wake combined are 406. How does that relate to the statewide 1,349?

**4. Sentences that sounded machine-written**

- "Both sides agree the rate is up. They differ on how much it matters and on whether a voter who has to vote again has been harmed." (announces and sums up)
- "The stakes run along party lines, since Democrats in North Carolina vote by mail at higher rates than Republicans." (explains something nobody asked, in a tidy summing-up shape)
- "Three counties have published counts, and they differ." (announces the next sentence)
- "What happens next depends on when the county finds the problem." (announces what the list below it shows)
- "A spoiled ballot is not a lost vote if the voter acts in time" (a reassuring thesis line)
- "A spoiled ballot does not count, and the voter is sent a new one or votes in person." (restates the table above it)
- "He called all of this a preliminary determination, not a final ruling on the merits." (hedge-closer)
- "Whether the new rule threw out thousands of ballots the old one would have counted, no statewide figure shows, because the Board's counts do not separate rejections by reason." (inverted and stiff)
- "Stein repeated his source as it read that day" (a heading that doesn't parse)
- "Both versions end with the same line: 'This story has been updated.'" (a flourish more than a fact)

**1. Five sentences**

On Nov. 19 the International Court of Justice ruled 9 to 6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the military's 2017 operations in northern Rakhine. It is the first time the court has found that a country itself committed genocide. The Gambia brought the case in 2019. The court ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible, let the Rohingya return and compensate them, but it can't enforce any of that beyond going to the Security Council, where China and Russia can block action. Six judges dissented, saying the evidence from three villages didn't show a wider genocidal pattern, and Myanmar's government calls the ruling unfair because it wasn't unanimous. Several outlets wrongly said Myanmar was "guilty" or the first country to breach the Genocide Convention.

**2. What it's really about**

A landmark genocide ruling with no means of enforcement, and press coverage that got its legal meaning wrong.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Who is "Myanmar" for compliance? Min Aung Hlaing's junta runs the country, and the story never says whether it has to act or what happens if it doesn't.
- Why does the court find a failure to prevent genocide at 9 to 6, the same split as the genocide finding? What does "people acting alongside its forces" mean?
- What does "the court treated incitement of its own forces as part of the genocide finding" mean? It's unclear why the 14 to 1 vote is "in Myanmar's favor."
- How do 700,000 who fled in 2017 and 815,000 in northern Rakhine square with 1.2 million refugees in Bangladesh now? The story doesn't explain the gap.
- What are the "three other explanations," and why did the majority reject each? I only get the dissent's side of this.
- Who are the 9 majority judges? I only get the six dissenters.
- What's the Arakan Army's role, and who holds the land the Rohingya would return to?
- Why does the Min Gyi paragraph jump to "conduct across the region" with no evidence for the region?
- What did Aung San Suu Kyi argue, and why did the civilian government defend the military?

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**

- "The court found supported a count of 750 dead there, compiled by Rohingya refugees and found credible by the UN fact-finding mission." This is broken grammar, not just machine-sounding.
- "The majority found that conduct across the region, not only at Min Gyi; that is the point the six dissenting judges dispute, below." The sentence is unfinished and announces what comes next.
- "The ruling, finding by finding" / "The court ruled on each of The Gambia's charges separately." It announces the table, and "charges" is the criminal wording the story's own Catch section says not to use.
- "The last line went in Myanmar's favor; the court treated incitement of its own forces as part of the genocide finding." It explains the table without clarifying it.
- "Genocide under the convention takes two things: certain acts against a group, and intent to destroy that group." This is textbook explaining that nobody asked for.
- "Between The Gambia's filing in 2019 and the judgment came an emergency order, a coup and a ruling that the court could hear the case." This sums up the timeline right before the timeline.
- "AP reported the ruling right" and "The reports below said something the judgment does not support." These announce the table and grade the press.
- "It made no order to stop the genocide, because the genocide was not found to be ongoing." It appears twice, once in the table and once in the closing section.
- "Return depends on more than Myanmar's government: much of the Rohingya homeland is now held by the Arakan Army..." It's a tidy-sounding takeaway line.
- "The court cannot enforce any of it beyond reporting Myanmar to the UN Security Council." This restates the subhead, which already says the court has no power to enforce.

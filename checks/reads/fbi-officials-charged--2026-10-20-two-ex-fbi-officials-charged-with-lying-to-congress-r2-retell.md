**1. Five sentences**

1. Federal prosecutors in Washington charged two former FBI officials, Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, with lying to House Judiciary staff in 2023. The questions were about their role in the FBI's Hunter Biden investigations.
2. In 2020 the two ran the FBI task force meant to guard the election from foreign interference. Prosecutors say emails, messages and two current FBI employees show they were in the loop, including on the laptop, and that they lied to hide it.
3. The complaint was filed under seal on Oct. 20, and both surrendered on Oct. 23. A probable-cause hearing is set for Oct. 27 unless a grand jury indicts first.
4. The magistrate judge called the way the case was charged strange and said charges this old are rare. The defendants call it political, since it came 11 days before the midterms. Pirro and Grassley praise it, and Himes attacks it.
5. The maximum penalty is five years, and nothing is decided until a trial.

**2. What it's really about**

A thinly timed, untested government case over what two officials said about their own role, and whether it's a real false-statements case or an election-season one.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Why would an election-security task force be involved in a tax and money-laundering case at all? The "role" section answers this late and only in the government's own words.
- What exactly were the questions asked? Floris said "I did not" to "any involvement," but the filing says the task force did oversight and briefings. Is that involvement, or a quibble over the word?
- What is Round River, and is it part of the charges? It appears in a table row before it's explained, and the story says it's separate.
- Why file under seal, and why use a complaint instead of waiting for a grand jury? The story reports the judge's doubt, but no one gives the government's reason.
- Were they released? Did they plead? Did the judge set conditions?
- What did the two current FBI employees actually say, and why did it take until this month?
- Who is the committee staffer, and why didn't the committee follow up in 2023 if the answers were so plainly false?
- What is the "assessment to discredit the investigations" in Floris's table row?
- Did Hunter Biden's case itself end somehow? The story never says how the investigations resolved.

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**

- "The charges are allegations, and both are presumed innocent."
- "In the tables below, each answer is quoted or summarized from that statement, and the government's account beside it has not been tested in court." This announces the tables and then hedges.
- "Two answers carried qualifiers."
- "The law reaches a false answer only if it is material and made knowingly and willfully." Nobody asked, and it reads like a statute.
- "The judge ordered both sides back at 11 a.m. Tuesday, then spoke about the two defendants:" This announces the quote.
- "The rules set no quiet period before an election." This is a tidy non-conclusion after the insinuation in the sentence before it.
- "Neither step decides whether the answers were knowingly false; that would take a trial." This sums up.
- "Federal law gives prosecutors 30 days from the arrest, with some exceptions, to file an indictment or information." The "with some exceptions" is hedging.
- "Not a laptop case alone" and "Smaller slips." These are headline fragments, and the second one is a label with no content.
- "no deadline forced the timing: the five-year limit on the charge would have allowed it until 2028." This explains something nobody asked, and it reads as an implied accusation.

Other problems:

- The Oct. 27 hearing is stated four times.
- "Sworn statement," "affidavit" and "complaint" are used for what seems to be the same document.
- The "(see Why now)" and "(affidavit's summary)" asides in the table are scaffolding.
- The whole "Catch" section is about errors in other outlets' coverage. That's media criticism, not the story.

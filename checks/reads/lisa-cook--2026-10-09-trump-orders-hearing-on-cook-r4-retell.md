**1. Retell**
- Trump has ordered Fed governor Lisa Cook to a closed White House hearing on November 5, where three officials he picked will question her about mortgage statements from 2021.
- The Supreme Court blocked his first firing attempt in June because she had never been allowed to respond, but it said he could try again.
- The White House says she claimed two different homes as her main residence within two weeks, which can mean a cheaper loan. Cook's lawyers call the Atlanta form an honest mistake, and she hasn't been charged.
- Cook's lawyers doubt the hearing is genuine, because Trump keeps saying he has already decided and one committee member, Hassett, had already said she should take leave.
- After the hearing the committee reports to Trump, who can fire her whenever he likes. If he does, she goes back to court, and only then will judges rule on whether this counts as "cause."

**2. What it's really about**
Whether a legally required hearing is a real test of the charge or a formality on the way to removing a governor Trump calls an "automatic no vote."

**3. Questions I still had**
- What did the White House say after Cook's August 26 answer, and why was the written exchange not enough? The story says the memo doesn't say, and never gets further.
- Did Cook actually get a lower rate? Both sides are said to be silent on that, and I couldn't tell whether the 0.25 to 0.50 points was a claim about her loan or just a general range.
- Trump calls her an "automatic no vote," yet she has voted with the majority every time, including a 12-0 rate hike that Trump disliked. The story sets these side by side and doesn't reconcile them.
- Why would a fourth Trump appointee matter? Three of seven seats are his, and the story doesn't say what a fourth changes.
- Why were Lucas (EEOC) and Sonderling (ethics office) picked to judge a mortgage question? Hassett's issue is explained. Theirs isn't.
- The security-clearance "second home" line and the missing homestead exemption sit under Cook's case. It wasn't clear whether they help her or hurt her.
- Why is the hearing closed, and will the transcript ever come out? The correction section says the memo doesn't promise it.
- What lawsuit is the November 6 joint report to Judge Cobb in? It's the first I'd heard of it still being alive.
- Will Cook attend? The story says her lawyers are still deciding.

**4. Sentences that sounded machine-written**
- "Neither side disputes that she signed both forms, and the Supreme Court has ruled that she must have a chance to answer before any firing. The question is whether what she signed is cause." This announces the issue and sums up.
- "Is a mortgage form reason to fire her?" and "Is this about interest rates?" Both are rhetorical headings. The second section never directly answers its question.
- "The Court held neither." This is a clipped verdict line that sums up rather than informs.
- "The Supreme Court did not adopt her view or the government's, and left the question open." The same point is repeated from earlier.
- "It said cause is 'a substantial threshold,' a high bar," This glosses its own quote.
- "Their particular objection is Hassett." This is a staged lead-in.
- "She keeps voting in the meantime." It's a tidy closer.
- "The Fed's governors help set interest rates." This explains what nobody asked.
- "…Trump could try again, which he is doing." It's neat, but it reads as a wrap-up flourish.
- "The memo gives no reason why the written exchange was not enough." This points to a gap and moves on without exploring it.

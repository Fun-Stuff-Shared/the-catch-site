1) **What happened:** On Nov 16, Commerce's export bureau (BIS) issued a rule requiring a license to ship advanced AI chips to any overseas data center that supplies Chinese-headquartered companies more than 12M "aggregate TPP" of computing, which is roughly 800 H100s. Shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil need a license regardless, unless the end user is a U.S. company and the exporter reports the sale. It matters because Chinese firms, barred from buying these chips directly, had been renting them from Southeast Asian data centers. The rule targets that workaround and reaches chips already installed.

3) **Differences between modes:**
- The story opens with "the combined processing performance of the chips it rents to them". The facts and source versions define aggregate TPP as the sum of TPP ratings of a group of chips.
- The story says "any shipment of these chips there needs a license, whoever the customer, unless…". The facts version says "except when the ultimate consignee or end user is U.S.-headquartered… and the exporter files reports". The "whoever the customer" phrasing slightly overstates it.
- The story says a data center "has 90 days to get a license or face restrictions". The source says 90 days from the effective date. The story does give Nov 19 as the effective date, but never ties the two together into a deadline.
- The story attributes the rule to Treasury and the NSC. The source says "written by and at the direction of" them. This is close enough, though "wrote it" loses the "at the direction of" nuance.
- The ~800 H100 figure appears as McGuire's estimate in all modes. The story lede states it as "by the count of" McGuire, which is consistent.

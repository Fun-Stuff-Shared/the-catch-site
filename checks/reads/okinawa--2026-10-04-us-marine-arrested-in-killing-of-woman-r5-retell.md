**1. Five sentences**

A U.S. Marine, Devin Ballard, 20, was arrested in Okinawa on suspicion of strangling a 39-year-old woman, Anna Yagi, in a Naha hotel room and taking her wallet and backpack. Japanese police arrested him off base, so Japan holds him. Prosecutors have up to 23 days to decide whether to charge him, and he denies it. Japan's government protested, and the U.S. ordered a pause and a 30-day curfew, but its commander on Okinawa did not apologize. Okinawa's new governor says U.S. discipline has failed and wants the status of forces agreement revised, and Tokyo says only that it will talk with the U.S. about prevention.

**2. What it's really about**

Another killing has revived Okinawa's long-running demand to change the rules governing U.S. troops, and the governor now makes that demand from inside Tokyo's own political camp.

**3. Where I got lost**

- Subhead: "on this suspected crime". I stopped on "this suspected crime".
- "Police said no handover question arose". "Handover" isn't explained until later. Even then I had to piece together that the U.S. can keep a suspect it catches first.
- Two generals show up in the same paragraph, "Lt. Gen. Stephen Jost" and "Lt. Gen. Benjamin Watson". Each is described by a different commander title. Only Watson matters for the rest of the story.
- "status of forces agreement" and "SOFA" are both used. Later it's "the agreement", "a 1960 agreement" and "the agreement".
- "The night of October 3" includes "Anna Yagi lived in the Kokuba neighborhood of Naha". It's a detail with no use. I learned nothing else about who she was or whether she knew him.
- "a taxi to Naha from an entertainment district in Okinawa City, about 25 kilometers away". The hotel was in Naha, but at 11:30 that night he was back in Okinawa City. The story never says how he got there or when he left the room. It also doesn't say why the officer thought he was the man.
- "Officers waited until he was alone." I don't know why.
- "Ballard enlisted in August 2024 and is a combat engineer at Marine Corps Air Station Futenma". This sits in the middle of the arrest narrative. "Futenma" comes back later without explanation.
- "By October 6 many posts on X and other sites were blaming her". This is a sudden jump from the police timeline.
- "Because Gadson was a civilian, the two governments then signed an agreement in January 2017 on which civilians the status of forces agreement covers." I couldn't follow it.
- "In 2024 Okinawans learned of sexual-assault cases... that neither the national government nor the police had reported to the prefecture." This feels disconnected from the paragraphs around it.
- "up from 73 the year before and the most since 2003", followed shortly by "in 2003 it began a campaign". Two unrelated 2003s.
- "who accepts Tokyo's plan to keep the Futenma base on Okinawa but move it to a less populated area". The plan and the reason it matters are never explained.
- "Japan has made 6 such requests since, and 5 were granted." This sits oddly against the later "most of the cases".
- The Catch section corrects wire stories I never saw: "The Associated Press's first story, and NPR's copy of it, said...". I had no stake in those errors.
- The table has garbled columns. "What coverage said / Outlet / What the police and prefecture count" is followed by claims and counts in an order I couldn't match to outlets.
- "101 criminal cases, involving 80 people" next to "6,409 cases in Okinawa, involving 6,285 people". I couldn't tell which numbers matter.
- "31 of the 76 such facilities, or 40.8 percent" against "70.3 percent". I don't know why the story is arguing about which figure is right.
- The source list is 115 items, many in Japanese, with no hint of which ones matter.

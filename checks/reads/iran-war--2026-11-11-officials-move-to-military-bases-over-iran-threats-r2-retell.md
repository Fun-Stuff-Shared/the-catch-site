**1. Five sentences**

- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his home to a military base. The NYT reported it on Nov. 10, and on Nov. 13 he told CNBC that Iran had made death threats and the Secret Service told him to move.
- He runs the Treasury's pressure campaign against Iran, and he says the threats show it's working. Nobody has said what the threats were, which base he's on, or when he moved.
- The news came a day after Trump said Iran is "militarily effectively over." Meanwhile intelligence and justice officials have warned of a "heightened threat environment," and CNN reports Iran has drawn up options for a dirty-bomb or chemical attack. The White House says there are no credible threats to the homeland, and Iran calls it all Israeli disinformation.
- Several officials, including Rubio, Hegseth, Miller, Noem and Blanche, were already living on bases, mostly before the war started. Some coverage presents these moves as a new wave of Iran-driven relocations.
- Bessent is the only official on record as moving because of Iran. A House committee wants a Pentagon report on appointees in military housing by May 2027.

**2. What it's really about**

Media framing of a growing, Iran-driven wave of officials moving onto bases is stronger than the record supports. Only Bessent is documented that way, and the others moved earlier, for other reasons.

**3. Questions I still had**

- What exactly were the threats against Bessent? "More than an advanced stage" tells me nothing.
- Why did the WSJ photo matter, and why did he blame the WSJ at all? The sentence just restates itself.
- Why did Rubio, Hegseth, Miller and Noem move in 2025, if not because of Iran? Protests and a tabloid are mentioned for two of them, and nothing for the other two.
- Did Fox's "in recent weeks" about Miller and Rubio simply get it wrong, or did someone move again?
- Is the Trump contrast meant to show he's wrong or contradicting himself? The headline sets the two side by side and never says.
- What is the Hewitt quote answering, and what does it add?
- Why is the Spruance post here? It's a post by an embassy, not clearly tied to officials on bases.
- What is Operation Economic Outcast doing, and why would it make him a target?
- Who is the "senior administration official" at Fox, and who are the "three unnamed people" at the Times? They never get any more identification than that.
- Is Mullin the new DHS secretary? That's stated in passing.
- Is "the Catch" the claim that only Bessent moved because of Iran, or the gap between Trump's "over" and the threat warnings? The section only argues the first.

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**

- "On the record, one official has moved because of Iran: Bessent." It's a thesis announcement that works as a heading.
- "Other reports carried the same picture." It's filler transition, and the next sentences list quotes instead.
- "Those people told the Times that the role made him one of the officials Iran most wants to harm." "Those people" has no clear referent, and the sentence is vague about what the sources actually said.
- "the photo showed where the house was." This is tautological and explains nothing.
- "Iran's plotting against U.S. officials is older than the war." It's a summing-up, background sentence that says nothing new.
- "the request is committee report language, not law." It's a hedge that explains something nobody asked.
- "Iran rejects the accusations." It's a flat, announcing line, since the next sentence already gives the actual rejection.
- "Officials named in the coverage, by when each is on record as living on a base." This table caption is stilted.
- "How serious the threat is" / "What happens next." These headings announce rather than tell, and the "next" section holds one procedural item.

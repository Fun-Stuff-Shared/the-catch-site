**1. Five sentences**

- New Hampshire passed a law, HB 323, that took student IDs off the list of IDs poll workers must accept. It has been in force since June 2, and no court has blocked it.
- A federal judge, Laplante, left the law alone. He did order the Secretary of State to stop telling officials they couldn't even consider a student ID as backup evidence of who a voter is.
- On Oct. 22 the Supreme Court paused that order, citing the rule against changing election rules close to an election. Jackson dissented, and the order doesn't say how the others voted.
- The next day the state went back to saying student IDs can't prove identity. A registered student with no listed ID can still vote if a local official personally knows them and signs a statement.
- Early coverage said the Court approved or "ruled" on a ban, but the ban already existed and the Court only reinstated the Secretary's narrower instruction.

**2. What it's really about**

The Court's pause was a narrow, timing-based ruling about a state instruction, and much of the coverage reported it as the Court approving a student-ID ban.

- "What Judge Joseph Laplante of the federal court in New Hampshire blocked, in a lawsuit brought by the Coalition for Open Democracy, was narrower:" It's a long throat-clearing setup before the point.

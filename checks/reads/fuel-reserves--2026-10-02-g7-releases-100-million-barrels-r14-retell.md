**1. Five sentences**

- In March, IEA countries promised 400 million barrels of emergency oil, and about 325 million have been released.
- On October 2 the G7 announced a 100 million barrel release over four months, with diesel first, and Trump dropped a threatened U.S. diesel export ban.
- The White House says Europe agreed to a new 100 million barrels of diesel.
- The IEA and European officials say the 100 million is roughly what is left of the March promise, so it may be little or no new oil.
- Europe's governments are still deciding who releases what, and Germany and Japan say they will release no more.

**2. What it's really about**

Whether the G7's headline 100 million barrels is new oil or the unfinished March release relabeled.

**3. Where I got lost**

- Lede arithmetic: "400 million... about 325 million had gone out... about 100 million." 400 minus 325 is 75, not 100. The story only explains this four paragraphs later ("adds up to 426 million").
- "More than 75 million is still owed, because more than 400 million was promised... and some countries released more than they promised." If overdelivery is already inside the 325, I can't tell how the leftover is 100. I had to re-read it.
- "The U.S. share is all crude oil; the diesel in the White House's account is Europe's." Until then I thought the U.S. was supplying the diesel.
- "awarded contracts to exchange 133.5 million barrels." "Exchange" and "lends... pay it back with extra barrels" is a loan, but the word "exchange" doesn't say that. I don't know why the 40 million offer matters to the main question.
- "Europe had lost a major source of diesel before the war began." Which war? The Iran war was just introduced, and then it jumps to Russia in 2021–2022 and a 2023 ban.
- The Hormuz paragraph stacks numbers: 13.1 million barrels a day, 45 percent, "nearly three-quarters", $200, 94 percent, Brent 45 percent. I stopped tracking what they were for.
- "producers had stopped 62.9 percent of their oil output." Output of what, and why? A hurricane is mentioned in passing and never named.
- The Iran blockade, Iran's seven-day offer and Trump's "won't attack before November 3 elections" sit in the same paragraph as price moves. It felt like a separate story.
- "The catch" heading. The word is never explained, and the section starts with country positions, not a catch.
- "Japan, which pledged about 80 million barrels" shows up only here, in the middle of a list.
- "the IEA's governing board, which meets the week of October 12" versus "On October 7... member governments backed finishing the March release." I couldn't tell how those two meetings relate.
- Trump "had been weighing a ban" and "I'm still thinking about it" versus "It was never really ever on the table." This is only half-flagged as a contradiction.
- Italy is listed as "finished" in the European document, but later is "open to providing more."
- France appears with 8 million owed, 2 million possible extra, and 10 million for distributors. I couldn't tell whether these are the same barrels.

**4. Questions I still had**

- How much of the 100 million is actually new, in a number?
- How much will be diesel, and when does it reach the U.S.?
- Does this lower pump prices, and by how much?
- If overdelivery counts, who actually owes the remaining ~100 million?
- Did the U.S. really threaten a ban to force this, or was it bluster?
- Why does the new-versus-old distinction matter to the people paying for diesel?
- Do the last U.S. barrels count inside the 100 million?
- What does the hurricane do to U.S. supply?

**5. Machine-sounding sentences**

- "It did not say whether any of that diesel is beyond what Europe pledged in March." The "X did not say whether" form recurs about five times ("The statement names no amount...", "The IEA does not say whether its 100 million includes...", "It did not say whether the 10 million include the 8 million..."). It reads as a tic and as hedging.
- "The sources are unnamed, and their accounts of the demand differ." It explains what nobody asked.
- "Diesel is what farmers, truckers and drivers are paying for." It's filler.
- "Washington pushed harder." and "Washington says Europe is further behind." Both are thin summary lines.
- "4 months the period of the G7 release." This is a stat tile written as a fragment.
- The ending: a quote about the next two weeks, then a France sentence that says what it did not say. The story just stops with no landing.

**1. Five sentences**
- On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil from emergency stocks over four months. That includes a "substantial diesel release" in the first 20 days, with no diesel number given.
- The US had pushed Europe hard to release diesel and had threatened to ban US diesel exports. Hours after the deal, Trump said he wouldn't.
- Nobody can say how much of the 100 million is new. A March pledge of 400 million barrels was only about 325 million delivered, and the new deal "takes into account" what's already been released.
- The US, France, the White House and the press each describe the 100 million differently (crude, refined products, diesel) and don't agree on who supplies it. No barrel has been reported delivered.
- US diesel went from $3.897 to $6.382 a gallon since March, and Japan says it won't release more. The leaders meet again within days and report back in 20 days.

**2. What it's really about**
A big headline number that may be mostly old barrels, was extracted from Europe by an export-ban threat, and may not even be diesel, which is the thing that's short.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "does not say how much is beyond its March commitments" (headline): I didn't know what "beyond" meant until the 400 million appeared.
- "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled": it's unclear whether old barrels count toward the 100 million. It's the crux and it's never spelled out.
- "the US-Israeli war with Iran and Russia's war in Ukraine disrupted global markets": two wars dropped in with no setup.
- "lending crude from its reserve to companies, which repay the loans later with extra barrels": I couldn't tell whether a loan counts as a "release". The timeline then calls it an "Exchange".
- The US numbers: "172-million-barrel share," "more than 133 million" awarded, "up to 40 million more," and "about 325 million" released. I couldn't make them reconcile, or tell "awarded" from "released".
- "Japan does not plan to add more... on October 5": the story is dated October 2, so this jumps forward in time. I also couldn't tell whether Japan's 80 million is inside the 325.
- "A barrel counted as crude is not the diesel a pump sells": a sudden turn, and the point isn't set up.
- "Net exports of diesel and gasoil from the Gulf countries averaged 390,000 barrels a day... Gulf and Russian exports together were 1.6 million barrels a day lower than in February": a pile of numbers, and "gasoil" is never explained.
- "Yanbu on the Red Sea," "navigation rights through the Strait of Hormuz," "French presidency": unexplained names that just appear.
- "A post on X by Eric Daugherty": I don't know who that is. "Brooke Rollins" is identified only after the quote.
- "The captions of her television interview that day show something looser": I didn't know what it was looser than until the next paragraph.
- "Several European countries had released only a fraction of the crude and products they pledged" appears only in the timeline table. It's a major fact that is never in the prose.
- "120 million barrels of diesel from the European Union over the next six months": a third demand, alongside 100 million in 20 days and the French 50+50 proposal. I couldn't tell which was the real ask.
- Trump says an export ban "was never really ever on the table", but a day earlier he was "thinking about" it. Macron says there was no threat, while Reuters says there was. The story shows both and never connects them.
- Brent, WTI, "Cushing" and the percent-change tables are barely mentioned in the prose. I don't know why they're there.
- The tax order ("dyed diesel," "highway excise tax") feels unconnected. "Dyed diesel" is explained only at the very end.
- "Bids on the September 29 exchange offer were due October 6": past tense in a story dated October 2.
- The von der Leyen quote has a stray "RTÉ" label above and below it.
- The 50-item source list, "Source history" and "Coverage checked" mean nothing to me as a reader.

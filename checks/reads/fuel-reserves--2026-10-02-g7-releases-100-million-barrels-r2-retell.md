**1. Five sentences**
- The G7 leaders agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil, with a lot of diesel early.
- The White House says Trump got Europe to give up 100 million barrels of diesel.
- The G7's statement says the barrels are just the unfinished part of a 400 million barrel pledge from March.
- Germany says it is adding nothing new.
- A board meeting on October 14 and 15 is meant to settle who releases what.

**2. What it's really about**
Trump is claiming a new European concession, but the barrels were already promised in March, and the US is behind on its own share too.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "to ease the war's oil shock" (subhead). I didn't know which war until "the war with Iran" in the next section.
- The full weekly price table sits in the middle of the story. I had to scroll through about 40 rows to get back to the text.
- "That leaves 75 million on paper, but some countries released more than they promised. Counting only the countries that fell short, about 100 million barrels are still owed." I re-read this twice. It uses three different totals (325, 75, 100) with no one clear picture.
- "lent oil companies 133.5 million of its 172 million barrels, to be paid back later with extra." A loan was never set up as the way the US releases oil.
- "on September 29 it offered to lend up to 40 million more, the last of its share." Later: "the United States still has about 38.5 million barrels of its own March share to lend." The 40 and the 38.5 don't obviously match.
- "Hours after the call he ruled out a diesel export ban, saying…". The previous sentence is about Wright, but the quote reads like Trump. I couldn't tell who "he" was.
- "France has 8 million barrels left," then "France signalled it could add 2 million barrels of diesel." I couldn't tell how the 8 and the 2 relate.
- "Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries had nearly halted Russia's fuel exports." This is a new actor with no setup.
- "Seven commodity ships crossed Hormuz on October 6… Hurricane Isaias… 62.9 percent." These felt like they came from a different story.
- The headline quote in "The catch", "100 million barrels of refined diesel", was not clearly introduced as a quote.

**4. Questions I still had**
- Why would the White House's version and the G7's differ? Is Trump wrong, or spinning it?
- Which countries actually owe what, and who is the main laggard?
- Why did Germany not release its barrels? "The market did not want them" is given without explanation.
- Did the export ban threat actually cause anything, or was it just pressure?
- Will diesel prices fall? The story never says what this means for the pump price.
- What did the October 7 meeting decide? The story only mentions "next week".
- What is "updated October 8" updating?

**6. Most note-like sentences**
- "U.S. diesel went from $3.81 a gallon the week before the war to a record $6.53 on September 21, and was $6.20 on October 5." The figures are stacked, and the chart and table repeat them.
- "By October 2 about 325 million of the March barrels had been released." This opens a run of figures: 75 million, 100 million, "less than a quarter", 8 million, 133.5 of 172, 40 million. It reads like a ledger.
- "Seven commodity ships crossed Hormuz on October 6, the fewest since July 23, and on October 8, with Hurricane Isaias approaching, producers shut in 62.9 percent of U.S. Gulf oil output." Two facts arrive with no link to the story.

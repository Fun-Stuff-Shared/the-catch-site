**1. Five sentences**
- On Friday afternoon the FAA held every departing flight in the US for 4 hours 23 minutes after radar and radio links became unstable. Thousands of flights were diverted or cancelled.
- On Friday night Transportation Secretary Duffy blamed old equipment and asked Congress for money.
- On Saturday he said the FAA believes a router at one remote site flooded the network while the contractor L3Harris was switching an old copper line to fiber. He said the FAA hasn't determined whether the router or the work was at fault.
- FAA Administrator Bedford said there is no sign of an attack. He said the old network spread the problem from one center to the others and will keep breaking until it is replaced.
- Everything else (cyberattack, Microsoft, the AI tool, staffing) is denied or unsupported. Airlines expect a full schedule by Sunday, and the FAA will share its analysis later.

**2. What it's really about**
Officials first used the outage to argue for new equipment, then said it started with one contractor job at one site, and the cause is still undetermined.

**3. Questions I still had**
- Who is Mullin? He appears three times with no introduction.
- Does "the only one" mean the only site or the only router?
- "Bedford said no facility went down" sits next to "17 of 22 centers saw instability." What does that mean?
- Bedford says the job was upkeep, not modernization. L3Harris says 70% of the copper-to-fiber conversion is done, and Duffy calls the job a copper-to-fiber switch. Is the job modernization or not?
- The Catch says Duffy wanted $50 billion, and elsewhere the White House asked for $30.1 billion. Are these different asks?
- Why did it take four hours to isolate one site? "Sheets of paper" is the only explanation given.
- Several sources in the list are headlined "Software update triggered…" and "FBI probe." The story never says whether that theory is dead.
- Did the "old network" claim really get contradicted? Bedford's spread story fits Duffy's Friday claim, so the Catch headline may overstate the shift.
- Why does a 2001 inspector-general warning count as "evidence for" a cyberattack?

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**
- "What the officials' account leaves to the network's age is the spread." It is a convoluted way to say the old network spread it.
- "On Saturday Duffy gave what he said the FAA believes set it off." Attribution is stacked three deep. The "which he thinks was the only one" clause then repeats in the lede, the What happened section, the table and the Catch.
- "Duffy's account starts with a routine job." It announces the account instead of stating the job.
- "Which failed there, the router or the work, he says is not yet determined." This restates the lede a fourth time.
- "Officials say they do not believe it was one and see no evidence of one." This is a verdict box that repeats the row above it.
- "Officials and the company all deny it." / "Microsoft and Mullin both say they are separate." These are summing-up verdicts that add nothing.
- "Nothing in the record ties it to staffing" and "Whether staffing slowed the recovery is not in the record." This is process language about the "record," and the reader never sees the record.
- "Evidence for: None in the record." / "Evidence for: The timing." The template is filled in even where there is nothing to fill.
- "The old network spread the failure, by Bedford's account, but the officials now say one site started it." This is two hedges in one sentence.
- "Friday it was old switches. Saturday it was one router at one site." It is a tidy slogan that makes the story argue a contradiction it doesn't establish.

**1. Five sentences**

- The Army has about 9,700 pounds of lead azide, an explosive that has become shock- and friction-sensitive in storage, in two bunkers at an Iowa plant. It wants to blow it up in place and has moved the date several times, most recently to Wednesday, Nov. 25.
- EPA issued an emergency permit for the blast. A federal appeals court paused that permit on Nov. 19 after the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club sued.
- The government now says an emergency exclusion lets the Army detonate without the permit. It says waiting is the bigger danger because cold weather could set the material off uncontrolled.
- The blast will put lead into the air and onto the ground. The Army's maps showed it reaching 60 to 100 miles, and the Army says levels will be low, but it gave no numbers. Neighbors in Iowa and Illinois say they were told late.
- The groups say EPA skipped public comment and didn't properly weigh safer methods. An ex-EPA scientist says the cold is no worse than what the material has already sat through.

**2. What it's about**

How an agency that sat on a deteriorating explosive for months is now using an emergency to justify skipping the normal process, and whether the public got told in time.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Does the court rule before Nov. 25, and can the Army legally go ahead if it doesn't?
- Is the emergency exclusion a real, usable route, or a workaround? The story only says the government claims it.
- Who is right about the cold? The Army says it is dangerous. Thomas says the soil insulates the bunkers. The story states both and doesn't say who has the better case.
- What lead levels should nearby residents expect? "Well below" what level? The story says the Army gave no numbers.
- Why did the ethanol-water bath sit unchecked for 20 years, and who is responsible?
- What was the March "pause" at the plant, and why was the commander saying there was no immediate danger?
- What are the cleanup procedures the Army says it will specify?
- Which towns are in the "affected area," and how many people live there?
- Are people near the parks being told to leave on Nov. 25, as they were for Nov. 20?
- How is the "Catch" a catch? The table implies the Army's March "immediately began communicating" line is false, but the story never says so.

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**

- "The Army says it has now scheduled the detonation..." The "now" announces news instead of stating it.
- "Its five criteria for choosing a method include how quickly the plant can get back to its work." This promises five and gives one.
- "The Army published its first plume maps on Nov. 15, and the Iowa governor's office says it learned of the plan on Nov. 16." This repeats the last two table rows. It reads as a summing-up line.
- "The record that would show how the Army weighed the other methods is its analysis, sent to EPA in letters in August and September, which the groups say EPA has not made public." The sentence is built around the word "record" and is hard to follow.
- "That matches what Illinois EPA said the same day of the Army's modeling, which it said could reach Mercer County." The word order is tangled.
- "The court's Nov. 19 order is an administrative stay that holds EPA's permit until the court rules on the groups' emergency motion." It explains the legal term with a definition, and the same point is made three times in the piece.
- "It says the cleaning instructions should include specific procedures, but does not give them." This is a hedge describing a non-answer.
- "The Catch" section opens with the bare question "When did the Army publish its plume maps?" It never answers that question as asked, and it isn't what the section is about.
- "What the coverage got right and wrong" with the tags "overstated" and "checks out, for the first maps." These are internal grading labels, and the section reads as the author checking other outlets' work.
- "Air reach as The New York Times reported the Army's maps; dust areas by the environmental groups' reading of them." This caption is sourcing paperwork inside the story.

**1. Five sentences**
- A U.S. Marine, Devin Ballard, 20, was arrested in Okinawa for strangling a 39-year-old woman, Anna Yagi, in a Naha hotel room and taking her wallet and backpack. He denies it.
- Japanese police found him on a bar street after studying hotel camera images, so Japan has custody. The U.S. ambassador says this shows the legal agreement on U.S. forces works.
- The U.S. military put all troops on Okinawa under a 48-hour pause and a 30-day curfew. Its commander offered sympathy but no apology.
- Okinawa's new governor, who is backed by the prime minister's party, says U.S. discipline has failed and wants the agreement reviewed. Tokyo protests but prefers smaller fixes.
- Prosecutors have up to 23 days to decide whether to charge him, and Okinawa's assembly votes on a protest on October 9.

**2. What it's really about**
Each new crime by a U.S. serviceman on Okinawa renews the same dispute over whether small fixes can ever be enough or the legal agreement itself has to change.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "a suspect the U.S. military catches first stays with the U.S. until Japan charges him. Police said that never became an issue here." I couldn't tell what "that" refers to, or why police were even asked.
- "When the room was not vacated by checkout time, a hotel employee checked it at about 4:15 a.m." They entered at 2:10 a.m., so a checkout deadline two hours later makes no sense. It reads like a typo or a missing step.
- "The night of October 3" opens with events in the early hours of October 3 and then jumps to "At about 11:30 that night." I had to recount the days. I also never learned when the man left the hotel.
- "Japan protested to the U.S. at several levels." I didn't know who protested to whom until the later section.
- "The training he ordered covers alcohol awareness." Training hadn't been mentioned before. The next line, "The police have not said whether Ballard had been drinking," seems to hint at something without saying it.
- "By October 6 many posts on X and other sites were blaming her." This jumps from the brother's statement to online blame, with no link to the rest of the story.
- "70.3 percent of the land in Japan used only by U.S. forces." I misread it at first as 70% of all U.S. land. It is only explained much later, under "What the 70 percent measures."
- "Kenneth Franklin Gadson, a former Marine... a civilian working on a base... an agreement clarifying which civilians the status of forces agreement covers." Dense, and I couldn't see why it matters to this case.
- The 2024 paragraph packs in unreported sexual-assault cases, an information-sharing system, the 1–5 a.m. bar ban, joint patrols and the arrest statistics. It reads as a list.
- "most since 2003" followed by "a nationwide campaign for revision that it began in 2003." Is the repeated year a coincidence? "Nationwide campaign" is also odd for a prefecture.
- "Koja accepts Tokyo's plan to move the Futenma base to a less crowded part of the island." I don't know what the plan is or where the base goes.
- "The catch" is a heading with no explanation of what it means. The section then corrects other outlets' errors, and I hadn't read those outlets.
- The "What coverage said" table has scrambled columns. I couldn't tell which quote goes with which outlet and which count.
- "AP's second story" appears with no first story introduced in the text.
- "Prosecutors can hold him for at most 23 days on this charge." "On this charge" suggests other charges could extend it, and that is never explained.

- When did he leave the hotel, and when did she die?

- Can he be held longer by rearrest on another charge?

**5. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "The governor now making that case is not an opponent of the bases." It's a pre-emptive contrast that nobody asked for.
- "Later cases brought more rules." This is a summarizing connective.
- "When Koja met Takaichi in Tokyo on October 7, he put Okinawa's complaint this way." This announces the quote.
- "How it came to this" is an announcing heading.
- "Police said that never became an issue here." It's vague and hedged.
- "Japan holds him because its police arrested him off base." The same point appears in the subhead ("so Japan holds him"), again in the third paragraph, and again in "Why Japan holds him."
- "Tokyo prefers targeted fixes to revising the agreement." It sums up the Motegi quote that follows.
- "On October 6 he would say only that Tokyo keeps the agreement under constant review." The "would say only" phrasing is odd and editorial.
- "The police have not said whether Ballard had been drinking." It insinuates a link and explains something nobody asked about.
- "Because he was off duty and the victim was Japanese, Japan has the first right to try him." It re-explains a point already made.

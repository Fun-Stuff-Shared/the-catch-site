**1. Five sentences**

1. On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil over four months, diesel first, and not to ban energy exports to each other.
2. Most or all of that oil seems to be what 32 countries promised in March but hadn't yet released. About 325 of the 400 million barrels promised then were out by October 2.
3. The White House says Trump got Europe to release 100 million barrels of diesel. Germany says no additional quantities are coming.
4. Diesel is short because the Iran war choked Hormuz and Russian fuel exports collapsed. U.S. diesel is near a record before the November 3 elections, and Washington had floated a U.S. diesel export ban that Europe feared.
5. The energy agency's board meets the week of October 12 to decide who releases what. France has said it will sell 10 million barrels, and nobody can say whether any of it is new.

**2. What it's really about**

It's about whether the G7 "deal" is new oil or old promises relabeled, and who gets to claim credit.

**3. Where I got lost**

- Top of the page: 400 pledged, 325 released, "about 100 million" left. I did the subtraction and got 75. The reason for the gap comes much later.
- "That is what 'commitments that have already been fulfilled' refers to." The paragraph before it is about Europe lagging. The next sentences are about countries over-delivering. The "that" points at the wrong thing.
- "Whose barrels" opens with the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve, 172 million, 133.5 million awarded, 38.5 million left, and a 40 million offer. I never learned whether those barrels are part of the 100 million. The paragraph then switches to Europe without connecting the two.
- "The March pledge and the U.S. share IEA counts for all members; Energy Department awards for the United States." This table caption is unreadable.
- Units shift between barrels, tonnes ("2.65 million tonnes… about 19.5 million barrels"), euro cents per litre and dollars per gallon.
- "refined fuels nearly 60 percent below, and diesel exports were about a quarter of what they had been." One figure is "below" and the next is "a quarter of". I had to convert to compare them.
- "Why diesel is short" goes from Hormuz to Houthis, then to the 2023 EU Russian ban, Ukrainian strikes, Russia's export ban and Chinese refiners. It reads as a list of facts, not a path.
- Who is closing Hormuz and attacking tankers is never said. "The war… choked the Strait" and "heaviest week of attacks on tankers" have no actor.
- "The United States supplied about half of the EU's diesel imports in August, the agency said" appears twice. The second time it's "the IEA said, and Europe pushed back," which reads as a non-sequitur.
- The price table mixes weekly and daily series. It shows Brent at 113.96 on September 29, while the closing paragraph says Brent settled at $104.28 on October 8, "up 4.1 percent." I couldn't tell how it got from 114 to about 100.
- The von der Leyen quote block appears with no introduction. It also repeats a point already made in the paragraph above it.
- "The catch" as a section label told me nothing.
- The "Who / What they said" table repeats the paragraph right above it, and the lede already said it.
- Trump said on September 30 he was "thinking about" a ban, then on October 2 said it "was never really ever on the table." The story never notes the contradiction.
- "before 20 days": 20 days from when?
- The last paragraph jumps to Hormuz ship counts, Kpler, "shut in 62.9 percent" (jargon), Hurricane Isaias and Trump saying he won't attack Iran. It's loosely about the market, but the Iran line connects to nothing in the story.

**4. Questions I still had**

- Does the U.S. remaining ~38.5 million count inside the 100 million? If so, how much is Europe's?
- Where does the oil go, and will it lower U.S. diesel prices? The story never says what effect to expect.
- If diesel is at a record, why did German companies buy only "a first portion" and the market "saw no need for more"?
- What does "substantial diesel in the first 20 days" mean in barrels?
- Which countries over-delivered, and by how much? Does 426 vs 400 account for the 25 million gap?
- What happens if the board doesn't agree on who releases what?
- Who made the threat claim: which official, if any? The 120 million over six months and 100 million within 20 days accounts conflict, and the story doesn't say which is right.
- Is the U.S. export ban an actual proposal? Bills are in the source list, not the text.
- Does Japan, a G7 member, contribute anything new?

**5. Machine-sounding sentences**

- "That is what 'commitments that have already been fulfilled' refers to." (announcing)
- "The two sides agree on two things: no G7 member restricts energy exports to another, and diesel goes first." (summing up)
- "Crude came back faster than fuel." (aphorism)
- "Washington wanted more, and faster." (aphorism, and "more" than what?)
- "In Washington it became diesel." (clever kicker)
- "The stocks will reach a market that tightened again this week." (set-up line)
- "The IEA counts the barrels pledged and still unreleased at about 100 million, not the 75 million that 400 minus 325 would suggest." (restates the lede a third time)
- Headings built as "not X": "It is not 100 million new barrels" and "'Refined diesel' is the White House's word, not the G7's."
- "Whether there was a threat." (a heading that announces the section)

**1. Five sentences**
- Trump has ordered Fed governor Lisa Cook to a closed White House hearing on November 5 about whether he can fire her "for cause."
- The cause is the claim that she told two lenders in 2021 that both a Michigan house and an Atlanta condo were her main residence. That can get a cheaper mortgage rate.
- The Supreme Court blocked his first firing in June because she hadn't been allowed to respond. It said he could try again.
- Cook says it was an honest mistake and hasn't been charged. A committee of three Trump officials will recommend, but the decision is his, with no deadline.
- Trump said he formed the committee because she's "dishonest" and "an automatic no vote." Her lawyers doubt the hearing is genuine, especially because one committee member, Hassett, already said she should take leave.

**2. What it's really about**
Whether a president can use a mortgage-form allegation to take control of a Fed seat, or whether this is about getting lower rates.

**3. Questions I still had**
- Did she actually get a lower rate on either loan? The story says the White House doesn't claim she did, but it never says what the loan documents show.
- What does "automatic no vote" mean? Cook voted with the majority on every rate decision, including the 12–0 hike. The story sets the quote beside those facts but never resolves them.
- Why would a fourth Trump seat matter when he'd still hold only four of seven?
- Why is Hassett's earlier "take leave" remark disqualifying?
- What is the "joint report" to Judge Cobb, and what is the lawsuit still doing, if the Supreme Court already ruled?
- Will Cook attend? Is the closed hearing legally different from an open one?
- What did Cook's side say about the "both promises" letter? The White House quote is left unanswered.
- Why is the "catch" section about press errors? The press-error section (the Guardian, Mediaite and AP) read as a separate story, and I didn't know there was a catch until the end.

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "The question is whether what she signed is cause."
- "Neither side disputes that she signed both forms, and the Supreme Court has ruled that she must have a chance to answer before any firing." This is a recap.
- "The Court held neither." It announces a correction before giving it.
- "…and said Trump could try again, which he is doing." This is a tidy kicker.
- "Their particular objection is Hassett."
- "It said cause is 'a substantial threshold,' a high bar…" The gloss explains a quote nobody asked about.
- "…covers concerns about her integrity, the reason Trump gives" and "meaning over how she votes." Both explain what the reader had already understood.
- "The Fed's governors help set interest rates." This explains something nobody asked.
- "She keeps voting in the meantime." This is a closing summary.
- "If he fires her, she can go back to court, and only then will judges weigh whether the charges amount to cause." It states a prediction as certain.
- The headings "Is a mortgage form reason to fire her?" and "Is this about interest rates?" announce the next section instead of letting it follow.
- The "Trump's case" and "Cook's case" labels are formulaic.

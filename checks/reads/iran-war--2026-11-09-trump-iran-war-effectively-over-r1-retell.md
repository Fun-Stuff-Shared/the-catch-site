**1. Five sentences**
- On November 9, Trump said the Iran war is "effectively over" and that the U.S. is just holding the status quo, which is a naval blockade that has run since July.
- The U.S. is still firing on ships that try to run the blockade, and Iranian drones are still being shot down over Bahrain.
- Asked why he won't declare the war over, Trump said allies there could get hurt. He also said he could strike Iran "right now" and would have "no hesitation."
- He said this days after midterm losses that he and Vance partly blame on the war.
- Congress has no War Powers report on the recent strikes, and Massie has filed a resolution to pull U.S. forces out.

**2. What it's really about**
Trump calls the war over while the U.S. keeps fighting it, and he has avoided saying it's over in a way that would end the blockade or trigger Congress.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Which Gulf states told him they want the status quo? Qatar says it wants to end "neither war nor peace," so who is he talking about?
- Hurt by what? What would end the war and hurt the allies?
- Rubio says "not status quo" and Trump says "status quo." Is that a real split or a wording difference? The story quotes both and doesn't say.
- What do the midterms have to do with the timing? "Why he said it now" lays out the losses and never says whether they explain it.
- Why does the missing War Powers report matter, and what follows if there isn't one?
- What is the "other war" (Venezuela), and what are the "minor strikes" near Qeshm?
- What is Task Force Iron Gate, and what is the new "land" lockdown?
- Why is "no troops killed in five months" mentioned and then dropped?
- Does Massie's resolution have any chance?

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "Asked to declare the war over, he gave a reason not to": a cute framing line, not news.
- "His secretary of state put the pause in the same terms." Nothing earlier established a "pause," and Rubio's "not status quo" doesn't match Trump's "status quo."
- "Trump's own reason is the Gulf." This announces a point in a clipped, headline-ese way.
- "The administration says force stays ready." This sums up before the quotes that show it.
- "Trump has not declared the war over." It repeats the headline, and the reader already knows it.
- "November 6 to 15, from transcripts and official statements only." This is process talk nobody asked for.
- "Bahrain's statement does not say whose air defenses fired." and "no government record of the week dates them." These are disclaimers that read like hedging.
- "Iran's core demands before nuclear talks include ending the blockade itself." This ends the section on a fact with no stated point, so the reader has to work out why it's there.
- "He spoke six days after the midterm elections" and "in the interview where he also said no troops had been killed in five months": both have a stray space before the period. The second also drops in an unrelated fact.
- In the table, "Trump: 'it's not a war'" and "Trump: 'No. No hesitation'" sit in the "what forces did" column. Those are statements, not actions.

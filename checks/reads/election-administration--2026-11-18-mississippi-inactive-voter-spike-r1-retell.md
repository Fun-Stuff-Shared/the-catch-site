**1. Five sentences**

- Mississippi's list of inactive voters (registered, but limited to affidavit ballots) grew from 165,778 after 2024 to 230,107 before this month's election.
- The state began giving counties Experian credit-bureau address data in July 2025. Counties then flagged voters as moved, sometimes wrongly, and some voters never got the notice because it went to a bad address.
- Secretary of State Watson says the 230,000 is a five-year accumulation and that nobody was removed without a local official's review. The numbers show about 60,000 of the growth came in two years, and he told the AP the Experian data caused it.
- Hinds County accepted only about 34% of its affidavit ballots, but it's unclear whether Experian had anything to do with that.
- Democratic lawmakers asked for an investigation on Nov. 19, but a hearing before January is unlikely.

**2. What it's really about**

It's about whether a state used unverified commercial address data to make tens of thousands of voters inactive, and nobody can yet say how many of them had actually moved.

**3. Questions I still had**

- How many of the ~60,000 actually moved? The story admits it doesn't know.
- What is the 113,000 "flagged for review" figure, and how does it connect to the 60,000 or the 230,107?
- Why does the AP's 171,000 differ from the federal 165,778 for 2024? The story uses both without explaining.
- Did the rejected affidavit ballots belong to people the Experian data flagged? The story never links them.
- What does "The 230,107 is the whole inactive list" correct? I hadn't seen anyone claim otherwise until the Catch section.
- What did Experian say, to whom, and when? That paragraph has no source or date.
- What does "can now be purged" mean? When, and how many people?
- What did the state's "uploaded the rest of it in 2026" mean?
- Did the roughly 5,400 rejected ballots include voters who were wrongly flagged?

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**

- "How many of the flags were wrong is the first number the caucus asks for." (announces a point already made)
- "Whether the newly inactive had moved is the open question" and "How many of the roughly 60,000 had in fact moved is what the caucus wants the investigation to find." (the heading and its sentence restate the lede)
- "Watson's 'more than five years' leaves out the rise he himself put on the Experian data" (a thesis-style heading; "put on" is odd)
- "The 230,107 is the whole inactive list, and it is about 60,000 larger than in 2024" (rebuts a misreading the reader never met)
- "The same counts show the list grew by about 60,000 in the two years since the 2024 election." (a repeat)
- "so they alone could not have changed that result" (hedged)
- "The AP says it is not clear whether the Experian data played a part in the drop." (a hedge)
- "Karriem says voters are still asking whether their votes counted." (empty attribution)
- "Meanwhile, voters made inactive before the 2024 election…" (a filler transition)
- The headings "From a new law to the investigation request" and "From a flag to an affidavit ballot." (a template)
- "2016 to 2024: what the state reported to the U.S. Election Assistance Commission after each November election. 2026: the state's own count… a different source, not the same series." (an explanatory caveat)
- "From a presidential election to the next midterm, the list rose by 15,573 from 2016 to 2018 and fell by 1,225 from 2020 to 2022." (stiff)

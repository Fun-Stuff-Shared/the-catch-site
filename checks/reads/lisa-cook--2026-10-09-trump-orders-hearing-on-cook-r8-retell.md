**1. Five sentences**
- Trump ordered Fed governor Lisa Cook to a closed White House hearing on November 5. Three officials he picked will question her about claims she lied on two 2021 mortgages.
- This is his second try. The Supreme Court kept her in office in June because she'd had no chance to answer, but said he could try again.
- Cook denies wrongdoing and hasn't been charged. Her lawyers call the Atlanta form an inadvertent oversight, and they doubt the hearing is genuine, partly because of one of the three, Hassett.
- The committee reports to Trump, who has no deadline. If he fires her, she can go back to court.
- Until then she's still a governor and votes on rates October 28.

**2. What it's really about**
Whether "for cause" actually limits Trump, or whether this is a pretext to replace a Fed governor he wants gone over rates.

**3. Questions I still had**
- The story says Trump calls her an "automatic no vote," but she has voted with the majority every time, including 12–0 on a hike. What does "no vote" mean?
- Why did Trump's own appointees vote to raise rates?
- Cook "denies wrongdoing," yet her lawyers concede she wrongly signed "primary residence." What exactly is denied?
- Why is the hearing "closed"? Closed to whom?
- Who were the four dissenters at the Court, and why?
- Why did Trump add a hearing after the written exchange, when the Court said paper was enough?
- If neither side claims she got a lower rate, what did the form gain her?
- Is the bit about the "vacation home" quote an argument by her lawyers or by the writer? Who is rebutting whom?
- What happens to the board's balance if she's out? "A fourth seat needs Senate confirmation" is mentioned in passing, never worked through.

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**
- "A president can fire a Fed governor only 'for cause,' and their job is to tell him whether he has it." The "their" is unclear, and the sentence explains what the reader hasn't asked yet.
- "first, they say, they want to judge whether the hearing is 'a genuine one…'" Stagey, with stacked "they"s.
- "Neither side disputes that she signed both forms, so the fight is over whether those signatures amount to cause. The Supreme Court has already settled that she gets a chance to answer before any firing." Announces the framing.
- "The Supreme Court adopted neither her view nor the government's, and left that question open." Clipped summary line.
- "The memo does not mention her lawyers' answer, or say why the written exchange was not enough." Explains an absence.
- "Yet on the evening of October 10 Trump gave two reasons…" The "Yet" is a gotcha setup.
- "By their account, she started out by asking…" Hedged attribution that leaves the reader unsure who is vouching.
- "Whatever the committee recommends, the decision is Trump's, and nothing sets a deadline for it." This is repeated nearly verbatim in "What happens next."
- "Until Trump decides, Cook is still a governor, with a vote at the Fed's next rate meeting on October 28." Wrap-up line.
- "and only then will judges weigh whether the charges amount to cause." Another wrap-up, and it sits oddly against the Court section.
- The paired headings "Why Trump says it is" / "Why Cook says it is not" and "Whether a mortgage form is cause to fire her" read like templates, not headings from a person.

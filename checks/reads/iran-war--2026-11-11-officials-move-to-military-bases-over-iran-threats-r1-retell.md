**1. Five sentences to a friend**

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent left his Washington home and moved onto a military base. He said on CNBC that the Secret Service told him to, because Iran has threatened to kill him. This comes days after Trump said Iran is "effectively over," and Bessent runs the sanctions campaign against Iran. Several other officials (Rubio, Hegseth, Miller) already live on bases, but they moved in 2025, before the war, so the Iran-threat framing in some coverage is wrong or overstated. The government hasn't said what the threats were, though intelligence letters and a CNN report describe Iran stepping up efforts to target officials. Iran denies it and calls it a disinformation campaign.

**2. What it's really about**

Bessent is the one official on record who moved because of Iran, but coverage blurred him together with officials who moved earlier for other reasons, so the "growing list" is mostly not Iran-driven.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Which base, and when did he move?
- What exactly were the threats? Did anyone try anything, or is it just talk?
- Why did Rubio, Miller, Hegseth, Noem and Blanche each move? The story says only that they moved before the war.
- Why did the Times report that "at least half a dozen" moved the year before, and are those the same people in the table?
- Is the Fox claim that Miller and Rubio relocated "in recent weeks" a mistake, or did they move again?
- What does "more than an advanced stage" mean?
- How does the WSJ photo of his house connect to the move? It appears once and is dropped.
- Why is the Spruance embassy post in a story about Bessent?
- Did the attorney general, intelligence director and CIA director letter have any link to Bessent?
- Is the dirty-bomb report real? CNN's sources call it "options," and the White House says there are no credible threats.

**4. Sentences that sounded machine-written**

- "Who lives on a base, and since when." It's a heading, but it works as an announcement.
- "On the record, one official has moved because of Iran: Bessent." It's a summing-up verdict that arrives before the evidence and reads like a thesis statement.
- "Other reports carried the same picture." It's filler that sets up the contradiction.
- "Iran's plotting against U.S. officials is older than the war." It's an explanatory aside nobody asked for, with a bare-assertion rhythm.
- "How serious the threat is." It's a heading that announces, then the section mostly answers "unknown."
- "The request is committee report language, not law." It's a defensive clarification that reads like a hedge.
- "The government has not said what the threats against Bessent were, and the first report called their severity unclear." It hedges twice in one sentence.
- "Bessent says the threats mean 'what we are doing with Operation Economic Outcast is working.'" It's quoted spin that is passed along without comment, which is neutral to the point of flat.
- "Both had lived on bases since 2025, four months before the war began." The "four months" doesn't fit the timeline given elsewhere (Oct. 30, 2025 against a war start I can't place), and I couldn't check it.

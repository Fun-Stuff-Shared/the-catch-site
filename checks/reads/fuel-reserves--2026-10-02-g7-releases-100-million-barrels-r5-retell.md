**1. Five sentences**
- On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil over four months, with a big diesel release first.
- Those barrels were already promised in March, as part of a 400 million barrel pledge, and about 100 million of that pledge was never released.
- The White House says Europe agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel. The G7 statement names no diesel amount and covers the US, Japan and Canada too.
- Europe has added nothing new, and Germany, the EU and France are holding off. The US had pressured them with talk of a diesel export ban, then ruled the ban out.
- Diesel is at record prices, and an agency board meeting on October 14 and 15 is supposed to settle who releases what.

**2. What it's really about**
A "new" emergency release is mostly the unfinished old one, and the White House is selling it as a European diesel concession.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "the energy agency's chief Fatih Birol". The agency isn't named yet, and "the agency" is used for the rest of the piece.
- "The U.S. pledge of 172 million barrels is all crude." I didn't see why this mattered until later, when the Europe-diesel ask made sense.
- "three people close to the talks said France and Germany were told..." and "Macron said Trump made no threat on the call, and hours after it Trump ruled out a ban." Which call? It was never introduced.
- "Diesel is in tight supply. In the U.S. it has gone from $3.81 to $6.20..." This is a stray fact that is repeated verbatim in the next section.
- "Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries had nearly halted Russia's fuel exports." It arrives with no link to the G7 story.
- "That would leave 75 million, but some countries released more than they promised, and the countries that fell short still owe about 100 million." I re-read this twice.
- "awarded loans for 133.5 million" and then "exchange contracts". I don't know whether these are loans or exchanges. 172 minus 133.5 is 38.5, but the text says 40.
- "France has 8 million barrels left" and "France has floated 2 million barrels of diesel". Left of what? The source list also has France putting 10 million on the market, and the text never mentions it.
- "$3.81" and "$3.90 on March 2". These are different baselines for the same claim.
- "The sides: Both sides back the same plan". Then what are the sides?
- "recommitted to not restricting energy exports to one another" drops in with no setup.
- "Tenders", "Vitol" and "Saxo Bank" are unexplained.
- "next week" is relative to an unstated date.
- The 40-row price table sits in the middle of the story.

**4. Questions left**
- Is any of the 100 million actually new?
- How much diesel will Europe really release, and when?
- Which countries owe the 100 million, and how much does each owe?
- Why is demand for Germany's barrels weak when diesel is short?
- What did the October 7 meeting decide? It appears in the sources but not the text.
- Will this lower pump prices?
- Why would a US export ban hit Europe, and how much does that leverage matter?

**5. Machine-sounding**
- "The barrels were already promised."
- "Diesel is in tight supply."
- "A release is an offer: governments put stock on the market, and companies take it up or not."
- "Both sides back the same plan: ..."
- "Neither Germany nor the agency says why demand for Germany's offered stocks was weak while diesel is short."
- Headings like "The catch" and "The sides".

**6. Most like notes**
- "Germany says 'no additional quantities' as of now, EU officials are holding off 'pending a clearer IEA assessment,' and France has floated 2 million barrels of diesel without a firm offer." It is three sources and two quotes stacked, and the reader has to work out the point.
- "The United States has awarded loans for 133.5 million of its 172 million barrels; when it offered the last 40 million in June, companies took about 500,000, and it offered them again on September 29." It is a run of numbers and dates with no stated point.
- "U.S. diesel went from $3.81 a gallon the week before the war to a record $6.53 on September 21, and was $6.20 on October 5. In Europe diesel prices have doubled since late February." Prices are stacked with no reason to be there.

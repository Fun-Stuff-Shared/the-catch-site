**1. Five sentences**
The G7 leaders agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil over four months. In March, 32 International Energy Agency members had already pledged 400 million barrels, and about 325 million have gone out, which leaves roughly 100 million unreleased. The agency says finishing the March release would bring about that much to market, so the G7 number may not be new oil. The White House calls the deal 100 million barrels of European diesel, won by threatening a U.S. diesel export ban that Trump then dropped. Germany and Japan say they won't release more, France and Italy leave it open, and the agency's board meets the week of October 12 to decide who releases what.

**2. What it's really about**
Whether the G7's "new" 100 million barrels is just the unfinished March pledge relabeled as a diesel win.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "More than 75 million is still owed, because more than 400 million was promised… adds up to 426 million, and some countries released more than they promised." I had to redo the arithmetic: 400 minus 325 is 75, but the headline number is 100. The "over-delivered" clause muddies it further.
- "Exchange" in "contracts to exchange 133.5 million barrels." The next sentence says the oil is lent and repaid with extra barrels, but I didn't connect the two at first.
- The table. Eight rows mix IEA and Energy Department figures, and 133.5, 38.5 and 40 are hard to relate to the 100 million. I couldn't tell where the U.S. barrels fit.
- "through Saudi Arabia's Red Sea port of Yanbu and the Emirati port of Fujairah, Attacks in the Red Sea…" The comma and capital A look like a broken sentence.
- "diesel exports were about a quarter of their pre-war level, while its crude exports were just under 45 percent down." It compares "a quarter of" with "down 45 percent," so I had to convert one to match the other.
- "Russia had supplied 53 percent… until the European Union banned… from February 5, 2023." The story jumps back to 2023 and then to Chinese refiners, with "4.58 million tonnes" after barrels everywhere else.
- "a hurricane approaching the U.S. Gulf Coast, where producers had stopped 62.9 percent of their oil output." The hurricane appears from nowhere.
- "naval blockade of Iranian ports it has enforced since July 14" and "a ceasefire that includes Lebanon." Neither blockade nor Lebanon had been introduced.
- "Trump said talks were 'productive' and that the United States would not attack Iran before the November 3 elections." It reads as a different story.
- "blamed record pump prices on the wars" (plural). Which wars?
- The French quote, "considérer les options de libération potentielle de stocks strategiques." It is translated, but it interrupts the paragraph.
- The retail diesel prices (6.199, 6.529, 3.897) sit at the end of the White House paragraph, unconnected to it.
- "Euronews" is named in prose ("seen by Euronews"). I wondered who that was.
- France's numbers: 8 million still owed, "could release 2 million on top," then 10 million sold on October 7. I couldn't tell how they relate. The last sentence says the story doesn't know either.
- "After the G7 call" opens two consecutive sentences with different subjects, which blurs the order of events.
- Vitol's chief executive is quoted with no explanation of who Vitol is beyond "commodities trading house."
- The source list at the bottom, especially "Source history" lines like "'133 million barrels' U.S. Department of Energy reported on September 29." They are unexplained fragments, and 133 doesn't match the 133.5 in the text.

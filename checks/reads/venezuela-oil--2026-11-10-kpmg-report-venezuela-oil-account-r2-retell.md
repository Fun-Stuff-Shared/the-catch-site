**1. Five sentences**

- After U.S. forces seized Maduro in January, buyers of Venezuelan oil had to pay into an account the U.S. controls.
- A State Department report, made with KPMG, shows $12.26 billion went in through September and $12.11 billion went back out.
- Almost all of it left as dollars sold to Venezuelan banks, which resold them to businesses and individuals. The report doesn't say who those buyers were.
- Officials had promised audits, but the report says KPMG's work isn't an audit. Trump has claimed the money paid for the war, but the report shows no payment to any U.S. agency.
- Venezuela's government, its opposition and U.S. senators each offer a different account of who gained. The report can't settle any of them, and Democrats take over Congress in January.

**2. What it's really about**
The U.S. says it holds Venezuela's oil money, but nobody outside the government can see who ended up with it, and what officials promised (audits) and claimed (the war is paid for) doesn't match the report.

**3. Questions I still had**
- Who bought the dollars, and at what rate? The story says the data exists but isn't public.
- What are NABEP, Chevron's role and the "35% stake"? NABEP is never explained, and the stake sits oddly next to the claim that the money went elsewhere.
- Do the numbers reconcile? $12.26B in minus $12.11B out should leave about $150M, but the story says $168.6M is held. The $15.0M "still held" is also counted inside the $12.11B "paid out," and I couldn't tell whether the $192.9M of banknotes awaiting release is part of the $549.8M.
- Does Trump's "hundreds of billions" refer to the account or to something else? The Catch section jumps between the account and the NABEP deal. It says "the other two answers have no record behind them," right after describing a stake and a discount cargo.
- Which of Trump's "three answers" is "the second"? I had to count back.
- Why did the Times say $130M went to foreign debts when the public ledger shows $10.4M? Is the public report different from the confidential one, or is the Times wrong?
- What does "Vitol's role is a separate story" mean? The line is dropped in at the end with no explanation.
- Is $84.4M for KPMG a lot? And did the BDO review find anything?
- Who actually gets hurt or helped, in plain terms? Venezuelan consumers, insiders and U.S. taxpayers are all left abstract.

**4. Sentences that sounded machine-written**
- "Where the money went, as each side explains it, with the evidence for and against." This is a section blurb that announces what the section does.
- "Every explanation, tested" is a headline in the same mode.
- "Concentration among banks does not show concentration among buyers." It reads as a tidy rule, not as reporting.
- "which leaves it unproven, not disproved." This is a hedge-and-balance verdict line.
- "The money Trump was asked about went back to Venezuela. The NABEP deal's terms record no payment to the U.S. either." Two verdict lines summing up what the section just said.
- "the report answers part of that." It's vague and sums up, and it never says which part.
- "The report says it may be updated with future data." This is filler hedging.
- "Vitol's role is a separate story." It announces something and doesn't deliver it.
- "The Times worked from the confidential copy; these figures are from the public one." This is defensive sourcing nobody asked for.
- "That fits the report's account of the bolívars funding payrolls, but no record ties her figures to the account's." It's a stack of hedges.
- "A sale works like this:" This is a teacher's announcement, and the explanation after it is dense.
- "Who in Venezuela ended up with the dollars is in data the State Department holds and has not published." It's fine as a fact, but the story says it three or four times.
- "The report shows the process is working," an official said, in a statement issued anonymously." This is a fair quote, but "issued anonymously" is clunky.

The Rodríguez row and the Trump row each repeat their own point three times: in the summary, in the evidence and in the verdict.

**1. Five sentences**
- On November 16, Commerce issued a rule requiring a license to ship advanced AI chips to data centers outside the U.S. that rent more than a set amount of computing power to Chinese companies. For Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, any shipment of these chips needs a license.
- Chinese firms had been getting around the chip limits by renting from Southeast Asian data centers. A similar Biden-era rule was never enforced, and Commerce had promised a replacement.
- The rule landed days after Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang resigned over an affair with a former executive of Megaspeed, a data center company under smuggling investigation. Treasury Secretary Bessent then blamed Commerce for not blacklisting Megaspeed.
- Treasury and the National Security Council wrote the rule, and Commerce Secretary Lutnick reportedly wanted something leaner.
- Media coverage overstated it: it is a license requirement, not a ban, it doesn't cover U.S. data centers, and the dates were reported wrongly.

**2. What it's about:** Who drove this rule (Treasury and the White House over Commerce) and how much of it the coverage got wrong, set against a scandal that looks like the cause but may not be.

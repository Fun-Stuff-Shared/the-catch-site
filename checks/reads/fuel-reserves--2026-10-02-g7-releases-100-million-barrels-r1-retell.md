**1. Five sentences**

The G7 leaders agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels of oil through the IEA over four months, with a big diesel release in the first 20 days. They gave no number for the diesel and didn't say how much is new beyond the 400 million barrels pledged in March, of which about 325 million had been released. The US had been pushing Europe to release diesel and had threatened a diesel export ban. After the deal, Trump said he wouldn't authorize a ban. France, the White House and the press each describe the 100 million barrels differently (diesel and crude, or only refined diesel). A social media post claimed 100 million barrels of diesel were already on their way to the US, which the story says came from an agriculture secretary's "I think." Japan won't add to the release, and no one reports a barrel delivered.

**2. What it's really about**

A headline number that everyone describes differently, that may partly repackage earlier pledges, and that nobody has delivered.

**3. Where I got lost**

- "the US-Israeli conflict with Iran and Russia's war in Ukraine disrupted global markets": I'm dropped into a war I haven't been told about. It turns up later as "the war in the Middle East" and the "Strait of Hormuz," with no explanation of what has happened.
- "March commitments" is in the headline, but I don't learn what the March pledge was until the fourth paragraph.
- "about 325 million had been released," then "Awarded before that exchange: more than 133 million," then "172-million-barrel share." Released, awarded, pledged and exchanged are four different things. I couldn't tell how they fit together or whether 133 + 40 is meant to reach 172.
- "a loan they will repay later with extra barrels": which companies, and why does that count as a release?
- "The French presidency's account ... puts the release at up to 100 million barrels... of refined products, diesel and crude." It's dropped in as a one-line paragraph, and I didn't see why it mattered until "The catch."
- "No account of the October 2 meeting reports a barrel of the October release delivered." This sits alone mid-article, and the same point is repeated later.
- The "Whether the barrels help at the pump" paragraph jumps from crude vs. diesel to Gulf export numbers, Wright, refinery maintenance, Hormuz and "the Red Sea's Yanbu canal." I didn't know Yanbu, and I couldn't tell what "390,000 barrels a day" was being compared to.
- "That day European Union countries discussed a French proposal for 50 million barrels...": which day?
- US demands appear as "100 million barrels of diesel within 20 days" and, later, "120 million barrels of diesel over the next six months." They are never reconciled.
- "An X post the same day went further": whose post? The poster is never named, only "Rollins" is.
- The Rollins quotes ("For the short term, we're going to do everything we can," "I'm the secretary of agriculture, not energy") felt like a tangent before I reached the point.
- The article is dated October 2, but it reports October 5 and 6 events. The "Where this sits" table also repeats what the text already said.
- "Several European countries had released only a fraction of the crude and products they pledged" appears only in the table, and I couldn't connect it to the 325 of 400.
- "Brent," "WTI" and "Cushing" are only partly glossed. I didn't know why March 2 and February 27 are the comparison dates.
- The October 5 dyed-diesel tax order has nothing to do with the release, and the story says so itself ("It is not a reserve delivery").
- "The catch" is never defined for me. I didn't know what the catch was supposed to be.
- The price tables with "Diesel, week -0.147 dollars per gallon," and the "Source history" and "Sources" blocks, read as data dumps.

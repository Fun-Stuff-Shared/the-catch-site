**1. Five sentences**
- Employers added about 29,000 jobs in September, far below the roughly 90,000 economists expected. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1.
- The government revised July and August down by 60,000 combined, and it called the September changes "little," meaning too small to tell from no change.
- A White House official blamed federal job cuts for the weak total, but the story says federal jobs barely moved in September.
- The Fed raised interest rates on September 16 and meets again October 27-28, so weak jobs numbers might change what it does.
- Because immigration has slowed, the economy may need far fewer new jobs to keep unemployment steady, so 29,000 may be less alarming than it looks.

**2. What it's really about**
A small, noisy number is being read as either a slowdown or a boom, and the real question is how many jobs the economy now needs.

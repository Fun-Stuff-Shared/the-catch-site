**1. Five sentences**
- Japanese police arrested Lance Cpl. Devin Ballard, a 20-year-old Marine, on suspicion of strangling Anna Yagi, 39, in a Naha hotel room and stealing her wallet and backpack. He denies it.
- He was caught off base, so Japan holds him. The U.S. ambassador says that shows the status of forces agreement works.
- Japan's foreign and defense ministers protested. The U.S. ordered a two-day pause and a 30-day curfew, but its commander on Okinawa offered sympathy without an apology.
- Okinawa's new governor says U.S. discipline and education have failed and wants the agreement reviewed. Tokyo promises talks on prevention but doesn't commit to revision.
- Prosecutors have up to 23 days to decide whether to charge him.

**2. What it's really about**
Whether repeated U.S. crimes on Okinawa can be handled with discipline measures and protests, or whether the legal agreement itself has to change.

**3. Where I got lost**
- "the commander of U.S. forces in Japan" (summoned) and "Its commander on Okinawa, Lt. Gen. Benjamin Watson". Two commanders, and the first is never named.
- "Because Japanese police made the arrest, no handover question arose, police said". "Handover" isn't explained yet. The sentence also joins a police remark to the ambassador's quote.
- "SOFA" appears for the first time inside a quote, after the full name was used.
- "When the room was not vacated by checkout time, a hotel employee checked it at about 4:15 a.m." The couple entered at 2:10 a.m., so a checkout time of about 4 a.m. makes no sense.
- "By October 6 many posts on X and other sites were blaming her for what happened to her." This comes straight after the brother's statement. I didn't know why people would blame her, and the story never says what she was doing with the man.
- "Because Gadson was a civilian base worker, the two governments signed an agreement in January 2017 on which civilians the status of forces agreement covers." Rina Shimabukuro is never introduced. "Former Marine" versus "civilian base worker" and what the civilian agreement covers were unclear.
- "In July Tokyo started a new system". July of which year?
- "In a campaign across Japan for revision that it began in 2003". I couldn't tell what the campaign was.
- "Koja accepts Tokyo's plan to keep the Futenma base on Okinawa but move it to a less populated area". This jumps to an unconnected topic, and it never says why it matters to this story.
- "Of all the land in Japan set aside for the exclusive use of U.S. forces, 70.3 percent". The paragraph's next sentence starts "Their legal status", and I wasn't sure who "their" was.
- "Asked about revising the agreement in 2025, Motegi said…". Why a year-old answer?
- "when that trust is damaged". Which trust?
- "@USMC's core values". A social-media handle in running prose.
- In "The catch" section, AP, NPR, PBS, BBC, CBS, Al Jazeera and the Yomiuri appear without introduction. The story never named a news outlet until then, so I didn't know why they mattered.
- The table has a "What coverage said" heading, but the columns read like a header row followed by mismatched rows. I couldn't tell which cell was a claim and which was a correction.
- "the draft that asks for commanders to be disciplined". This sentence is tangled.
- The "Where he was arrested" item (Naha vs. Okinawa City) is a minor correction that interrupts the story.
- "He was promoted to lance corporal a year before the arrest" and the enlistment date don't connect to anything.

**4. Questions I still had**
- What ties Ballard to the crime beyond tattoos and a shaved head on camera? Did he say anything?
- Who was the man, and what was his relationship with Yagi? Why were people blaming her?
- If Japan already holds him and the agreement "worked" here, what exactly would revising it change? What does Okinawa want revised?
- Was he breaking any existing rule, such as the 1–5 a.m. bar ban or a curfew, when this happened?
- Which of the six handover requests was refused, and why?
- Will Tokyo actually seek revision? The story never says yes or no.
- What did the LDP's Hamada want instead of a protest?
- Does the Okinawa Policy Council meet, and when?
- What is the phone-cord detail? It appears only in the source list, not in the body.

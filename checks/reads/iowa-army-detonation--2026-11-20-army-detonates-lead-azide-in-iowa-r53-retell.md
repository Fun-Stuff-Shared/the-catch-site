**1. Five sentences**

The Army is going to blow up about 9,700 pounds of old, unstable lead azide stored in two bunkers at an Iowa ammunition plant. Two environmental groups sued over EPA's emergency permit for it, and an appeals court paused the permit on Nov. 19. On Nov. 23 the court lifted the pause and refused to block the permit, saying the real choice is a planned blast or an unplanned explosion. The Army moved the blast to Wednesday, Nov. 25, for better weather and to get its protective measures in place. The groups, Illinois and Iowa officials, and others say the public was told late and that safer alternatives weren't properly considered. Meanwhile the Army's advice for cleaning up lead dust ("hose it off") is disputed.

**2. What it's really about**

The Army and EPA decided in advance that the blast was the only option and told the public late, and the court has now let that decision stand.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Will the blast go ahead on Nov. 25, and what weather and permit conditions could stop it?
- If the Army says it can act under an emergency exclusion without the permit, why did it need the permit at all, and why does the court not weigh in?
- How much lead will actually reach nearby towns? The Army gave no numbers, and the maps are worst-case.
- Did the cold really raise the risk? The affidavit says the bunkers already went through similar cold. The court's ruling doesn't say how it weighed that.
- What do the Army's other rejected options look like? MuniRem is named, but I can't tell whether it would work or why EPA rejected it.
- Is "lead dust" dangerous at the levels expected? The story says "temporary and reversible discomfort" but not what the health risk is.
- Why did the story keep two numbers for the dust (more than 40 miles, about 25 miles) without saying which to trust?
- What are the Army's five criteria? The story names only one.
- What is the Catch? The timeline implies the Army said it communicated early while saying "no immediate danger" in March. The story never says that plainly.

- "The Army published its first plume maps on Nov. 15, and the Iowa governor's office says it learned of the plan on Nov. 16." This repeats two rows of the table above it.
- "With the pause lifted, EPA's permit is no longer on hold; the court says it took effect Nov. 13 and expires Feb. 11, 2027." It restates the lede.
- "The court's Nov. 19 order is an administrative stay that holds EPA's permit until the court rules on the groups' emergency motion. The court ruled on Nov. 23 and lifted it." This is explaining mechanics nobody asked about.
- "The roughly 100 miles reported for the Army's first maps was lead in the air. The air map's edge is the smallest amount the Army's samplers can detect, and the maps assume all the lead is released." This is a defensive explanation.
- "Postponing the blast, Telle wrote:" announces the quote.

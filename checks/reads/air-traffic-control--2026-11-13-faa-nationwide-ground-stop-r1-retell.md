**1. Five sentences**
- On Friday afternoon the FAA stopped every departing flight in the U.S. for 4 hours 23 minutes, which stranded thousands of flights and caused thousands of cancellations.
- Transportation Secretary Duffy says old analog switches and routers got overloaded with data and sent inconsistent data, so the FAA shut the system down.
- Homeland Security Secretary Mullin says new equipment and patches the FAA was installing appear to have overwhelmed the old system, and anonymous sources say a software update made the network crash.
- Nobody official has named the update or said why it failed on Friday rather than Thursday.
- Duffy is using the outage to ask Congress for $50 billion, and the story says the only trigger named on the record is the FAA's own upgrade, not the old hardware.

**2. What it's about**
The administration is citing old equipment to argue for $50 billion while its own officials' account points to the upgrade as the trigger.

**3. Questions left**
- What was the update or patch, who pushed it, and what did it touch?
- Why did the failure hit at 3:10 p.m. Friday and not earlier?
- Is the "second nationwide stop since 9/11" claim only from two unnamed FAA people?
- Who are "the officials" the tables lean on, and do Duffy and Mullin actually disagree?
- How many passengers were affected, beyond flight counts?
- The "Said by" column has no explanation for the odd labels ("Guesses from the word 'telecommunications'"). Who was actually guessing?
- What does "mixed" or "Partly supported" mean for a cause nobody has confirmed?
- How long will recovery take beyond Saturday?
- Is the $30.1 billion request connected to the $50 billion, or separate?

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "Eight explanations in circulation, each set against the record."
- "Their finding would settle it." (also "the FAA's finding would settle it")
- "No evidence so far, and officials are not ruling it out: Mullin says they are still looking, and Duffy said he did not want to rule out nefarious activity." Verdict boxes repeat the row above them.
- "Evidence for: None in the record."
- "On the record, this is what broke. It is the officials' account, and the FAA's investigation is not finished." The "investigation is not finished" clause recurs in nearly every verdict.
- "'Old hardware' says what broke, not why it broke on Friday." This announces the argument as a slogan.
- "The case for $50 billion rests on the old hardware. The only cause of Friday's failure the administration has named is the upgrade itself…" This is a tidy summing-up line that argues a point.
- "the outage now sits in the middle of the fight over how much Congress gives the FAA and for what." This is a generic wrap-up.
- "Not everything stopped:" and "The network has failed a single facility before." Both are stagey setups.
- "Verizon's denial covers Verizon, and no record ties another carrier to Friday." This is a neat aphorism, and "No record links them" and "no record links it" repeat as a tic.
- "the account of what broke is Duffy's." This is source-admission ceremony that few readers need.

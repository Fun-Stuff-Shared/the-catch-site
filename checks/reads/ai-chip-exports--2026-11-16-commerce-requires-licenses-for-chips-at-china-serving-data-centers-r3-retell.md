**1. Five sentences**

- On November 16 the Commerce Department issued a rule requiring a U.S. license to ship advanced AI chips to data centers outside the U.S. that rent more than a set amount of computing power to Chinese companies. It also requires licenses for chips going to five countries, including Malaysia and Singapore.
- Chinese firms can't buy the best chips directly, so they rent them from Southeast Asian data centers. The rule closes that route, and data centers already serving them have 90 days to get licensed.
- The rule had been promised since May 2025 and reportedly got presidential approval in October, with Commerce Secretary Lutnick resisting. It landed days after Nvidia's CEO resigned over an affair with an executive of Megaspeed, a data center company under smuggling investigation, and after the Treasury Secretary blamed Commerce for not blacklisting that company.
- Commerce says Treasury and the National Security Council wrote the rule. Commerce also says it was already working on remote-access rules and had been investigating Megaspeed since 2024.
- Some coverage said remote access is "banned" or that the rule "took effect immediately," and the story says both are wrong. The rule is a case-by-case license requirement, it covers only data centers abroad, and it takes effect when published on November 19. Chinese firms can still train on advanced chips in U.S. data centers.

**2. What it's really about**

A fight inside the administration over how hard to hit China's workaround, which Treasury and the White House won over Commerce, and which still leaves a large gap.

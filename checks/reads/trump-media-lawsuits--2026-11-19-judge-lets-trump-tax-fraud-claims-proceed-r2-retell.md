**1. Five sentences**

- A federal judge in Tampa let Trump's defamation suit against The New York Times, three of its reporters and Penguin Random House go forward on 7 of 34 statements. He threw out the other 27.
- Six of the seven are tax-fraud claims from the book *Lucky Loser*. Those claims are against Penguin and the two authors, not the Times company.
- The seventh is a Times article by Peter Baker saying authorities looked into Trump's Mafia ties and found money-laundering violations.
- The judge's reasoning: anyone who publishes a claim of complex crime without a qualified expert's explicit opinion must know the claim is probably false. He said the defendants' experts, who called the scheme "suspicious" and said it "smells like a crime", didn't meet that bar.
- Much of the coverage got this wrong, for example by tying the tax claims to the Times. The defendants answer by Dec. 17, and truth hasn't been decided.

**2. What it's really about**

The ruling is narrower than the headlines said, and the judge's "you must have an expert" rule is the part that matters.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Why does the Times-versus-Penguin split matter, if the authors are Times reporters and still sued? What does the Times gain or lose?
- Which of the 27 dismissed statements fell for which reason? The story says the judge didn't say.
- Is the judge's rule normal law or a stretch? Stern says it "completely misconstrues" actual malice, but the story never says how.
- Does the rule apply to Baker's sentence? It isn't a tax claim, and the sentence is a summary of past government actions.
- Is Baker's sentence true? The story gives the Taj Mahal and 1988 stock facts but never says whether they support "penalized him".
- Is the 2018 reporting actually solid? "Never charged" is Trump's falsity argument, and the story doesn't weigh it.
- Why can't Trump amend again, and why does the judge's order leave the dismissed statements' use at trial open?
- Who is Jim Ledley, and did the defense actually have an expert's explicit opinion or not?
- What are the two reserved arguments (one-year limit, New York law) worth if they win?
- What does Trump gain by winning on the book claims, given Penguin has an independent-contractor defense?

**1. Five sentences**
- Companies asked Commerce for licenses to sell about 3.4 million Nvidia and AMD AI chips to Chinese buyers. By May 11, Commerce had approved 879,868 of them, but all were marked "not shipped."
- The House Foreign Affairs Committee made the table public on November 19. About 1.9 million chips' worth of requests were returned without a decision, and one request was rejected.
- Several approved buyers (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu, BYD) are on the Pentagon's Chinese-military-company list. That is 272,000 chips, about 31% of the approvals.
- News outlets and a former official said the chips had already gone to China. The table doesn't show that, though Nvidia says licensed H200s did ship later, and it has now stopped.
- The same week, Nvidia's CEO resigned and Commerce issued a new rule. Democrats say Commerce approved these licenses with little scrutiny.

**2. What it's really about**
Press coverage conflated "approved" with "shipped," and the data shows a licensing regime that approved a lot on paper, with unclear real-world effect.

- "Nvidia's interim chief executive, Colette Kress, explained the figure:" It announces an explanation, and the quote that follows doesn't explain the figure.
- "Nvidia says licensed H200s reached China later, in its quarter that ended October 25; What shipped after May has the details." This is signposting, with a stray capital after the semicolon.
- "...and other reports also misread the numbers, as the table below shows." It announces the table.
- "The Hill wrote that 'the order was fulfilled'; the table it describes shows the approvals unused as of May 11..." The semicolon chain reads as a built-up rebuttal.
- "A license is permission to ship." It explains something nobody asked.
- "Returning an application is not denying it: BIS sends an application back when, among other reasons, the applicant asks..." This is defensive hedging, followed by "The table gives no reason for any return."
- "The table gives no reason for any return." It appears twice: once in the body and once as the full verdict in the explanations box. The repeat reads as filler.

- "Commerce says its approvals rest on the January rule's added national-security requirements and on deliberation among several agencies." This is boilerplate press-office phrasing that says nothing specific.

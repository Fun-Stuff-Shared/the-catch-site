**1. Five sentences**

- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his house onto a military base on Secret Service advice. He said Iran made death threats against him and that a Wall Street Journal photo of his house also pushed him to move.
- The NYT broke it on Nov. 10, and Bessent confirmed it on CNBC on Nov. 13.
- Fox News then said Rubio and Stephen Miller were also moving onto bases "in recent weeks" as Iran targets officials, and USA Today and Israel Hayom ran with that.
- The story says Rubio, Hegseth, Miller and Noem were already living on bases in 2025, before the war, and that Blanche has been on one since early 2026. It also says no named official has tied any move other than Bessent's to Iran.
- Meanwhile, intelligence officials say Iran's plotting has increased, CNN reports Iranian plans for dirty-bomb or chemical attacks, the White House says there is no credible homeland threat, and Iran calls it all a smear.

**2. What it's about**

Iranian threats are real, but "officials are hiding on bases from Iran" is overstated, because most of those officials were already on bases for other reasons.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Why did Rubio, Hegseth, Miller and Blanche live on bases? Only Miller (protests) and Noem (a tabloid story) get a reason. The story says Rubio and Hegseth were on Fort McNair by October 2025 but not why.
- Is Fox simply wrong that Rubio and Miller moved "in recent weeks"? Or did they move again to a different place? The story leaves this open.
- What was the threat to Bessent? Did anyone act on it?
- Why does Hugh Hewitt's question, and Trump's answer about what happens to Iranians who kill an official, appear here?
- What does the Spruance post have to do with this? Who are those officers?
- How do the dirty-bomb report and the Mullin "aspirational threats" line bear on why officials moved?
- Is "effectively over" meant to contrast with the threats? The story never says.
- What happened to Noem, and when did Mullin take over DHS?
- Which side is the story on? The Catch section doesn't say what the facts add up to.

**4. Sentences that sound machine-written**

- "In the reports and statements checked for this story, no named official or spokesperson has tied any other official's move to Iran." It hedges and describes the story's own method.
- "Neither man's reason is quoted." In the dek, Blanche has just been mentioned, so it's unclear who "neither man" means.
- "Officials named in the coverage, by when each is on record as living on a base." This is the table caption.
- "On the record, only Bessent's move is tied to Iran." It reads like a heading that announces the point.
- "Other outlets also tied the earlier moves to Iran." It is vague about which moves are "earlier."
- "Iran's plotting against U.S. officials is older than the war." This is a background aside that nobody asked for, and it ends the Catch section without a conclusion.
- "the request is committee report language, not law." It explains something the reader didn't ask about.
- "The government has not said what the threats against Bessent were, and the first report called their severity unclear." This is a summing-up that gives no new information.
- "How serious the threat is." The heading promises an answer, and the section lists claims from both sides without giving one.
- "Several senior officials were moved into military housing... Asked in April why his Cabinet secretaries lived on bases, Trump said, 'Well, they choose to. I'm not sure that-- they need to.'" "Moved" and "choose to" pull against each other, and the story never reconciles them.

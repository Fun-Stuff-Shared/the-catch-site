**1. Five sentences**
- On Nov. 19 the UN's top court ruled 9 to 6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the 2017 military "clearance operations".
- It is the first time the court has found a country itself committed genocide, in a case The Gambia brought in 2019.
- The court ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible, let the Rohingya return, and compensate them, with the amount set later.
- The court can't enforce any of this beyond going to the Security Council, where China or Russia can block action, and Myanmar's government has already rejected the ruling.
- Six judges dissented because three villages didn't prove genocide across all of northern Rakhine, and driving the Rohingya out could explain the evidence too.

**2. What it's really about**
A landmark ruling that is legally real but can't be enforced, and that much of the press described wrongly ("guilty", "first state").

**3. Questions I still had**
- The story says "the 15 judges", but Pillay and Kress are ad hoc judges chosen by the two parties. Is the court 15 or 17 here, and who are the other seven in the majority?
- Who are "people acting alongside its forces" in the prevention finding? Militias, civilians?
- What was the evidence for "measures meant to prevent births"? It's named as a finding and never explained.
- What does the ruling change for Min Aung Hlaing personally? The ICC request has no public decision.
- What did the Rohingya or Bangladesh say? There are no Rohingya voices after the ruling, only governments and lawyers.
- Why Britain expelled a defence attaché and what the US will actually do at the Security Council. The US position is only a warning.
- How the dissent's "driving out" reading differs from the majority's "no evidence of a plan to move them". The majority's answer to the Croatia 2015 comparison is missing.

**4. Machine-sounding sentences**
- "The genocide finding split the court 9 to 6." It restates the headline as a caption.
- "From the court's judgment. The last line went in Myanmar's favor: the court found it did not incite private people to commit genocide, and counted any incitement of its own forces within the genocide finding itself." It's a footnote that explains the table row.
- "Whether what happened at Min Gyi and two other villages shows one pattern across northern Rakhine is what the six dissenting judges dispute." It announces the dissent before the dissent section.
- "Where they parted from the majority was on what that proved." It's a signpost.
- "What is new on Nov. 19 is that the court found a country itself committed genocide." It repeats the lede after the section just corrected the "first" claim.
- "AP described what is new correctly, as..." It grades coverage, and the section heading "What the coverage got right and wrong" does the same.
- "In rejecting the ruling, the government's spokesperson did not address what the court ordered Myanmar to do." It's a gotcha built on what wasn't said.
- "...restitution it has made, meaning the return, property and other measures above, and..." The "above" cross-reference is stiff.
- "They also accepted that soldiers killed Rohingya... acts that meet the physical elements of genocide, many of them going far beyond what fighting the insurgents required." It's overloaded with legal jargon, and "physical elements" is never explained.

**1. Five sentences**

- On October 20 the Justice Department announced a $25 million program that pays $10,000 to $30,000 to people whose fraud tips lead to criminal charges, if the department chooses to pay.
- Trump said at the Oval Office event that tipsters get 1% with no limit, that charges aren't required, and that he'd be the arbitrator.
- The written terms say none of that: the payment is a flat range, it requires charges, and the Justice Department decides with no appeal.
- A White House official said Nick Shirley, who made a video about Minneapolis day cares, would get $10,000. The department's documents never mention him, and the fraud charges against the day care's owner came months before the video's payoff.
- Several outlets reported Trump's 1% as the program's terms, and critics say the discretion invites political targeting.

**2. What it's really about**

The gap between what the president said a new program pays and what its written rules say, and who gets to decide in practice.

**3. Questions I still had**

- Who is Nick Shirley, and what did his video show? He's only "posted a video about Minneapolis day care centers."
- Is the $10,000 for Shirley real, and under which rules? The story says no one explained, but doesn't say whether anyone has asked.
- Has the White House or the department responded to the 1% discrepancy?
- What is the "reporting site" in "The reporting site says a tipster may be eligible…"? Is it america.gov, fraud.gov, or something else? Why would the site differ from the terms?
- Why does the Privacy Act exemption matter in practice? What can't a named person do now?
- What are "special services" in the first target list?
- Who is Colin McDonald beyond a title, and why does his October 1 order matter?
- Is Trump's "arbitrator" claim just talk, or is there any mechanism for it?
- Has anyone actually been paid under it?

**4. Sentences that sounded machine-written**

- "On one point the written rules are looser than Trump's version." This announces a point before making it.
- "Others in the administration gave versions of their own." It's a transition line that adds nothing.
- "Could a tipster be paid 1 percent with no limit? Not under the reward law the terms cite…" This is a self-posed rhetorical question.
- "What is Shirley's $10,000 for, and under which rules?" Same device.
- "…such an award would need a different statute, and it is two-fifths of the $25 million Treasury committed." The "it" is ambiguous, and the clause is stuffed in.
- "Its terms set each award at $10,000 to $30,000 per tip; Trump's 1 percent would take new terms, and any award above $3 million a statute other than the reward law they cite." This restates earlier material as a summing-up, and the last clause is broken grammar.
- "Smaller: the award does not wait for a conviction" and "Each gave Trump's Oval Office account as the program's terms." Both are tidy summary labels.
- "The terms answer the risk of false tips: the department can permanently bar people…" This frames the rule as if it answered a question nobody asked.
- "…any award the department chooses to make is paid at or around the time the charges are filed." This adds a new fact in the Catch section that was never set up earlier.

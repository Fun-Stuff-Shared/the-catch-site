The jobs report · September 2026
Payrolls rose by an estimated 29,000 in September, a change too small for the survey to tell from none
Economists surveyed by Reuters had nonfarm payrolls likely up by 90,000. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said payrolls and the 4.2 percent unemployment rate both changed little. July and August together are 60,000 lower than previously reported.

What happened
The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, October 2, 2026, that payroll employment rose by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent, and said both changed little.
Private payrolls rose by 46,000 and government payrolls fell by 17,000.
The bureau also revised the two prior months down by 60,000 together: August from a gain of 162,000 to 133,000, and July from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000.
The establishment survey counts jobs on employer payrolls, and employees appearing on more than one payroll are counted separately. The household survey counts people, and individuals are counted only once. A jobs number and a people number can move differently in the same month.
On September 16 the Federal Reserve raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point in support of its two goals, maximum employment and price stability. The bureau's next monthly jobs report, for October, is scheduled for November 6, after the committee meets on October 27-28. On prices, the personal consumption expenditures price index for August was up 3.4 percent from a year earlier, and this report put average hourly earnings up 3.0 percent over the past 12 months.
The catch
USA Today headlined the release “September jobs report shows slowdown in hiring, unemployment ticks up”. The bureau says an over-the-month employment change of about 122,000 is statistically significant in the establishment survey. September's estimate of 29,000 is under that bar, and the bureau's own summary is that payrolls “changed little”. The 29,000 is a net change in payroll jobs; hires are a separate count of “all additions to the payroll” during the month, and September's has not been published.
By the household survey's estimates, employment rose by 406,000, under that survey's bar of about 650,000 for an employment change, and the labor force rose by 485,000. The estimated labor force grew by more than estimated employment, which is why the count of unemployed rose by 78,000 and the rate went from 4.1 to 4.2 percent.
A St. Louis Fed note published October 2 gives the rate without rounding: it “increased slightly from 4.141% to 4.175%”, so most of the visible tenth is rounding.
The national rate moved a tenth of a point while the rate for Black workers rose a full point in one month, to 7 percent. A year earlier the rate was 7.6 percent.
From weekly payroll data, ADP Research says private-sector employment increased by 90,000 jobs in September, separate from the bureau's 46,000.

September's gain of 29,000 followed August's revised 133,000 and an average monthly gain of 45,000 over the prior 12 months. The unemployment rate has remained in a narrow range of 4.1 percent to 4.3 percent since March. The Associated Press put average gains at 68,000 jobs a month so far this year, and 2025 at 9,700 jobs a month.
What we do not know yet
In the establishment survey, estimates for the most recent two months are labeled preliminary because they are based on incomplete returns. September's count of hires comes with the Job Openings and Labor Turnover release on November 3. The newest count is August's, when hires changed little at 5.2 million.
The bureau gave no reason for the size of the change.

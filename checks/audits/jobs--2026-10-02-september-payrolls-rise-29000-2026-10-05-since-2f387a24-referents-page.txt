The jobs report · September 2026
Payrolls rose by an estimated 29,000 in September, a change too small for the survey to tell from none
Economists surveyed by Reuters expected payrolls to rise by 90,000. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said payrolls and the 4.2 percent unemployment rate both changed little. July and August together are 60,000 lower than previously reported.

What happened
The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, October 2, 2026, that payroll employment rose by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent, and said both changed little.
Private payrolls rose by 46,000 and government payrolls fell by 17,000.
Yahoo Finance names Kevin Hassett the director of the National Economic Council. He told reporters outside the White House on October 2, “What's happened is because we've reduced federal employment by 300,000 workers, then the numbers, uh, are a little bit lower if you don't account for that.” Federal employment was 3,009,000 in December 2024 and 2,682,000 in September 2026, down 327,000. September's own change was a drop of 1,000, from 2,683,000 in August. Reuters wrote that government payrolls contracted 17,000, mostly in local government, excluding education.
The bureau also revised the two prior months down by 60,000 together: August from a gain of 162,000 to 133,000, and July from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000.
The establishment survey counts jobs on employer payrolls. The household survey counts people. A jobs number and a people number can move differently in the same month. The payroll count comes from the first survey, and the unemployment rate from the second.
The Federal Reserve's two goals are maximum employment and price stability. On September 16 the Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate, its primary means of adjusting the stance of monetary policy, by 1/4 percentage point. The increase was in support of those goals. This report is the employment reading before that committee meets on October 27-28; the next one, for October, is scheduled for November 6. On prices, the personal consumption expenditures price index for August, whose annual change is how the committee measures inflation, was up 3.4 percent from a year earlier. This report put average hourly earnings up 3.0 percent over the past 12 months.
Reuters wrote that the slowdown was “almost taking another interest rate hike from the Federal Reserve this month off the table”. The same dispatch said financial markets initially lowered bets on a rate hike at the October 27-28 meeting to 13 percent before lifting them to about 23 percent, little changed from Thursday, CME Group's FedWatch tool showed.
The catch
USA Today headlined the release “September jobs report shows slowdown in hiring, unemployment ticks up”. The 29,000 is a net change in payroll jobs, not a count of hires, and it is under about 122,000, the monthly change the bureau says is statistically significant. Hires, “all additions to the payroll” during the month, are counted in a separate survey, and September's count is scheduled for November 3.
The rounded unemployment rate rose from 4.1 to 4.2. The bureau's summary says payrolls and that rate both “changed little”.
By the household survey's estimates, employment rose by 406,000, which is under the 650,000 that survey needs before a change is statistically significant. The labor force, the employed plus the unemployed, rose by 485,000. The unemployment rate is the number unemployed as a percent of the labor force. The labor force grew by more than employment did, so the count of unemployed rose by 78,000 and the rate went from 4.1 to 4.2 percent.
Most of that tenth is rounding. Before rounding, the rate “increased slightly from 4.141% to 4.175%”, the St. Louis Fed wrote on October 2, a rise of about 0.03 point. The rate has stayed between 4.1 percent and 4.3 percent since March.
The bureau named one group whose rate increased: Black workers, from 6.0 percent in August to 7.0 percent in September. A year earlier it was 7.6 percent.

September's 29,000 followed August's revised 133,000. It is below the bureau's average monthly gain of 45,000 over the prior 12 months. The first bar is October 2025, a loss of 140,000. The bureau's three-month average is 51,000 a month through September 2026, against 23,000 through September 2025.
Reuters said economists estimated that the economy needs to create roughly 50,000 jobs per month to keep up with growth in the working-age population. Yahoo Finance reported that, by the calculations of Chris Phelan, chair of the President's Council of Economic Advisers, the economy needs to add only about 40,000 jobs per month to keep the unemployment rate at its current level. The Associated Press wrote that “the break-even point could now be as low as zero jobs a month, down from 150,000 a year or two ago”. September's 29,000 is under the first two figures. The three-month pace of 51,000 sits next to the economists' roughly 50,000. The Associated Press's zero would put both above that possible break-even.
What we do not know yet
The payroll figures for August and September are preliminary: the bureau says they rest on incomplete returns from the employers it surveys. The release states no cause for the 29,000 change. Reuters wrote that economists noted payrolls “have a tendency to underperform when the Labor Day holiday falls relatively late in September, as was the case this year.” Whether hiring slowed is not known yet: hires are counted separately, August's hires were 5.2 million, and September's are scheduled for November 3.

## Reconstructed story

On August 7, BLS published a preliminary July payroll estimate of minus 23,000 and an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent. BLS characterized both as having “changed little.” The payroll estimate was too small to establish a decline statistically. The household survey showed employment down 87,000 and the labor force down 264,000, also weak but noisy monthly estimates.

The broader July labor-market record supported a stall, not a demonstrated reversal. The separate JOLTS survey reported 7.3 million openings, 5.1 million hires, 5.1 million separations, 3.1 million quits, and 1.7 million layoffs and discharges, all “little changed.” [JOLTS pin](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/bls-jolts-2026-07.txt), bytes 4291-4577.

On September 4, later employer responses and recalculated seasonal factors revised July to plus 21,000. Revised July private payrolls rose 71,000 while government payrolls fell 50,000. August then added 162,000 jobs. Local government education added 42,000 in August, which BLS said largely offset the prior decrease. The labor force grew by 683,000, participation rose to 61.6 percent, and U-6 fell from 7.9 to 7.7 percent. These later observations disprove the article’s continued “reverse” framing and resolve its immediate labor-force-exit question in the opposite direction, while the three-month payroll average of about 71,000 still describes subdued growth.

The independent lineages are limited. CES supplies the payroll figures. FRED and ALFRED preserve CES values and vintages but are not independent confirmation. CPS supplies the household measures. JOLTS is a separate survey. QCEW provides administrative-record benchmarking. Real Earnings combines CES earnings with CPI. CNBC, CBS, and NBC largely repeat BLS figures, so outlet agreement does not add independent payroll evidence.

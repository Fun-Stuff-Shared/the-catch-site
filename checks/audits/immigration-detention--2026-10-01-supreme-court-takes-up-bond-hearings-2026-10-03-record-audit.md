- **Major, grade A: The legal hinge.** The page says the Court will decide whether the statute requires holding people “who are in the United States and were not admitted.” The dispute turns on two different phrases: **“applicant for admission”** and **“seeking admission.”** The [Eighth Circuit opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/03/253248P.pdf) calls their relationship “the central inquiry”; the [Fourth Circuit opinion](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/257044.P.pdf) explains that its petitioners contested the second condition. The correct account is that the government treats the phrases as equivalent, while the Second Circuit held that someone arrested in the interior long after entry is not then seeking admission.

- **Major, grade A: The conflict is nationwide.** The page says only that “Divided panels in the Fifth and Eighth Circuits agreed with the government.” The [Fourth Circuit’s September 10 opinion](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/257044.P.pdf) identifies nine other circuits on the opposite side and says “over five hundred” district judges had rejected the government’s reading in “over twenty thousand individual cases.” Those are the court’s descriptions, not independently audited counts of people detained. The correct account is a broad circuit split and a large volume of litigation, rather than a disagreement confined to the three circuits named on the page.

## 8. Reconstructed Story

**Established:** In July 2025, DHS adopted a new reading that places people present without admission under mandatory-detention authority rather than the bond process previously used for many people arrested in the interior. The Board adopted that position. Barbosa da Cunha, arrested years after entry, challenged the denial of a bond hearing; a district court ordered a hearing or release, an immigration judge released him on bond, and the Second Circuit affirmed the statutory ruling. Other circuits split. On October 1, 2026, the Supreme Court granted review of whether § 1225(b)(2)(A) requires detention of people like him. [ICE guidance](https://iptp-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/2025.07.08_ICE_-_Interim_Guidance_Regarding_Detention_Authority_for_Applicants_0JGK0CH.pdf), [Second Circuit opinion](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-detention/data/sources/immigration-detention/ca2-cunha-opinion-2026-04-28.txt:155), [order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/100126zr_6j37.pdf).

**Disputed:** Whether the statute’s definition of an applicant for admission makes a person living inside the country “seeking admission” when later arrested. The [Eighth Circuit](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/03/253248P.pdf) says yes; the [Second](https://www.justice.gov/d9/2026-07/barbosa_da_cunha_hold_pet_-_final.pdf) and [Fourth](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/257044.P.pdf) say no for people in the circumstances before them. The separate due process question remains open in this grant.

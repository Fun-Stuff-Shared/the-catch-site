What happened
Macklemore was removed from eight scheduled appearances on Ed Sheeran's stadium tour, the Loop Tour, on September 14. Macklemore said Sheeran and his team made the decision; tour promoter Messina Touring Group said venues had told it they would not allow concerts with Macklemore on the lineup. Macklemore added, “I am not a victim. Ed Sheeran is not a victim. Pink is not a victim,” and said all three still had careers, money, safety and audiences around the world.
Sheeran's official North American schedule listed 27 shows: 17 were scheduled through September 14, and 10 came afterward. Macklemore had been scheduled for eight of the later dates and had already appeared at both MetLife Stadium dates, on September 4 and 5.
On September 15, Finneas, Aaron Rowe, Lukas Graham and Beoga announced that they were leaving.
AP asked three music-business participants who were not involved in Sheeran's tour how a stadium tour normally works. They said a promoter finances the production, pays the acts and deals with venues; a venue can refuse a lineup; and a stadium-level headliner generally has substantial approval over the show.
At the first MetLife show, Macklemore said “Free Palestine,” displayed images of destruction in Gaza and performed “Hind's Hall.”
Sheeran said Macklemore had asked to join the tour the previous year and he agreed because he respected him as an artist. He said Macklemore, like all his support acts, chose his own setlist. Sheeran said venues threatened to pull shows after Macklemore's performances because of how part of his message was conveyed. He spent the week speaking with them, including Robert Kraft, owner of the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium, but could not change the venue and promoter decision. Sheeran said Macklemore's contract was with the promoter, not him. He said he would not abandon fans, crew, support acts or musicians who relied on him for work, and that his audience included children and families who did not expect a political forum.

Why Macklemore was removed
The American Jewish Committee, an advocacy group that criticized the performance, said its New Jersey office contacted the Meadowlands after the first show. AJC said stadium administrators raised its concerns directly with the tour and artists and offered refunds before the second show to ticket holders who chose not to attend; MetLife has not commented on AJC's account.
Macklemore repeated his call for a “Free Palestine” at the second MetLife show.
The first performance drew criticism from pro-Israel groups. After that show, the Israeli American Council launched a petition calling for Macklemore's removal. It addressed Sheeran, tour leadership and every venue hosting the tour, and asked whether his speech, visuals and “Hind's Hall” had been approved in advance.
Kraft confirmed that Gillette Stadium had refused Macklemore. He said Macklemore's participation on September 25 and 26 would cross the venue's line against providing a platform for hate speech, citing Macklemore's recent actions, material from the MetLife Stadium stage and what Kraft described as a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery. Kraft said the decision was not intended to diminish innocent Palestinians' suffering or deny anyone the right to advocate for them. He said such advocacy should not obscure Hamas's responsibility.
Macklemore said Sheeran maintained an “I don't take sides” position. He said he had made the same statements on stages for almost three years and argued that their association with a larger artist, major U.S. stadiums and bigger audiences made them newly risky.
Macklemore said antisemitism was real, but argued that criticism of Israel or opposition to Zionism should not be conflated with hatred of Jewish people.
Macklemore said Sheeran told him that Kraft had rallied other stadium owners. He also said Sheeran told him that many people Sheeran had spoken with found “Free Palestine” and the Palestinian flag hurtful.
Alan Clendenin, a Tampa Sports Authority board member and Tampa City Council chair, said the lineup change had nothing to do with the public agency that manages Raymond James Stadium. Messina said venues had objected. Rolling Stone's seven-venue list of alleged objectors named Gillette Stadium, Raymond James Stadium, Lincoln Financial Field, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Lucas Oil Stadium, Bank of America Stadium and AT&T Stadium.
Of the seven venues on Rolling Stone's alleged-objection list, it identified Bank of America Stadium and Gillette Stadium as privately owned. It said the other five were owned or operated by cities, states or other public bodies.
Earlier that summer, Raymond James Stadium hosted two Kanye West concerts under a promoter contract clause that barred cancellation based on his past public statements or viewpoints; lawyers told local news that canceling likely would have violated the First Amendment because the stadium is publicly owned and operated. Venue refusals can lead a promoter to remove an act from a national tour, but no public Sheeran-tour contract shows whether Macklemore had a similar clause or who made the final decision.
The Wall Street Journal reported, citing unnamed sources, that Carolina Panthers owner David Tepper, Atlanta Falcons owner Arthur Blank and Philadelphia Eagles owner Jeffrey Lurie joined the pressure. Their communications and the tour contracts remain private.

The catch
Who held final authority remains unresolved.
Macklemore attributed the removal to Sheeran and his team. Sheeran attributed it to the promoter and said the venues and promoter made the final decision. Messina said venues would not accept the lineup. The industry participants interviewed by AP were not involved in this tour.
The four departures covered different tour roles.
Rowe and Lukas Graham would have replaced Macklemore on the remaining U.S. dates. Beoga described itself as Sheeran's band for the remaining U.S. tour. Finneas was scheduled for South America later in the year.
Four decisions, four roles
Why the other artists left
Finneas said artists should not be silenced when they speak for oppressed people and withdrew from his upcoming dates with Sheeran.
Aaron Rowe withdrew as a North American support act. He said he could not accept billionaires using their power to silence people who speak up.
Beoga departed as Sheeran's band for the remaining U.S. dates. The band said it would not play venues that silence artists for saying “Free Palestine.”
Beoga blamed what it called “zionist lobbies” for Macklemore's removal and told people to research Kraft, his relationship with Israel and the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement.
Lukas Graham objected to wealth determining who gets to speak and withdrew from the remaining dates. The band also said there was not one single reason for its decision.

What happened next
On September 17, the Philadelphia chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations, a civil-rights advocacy group known as CAIR-Philadelphia, announced that it had submitted a Pennsylvania Right-to-Know request concerning the Philadelphia lineup decision.
CAIR-Philadelphia said its request did not presume what any records would show. Pennsylvania gives an agency five business days after it receives a request to respond.
On September 18, the Massachusetts attorney general's office told Boston 25 it was reviewing at least 25 complaints requesting refunds from Ticketmaster. The office said the ticket terms state that “opening acts are subject to change” and that lineup changes do not necessarily entitle ticket purchasers to a refund.
Ticketmaster told Boston 25 that organizers set refund policy. The tour FAQ says, “All sales are final.”
The September 19 Philadelphia show went ahead, and Sheeran began alone with no support act. On September 20, Lincoln Financial Field's own event page still listed Macklemore, Lukas Graham and Aaron Rowe for the show and said it had last been changed on September 17.
On stage in Philadelphia on September 19, Sheeran opened by saying he had made mistakes during the week and apologizing for disappointing fans. He called the situation in Gaza “catastrophic and unjustifiable” and said he worried about venues preapproving performances.

What we do not know yet
As of September 20, no executed artist, promoter or venue contract was public.
Communications among Kraft, Messina, Sheeran's representatives and each venue were not public. It was not known whether Kraft coordinated broader venue pressure or who made the final decisions at Lincoln Financial Field, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Lucas Oil Stadium, Bank of America Stadium and AT&T Stadium.
No rebuilt support lineup for the September 25 through November 7 dates was public.
The ticket terms described by the Massachusetts attorney general's office allow opening acts to change. Massachusetts law separately requires licensed ticket sellers to guarantee a refund when a ticket “fails to conform to its description as advertised,” unless the purchaser approved a substitution of tickets.
The attorney general's September 18 statement did not say whether removing an advertised opening act met the law's ticket-description test.

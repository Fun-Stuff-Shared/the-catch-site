What happened
On Friday, October 2, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, the Bureau of Labor Statistics published the Employment Situation for September. Payroll employment rose by 29,000 and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent. The bureau said both changed little.
Private payrolls rose by 46,000. Government payrolls fell by 17,000.
The July change was revised down by 31,000, from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000. The August change was revised down by 29,000, from a gain of 162,000 to a gain of 133,000. July and August together are 60,000 lower than previously reported.
The August release had reported that payroll employment increased by 162,000 and that the unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1 percent.
The payroll figure and the unemployment rate come from two surveys. “The household survey measures labor force status, including unemployment, by demographic characteristics. The establishment survey measures nonfarm employment, hours, and earnings by industry.” September's payroll change is the establishment figure, and the unemployment rate is the household figure.
Average hourly earnings increased by 3 percent over the past 12 months. On September 16 the Federal Reserve's committee decided to “raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent.” The Personal Income and Outlays release of Wednesday, September 30, 2026, reports the PCE price index. The PCE price index for August increased 3.4 percent from the same month a year earlier. PCE is the release's abbreviation for personal consumption expenditures.
The catch
USA Today headlined the release “September jobs report shows slowdown in hiring, unemployment ticks up”. In the establishment survey, an over-the-month employment change of about 122,000 is statistically significant, and the household survey's threshold is about 650,000. The bureau's word for the 29,000 change was that it changed little.
The household count of people employed rose by 406,000 from August. The rise in the number of people employed is under the household survey's bar of about 650,000. The labor force rose by 485,000. The number of people unemployed rose by 78,000. The unemployment rate is the number unemployed as a percent of the labor force.
A St. Louis Fed note dated Oct. 2, 2026, says more precise data show the unemployment rate “increased slightly from 4.141% to 4.175%.”
The unemployment rate for people who are Black increased, to 7 percent. On the bureau's row for Black or African American workers, July was 6.3 percent and August was 6 percent. A year earlier the rate was 7.6 percent, and the change from August was 1 point.
ADP's own private-sector count “increased by 90,000 jobs in September”. The bureau's private payroll line rose by 46,000.
“A Labor Department measure of gross hiring – before subtracting those who quit or lose their jobs – has been stuck in a rut for more than two years.” That hiring count, before quits and job losses are subtracted, is not the payroll change of 29,000.

September's gain of 29,000 followed an average monthly gain of 45,000 over the prior 12 months. The August release had reported a gain of 162,000, with unemployment unchanged at 4.1 percent. The unemployment rate has remained in a narrow range of 4.1 percent to 4.3 percent since March.
The Associated Press put average gains at 68,000 jobs a month so far this year. It put 2025 at 9,700 jobs a month.
A Reuters survey of economists had nonfarm payrolls likely up by 90,000 last month. Estimates in that survey ran from 35,000 to 180,000. September's gain of 29,000 is below the low of 35,000.
What we do not know yet
In the establishment survey, estimates for the most recent two months are labeled preliminary because they are based on incomplete returns. Hires changed little at 5.2 million in August. The Job Openings and Labor Turnover release for September is scheduled for Tuesday, November 3, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. On the Fed's 2026 meeting calendar, October 27-28 follows the September 15-16 meeting. The Employment Situation for October is scheduled for Friday, November 6, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.
The September employment release gives no reason for the size of the 29,000 payroll change. The latest Federal Reserve policy decision among the records of this release is the September 16 statement, which does not decide the October 27-28 meeting.

What happened
Macklemore was removed from eight scheduled appearances on Ed Sheeran's stadium tour, the Loop Tour, on September 14. Macklemore said Sheeran and his team made the decision; tour promoter Messina Touring Group said venues had told it they would not allow concerts with Macklemore on the lineup.
Ten North American shows remained on Sheeran's official schedule after September 14, but Macklemore had been scheduled for eight of them. He had already appeared at both MetLife Stadium dates, on September 4 and 5. The Loop Tour continued without him: its first date after the removal went ahead in Philadelphia on September 19, where Sheeran began alone with no support act.
The next day, Finneas, Aaron Rowe, Lukas Graham and Beoga announced that they were leaving. They did not all hold the same job: Rowe and Lukas Graham had been replacement U.S. support acts, Beoga had played during Sheeran's set, and Finneas was booked for the South American leg.
AP asked three music-business participants who were not involved in Sheeran's tour how a stadium tour normally works. They said a promoter finances the production, pays the acts and deals with venues; a venue can refuse a lineup; and a stadium-level headliner generally has substantial approval over the show.
Sheeran said support acts chose their own setlists. He said venues threatened to pull shows after Macklemore's performances and that he spent the week speaking with them, including Robert Kraft, owner of the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium, but could not change the venue and promoter decision.
Sheeran said Macklemore's contract was with the promoter, not him. He said he would not abandon fans, crew, support acts or musicians who relied on him for work, and that his audience included children and families who did not expect a political forum.

MetLife to venue objections
Why Macklemore was removed
At the first MetLife show, Macklemore said “Free Palestine,” displayed images of destruction in Gaza and performed “Hind's Hall.”
The American Jewish Committee, an advocacy group that criticized the performance, said its New Jersey office contacted the Meadowlands after the first show. AJC said stadium administrators raised its concerns directly with the tour and artists and offered refunds before the second show to ticket holders who chose not to attend; MetLife had not commented on AJC's account by September 20.
Macklemore repeated his call for a “Free Palestine” at the second MetLife show.
The first performance drew criticism from pro-Israel groups. The Israeli American Council addressed its petition to Sheeran, tour leadership and every venue hosting the tour, called for Macklemore's removal, and asked whether his speech, visuals and “Hind's Hall” had been approved in advance.
Kraft confirmed one venue decision. He said Gillette Stadium determined that Macklemore's participation on September 25 and 26 would cross its line against providing a platform for hate speech, citing Macklemore's recent actions, material from the MetLife Stadium stage and what Kraft described as a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery.
Macklemore said Sheeran maintained an “I don't take sides” position. He said he had made the same statements on stages for almost three years and argued that their association with a larger artist, major U.S. stadiums and bigger audiences made them newly risky.
Macklemore said antisemitism was real, but argued that criticism of Israel or opposition to Zionism should not be conflated with hatred of Jewish people.
Macklemore said Sheeran told him that Kraft had rallied other stadium owners and described “Free Palestine” and the Palestinian flag as hurtful. Kraft confirmed the Gillette refusal but did not address Macklemore's allegation of wider coordination.
Gillette Stadium's owner confirmed that it would not allow Macklemore to perform there. Alan Clendenin, a Tampa Sports Authority board member and Tampa City Council chair, said the lineup change had nothing to do with the public agency that manages Raymond James Stadium. Messina said venues had objected. Rolling Stone reported that Lincoln Financial Field, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Lucas Oil Stadium, Bank of America Stadium and AT&T Stadium objected; none of those five venues had confirmed its account by September 20.
Bank of America Stadium and Gillette Stadium were the only two on that seven-venue list that Rolling Stone identified as privately owned. The other five were owned or operated by cities, states or other public bodies.
At Gillette Stadium, stadium or artist management reserved the right to bar signs or banners because of their message content.

The Wall Street Journal reported, citing unnamed sources, that Carolina Panthers owner David Tepper, Atlanta Falcons owner Arthur Blank and Philadelphia Eagles owner Jeffrey Lurie joined the pressure. Their communications and the tour contracts remain private.

The catch
The four artists who left held different tour roles.
Who held final authority remains unresolved.
Macklemore attributed the removal to Sheeran and his team. Sheeran attributed it to the promoter and said the venues and promoter made the final decision. Messina said venues would not accept the lineup. The industry participants interviewed by AP were not involved in this tour, and no executed artist, promoter or venue contract is public.
Four artists left, but they were not four remaining openers.
Rowe and Lukas Graham were replacement U.S. support acts. Beoga described itself as Sheeran's band for the remaining U.S. tour. Finneas was scheduled for South America later in the year.
Four decisions, four roles
Why the other artists left
Finneas said artists should not be silenced when they speak for oppressed people and withdrew from his upcoming dates with Sheeran.
Aaron Rowe withdrew as a North American support act. He said he could not accept billionaires using their power to silence people who speak up.
Beoga departed as Sheeran's band for the remaining U.S. dates. The band said it would not play venues that silence artists for saying “Free Palestine.”
Lukas Graham objected to wealth determining who gets to speak and withdrew from the remaining dates. The band also said there was not one single reason for its decision.

What happened next
On September 17, CAIR-Philadelphia announced that it had submitted a Pennsylvania Right-to-Know request concerning the Philadelphia lineup decision.
CAIR-Philadelphia said its request did not presume what any records would show. Pennsylvania's five-business-day response period had not run by September 20.
On September 18, the Massachusetts attorney general's office told Boston 25 it was reviewing at least 25 complaints requesting Gillette Stadium refunds from Ticketmaster. That number counts complaints, not purchasers. The office said changes to the lineup “do not necessarily entitle ticket purchasers to a refund.”
Ticketmaster told Boston 25 that organizers set refund policy. The tour FAQ says, “All sales are final.”
Sheeran opened by saying he had made mistakes during the week and apologizing for disappointing fans. He called the situation in Gaza “catastrophic and unjustifiable” and said he worried about venues preapproving performances.

What we do not know yet
As of September 20, no executed artist, promoter or venue contract was public.
Communications among Kraft, Messina, Sheeran's representatives and each venue were not public. It was not known whether Kraft coordinated broader venue pressure or who made the final decisions at Lincoln Financial Field, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Lucas Oil Stadium, Bank of America Stadium and AT&T Stadium.
No rebuilt support lineup for the September 25 through November 7 dates was public by September 20.
Massachusetts law requires licensed ticket sellers to guarantee a refund when a ticket “fails to conform to its description as advertised,” unless the purchaser approved a substitution. Whether removing an opening act met that test remained unresolved while the attorney general's office reviewed the complaints.

The UK publication of the G7 statement and the French publication of the same English statement are one evidentiary lineage. They do not count as two independent confirmations of delivery or implementation.

## 8\. Reconstructed Story

The October 2 G7 action was an acceleration and reconfiguration of emergency-stock policy inside the March IEA framework. The leaders agreed to a release of up to roughly 100 million barrels over four months, with diesel front-loaded but unnumbered, while around 325 million barrels of the March 400 million action had already been released. The exact October increment beyond March, product split, national shares, and actual deliveries remained unresolved.

The immediate problem was particularly acute in refined products. Gulf diesel and gasoil exports had fallen sharply from prewar levels, Russian supply was also constrained, and the G7 paired reserve releases with refinery-maintenance coordination and efforts to raise refined-product output. The French presidency simultaneously reported that flows through Hormuz and Yanbu had begun increasing, so the supply picture was severe but changing.

The political negotiation included U.S. pressure on Europe for faster existing deliveries and additional supplies. Reporting described a threatened U.S. diesel export ban as part of that pressure; a European diplomat called it blackmail. Trump later said the ban was never really on the table, and Macron was reported as saying Trump had not threatened an embargo. The G7 ultimately committed not to impose energy export restrictions on one another, and Trump publicly ruled the ban out.

By October 5 the package still lacked a public country-by-country and product-by-product implementation ledger. Japan said it did not plan a further reserve release at that stage. The strongest supported account therefore remains an agreed emergency package whose concrete allocation and delivery were still developing.

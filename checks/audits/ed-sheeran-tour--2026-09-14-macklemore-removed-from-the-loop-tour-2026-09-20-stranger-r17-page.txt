What happened
On September 14, Macklemore announced that he had been removed from eight scheduled appearances on Ed Sheeran's stadium tour, the Loop Tour. Macklemore said Sheeran and his team made the decision; tour promoter Messina Touring Group said venues had told it they would not allow concerts with Macklemore on the lineup.
Sheeran said Macklemore's contract was with the promoter. The artist, promoter and venue records saved through September 20 do not include that contract or its terms for content and removal.
The dispute reaches beyond one lineup because three parties can shape what a stadium audience hears. AP asked three music-business participants who were not involved in Sheeran's tour to explain how authority is divided among a venue, promoter and headliner: a venue can refuse an act, a promoter pays the performers and deals with venues, and the headliner generally has substantial approval over the show.
The objections followed Macklemore's first MetLife Stadium show on September 4. There, he said “Free Palestine” and showed images of destruction in Gaza; he later said Sheeran told him those words and “the image of the Palestinian flag” had hurt people Sheeran spoke with. Macklemore also performed “Hind's Hall.” The song was written after the 2024 campus protests, refers to Columbia University and is named for Hind Rajab, a five-year-old Palestinian girl killed in Gaza.
Pink, a singer, reposted an Instagram message from StopAntisemitism, a pro-Israel campaign group, that accused Macklemore of ambushing fans; she later said she had not asked for him to be silenced or fired. Macklemore said, “I am not a victim. Ed Sheeran is not a victim. Pink is not a victim,” and said all three still had careers, money, safety and audiences around the world.
Sheeran's official North American schedule listed 27 shows, with 10 after September 14. Macklemore had been scheduled for eight of the later dates and had already appeared at both MetLife Stadium dates, on September 4 and 5.
On September 15, Finneas, Aaron Rowe, Lukas Graham and Beoga announced that they were leaving. Rowe and Lukas Graham were billed as U.S. support alongside Macklemore on his eight remaining dates, Beoga was performing several songs inside Sheeran's set, and Finneas was booked for the South American dates.
Sheeran said Macklemore had asked to join the tour the previous year and he agreed because he respected him as an artist. He said Macklemore, like all his support acts, chose his own setlist. Sheeran said venues threatened to pull shows after Macklemore's performances because of how part of his message was conveyed. He spent the week speaking with them, including Robert Kraft, owner of the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium, but could not change the venue and promoter decision. He said he would not abandon fans, crew, support acts or musicians who relied on him for work, and that his audience included children and families who did not expect a political forum.

Why Macklemore was removed
The first performance drew criticism from pro-Israel groups. After that show, the Israeli American Council, an advocacy group, launched a petition calling for Macklemore's removal. It addressed Sheeran, tour leadership and every venue hosting the tour, and asked whether his speech, visuals and “Hind's Hall” had been approved in advance.
The American Jewish Committee, an advocacy group that criticized the performance, said its New Jersey office contacted MetLife Stadium about the show. AJC said stadium administrators raised its concerns directly with the tour and artists and offered refunds before the second show to ticket holders who chose not to attend.
An unnamed person told The Wall Street Journal that representatives of several NFL owners contacted the promoters after the first show and “heard back that Macklemore would leave out any mention of the issue from his act.” The Journal did not say who gave the assurance.
At the second MetLife show, Macklemore again called for a free Palestine. He addressed “all of my Jewish brothers and sisters,” said criticism of Israel and apartheid and being against genocide were not criticism of them, and said his message was peace, love, dignity, respect and equality.
CBS New York reported no response from MetLife Stadium to its request by September 7. MetLife Stadium's news page, as saved September 21, carried no statement about the shows.
Robert Kraft, the Gillette Stadium owner, confirmed that the venue had refused Macklemore. He said allowing Macklemore to appear on September 25 and 26 would make the venue a “platform for hate speech.”
Kraft cited Macklemore's recent actions, “material shared from the stage during Ed Sheeran's shows in New Jersey, and a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery.” He said the decision was not intended to diminish innocent Palestinians' suffering or deny anyone the right to advocate for them, and that such advocacy should not obscure Hamas's responsibility.
Macklemore said Sheeran maintained an “I don't take sides” position. He said he had made the same statements on stages for almost three years and argued that their association with a larger artist, major U.S. stadiums and bigger audiences made them newly risky.
Macklemore said antisemitism was real, but argued that criticism of Israel or opposition to Zionism should not be conflated with hatred of Jewish people.
Macklemore said Sheeran told him that Kraft had rallied other stadium owners.
In reporting Messina's claim that venues had objected, Rolling Stone named seven alleged objectors: Gillette Stadium, Raymond James Stadium, Lincoln Financial Field, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Lucas Oil Stadium, Bank of America Stadium and AT&T Stadium. Alan Clendenin, a Tampa Sports Authority board member and Tampa City Council chair, said the lineup change had nothing to do with the authority, which manages the county-owned Raymond James Stadium.
Rolling Stone identified Bank of America Stadium and Gillette Stadium as privately owned. Official records show that Georgia's stadium authority owns Mercedes-Benz Stadium. Indiana's stadium authority owns Lucas Oil Stadium. Arlington owns AT&T Stadium. Hillsborough County owns Raymond James Stadium. Philadelphia owns the land under Lincoln Financial Field. The Falcons organization operates Mercedes-Benz Stadium, the Cowboys operate AT&T Stadium, and public boards operate Lucas Oil Stadium and Raymond James Stadium.
Earlier that summer, Raymond James Stadium had booked a different artist under terms that FOX 13 reported limited the stadium authority's power to cancel. The outlet said the contract for two Kanye West concerts “explicitly blocks” the Tampa Sports Authority from canceling or interfering because of the artist's identity, past statements or political views, and that the promoter had requested the clause. The contract itself had not been published by September 20, so those terms remain FOX 13's account. It also reported that West's team accepted lawsuit liability and the promoter agreed to pay more than $2 million in stadium expenses plus $5 for each ticket sold. Before the concerts, FOX 13 said the authority would collect those fees while West's team would keep the remaining revenue, which the outlet put at possibly more than $20 million. In August, the Tampa Bay Times reported that the authority made an estimated $3.44 million from the two shows. It said all spending on food, drinks and parking, plus part of merchandise and ticket sales, went to the authority. Venue refusals can lead a promoter to remove an act from a national tour, but no public Sheeran-tour contract shows whether Macklemore had a similar clause or who made the final decision.
The Wall Street Journal reported, citing unnamed sources, that Carolina Panthers owner David Tepper, Atlanta Falcons owner Arthur Blank and Philadelphia Eagles owner Jeffrey Lurie joined the pressure.

The catch
Who held final authority remains unresolved.
Macklemore named Sheeran and his team; Sheeran named the promoter; Messina said venues rejected the lineup. Those accounts name three different deciders, and none resolves the conflict.
The four departures covered different tour roles.
Rowe and Lukas Graham were advertised as U.S. support, Beoga performed songs inside Sheeran's set, and Finneas was booked for South America. Their departures did not leave four identical opening slots on the remaining U.S. dates.
Four decisions, four roles
Why the other artists left
Finneas said artists should not be silenced when they speak for oppressed people and withdrew from his upcoming dates with Sheeran.
Aaron Rowe withdrew as a North American support act. He said he could not accept billionaires using their power to silence people who speak up.
Rowe later said the criticism should focus on Kraft rather than Sheeran, while maintaining that Macklemore should not have been removed.
record
Beoga departed as Sheeran's band for the remaining U.S. dates. The band said it would not play venues that silence artists for saying “Free Palestine.”
Beoga blamed what it called “zionist lobbies” for Macklemore's removal and told people to research Kraft, his relationship with Israel and the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement.
Lukas Graham objected to wealth determining who gets to speak and withdrew from the remaining dates. The band also said there was not one single reason for its decision.

What happened next
On September 17, the Philadelphia chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations, a civil-rights advocacy group known as CAIR-Philadelphia, announced that it had submitted a Pennsylvania Right-to-Know request concerning the Philadelphia lineup decision.
CAIR-Philadelphia said its request did not presume what any records would show. Pennsylvania gives an agency five business days after it receives a request to respond.
On September 18, the Massachusetts attorney general's office told Boston 25 it was reviewing at least 25 complaints requesting refunds from Ticketmaster. For ticket purchasers generally, the office said lineup changes do not necessarily entitle them to a refund.
Ticketmaster told Boston 25 that organizers set refund policy. The tour FAQ answers buyers who changed their minds by saying, “All sales are final”; it does not address refunds sought after an advertised act was removed.
The September 19 Philadelphia show went ahead, and Sheeran began alone with no support act. He later introduced a new five-member backup band, made up of a drummer, guitarist, bass player, keyboardist and violinist, that had joined with three days' notice. He said he removed a secondary stage used at other concert stops because he feared what could happen if he stood in the middle. A small group of pro-Palestinian demonstrators gathered outside before the concert. On September 20, Lincoln Financial Field's event page still listed Macklemore, Lukas Graham and Aaron Rowe for the show.
On stage in Philadelphia on September 19, Sheeran told the crowd, “I've had to take and make difficult decisions, and I am making mistakes, and I am so, so sorry.”
Sheeran posted a video from Philadelphia on Instagram. The Guardian published the clip the next day, carrying his words that the situation in Gaza was “catastrophic and unjustifiable.” He also said he worried about venues preapproving performances.
AEG Presents' official schedule, as saved September 21, still listed Macklemore, who had been removed, and Lukas Graham and Aaron Rowe, who had withdrawn, on the dates that had booked Macklemore; it listed Rowe on the two Hollywood dates. That schedule and the artists' statements named no replacement opening act for the remaining dates. The only personnel added after the changes that the reports saved through September 20 identify are the five members of the backing band Sheeran introduced in Philadelphia after three days' notice. Those reports do not say whether the band continued to later dates.

What we do not know yet
The artist, promoter and venue records saved through September 20 include no executed agreement assigning authority over the Loop Tour lineup.
Macklemore said Sheeran told him Kraft had rallied other stadium owners, Sheeran said he spoke with venues including Kraft, and The Wall Street Journal reported contacts between NFL owners' representatives and the promoters. The Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that Panthers owner David Tepper and Falcons owner Arthur Blank had made up their minds and Eagles owner Jeffrey Lurie would soon reach the same conclusion as the owners prepared to address Macklemore's place on the bill. The statements and reports saved through September 20 do not reproduce the calls or show who held contractual authority over the lineup, and none names an actor for Lucas Oil Stadium or AT&T Stadium.
The terms for Gillette Stadium's Account Manager ticket site say, “No refund will be owed if an opening act or festival performer is changed or canceled.” Massachusetts law separately requires licensed ticket sellers to guarantee a refund when a ticket fails to conform to its advertised description, unless the purchaser preapproved a substitution of tickets.
The attorney general's September 18 statement did not say whether removing an advertised opening act met the law's ticket-description test.

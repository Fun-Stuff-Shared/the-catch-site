G7 will release 100 million barrels over four months and does not say how much is beyond its March commitments
The leaders' statement promises a substantial diesel release in the first 20 days and gives no number for it. Of the 400 million barrels International Energy Agency members pledged in March, about 325 million had been released.

What happened
On October 2, 2026, the leaders of the G7 countries agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil over four months, coordinated through the International Energy Agency, to begin immediately. Their statement promises a substantial diesel release within the first 20 days and gives no number for it. It sets that release "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled."
Weekly U.S. retail diesel was 6.382 dollars a gallon on September 28. On March 2 it was 3.897. The Independent says prices have rocketed in recent months because of the US-Israeli conflict with Iran and Russia's war in Ukraine. The G7 says the measures are meant to "shield households and businesses from price shocks", and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said American farmers, truckers, and businesses should not be left carrying the burden of a global diesel shortage. The European Commission's Energy Union task force said EU diesel supply remains stable for the time being, but prices remain high.
The French presidency's readout puts the ceiling at up to 100 million barrels within four months. The readout's own wording places refined products, diesel, and crude oil under that ceiling.
In March, International Energy Agency members pledged 400 million barrels; by October 2 around 325 million of that March action had been released. The October statement does not say how much of its release is beyond those commitments. On September 29 the United States offered an exchange of up to 40 million barrels of crude, continuing the 172 million barrels it announced in March. On September 30 President Donald Trump was still thinking about banning diesel exports. On October 1 Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said European partners should accelerate delivery on their existing commitments and make additional supplies immediately available. On October 2 the leaders agreed to that release, and hours later President Trump said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel. The joint statement commits the seven countries to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between themselves.
On October 5, Japanese Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara told a press conference, "Japan has been releasing oil from reserves for some time now and there are no plans for a further release at this stage." Reuters reported that in March Japan said it would release about 80 million barrels of oil from its reserves as part of the coordinated agreement. His remark is one member's position on a further release. It does not allocate the October 100 million barrels, and the October 2 statement's promise to begin immediately is not a barrel shown delivered.
The G7 statement, the French readout and the International Energy Agency's account of the meeting do not say whether any October barrel has been delivered.
The International Energy Agency said in March that the overall release of emergency stocks will largely consist of crude oil, while in Europe the contributions will primarily take the form of refined oil products. A barrel counted as crude is not the diesel a pump sells. The October decision also says the countries will coordinate maintenance schedules across their refineries to prevent simultaneous shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization where feasible, and it encourages engagement with countries that hold significant refining capacity to boost production of refined products, particularly diesel. The International Energy Agency's September report says net exports of diesel and gasoil from the Gulf countries averaged 390,000 barrels a day in August, just over a quarter of levels before the war in the Middle East, and that exports from the Gulf and Russia together were 1.6 million barrels a day lower than in February. The French presidency's readout says volumes moving through Hormuz and the Yanbu canal in the Red Sea have been rising in recent days. On September 30, Energy Secretary Chris Wright said diesel supplies are "tight" because conflicts involving Russia and the Middle East have cut exports, and that American refiners are running at record highs.
The catch
A diesel release, and no number for it
President Donald Trump posted that Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil and that the process will begin immediately. Before the statement, Reuters reported that the United States had demanded 100 million barrels of diesel from large European countries within 20 days. The same report said European Union countries that day discussed a French proposal for 50 million barrels of diesel from Europe and 50 million barrels of crude oil from International Energy Agency members. Those were positions before the agreement. The Wall Street Journal's lede said the countries would "release 100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel from their emergency stocks". The joint statement gives no diesel number, names no country that will supply it, and reports nothing delivered.
An X post said "100 MILLION BARRELS OF DIESEL are currently en route to the United States," and attributed that to Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins. A Scripps interview with Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins the same day has her saying the administration would do everything it could in the short term. The interview does not carry the en-route claim.

The export ban
On October 1, Reuters reported that the United States had asked the European Union to release 120 million barrels of diesel in the next six months. Politico reported that one European diplomat characterized the White House’s demands for a release against the threat of a U.S. export ban explicitly as “blackmail.”
On October 2 President Trump said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel. He added, "It was never really ever on the table, but what Europe did was a great thing." The National reported that President Macron said Trump did not threaten to issue an embargo on US diesel.
European Commission spokeswoman Anna-Kaisa Itkonen said, "We fully reject any ban on diesel."
Ursula von der Leyen, European Commission president · October 2, 2026 · X post, via RTÉ
We welcome the decision of G7 countries not to impose any export bans on allies and the continued solidarity between partners. We support an IEA-coordinated release of fuel stocks.
RTÉ
What comes next
On October 2 the G7 said it would meet again through the International Energy Agency in the coming days to discuss more diesel releases if needed. It invites the agency to monitor the impact of the measures and whether the commitments are carried out. It says a follow-up report should be delivered before 20 days and should "include actionable recommendations to enhance future responses, including stocks replenishment." It does not say who delivers the report. On October 5 Japan's chief cabinet secretary said there are no plans for a further release at this stage. Reuters reported that it was uncertain how much of the new release would come from what remains from the 400 million barrels of oil that the 32 countries that are members of the International Energy Agency agreed to release in March.
Participating companies return the barrels borrowed in the September 29 exchange later with extra barrels. Bids are due October 6. Five previous solicitations awarded more than 133 million barrels across four completed exchanges, and deliveries under the four exchanges are scheduled for November and December.
What we do not know yet
How much of the 100 million barrels is beyond the March pledge, how the release divides between diesel and crude, and which other countries will supply it, the October 2 documents do not say. On October 5 Japan said it has no plans for a further release at this stage. On October 2, Brent settled down 6 cents, or 0.06%, at $102.25 a barrel, and WTI finished down $1.76, or 1.90%, at $91.11 a barrel. Those crude settlements do not show a change in retail diesel. The newest weekly diesel reading is September 28 and the newest Brent reading in the pinned series is September 29.

What happened
The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, October 2, 2026, that payroll employment rose by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent, and said both changed little.
A Reuters survey of economists had nonfarm payrolls likely up by 90,000 last month.
Private payrolls rose by 46,000. Government payrolls fell by 17,000.
July and August together are 60,000 lower than previously reported.
The August release had reported that payroll employment increased by 162,000 and that the unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1 percent.
The payroll figure and the unemployment rate come from two surveys. September's payroll change is the establishment figure, and the unemployment rate is the household figure.
On September 16 the Federal Reserve raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point. On September 30 the personal consumption expenditures price index for August increased 3.4 percent from a year earlier. On October 2 this report arrived. The committee meets next on October 27-28, and the October employment report is scheduled for November 6, so this is the last monthly jobs report before that meeting.
The catch
USA Today headlined the release “September jobs report shows slowdown in hiring, unemployment ticks up”. In the establishment survey, an over-the-month employment change of about 122,000 is statistically significant, and the household survey's threshold is about 650,000. 29,000 is far under that bar, so the survey cannot tell September's change from no change at all.
The household count of people employed rose by 406,000 from August. The rise in the number of people employed is under the household survey's bar of about 650,000. More people were working in September, and the labor force grew by more than employment did (485,000 against 406,000). The count of unemployed rose by 78,000 and the rate moved from 4.1 to 4.2 percent.
Unrounded, a St. Louis Fed note dated Oct. 2, 2026, says the rate “increased slightly from 4.141% to 4.175%”, so most of the visible tenth is rounding.
The national rate moved a tenth of a point while the rate for Black workers rose a full point in one month, to 7 percent. July was 6.3 percent and August was 6 percent. A year earlier the rate was 7.6 percent.
The ADP National Employment Report, an independent measure of the labor market produced by ADP Research, says private-sector employment increased by 90,000 jobs in September, a different count from the bureau's private payrolls, which rose by 46,000.

September's gain of 29,000 followed an average monthly gain of 45,000 over the prior 12 months. The unemployment rate has remained in a narrow range of 4.1 percent to 4.3 percent since March. That 45,000 is the prior 12 months; the Associated Press put average gains at 68,000 jobs a month so far this year, and 2025 at 9,700 jobs a month.
What we do not know yet
In the establishment survey, estimates for the most recent two months are labeled preliminary because they are based on incomplete returns. September's count of gross hires has not been published. It comes with the Job Openings and Labor Turnover release on November 3. The newest count is August's, when hires changed little at 5.2 million. On the Fed's 2026 meeting calendar, October 27-28 follows the September 15-16 meeting. The Employment Situation for October is scheduled for Friday, November 6, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.
The bureau gave no reason for the size of the change. The Fed has decided nothing since September 16 and meets next on October 27-28.

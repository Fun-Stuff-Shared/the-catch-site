What happened
On October 2, 2026, G7 leaders agreed in their statement to a coordinated release of 100 million barrels through the International Energy Agency "to begin immediately over 4 months", "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled", with what they called a "frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners". The same statement says the group will coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries to prevent simultaneous capacity shutdowns and "temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible", and "We also encourage engagement with countries holding significant refining capacities to boost global production of refined products, particularly diesel". It reaffirms a commitment to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries.
The French presidency's readout states a ceiling, "jusqu'à 100 millions de barils sous 4 mois", up to 100 million barrels within 4 months. That readout names refined products, notably diesel and crude.
In March, International Energy Agency members pledged 400 million barrels; by October 2 around 325 million had been released, and that day the G7 set 100 million barrels over 4 months without saying how much of it is beyond the March pledge. The October 2 statement asks the agency to "monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments".
On September 30 President Donald Trump said he was still "thinking about" banning diesel exports. On October 2, hours after the G7 agreement, he said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel, and the joint statement commits the seven countries to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between themselves.
The IEA's March contributions page says the overall release of emergency stocks will largely consist of crude oil, while in Europe the contributions will primarily take the form of refined oil products. A barrel counted as crude is not the diesel a pump sells. On September 30, Energy Secretary Chris Wright said diesel supplies are "tight" because of export loss related to conflicts in Russia and the Middle East.
The October 2 statement calls for the immediate and full restoration of navigational rights and principles in the Strait of Hormuz. Net exports of diesel/gasoil from the Gulf countries averaged 390,000 barrels a day in August, just over a quarter of levels before the war in the Middle East. Combined, net exports of diesel/gasoil exports from the Gulf and Russia in August were 1.6 million barrels a day lower than in February.
The catch
A "massive amount" of diesel, to "begin immediately"
President Donald Trump posted that Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil and that the process will begin immediately. The joint statement gives no number for the diesel, names no country that will supply it, and records no barrel delivered; it calls the diesel release "substantial" and places it in the first 20 days.

100 million barrels of diesel in 20 days, or 50 and 50
Reuters reported a demand to "release 100 million barrels of diesel within a 20-day window". A Friday, October 2, proposal, before the statement, said European countries would release 50 million barrels of diesel and International Energy Agency members would release 50 million barrels of crude oil.
100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel
The Wall Street Journal's lede said the countries would "release 100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel from their emergency stocks". IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol noted that around 325 million barrels of the IEA collective action announced on 11 March have so far been released, representing over 80% of the 400 million barrels originally pledged in the action.

The export ban
H.R. 10422, introduced September 16, would bar diesel exports after fourteen consecutive days above $5 a gallon, and "was referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs".
On September 30 President Donald Trump was still "thinking about" banning diesel exports. On October 1 Bessent said European partners should accelerate delivery on their existing commitments and make additional supplies immediately available. On October 2 the statement reaffirms a commitment to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries. The same day a Reuters brief said President Donald Trump will not authorize an export ban on diesel.
An EU spokeswoman told France 24, "We fully reject any ban on diesel."

What happened next
The statement says the group will convene in the context of the IEA in the coming days to discuss the possibility of additional diesel releases as necessary. The statement says a report should be delivered before 20 days.
Bids on the September 29 exchange are due October 6, 2026. Deliveries under awarded exchanges are scheduled for November and December 2026.
What we do not know yet
The IEA's October 2 account puts the 325 million barrels on the March collective action. The October statement does not assign that tally to a portion of its own 100 million barrel timetable. France 24 said 80 percent of overall IEA stocks were "still in our pocket", which is overall stocks, not the unreleased share of the March 400. The German FAQ says the IEA had asked Germany to release 2,6 million tonnes from its reserve, a March request. RTÉ reported that US oil prices dropped sharply, as much as 5% at one point. The diesel series' last row is September 28, and Brent's last row is September 29.
The joint statement, the French readout, the IEA's October 2 account, and the Energy Department's September 29 request name no amount of the 100 million that is beyond the March pledge, no diesel-versus-crude split of the October volume, no country share of it, and no October barrel delivered.

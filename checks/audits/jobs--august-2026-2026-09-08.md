What is missing is material: the page has no evidence that August hiring snapped back, converts an immigration hypothesis into fact, overstates the number of affected Haitian TPS care workers, omits official evidence that job growth had meaningful breadth, and makes two revision-count errors.

This audit is bounded as of September 8, 2026. I read all 69,788 built-page bytes, checked all 35 manifest records, confirmed all 35 stored text hashes, and compared the principal BLS and FRED series with their live versions. The manifest contains zero video pins, so there was no video or transcript denominator to process.

The core reported values do check out: payrolls +162,000; unemployment 4.1 percent; June and July revisions +55,000 combined; three-month payroll average 71,333; twelve-month average 50,250; 2026 average 80,375; nominal hourly earnings growth 3.09 percent; and the major sector point estimates.

## 1. Verification findings

### 1. HIGH: The headline reports hiring, but the record measures net payroll employment

**Page bytes:** [built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/dist/events/jobs/august-2026/index.html:1), byte 134: `"Hiring snaps back: payrolls rise by 162,000"`; byte 40138: `"hiring resumed"`.

**Record:** [BLS release](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/bls-empsit-2026-08.txt:1), byte 4961, says the establishment survey measures `"nonfarm employment, hours, and earnings by industry."` The [July JOLTS record](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/bls-jolts-2026-07.txt:1), byte 14484, separately defines hires as additions to payroll during the reference month. BLS will not publish August JOLTS until September 29. The latest distinct hiring lineage, [LinkedIn through July](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/workforce-data/reports/linkedin-workforce-report-august-2026), reported hiring 7.6 percent below July 2025 and 26 percent below February 2020.

**Correct account:** Net payroll employment rebounded in August. Whether gross hiring rebounded is unknown. The headline and repeated “hiring resumed” language collapse employment stocks and hiring flows, leaving job seekers and new entrants out of the account.

### 2. HIGH: A Haitian TPS hypothesis is promoted to fact, and its workforce denominator is wrong

**Page bytes:** byte 35432: `"One specific supply shock sits inside the August figures."`; byte 35640: `"scrambling to replace them in August, with more than 100,000 of them estimated to have worked in low-wage elder care."`

**Record:** [KPMG](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/coverage/kpmg-august-jobs.txt:6), byte 646, uses expressly tentative language: `"That suggests that the layoffs ... contributed to earlier layoffs"`. BLS itself says at byte 17381 that its surveys are `"not designed to identify the legal status of workers."`

The underlying workforce evidence does not support the page’s denominator. A [Supreme Court brief](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1083/404361/20260413171600038_25-1083%2025-1084%20Brief.pdf) distinguishes about 111,000 Haitian immigrants in all health-care positions from estimates of 13,000 Haitian TPS nursing assistants and 8,000 caregivers. The underlying [FWD.us fact sheet](https://www.fwd.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Haiti-TPS-Fact-Sheet_January-2026.pdf) gives those same 13,000 and 8,000 estimates.

**Correct account:** Haitian TPS work authorization ended in late July, and documented employer layoffs make a labor-supply effect plausible. No cited evidence identifies its contribution to July payroll losses, the August rebound, or replacement hiring. More than 100,000 describes Haitian immigrants in health care, not Haitian TPS workers in elder care.

### 3. MEDIUM: The household survey cannot confirm the direction claimed

**Page bytes:** byte 30371: `"The household figures confirm the direction of the month, not its size."`; byte 31349 calls the flat unemployment rate a version that `"reads as strength"`.

**Record:** [BLS release](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/bls-empsit-2026-08.txt:1), byte 16535, gives a household-survey significance threshold of about 650,000. Estimated household employment rose 569,000, below that threshold. BLS describes the employment-population ratio as having “changed little.”

**Correct account:** The household point estimates moved positively, but sampling uncertainty is too large to confirm even their direction. The page discloses the threshold and then draws the conclusion the threshold rules out.

### 4. MEDIUM: The page’s revision arithmetic is wrong

The page’s own table reports:

- Byte 49618: May, +172 first estimate, +63 current, revision -109.
- Byte 49706: June, +57 first estimate, +31 current, revision -26.

**Record:** [ALFRED vintages](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/alfred-payems-vintages-2026.csv:1) and [current PAYEMS](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/PAYEMS-2026-09-08.csv:1045) produce revisions of +30, -64, +36, +33, -109, -26, and +44 from January through July.

**Correct account:** Four months were revised up and three down. The two largest downward revisions were May and February, not May and June.

### 5. MEDIUM: One Governor is generalized into “the Fed,” and the policy inference exceeds his statement

**Page bytes:** byte 55202: `"Fed's own reasoning ... ran the other way from both"`; byte 56892: `"A strong jobs report removed a reason to hold"`.

**Record:** [Waller speech](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/fed-waller-2026-09-03.txt:1), byte 12141, says the labor market was `"not a large factor"` in his policy determination; byte 16770 says his decision would be heavily influenced by August inflation. Chairman Warsh had separately said the Fed’s predominant focus should be prices in his [August 28 speech](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm).

**Correct account:** Markets raised the probability they assigned to a hike. Before the report, Waller and Warsh both considered the labor market stable and inflation the decisive issue. The jobs report bolstered the market case for a hike, but it did not establish the FOMC’s reasoning or remove a stated reason Waller had for holding.

## 2. Completeness discovery findings

### 6. HIGH: Counterevidence on the breadth of job growth is omitted

**Page bytes:** byte 11135: `"The gain was concentrated in two places."`; byte 19459 says 71,000 is `"the number to carry, not 162,000"`; byte 20023 asserts the payback interpretation `"is not a minority view."`

**Record:** [BLS summary table B](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.b.htm) reports a private-industry diffusion index of 55.6, where 50 means equal numbers of industries gaining and losing employment. The pinned [Yahoo record](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/coverage/yahoo-chart-august-jobs.txt:18), byte 2427, itself says there was `"encouraging breadth outside the two volatile categories."` The [official confidence-interval table](https://www.bls.gov/charts/employment-situation/otm-employment-change-by-industry-confidence-intervals.htm) also confirms a statistically significant total gain, while several broad sector estimates remain individually uncertain.

**Correct account:** Restaurants and local education supplied 62 percent of the point-estimate gain, so growth was concentrated by magnitude. It was not narrowly confined to those industries. The official breadth measure improved to 55.6. Seasonal payback is a credible competing interpretation, not a demonstrated consensus, and the available sources provide no denominator for “not a minority view.”

### 8. LOW: The decline in negative months is assigned to only one of two causes

**Page bytes:** byte 17751: `"down from five a month ago because July flipped."`

**Record:** [PAYEMS CSV](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site/data/sources/PAYEMS-2026-09-08.csv:1041) shows that August 2025, a negative month, also rolled out of the twelve-month window. Using the current vintage, the prior window has four negative months; it reaches five only by also restoring July’s first estimate of -23,000.

**Correct account:** The count fell from the earlier reported five to the current three for two reasons: July was revised positive and negative August 2025 left the rolling window.

## Reconstructed story

August payroll employment rose by a statistically significant but still imprecise 162,000, and June and July were revised upward by 55,000 combined. The three-month pace was about 71,000 and the twelve-month pace about 50,000, so one strong estimate did not restore the 2023 to 2024 pace.

Restaurants and local-government education contributed 101,000 of the point estimate, with seasonal effects plausibly explaining part of both movements. At the same time, the official diffusion index rose to 55.6, so gains were broader than the page’s payback framing conveys. Both facts belong in the account.

The unemployment rate remained 4.1 percent. Household employment, labor force, and participation point estimates improved, but household employment did not clear the survey’s significance threshold. Nominal wage growth slowed to 3.1 percent; involuntary part-time employment fell; long-term unemployment increased. The evidence supports a stable, low-layoff labor market, not yet a demonstrated rebound in hiring.

The end of Haitian TPS work authorization was real and caused documented layoffs, but neither BLS nor the cited economists measured its national payroll effect. The page’s replacement-hiring mechanism remains a hypothesis, and its 100,000-plus elder-care denominator conflates all Haitian health-care workers with Haitian TPS caregivers.

Markets repriced toward a September rate hike after the release. Fed officials had already described employment as stable and inflation as their predominant concern. August inflation, not this payroll estimate alone, remained the unresolved policy input.

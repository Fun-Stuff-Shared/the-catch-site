# What You're Missing

The [built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fuel/dist/events/fuel-reserves/2026-10-02-g7-releases-100-million-barrels/index.html) reports the G7 announcement accurately in its narrow terms, but leaves out facts needed to understand what supply was new, why diesel was the focus, and what changed in the negotiations.

1. **Major: The 100 million barrels cannot be counted as 100 million newly pledged barrels.** The page says “coordinated release beginning immediately” and places it beside “the March 11 agreement of 400 million barrels.” The [G7 statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/g7-leaders-statement-on-global-energy-security-and-market-stability) says it is implementing commitments while “taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled.” The [IEA’s October 2 account](https://www.iea.org/news/executive-director-participates-in-g7-leaders-meeting-on-energy-security-and-markets) says **around 325 million barrels** of the March action had already been released. The correct account is a new timetable and diesel priority within an existing commitment framework. The records do not establish how much of the October figure is additional to March. This changes *what happened* and the easiest wrong reading.

2. **Major: Neither the diesel amount nor the country allocations are specified.** The page’s headline is “G-7 Agrees to Release 100 Million Barrels of Diesel and Crude,” and its lead promises a “substantial diesel release in the first 20 days.” The [French presidency’s readout](https://www.elysee.fr/emmanuel-macron/2026/10/02/visioconference-des-dirigeants-du-g7-sur-la-situation-energetique-mondiale) says “jusqu'à 100 millions de barils sous 4 mois,” meaning *up to* 100 million over four months. The [G7 statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/g7-leaders-statement-on-global-energy-security-and-market-stability) calls the early diesel amount “substantial” without a number; [Reuters’ account](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/europe-weighs-new-diesel-stocks-release-after-us-pressure-sources-say-ce785ddadf8ef124) says the agreement gave no product or country breakdown. The correct account is a combined oil and fuel-products plan, with its diesel share and actual deliveries unresolved. This changes *what is unresolved*.

3. **Major: The page omits the supply mechanism behind the price chart.** Its “Prices” section gives “6.382 dollars a gallon” for diesel and crude benchmarks, but does not explain why releasing crude alone may do little for a diesel bottleneck. The [IEA’s September market report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026) puts August Gulf diesel and gasoil net exports at **390,000 barrels a day**, just over a quarter of their prewar level, and estimates combined Gulf and Russian diesel exports **1.6 million barrels a day below February**. The [IEA’s October 2 account](https://www.iea.org/news/executive-director-participates-in-g7-leaders-meeting-on-energy-security-and-markets) says Middle Eastern crude exports had recovered substantially while refined-product flows remained severely constrained, compounded by attacks on Russian refineries. The correct account is a continuing shipping and refining shock with a particularly tight diesel market. This changes *why it matters* and the concept a reader needs.

4. **Major: The export-ban confrontation and its outcome are missing from the story.** The page’s chronology says “Bessent asks Europe for more supply,” and its detail view quotes the G7 pledge to “refrain from export restrictions.” That leaves out the counterparty’s response. In the [European Commission’s October 2 briefing transcript](https://audiovisual.ec.europa.eu/en/media/video/I-295561), its spokesperson said the EU “fully reject[ed]” a US diesel ban and was ready to work through the IEA. After the agreement, [Reuters’ recorded account of Trump’s remarks](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-us-will-not-be-doing-diesel-export-ban-ce785ddbda88f720) quotes him saying, “we’re not going to be doing the export ban.” The correct account is that a threatened US restriction formed part of the negotiations, the EU opposed it, and Trump then ruled it out. The two House bills shown on the page were proposals, not the threatened executive action. This changes *what changed* on October 2.

5. **Major: The reserve release was one part of the G7 decision.** Under “The release,” the page leads with the 100 million barrels and does not carry the refinery measure. The [same G7 statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/g7-leaders-statement-on-global-energy-security-and-market-stability) says members will “coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries” and increase use of capacity where feasible. The correct account is a package addressing refinery output and trade as well as stocks. This changes *what happened*, especially given the refined-product constraint.

6. **Moderate: High prices did not mean an immediate EU supply failure.** The page presents the price rise without that distinction. The [European Commission’s October 2 task-force account](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/energy-union-task-force-meets-ensure-coordination-diesel-supplies-and-prices-europe-2026-10-02_en) says EU diesel supply remained stable for the time being while prices were high. At the same time, [EIA’s September 30 stock table](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/PET_STOC_WSTK_A_EPD0_SAE_MBBL_W.htm) records **105.180 million barrels** of US distillate stocks for September 25, down from **107.431 million** a week earlier. The correct account distinguishes physical availability by region from severe global market tightness. This changes *why it matters*, without negating the price data.

I have not treated supporting detail already available in “Just the facts” or “Show the work” as a missing story finding.

# Is There More to the Story?

**Materially more to the story.** The announcement is real, but the page does not tell readers how it relates to barrels already pledged and released, why diesel differs from crude, or how the export-ban dispute ended. The principal limit is implementation: I found no accessible October 2 allocation or delivery ledger establishing the product split or the net new volume.

# Completeness Verdict

**Accurate but materially incomplete.** This is a bounded audit of implementation, not a finding that the announced barrels have already reached buyers.

# Source and Corroboration Problems

The UK and French publications of the English G7 statement are **one joint-decision lineage**, not two independent confirmations. The French readout is a separate account of the same meeting. The IEA provides a distinct implementation tally; EIA supplies independent market data; the Commission records its own response. Reuters and AP contribute reporting, but outlets repeating the G7 text do not multiply confirmation of delivery.

The pinned [AP copy](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fuel/data/sources/fuel-reserves/ap-g7-release-2026-10-02.txt) is shorter than the [live AP article](https://apnews.com/article/trump-europe-diesel-fuel-prices-774a360d1646d9ce8aba764fdd9959d2), which now explicitly raises whether October’s barrels are additional to March. The IEA’s March page also links to a country-contributions workbook. I read it directly; its current rows total about **426 million barrels of listed contributions**, a different measure from the **400 million barrel announced action** and from barrels actually released. Those figures should not be summed as separate releases. The [Energy Department’s linked solicitation portal](https://www.spr.doe.gov/doeec/ActiveDocs.htm?type=exchange) exposed no usable solicitation details in the accessible view.

# Reconstructed Story

**Established:** On October 2 the G7 agreed to a coordinated release plan of 100 million barrels over four months, prioritizing an unspecified amount of diesel in the first 20 days. Its statement ties implementation to March commitments, addresses refinery capacity, and opposes energy export restrictions among members. The IEA reported roughly 325 million barrels released from the March action by the time of the meeting. The EU opposed a proposed US diesel export ban, and Trump later said it would not proceed. [G7 statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/g7-leaders-statement-on-global-energy-security-and-market-stability), [IEA account](https://www.iea.org/news/executive-director-participates-in-g7-leaders-meeting-on-energy-security-and-markets), [Commission briefing](https://audiovisual.ec.europa.eu/en/media/video/I-295561), [recorded Trump remarks](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/trump-says-us-will-not-be-doing-diesel-export-ban-ce785ddbda88f720).

**Strongly supported interpretation:** The urgent problem was constrained diesel production and trade amid the Middle East war and attacks on Russian refining, while EU physical supply remained stable for the time being. Reserve releases may ease pressure but do not themselves restore those flows. [IEA market report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026), [Commission account](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/energy-union-task-force-meets-ensure-coordination-diesel-supplies-and-prices-europe-2026-10-02_en).

**Unknown:** The October plan’s country shares, diesel volume, delivered volume, and net addition to the March commitment. No checked record justifies treating an announced, awarded, and delivered barrel as the same thing.

What happened
On October 2, 2026, the G7 leaders agreed to a coordinated release through the International Energy Agency of 100 million barrels over four months, beginning immediately, with a substantial diesel share in the first 20 days and no number for that diesel. They will coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries to prevent simultaneous capacity shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible, and they will refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries. They set the release "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled," and did not say how much of the 100 million is new beyond the March commitments.
U.S. retail diesel on September 28 was above its March 2 price. The G7 leaders said the measures are meant to "shield households and businesses from price shocks". Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said American farmers, truckers, and businesses should not be left carrying the burden of a global diesel shortage. EU diesel supply remains stable for the time being, but prices remain high. Prices have rocketed in recent months because of the US-Israeli conflict with Iran and Russia's war in Ukraine.
France puts the ceiling at up to 100 million barrels within four months, covering refined products, diesel, and crude.
By October 2, members had released most of what they pledged in March, and the United States was still carrying out the share it announced then. On September 29 the United States offered an exchange of crude, and the companies that take the barrels will return them later with additional premium barrels. On September 30 President Donald Trump was still thinking about banning diesel exports. On October 1 Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said European partners should accelerate delivery on their existing commitments and make additional supplies immediately available. On October 2 the leaders agreed to a coordinated release of 100 million barrels over four months, with a substantial diesel share in the first 20 days. Hours later President Trump said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel. The seven countries committed to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between themselves. The March release was still being carried out, the October statement set a four-month timetable with an early diesel share, and the United States dropped the export-ban threat.

A barrel counted as crude is not the diesel a pump sells. In March the overall release of emergency stocks was to consist largely of crude oil, while in Europe the contributions were to be primarily refined products. That split between crude and refined products remained subject to change. In October the countries will coordinate maintenance schedules across their refineries to prevent simultaneous shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization where feasible, and they encourage engagement with countries holding significant refining capacity to boost global production of refined products, particularly diesel. Net exports of diesel and gasoil from the Gulf countries averaged 390,000 barrels a day in August, just over a quarter of levels before the war in the Middle East, and exports from the Gulf and Russia together were 1.6 million barrels a day lower than in February. Volumes moving through Hormuz, the Strait of Hormuz where the G7 leaders call for the restoration of navigational rights, and through the Yanbu canal in the Red Sea have been rising in recent days. On September 30, Energy Secretary Chris Wright said diesel supplies are "tight" because conflicts involving Russia and the Middle East have cut exports, and that American refiners are running at record highs.
The catch
A diesel release, and no number for it
The G7 text commits the leaders to a coordinated release of 100 million barrels over four months, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days, and it names no diesel amount and no country that will supply the barrels. Before that agreement the United States had demanded 100 million barrels of diesel from large European countries within 20 days. European Union countries that day discussed a French proposal for 50 million barrels of diesel from Europe and 50 million barrels of crude oil from International Energy Agency members. The demand and the proposal were positions before the agreement. The Wall Street Journal's lede said the countries would "release 100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel from their emergency stocks". France's account of the meeting covers refined products, diesel, and crude. President Donald Trump posted that Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil and that the process will begin immediately. On October 5 the White House said he had negotiated with Europe to release 100 million barrels of refined diesel from its strategic reserves within the next four months. None of the White House claim, the joint text, or the French readout is a record of barrels delivered.
The words "100 MILLION BARRELS OF DIESEL are currently en route to the United States" are an X post's wording, attributed to Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins. In the published excerpts of her interview she says the administration would do everything it could in the short term.
A later turn of the interview takes up a diesel export ban. Asked about that ban, she said she is the agriculture secretary, not energy, and is not in those conversations. In the closed captions she says, "100 million barrels that will be headed this way very, very soon. fact, I think they’re moving out right now." She does not call the barrels diesel in that passage. That post turns her I think into a flat claim and adds diesel.

The export ban
The United States has asked the European Union to release 120 million barrels of diesel in the next six months, according to an October 1 account. One European diplomat called the White House’s demands for a release, set against the threat of a U.S. export ban, “blackmail.”
On October 2 President Trump said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel, adding, "It was never really ever on the table, but what Europe did was a great thing." President Macron said Trump did not threaten to issue an embargo on US diesel.
The European Commission rejected a diesel ban. Spokeswoman Anna-Kaisa Itkonen said, "We fully reject any ban on diesel."
Ursula von der Leyen, European Commission president · October 2, 2026 · X post, via RTÉ
We welcome the decision of G7 countries not to impose any export bans on allies and the continued solidarity between partners. We support an IEA-coordinated release of fuel stocks.

What comes next
The leaders will meet again through the International Energy Agency in the coming days to discuss more diesel releases if needed. They invite the agency to monitor the impact of the measures and whether the commitments are carried out. A follow-up report should be delivered before 20 days and should "include actionable recommendations to enhance future responses, including stocks replenishment." On October 5 President Trump signed an order saying restricted global diesel supply has led to rising prices and telling the Treasury secretary to defer the excise tax incurred from October 5 through December 31, 2026, if he determines the relief is authorized. Within five days the secretary is to direct the Internal Revenue Service to announce it will not impose a penalty when dyed diesel is used on the highway in that period. The White House says the order allows off-road dyed diesel for highway use. The measure is a tax and penalty step on U.S. fuel, not a reserve delivery. How much of the new release would come from what remains from the 400 million barrels of oil that the 32 countries that are members of the International Energy Agency agreed to release in March is uncertain.
Participating companies will return the barrels they borrow in the September 29 exchange later with extra barrels. Bids are due October 6. Solicitations before the September 29 exchange had already awarded barrels, and deliveries under awarded exchanges are scheduled for November and December.
What we do not know yet
How much of the 100 million barrels is beyond the March pledge, and which countries will supply it, is unstated. The joint text leaves the split between diesel and crude unstated. On October 5 the White House said the release is 100 million barrels of refined diesel from Europe's strategic reserves within four months.

1. What happened: **Answered**, no effort. "The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, October 2, 2026, that payroll employment rose by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent, and said both changed little."
2. Why it matters: **Partly.** The nearest sentence is "The bureau's next monthly jobs report, for October, is scheduled for November 6, after the committee meets on October 27-28." It never says what a jobs report tells the Fed or an ordinary reader.
3. The easy wrong reading: **Answered**, some work. "September's estimate of 29,000 is under that bar, and the bureau's own summary is that payrolls “changed little”." The page quotes the USA Today headline but never says it misleads.
4. Two surveys: **Partly.** "The establishment survey counts jobs on employer payrolls... The household survey counts people, and individuals are counted only once." I had to infer that 29,000 is the first and 4.2 percent the second.
5. What changed: **Answered.** "August from a gain of 162,000 to 133,000, and July from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000."
6. What is unresolved: **Answered**, with hunting. "In the establishment survey, estimates for the most recent two months are labeled preliminary because they are based on incomplete returns."

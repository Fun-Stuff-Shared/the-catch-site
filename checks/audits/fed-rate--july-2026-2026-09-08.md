### Reconstructed story

On July 29, 2026, the FOMC held its target range at 3.50-3.75 percent by a 9-3 vote. Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan preferred an immediate 25-basis-point increase. It was the first meeting since September 2016 with three dissents all seeking tighter policy.

The split was important but not new. In June, every participant supported holding at that meeting, while a few already said there was a case for raising rates. Nine of 18 June projections placed the appropriate year-end rate above the current midpoint.

The July minutes describe two bounded positions. Most participants wanted another intermeeting data cycle to clarify whether supply-related inflation would fade; several favored an immediate hike because they saw broad price pressure and insufficient restraint. “Many” said tightening would become necessary if inflation failed to decline, which is conditional rather than a commitment.

Warsh was also reshaping communication. He reduced ordinary forward guidance, welcomed visible disagreement, and proposed discussing a six-meeting annual schedule. He did not commit the Committee to a September hike, and the schedule proposal had not been adopted.

By early September, the question remained open. Warsh still emphasized inflation and weak policy restraint but explicitly declined to precommit. Waller said an improving August inflation report could justify another hold, while a hot report could justify a hike. August employment then rose by 162,000 with unemployment unchanged at 4.1 percent. The immediate decision boundary became the August inflation data and the September 15-16 meeting, not merely the existence of the July dissents.

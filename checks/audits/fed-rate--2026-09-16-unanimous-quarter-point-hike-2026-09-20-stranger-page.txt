The federal funds rate · September 16, 2026
Fed raises rates a quarter point on a unanimous vote
The first increase since July 2023 was widely expected. The change was a 9–3 hold becoming a 12–0 hike, a tougher inflation message and 16 of 18 policymakers expecting another increase this year.

The decision
What happened
Financial markets put 94 percent odds on a quarter-point increase the day before the decision. The new information was how completely the committee had moved: July's 9–3 hold became a 12–0 increase, and 16 of 18 policymakers expected another increase by year-end.
The Federal Open Market Committee raised its target range by one-quarter percentage point to 3.75–4.00 percent, effective September 17.
record
It was the first increase since July 27, 2023.
The first increase since 2023 Upper limit of the target range
The upper bound fell in six steps from September 2024 through December 2025, then rose a quarter point effective September 17, 2026.
Seven weeks earlier, most policymakers had wanted more information before acting, though several already favored an increase.
Warsh had set a public test in late August: inflation had to move toward the Fed's goal “clearly and at sufficient speed.” By September, consumer prices were still 3.4 percent above a year earlier, and gasoline had supplied more than one-third of August's monthly increase.
Headline economic growth slowed to 1.5 percent from 2.1 percent. Beneath that slowdown, consumer spending and private fixed investment grew 4.2 percent, a measure of domestic demand.
Warsh gave three reasons for acting: a stronger economy, inflation trends that still failed his test and a changed view of the most and least likely geopolitical outcomes. By September, officials were no longer counting on the Iran-war energy shock being temporary, according to the Associated Press.
The wording also hardened. July attributed part of the inflation problem to supply shocks in sectors including energy. September dropped that explanation and said tighter policy would support a “timelier return” to 2 percent.
The mechanism
What a rate hike can change
A higher overnight rate makes borrowing more expensive and slows consumer and business spending. That reduces demand across the economy.
That tool leaves the price of oil and gasoline untouched. Warsh's concern was the next step: the shock broadening into other prices and inflation expectations.
record
Lower oil and gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, disrupted refining and Red Sea shipping had already raised energy and transport costs. CBO estimated that energy added 2.3 percentage points to the annualized second-quarter increase in the Fed's preferred household-price measure. Prices outside food and energy were expected to stay elevated longer because the higher transport costs reached other goods and services more slowly.
Interest-rate changes reach spending, jobs and prices with a delay. The energy shock could fade before tighter credit has fully slowed the economy.
Borrowers and savers
Who feels it
Credit-card rates can respond quickly because most cards carry variable rates tied to banks' prime rate. Auto loans and adjustable-rate mortgages can also become more expensive, while savings accounts and certificates of deposit may pay more.
checked
Fixed mortgage rates tend to follow the 10-year Treasury yield. Existing fixed-rate loans keep the rate in their contract.
Consumer-finance analyst Matt Schulz expected one quarter-point increase to have a small effect. The burden grows if several increases stack up over time.
Warsh said persistent inflation falls especially hard on people who live from paycheck to paycheck without financial assets, home equity or retirement-plan equity. Stable prices protect the buying power of their take-home pay.
The signal beyond the vote
The projections
Sixteen of 18 policymakers expected at least one more quarter-point increase by the end of 2026. Their median year-end rate rose from 3.8 percent in June to 4.1 percent in September.
Each individual projection gives where one policymaker thought the rate should stand at year-end. Warsh submitted no projection and declined to guide the next decision.
Policymakers projected inflation in the Fed's preferred household-price measure at 3.7 percent for 2026, up from 3.6 percent in June, and 2.3 percent for 2027. Their median reaches 2 percent in 2029.
The catch
The matching 4.1 percent medians conceal different paths
CBS wrote that “policymakers expect to hold rates steady throughout 2027.” Each year's 4.1 percent figure is the middle of a separate set of individual year-end judgments. It does not establish a committee decision to hold at every 2027 meeting.
The White House and the central bank
The pressure around the decision
About two hours after the announcement, President Donald Trump demanded rates of 1 percent or less and called for the Fed to lower them fast.
checked
Trump said the next morning that he had told Warsh to vote with the board because the result would not change. He called it “a very tough board” and “very hostile.”
Warsh described central-bank independence as “a two-way street” and said trade and fiscal policymakers should stay in their lane.
The pressure's effect on the vote remains unresolved. All 12 voters backed the increase, and Warsh declined to discuss conversations with Trump.
The dispute was also about what a supply shock calls for. Before the meeting, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued that the Fed should wait for a shock to spread through the economy before raising rates.
September 17 and 18
What happened next
The 10-year Treasury yield slipped the next day, a move the Associated Press tied to easing inflation worries. That yield strongly influences mortgage rates, while the Fed controls a short-term rate.
checked
By September 18, market-implied odds of another increase in October had risen to about 58 percent from 42 percent a week earlier. Goldman Sachs expected an October increase, while Bank of America expected increases in October and December. Natixis strategist Jack Janasiewicz instead saw the move as a reversal of the “insurance cuts” from 2025 rather than the start of an aggressive tightening cycle.

Open questions
What we do not know yet
Whether another increase follows this one. The year-end views are individual judgments, and Warsh declined to prejudge the next decision.
How long the Iran-war energy shock lasts.
What the next inflation and growth reports show. August inflation in the Fed's preferred measure and the annual national-account updates, including GDP revisions, are due September 30.
What the September deliberations add. The minutes are due October 7, three weeks after the decision.

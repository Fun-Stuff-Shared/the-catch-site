The federal funds rate · September 16, 2026
Fed raises rates a quarter point on a unanimous vote
The first increase since July 2023 was widely expected. The change was a 9–3 hold becoming a 12–0 hike, a tougher inflation message and 16 of 18 policymakers expecting another increase this year.

The decision
What happened
CME Group's FedWatch futures-market gauge put 94 percent odds on a quarter-point increase the day before the decision. Markets expected the increase; the committee's 9–3 hold in July became a 12–0 increase in September.
The Federal Open Market Committee raised its target range by one-quarter percentage point to 3.75–4.00 percent, effective September 17.
record
It was the first increase since July 27, 2023.

Seven weeks earlier, most policymakers had wanted more information before acting, though several already favored an increase.
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh had set a public test in late August: inflation had to move toward the Fed's goal “clearly and at sufficient speed.” By September, the Consumer Price Index was 3.4 percent above a year earlier, and gasoline had supplied more than one-third of August's monthly increase.
Real gross domestic product grew at a 1.5 percent annual rate in the second quarter, down from 2.1 percent in the first quarter. Consumer spending plus private fixed investment grew 4.2 percent in the second quarter, faster than headline growth.
Warsh pointed to a stronger economy and inflation trends that still failed his test. By September, officials were no longer counting on the energy shock from the war in Iran being temporary, according to the Associated Press; the war had reduced oil and gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.
The wording also hardened. In July, the committee attributed part of the inflation problem to supply shocks in sectors including energy. In September, the committee dropped that explanation and said tighter policy would support a “timelier return” to 2 percent.
The mechanism
What a rate hike can change
A higher overnight rate makes borrowing more expensive and slows consumer and business spending. That reduces demand across the economy.
That tool leaves the price of oil and gasoline untouched. Warsh's concern was the next step: the shock broadening into other prices and inflation expectations.
record
Disrupted refining and Red Sea shipping added to the energy and transport costs. The Congressional Budget Office estimated that energy added 2.3 percentage points to the annualized second-quarter PCE inflation rate. CBO expected core PCE inflation, which excludes food and energy, to remain elevated longer because higher energy prices reach nonenergy goods and services more slowly.
Interest-rate changes reach spending, jobs and prices with a delay. The energy shock could fade before tighter credit has fully slowed the economy.
Borrowers and savers
Who feels it
Credit-card rates can respond quickly because most cards carry variable rates tied to banks' prime rate. Auto loans and adjustable-rate mortgages can also become more expensive, while savings accounts and certificates of deposit may pay more.
checked
Fixed mortgage rates tend to follow the 10-year Treasury yield. Existing fixed-rate loans keep the rate in their contract.
Consumer-finance analyst Matt Schulz expected one quarter-point increase to have a small effect. He said several increases would matter more as they stacked up over time.
Warsh said people without financial assets, home equity or retirement-plan equity live on paychecks that arrive every few weeks. With inflation at the Fed's 2 percent objective, he said, their wages could “deliver real take home pay increases.”
The signal beyond the vote
The projections
Sixteen of 18 policymakers expected at least one more quarter-point increase by the end of 2026. Their median year-end rate rose from 3.8 percent in June to 4.1 percent in September. The 2027 median was also 4.1 percent.
Each individual projection gives where one policymaker thought the rate should stand at year-end. Warsh submitted no projection and declined to guide the next decision.
The Consumer Price Index was 3.4 percent above a year earlier in August. The Fed's preferred measure, the PCE price index, was 3.7 percent above a year earlier in July. Policymakers projected a 3.7 percent rise in PCE prices from the end of 2025 to the end of 2026, up from their 3.6 percent June projection, and 2.3 percent for 2027. Their median reaches 2 percent in 2029.
The catch
Each 4.1 percent median describes its own year
CBS wrote that “policymakers expect to hold rates steady throughout 2027.” The two figures are separate midpoints of officials' individual year-end judgments, one for 2026 and one for 2027.
The White House and the central bank
The pressure around the decision
About two hours after the announcement, President Donald Trump demanded rates of 1 percent or less and called for the Fed to lower them fast.
checked
Trump portrayed Warsh as unable to change the result, saying he had told the chair to cast the same vote as the rest of the board. He called it “a very tough board” and “very hostile.”
Warsh described central-bank independence as “a two-way street” and said trade and fiscal policymakers should stay in their lane.
All 12 voters backed the increase, and Warsh declined to discuss conversations with Trump.
The dispute was also about what a supply shock calls for. Before the meeting, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued that the Fed should wait for a shock to spread through the economy before raising rates.
September 17 and 18
What happened next
The 10-year Treasury yield slipped the next day, a move the Associated Press tied to easing inflation worries. That yield strongly influences mortgage rates, while the Fed controls a short-term rate.
checked
By September 18, CME Group's FedWatch futures-market gauge put the odds of another increase in October at about 58 percent, up from 42 percent a week earlier. Goldman Sachs expected an October increase, while Bank of America expected increases in October and December. Natixis strategist Jack Janasiewicz said, “We remain unconvinced that this is the start of an aggressive new tightening cycle.”

Open questions
What we do not know yet
Whether another increase follows this one. The year-end views are individual judgments, and Warsh declined to prejudge the next decision.
How long the Iran-war energy shock lasts.
What the next inflation and growth reports show. August inflation in the Fed's preferred measure and the annual national-account updates, including GDP revisions, are due September 30.
What the September deliberations add. The minutes are due October 7, three weeks after the decision.

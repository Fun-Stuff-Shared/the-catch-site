The federal funds rate · September 16, 2026
Fed raises rates a quarter point on a unanimous vote
The first increase since July 2023 was widely expected. The change was inside the committee: July's 9-to-3 vote to hold became a unanimous vote to raise, and 16 of 18 policymakers saw at least one more increase this year as appropriate.

The decision
What happened
The Federal Open Market Committee raised its target range by one-quarter percentage point to 3.75–4.00 percent on September 16, effective September 17. It was the first increase since July 27, 2023.
record
Futures markets had put 94 percent odds on a quarter-point increase the day before. The change was inside the committee. In July, nine of 12 voters had left the target range at 3.50–3.75 percent while three wanted an increase. In September, all 12 voted to raise it.

The officials who waited in July got seven more weeks of evidence that the economy was holding up while inflation stayed well above the 2 percent goal. August added 162,000 jobs, retail sales rose 1.2 percent, and the Consumer Price Index was 3.4 percent above a year earlier, with gasoline supplying more than one-third of the month's increase.
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh had set a public test in late August: inflation had to move toward the Fed's goal “clearly and at sufficient speed.” After the September meeting he said the economy had strengthened and inflation trends were still failing that test.
In July, the committee had attributed part of the inflation problem to supply shocks in sectors including energy. In September it dropped that explanation and said tighter policy would support a “timelier return” to 2 percent. The war in Iran had reduced oil and gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, and officials were no longer counting on that shock being temporary, the Associated Press reported.
A higher overnight rate makes borrowing more expensive and slows consumer and business spending. That reduces demand across the economy.
That tool leaves the price of oil and gasoline untouched. Warsh's concern was the next step: the shock spreading from oil and gasoline into other prices and inflation expectations.
record
Disrupted refining and Red Sea shipping added to energy and transport costs. The Congressional Budget Office estimated that energy added 2.3 percentage points to the annualized second-quarter inflation rate in the PCE price index, the measure the Fed's 2 percent goal is set in.
Interest-rate changes reach spending, jobs and prices with a delay. The energy shock could fade before tighter credit has fully slowed the economy.
Borrowers and savers
Who feels it
Credit-card rates can respond quickly because most cards carry variable rates tied to banks' prime rate. Auto loans and adjustable-rate mortgages can also become more expensive, while savings accounts and certificates of deposit may pay more.
checked
Fixed mortgage rates tend to follow the 10-year Treasury yield more than the Fed's overnight rate.
Consumer-finance analyst Matt Schulz expected one quarter-point increase to have a small effect. He said several increases would matter more as they stacked up over time.
Warsh said people without financial assets, home equity or retirement-plan equity live on paychecks that arrive every few weeks. With inflation at the Fed's 2 percent objective, he said, their wages could “deliver real take home pay increases.”
The signal beyond the vote
The projections
Sixteen of 18 policymakers saw at least one more quarter-point increase by the end of 2026 as appropriate: 12 marked one more, 4 marked two, and 2 marked none. Their median year-end rate rose from 3.8 percent in June to 4.1 percent in September. The 2027 median was also 4.1 percent.
Each projection is one policymaker's judgment of where the rate should stand at year-end, not a forecast of what the committee will do. Warsh submitted no projection and declined to guide the next decision.
The PCE price index was 3.7 percent above a year earlier in July. Policymakers projected a 3.7 percent rise in PCE prices from the end of 2025 to the end of 2026, up from their 3.6 percent June projection, and 2.3 percent for 2027. Their median projection for PCE inflation reaches 2 percent in 2029.
The catch
Each 4.1 percent median describes its own year
CBS wrote that “policymakers expect to hold rates steady throughout 2027.” A hold throughout the year would require the committee to make the same rate decision at every 2027 meeting. The projections give only one median of officials' individual judgments for the end of 2026 and another for the end of 2027.
September 16 to 18
What happened next
About two hours after the announcement, President Donald Trump demanded rates of 1 percent or less and called for the Fed to lower them fast.
checked
Trump portrayed Warsh as unable to change the result, saying he had told the chair to cast the same vote as the rest of the board. He called it “a very tough board” and “very hostile.”
Warsh declined to discuss conversations with Trump. He described central-bank independence as “a two-way street” and said trade and fiscal policymakers should stay in their lane.
The dispute was also about what a supply shock calls for. Before the meeting, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had argued that the Fed should wait for a shock to spread through the economy before raising rates.
The 10-year Treasury yield slipped the next day, a move the Associated Press tied to easing inflation worries. That yield strongly influences mortgage rates, while the Fed controls a short-term rate.
checked
By September 18, futures markets put the odds of another increase in October at about 58 percent, up from 42 percent a week earlier. Goldman Sachs expected an October increase, while Bank of America expected increases in October and December. Natixis strategist Jack Janasiewicz said, “We remain unconvinced that this is the start of an aggressive new tightening cycle.”

Open questions
What we do not know yet
Whether another increase follows this one. The year-end views are individual judgments, and Warsh declined to prejudge the next decision.
How long the Iran-war energy shock lasts.
What the next inflation and growth reports show. August inflation in the Fed's preferred measure and the annual national-account updates, including GDP revisions, are due September 30.
What the September deliberations add. The minutes are due October 7, three weeks after the decision.

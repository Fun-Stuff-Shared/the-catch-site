What happened
On October 2, 2026, the leaders of the G7 countries agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil over four months, coordinated through the International Energy Agency. Their statement promises a "substantial diesel release within the first 20 days" and gives no number for it. It counts the 100 million "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled" and does not say how much of it is new.
The war in the Middle East, the US-Israeli conflict with Iran, together with Russia's war in Ukraine, has pushed fuel prices up in recent months, and the International Energy Agency says the war in the Middle East is creating "the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market". Weekly U.S. retail diesel was 6.382 dollars a gallon on September 28. On March 2 it was 3.897.
The French presidency's readout puts the figure as a ceiling: up to 100 million barrels within four months, notably of diesel and crude oil.
In March, International Energy Agency members pledged 400 million barrels; by October 2 around 325 million had been released, and that day the G7 set 100 million barrels over 4 months without saying how much of it is beyond the March pledge. The October 2 statement asks the agency to "monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments".
On September 30 President Donald Trump said he was still "thinking about" banning diesel exports. On October 2, hours after the G7 agreement, he said he will not authorize an export ban on diesel, and the joint statement commits the seven countries to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between themselves.
The IEA's March contributions page says the overall release of emergency stocks will largely consist of crude oil, while in Europe the contributions will primarily take the form of refined oil products. A barrel counted as crude is not the diesel a pump sells. On September 30, Energy Secretary Chris Wright said diesel supplies are "tight" because conflicts involving Russia and the Middle East have cut exports.
The October 2 statement calls for the immediate and full restoration of navigational rights and principles in the Strait of Hormuz. Net exports of diesel/gasoil from the Gulf countries averaged 390,000 barrels a day in August, just over a quarter of levels before the war in the Middle East. The French presidency's October 2 readout says volumes moving through Hormuz and the Yanbu canal in the Red Sea have been increasing in recent days.
The catch
A "massive amount" of diesel, to "begin immediately"
President Donald Trump posted that Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil and that the process will begin immediately. The joint statement puts no number on the diesel, names no country that will supply it and reports nothing delivered; it calls the diesel release "substantial" and places it in the first 20 days.

100 million barrels of diesel in 20 days, or 50 and 50
Reuters reported that the United States had demanded that large European countries release 100 million barrels of diesel within 20 days. A proposal on Friday, October 2, before the statement, said European countries would release 50 million barrels of diesel and International Energy Agency members would release 50 million barrels of crude oil. The statement adopted neither number for diesel.
100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel
The Wall Street Journal's lede said the countries would "release 100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel from their emergency stocks". The statement counts the 100 million "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled" and does not say how much of it is beyond the March pledge.

The export ban
H.R. 10422, introduced September 16, would bar diesel exports after fourteen consecutive days above $5 a gallon, and "was referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs".
European Commission spokeswoman Anna-Kaisa Itkonen said, "We fully reject any ban on diesel."
Ursula von der Leyen, European Commission president · October 2, 2026 · X post, via RTÉ
We welcome the decision of G7 countries not to impose any export bans on allies and the continued solidarity between partners. We support an IEA-coordinated release of fuel stocks.
RTÉ
What comes next
The G7 says it will meet again through the International Energy Agency in the coming days to discuss more diesel releases if needed, and it asks the agency for a follow-up report within 20 days on how the measures are working. Separately, the U.S. Energy Department is offering up to 40 million barrels of crude from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve as an exchange, in which companies take oil now and return it later with extra barrels. Bids are due October 6, and deliveries under awarded exchanges are scheduled for November and December.
What we do not know yet
Four things are not in the G7 statement, the French readout, the International Energy Agency's account or the Energy Department's request: how much of the 100 million barrels is beyond the March pledge, how it divides between diesel and crude, which countries will supply it, and whether any of it has been delivered. The prices are from before the decision: the newest weekly diesel reading is September 28 and the newest Brent reading is September 29.

# What You're Missing

1. **Major | The decision’s causal explanation**

   - **Page bytes:** “Fed raises rates a quarter point on a unanimous vote” and “Inflation remains elevated.” [Built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/dist/events/fed-rate/2026-09-16-unanimous-quarter-point-hike/index.html:1)
   - **Record bytes:** Warsh said, “So we removed a dose of accommodation.” He identified three changes since July: a stronger economy, summer inflation that had not improved enough, and a changed geopolitical assessment. [Press conference transcript](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/fomc-press-conference-2026-09-16.txt:320)
   - **Correct account:** The Fed did not raise rates merely because inflation remained above target. Officials concluded that demand and financial conditions could withstand less accommodation, inflation progress had stalled, and geopolitical risks made further delay less acceptable. This changes “what changed” and “why it matters.”

2. **Major | The projections conceal the breadth and size of the policy shift**

   - **Page bytes:** “The new projections put the median year-end rate at 4.1 percent in 2026 and 2027.” [Built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/dist/events/fed-rate/2026-09-16-unanimous-quarter-point-hike/index.html:1)
   - **Record bytes:** “Twelve of 18 FOMC officials projected a single rate hike” and “Four officials projected two hikes.” [The Hill pin](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/coverage/the-hill-decision-2026-09-16.txt:8) The SEP moved the 2026 median from 3.8 in June to 4.1 in September and the 2027 median from 3.6 to 4.1. [SEP table](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/fomc-sep-2026-09-16.txt:54)
   - **Correct account:** Sixteen of 18 participants projected at least one additional 2026 increase, including four projecting two. The median alone understates how broadly the committee’s expected path moved upward. These are conditional assessments, not commitments or the likeliest forecast, as the SEP itself cautions. [SEP note](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/fomc-sep-2026-09-16.txt:1147)

3. **Major | The inflation composition and the limit of Fed power**

   - **Page bytes:** “Inflation remains elevated. Today’s policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal.” [Built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/dist/events/fed-rate/2026-09-16-unanimous-quarter-point-hike/index.html:1)
   - **Record bytes:** BLS reported, “The index for gasoline rose 3.9 percent in August, accounting for over one third of the monthly all items increase.” Core CPI was 2.4 percent year over year. [BLS CPI release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm) Warsh said, “We cannot affect any individual price,” but the Fed can try to stop second- and third-order effects. [Transcript](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/fomc-press-conference-2026-09-16.txt:186)
   - **Correct account:** Inflation was not simply a broad demand problem. Energy was a large direct contributor, while underlying inflation still remained above target. The hike was intended to prevent the supply shock from spreading into expectations and other prices, not to reduce oil supply costs directly. This is the central concept needed to understand the decision.

4. **Major | Consequences for households and businesses**

   - **Page bytes:** The records list identifies “the decision’s reported effects on borrowers and savers,” but those effects do not appear in the story account. [Built page](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/dist/events/fed-rate/2026-09-16-unanimous-quarter-point-hike/index.html:1)
   - **Record bytes:** “The hike will likely make it even costlier to borrow for homes, autos and other purchases,” while savers will “earn a bit more interest.” [PBS/AP pin](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/coverage/pbs-consumer-effects-2026-09-16.txt:3)
   - **Correct account:** Credit-card and other variable borrowing rates can respond quickly, deposit yields may rise, and auto financing can become dearer. Mortgage rates are influenced more directly by Treasury yields. One quarter-point move is modest, but repeated increases can become material. Without this, the story does not answer why the decision matters to a stranger.

### Major 2: Projection distribution omitted

- **Claim or omission:** The page supplies only the 4.1 percent median.
- **Account bytes:** “The median year-end rate at 4.1 percent in 2026 and 2027.”
- **Broader record:** Twelve participants projected one additional 2026 hike and four projected two. [The Hill pin](/Volumes/4/GitHub/the-catch-site-wt-fed/data/sources/fed-rate/coverage/the-hill-decision-2026-09-16.txt:8)

### Major 3: Inflation mechanism omitted

- **Claim or omission:** The page does not distinguish direct energy inflation from broader persistence.
- **Account bytes:** “Today’s policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal.”
- **Broader record:** Gasoline accounted for over one third of August’s monthly CPI rise, while core CPI was 2.4 percent. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm)
- **Why it matters:** It prevents the mistaken inference that rate policy can directly repair an oil-supply shock.
- **Recommended addition:** Explain the supply-shock and pass-through distinction, including the risk that the shock could fade before lagged tightening takes effect.

### Major 4: Reader consequences omitted

## 7. Source and Corroboration Problems

The 19 listed records do not represent 19 independent confirmations.

- The statement, implementation note, SEP release, SEP PDF, transcript, video, calendar, and longer-run-goals document are eight Federal Reserve artifacts. They establish different parts of the record but remain one institutional source family.
- The two FRED series and their metadata pages reduce to two underlying data lineages. CSV and metadata copies do not corroborate each other independently.
- The official video and transcript are two renderings of the same press event. Frame-by-frame review found no separate on-screen source beyond the SEP charts.
- The SEP release and full SEP are one projection lineage.
- The PBS record is an Associated Press story, not an independent PBS reporting lineage.
- ABC, CBS, Guardian, The Hill, AP, and CNBC repeatedly confirm the decision, but that fact ultimately originates with the FOMC.
- BLS, BEA, CBO, EIA, and the New York Fed provide genuinely separate primary lineages for the economic and operational context.
- The consumer-effects account has one principal editorial lineage, AP. The political claim originates in Trump’s post and is repeated by several outlets.

The larger source problem is utilization: the page collected consumer, political, and post-decision interpretation records but left their material content outside the reader-facing account.

## 8. Reconstructed Story

**Established:** On September 16, 2026, the FOMC unanimously raised its target range by 25 basis points to 3.75 to 4.00 percent, effective September 17. Officials judged that economic activity and financial conditions could withstand less accommodation while inflation remained too high.

**Established:** The immediate inflation picture combined a large energy contribution with still-elevated underlying inflation. The Fed could not lower oil prices directly. Its stated purpose was to prevent the shock from spreading into broader prices and expectations.

**Established:** The projections moved materially upward. Sixteen of 18 participants expected at least one additional 2026 increase, but the projections were conditional and Warsh supplied no projection of his own.

**Strongly supported:** The economic picture was mixed but resilient. Headline second-quarter GDP slowed, while private domestic demand, employment, investment, and credit conditions supported the Fed’s stronger assessment.

**Strongly supported:** The increase can raise variable borrowing costs and deposit yields. A single move is modest, while a sequence would have more substantial household and business effects.

**Disputed:** Analysts disagreed about whether the move began an open-ended tightening cycle or merely reversed earlier insurance cuts. The durability of the geopolitical energy shock also remained disputed.

**Unknown:** The timing and number of later increases, the future oil path, the effect on inflation expectations, and the ultimate household burden were not settled on September 19.

## 9. Remaining Unknowns

- The September meeting minutes had not yet been published. The official schedule says regular-meeting minutes arrive three weeks after the decision.
- August PCE data and BEA’s annual national-account revisions were scheduled for September 30.
- The oil and gasoline path could still materially alter both inflation and the appropriate policy response.
- The SEP cannot establish the actual future rate path because it is conditional on incoming data and each participant’s policy assumptions.
- It remains unknown whether one increase will materially slow demand or whether several increases will follow.
- Political pressure and the approach of the midterm election may affect public interpretation, but no accessible evidence establishes that they determined the committee’s vote.

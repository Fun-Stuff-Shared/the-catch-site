# Fuel reserves: 2026-10-02 G7 release

## Entering

A stranger arrives from the headline "G-7 Agrees to Release 100 Million Barrels of Diesel and Crude," with 100 million barrels as the number they half remember and a US threat to ban diesel exports somewhere in that day's coverage. The president's post makes the easy reading concrete: Europe has agreed to release a massive amount of diesel and the process begins immediately. What was already on the table was March's commitment to release up to 400 million barrels, a US exchange of up to 40 million barrels still out for bids, and a president who on September 30 was still saying he was thinking about a ban.

## Exiting

1. What happened: On October 2, 2026, G7 leaders agreed to carry out commitments, counting those already fulfilled, with a coordinated IEA release of 100 million barrels over four months beginning immediately, including a substantial diesel release in the first 20 days with no diesel number; the French readout's ceiling is up to 100 million barrels within four months, and the readout groups refined products, diesel, and crude oil together; the same statement coordinates refinery maintenance and pledges no energy-export restrictions among members. (`govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02`; `elysee-g7-readout-2026-10-02`)
2. Why it matters to someone who does not follow this subject: US retail diesel was $6.382 a gallon on September 28, up from $3.897 on March 2, and the Treasury secretary said farmers, truckers and businesses were carrying a global diesel shortage. (`gasdesw-2026-10-03`; `bessent-post-2026-10-01`)
3. The easiest wrong reading, the one the headline or the coverage invites: That Europe had just agreed to a new, counted, immediate release of 100 million barrels of diesel on top of March, or to the 100 million barrels of diesel inside 20 days that sources said Washington had demanded. (`trumpstruth-post-2026-10-02`; `reuters-eu-proposal-2026-10-02`; `govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02`)
4. The one concept a stranger must hold to follow it, the mechanism: A barrel released as crude is not diesel at the pump. March's program was largely crude, with Europe's share mainly refined products, and the October text adds refinery coordination because diesel is the tight fuel. (`iea-contributions-2026-03-19`; `govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02`)
5. What changed: Before October 2 the standing act was March's release, announced as 400 million barrels of which the US share was 172 million and more than 133 million had already been awarded, with several European countries described as having released only a fraction and a further US exchange of up to 40 million still open; on October 2 members pledged not to restrict energy exports to each other, after the president had still been thinking about a diesel-export ban on September 30, and he then said he would not authorize one. March was the sixth collective action of this kind, after 1991, 2005, 2011 and twice in 2022. (`doe-spr-release-2026-03-11`; `doe-spr-exchange-2026-09-29`; `reuters-no-export-ban-2026-10-02`; `iea-contributions-2026-03-19`)
6. What is unresolved as of the newest dated record: As of October 5, Japan's chief cabinet secretary has said Japan has been releasing oil and has no plans for a further release at this stage. The October 2 documents still do not say how the 100 million splits between diesel and crude or among the other countries, how much of it is beyond the March commitment, or that any October barrel has been delivered. The weekly retail diesel series stops on September 28. October 2 Brent and WTI settlements are a crude print and do not move that series. (`govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02`; `gasdesw-2026-10-03`; `reuters-japan-no-release-2026-10-05`)
7. The next five questions a curious stranger asks, in order: What did the G7 agree to release, and what did the text not count?; Is the 100 million new oil, or part of the March commitment?; Why name diesel if crude is a different barrel?; What happened to the US threat to ban diesel exports?; What is still open, and what is only scheduled? (`govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02`)

The overlength motion says counsel were retained "only yesterday, November 18, 2026," and that the stay motion "must be filed on November 19, 2026 in order to be timely heard."

Earthjustice PDF, which matches the copy I text-extracted: https://earthjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026-11-19-emergency-motion.pdf (linked from https://earthjustice.org/document/emergency-motion-to-halt-planned-detonation-at-ammunition-plant-in-iowa). The petition PDF I opened is the CBS2 Iowa copy: https://cbs2iowa.com/resources/pdf/ac0e535a-b1b6-4c91-aea4-2c049e2226bf-20261119IowaPetitionforReview.pdf

The filing does not say EPA failed to consider a contained detonation chamber. I searched the motion text for that phrase and did not find it. What it does say EPA failed to require, in the motion's own words:

- "Chemical Neutralization (Non-Detonation)," which the Army's EA "eliminated from further consideration 'due to unacceptable risk to human safety.'" The motion says the Army gave "no supporting data" and did not explain "why no mitigative measures can be taken." It points to EPA's January 3, 2025 OB/OD alternatives compendium as "listing MuniRem as a treatment technology that has been implemented for lead azide," and to a National Academies description of MuniRem as "sulfur reduction chemistry" that "has been demonstrated to…dissolve and neutralize explosive residues and propellants."
- "mobile treatment units as an alternative to open detonation," a class the motion says "the Army did not consider…at all."
- The EA, the motion says, "looked only at" mitigated open detonation, no action, chemical neutralization, "transport to the plant's on-site open detonation unit, and unmitigated controlled detonation."

Relief requested, from the motion: a ruling "by 10:00 AM Central" on November 20, 2026, "one hour prior to the time of scheduled open detonation," or else "an administrative stay of the emergency permit until such time as this motion can be fully heard."

Lead: Reuters wrote that EPA did not immediately respond to a request for comment. I did not open an EPA or Army court response, and I did not find one.

## 3. Environmental Assessment and mitigated FONSI

EA (70 pages), posted by the Army as the November 18 assessment: https://d34w7g4gy10iej.cloudfront.net/pubs/pdf_78364.pdf

Mitigated FONSI, DVIDS issue dated November 18, 2026, "Middleton, Iowa" on the cover: https://d34w7g4gy10iej.cloudfront.net/pubs/pdf_78363.pdf and https://www.dvidshub.net/publication/issues/78363

Document number in the footer: IAAAP-EA-2026-01. The FONSI signature block is for Daniel V. Nosse, Lieutenant Colonel, commander. The signature and date lines on the copy I opened are blank.

Alternatives in the FONSI, section 4, which I read:

- Selected: "Alternative 1: Mitigated Controlled Detonation."
- "No Action," carried as the baseline. Rejected as the choice because it "allows the explosion hazard to escalate, guarantees persistent RCRA violation, and risks catastrophic accidental detonation."
- "Chemical Neutralization," eliminated: "adding caustic chemical reagents to dissolve and decompose the azide over 7 to 22 operational days" would require "opening drums and physically agitating shock-sensitive crystals, creating an extreme and unacceptable risk of fatal accidental detonation."
- "Transport to On-Site Open Detonation (OD) Unit," eliminated because "loading, transporting, and burying degraded barrels introduces severe shock and friction risks."
- "Unmitigated Controlled Detonation," eliminated for "unacceptable releases of airborne lead and heavy metal dispersal that would violate RCRA and Clean Air Act thresholds."

Air concentrations: the EA does not give an offsite lead number and does not compare the plume to the lead NAAQS of 0.15 µg/m³. Section 3.3.4 says preliminary modeling by the Interagency Modeling and Atmospheric Assessment Center predicts that "airborne concentrations of pollutants could exceed the Protective Action Criteria (PAC)-1 level" and "will not reach" PAC-2 or PAC-3. It says the event "will not alter Des Moines County's long-term NAAQS attainment status." The FONSI says the same PAC comparison and that "Long-term regional attainment of National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) will not be impacted." The only NAAQS-violation sentence I found is about an accidental blast under the no-action case, which "potentially violat[es] NAAQS standards."

Soil: I found no deposition estimate in mass per area. The FONSI cleanup goal is "the EPA Regional Screening Level (RSL) for industrial soil of 800mg/kg for lead." Sampling is "multi-incremental soil sampling across 44 Decision Units extending up to 3,000 feet." Waste "exceeding 5.0mg leachable lead" is classified D008.

Lead, not in the EA I opened: a Des Moines Register story says the Army map marks where levels "may exceed 0.18 micrograms per cubic meter," and quotes Justine Barati comparing that with "84,000 to 420,000 micrograms of lead per cubic meter" as typical lead in soil. I did not open that article, so I am not treating those figures as verified.

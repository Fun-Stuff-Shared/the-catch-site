### C9 — Jury-duty exception is broader than the law

**Claim:** “A voter on inactive status who does not answer the notice, update their registration or vote in their county through the second federal general election after the notice is moved to purged status, unless they are in the military or have served or been summoned for jury duty.”

**Record:** `hb1310-2023`, Section 23-15-152(4):

> “A registered voter who votes at least once in any election in the registered voter's county or municipality of registration during the period beginning from the date of the delivery of the confirmation notice provided in this subsection (4) or who is active or reserve military or who serves on jury duty or responds to a summons for jury duty shall not be purged from the Statewide Elections Management System.” 

**Finding:** The statute protects someone who serves on jury duty or **responds to a summons**, not necessarily anyone who merely receives a summons. The claim also uses “have served,” without limiting when that service occurred.

**Wrong reader takeaway:** Receiving a jury summons alone, or having served on a jury at any point in the past, automatically protects a voter from purging.

### C19 — Statewide ranking attributed to the wrong source

**Claim:** “DeSoto County's commissioners said they moved nearly every voter flagged on the Experian-sourced lists, more than 15,000, to inactive between the primaries and the midterms, more than any other county.”

**Record:** `mt-2026-11-12`:

> “DeSoto moved more voters to inactive status than any of Mississippi’s 82 counties in the eight months between the primaries and the midterms.”

The article continues:

> “During that time, the county’s part-time election commissioners said, they moved to inactive nearly every voter flagged on the address lists that the secretary of state sourced from Experian’s data: more than 15,000 voters.” 

**Finding:** The commissioners are the source for the assertion that they inactivated nearly every flagged voter, totaling more than 15,000. The statewide comparison—more inactivations than any other county—is presented as the publication's finding, not a statement by the commissioners.

**Wrong reader takeaway:** DeSoto's election commissioners themselves made the statewide ranking claim, rather than the comparison coming from the reporting.

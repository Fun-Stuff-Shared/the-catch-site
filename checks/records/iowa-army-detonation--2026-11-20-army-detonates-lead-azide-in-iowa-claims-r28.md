### C1 — [maps-630-areas]

**Claim:** The Army's deposition map updated at 6:30 p.m. Nov. 24 shows lead settling above 100 micrograms per square foot, EPA's maximum for windows, over 30 square miles, and above 5 micrograms per square foot, EPA's maximum for floors, over 146 square miles; it is stamped as of 241727NOV26.

**Record:** `army-maps-update-2026-11-24`

**Exact words:**
> "POTENTIAL EXPOSURE TO TOXIC LEAD DUST"  
> "LEAD SURFACE DEPOSITION"  
> "This model reflects taking deliberate steps (i.e., in a bunker with additional countermeasures) to reduce, contain and control the spread of hazardous dust or particles after a detonation has occurred."  
> "DTG: 25NOV2026 0900 CST"  
> "100 µg/ft2 30 mi2"  
> "5 µg/ft2 146 mi2"  
> "a/o 241727NOV26" 

**Finding:** The record identifies a modeled deposition scenario for a detonation dated November 25 at 9 a.m. CST, not observed lead deposition. The claim's description of lead settling, without establishing that it is projected, risks presenting modeled contamination as an actual event.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That lead had already settled across the specified areas at the stated concentrations, rather than those areas representing potential deposition under the Army's mitigation model.

### C2 — [maps-0300-areas]

**Claim:** The Army's deposition map updated at 3 a.m. Nov. 25, stamped as of 250300NOV26, shows lead settling above 100 micrograms per square foot, EPA's maximum for windows, over 27 square miles, and above 5 micrograms per square foot, EPA's maximum for floors, over 199 square miles.

> "LEAD SURFACE DEPOSITION"

> "DTG: 25NOV2026 0900 CST"

**Finding:** The record establishes modeled surface deposition, not measured contamination. The map's November 25, 9 a.m. CST scenario timestamp is six hours after the stated 3 a.m. update. The claim does not distinguish projected deposition from actual deposition.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the November 25 update documents lead already deposited across 27 and 199 square miles, rather than modeling potential contamination from a future detonation scenario.

### C3 — [maps-0300-hours]

**Claim:** The Army's dispersion map updated at 3 a.m. Nov. 25 shows the plume a half hour, three and five hours after the blast, drawn to the lead air sampling method's detection limit.

**Record:** `army-maps-update-2026-11-25-0300`

**Exact words:**
> "LEAD AIR DISPERSION"  
> "Mitigated 0.26 Dispersion"  
> "This model reflects taking deliberate steps (i.e., in a bunker with additional countermeasures) to reduce, contain and control the spread of hazardous dust or particles after a detonation has occurred."  
> "DTG: 25NOV2026 0900 CST"  
> "Airborne Concentration 0.18µg/m3 – Lead Air Sampling Method Detection Limit"  
> "T + 0.5"  
> "T + 3"  
> "T + 5"  
> "Issued Nov. 25, 2026"  
> "a/o 250300NOV26" records(20261125-093144).md

**Finding:** The record supports the three modeled time intervals and the detection-limit reference. However, the map is a dispersion model with a scenario timestamp of 9 a.m. CST on November 25. The claim's reference to the plume appearing at intervals "after the blast" risks presenting modeled future conditions as observations following an actual detonation.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the blast had already occurred and the Army was mapping its actual airborne lead plume at three elapsed times, rather than depicting projected dispersion at those intervals.

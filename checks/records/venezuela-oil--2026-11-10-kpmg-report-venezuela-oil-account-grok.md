## Stated reasons

- **Trump, January 9 order and fact sheet.** Creditor attachment would threaten the effort to stabilize Venezuela and, with it, U.S. aims on migration, narcotics, Iran, and Hezbollah. Funds stay Venezuelan sovereign property.
- **Rubio, January 28.** Short-term U.S. control so revenue is not seized and so the interim government does not collapse. “We only control the dispersal of the money, we don’t control the actual money.”
- **The report.** Qatar was a stopgap so stored oil could be sold before wells were shut in and before creditors could attach the first payments. OFAC licenses require covered buyers to pay the custody account. Releases are for macroeconomic, public-sector, humanitarian, and operational uses requested by the central bank.
- **Unnamed State official, November 12, via Reuters/EFE.** The process is working, and the money now benefits Venezuelans rather than a “cleptocracia.”
- **Rodríguez, November 12.** Ninety-nine of every hundred oil and mining dollars went to public salaries and pensions.
- **Democratic senators, September 16.** They do not offer a benign purpose. Their stated concern is control without a congressional appropriation, possible steering to favored firms, and money-laundering risk after a six-month AML enforcement pause.

## Claims to check

- **“The U.S. took $12 billion.”** The report says the opposite on title: sovereign Venezuelan property, 98.7 percent disbursed. Trump’s November 2 and November 11 lines (“we take hundreds of billions,” “we paid for the cost of the war many, many times over”) are not what the account shows. A Pentagon-cited Iran-war cost of about $47 billion, reported elsewhere, is larger than this account. I did not verify that war figure.
- **“$11.7 billion to currency auctions and $130 million to foreign bills.”** That is the Times’s November 10 reading, repeated widely. The public ledger is about **$11.96 billion** through three FX channels and **$133.1 million** in vendor payments, which include KPMG, Greenberg Traurig, dialysis supplies, PAHO, and banknote logistics, not only foreign bills.
- **“KPMG audited the account.”** Rubio told Congress it was audited by KPMG. The report’s own stakeholder table says the services are not an audit or attestation. The senators said the contract was **$84 million**. The vendor table shows **$18.2 million** paid on April 10. I did not find the contract.
- **“99 percent went to salaries.”** Rodríguez’s January–November budget series. The U.S. report traces dollars to banks and then to firms and individuals. It does not show a $14.7 billion payroll. The report says bolívares collected when banks sell the dollars can fund government spending. Those are different units.
- **“Almost nothing reached people.”** Direct vendor payments are about 1 percent. Most dollars were sold through banks. The report’s sample says 23 percent of downstream dollars went to individuals. It does not identify them.
- **“$18.8 billion exported, so $6.5 billion is missing.”** De La Cruz’s own thread says the $18.8 billion is a reference-price estimate, not invoices, and that Chevron and NABEP use other structures. The gap is not, on his own terms, a proven loss.
- **“Trafigura and Vitol were 61 percent.”** Consistent with the two redacted totals. Names are not in the public PDF. Source is the Times and Bloomberg.
- **Democrats now control both chambers.** Reported by bne IntelliNews and others. I did not verify the election results.
- **Report dated October 29 and sent to Congress in early November.** Reuters and El Informador. The PDF I read has no date line on the pages I opened. File metadata says created November 12.

## Competing explanations

- **Custodianship that is working.** Held by the unnamed State official and the report’s own language. The money stayed Venezuela’s, creditors were blocked, and nearly all of it was released, mostly to support the bolívar, with sanctions and AML checks. Strongest short form is the EFE line: a kleptocracy used to loot the oil, and within a year the revenue is reaching Venezuelans.
- **Temporary stabilization under U.S. instructions.** Rubio, January 28. Washington does not own the money. It decides disbursement so payroll, medicine, and basic government do not collapse during a transition. He called it an interim step.
- **Spoils and reimbursement.** Trump, November 11, and the January 4 comments. The oil pays the United States back and will reach “hundreds of billions.” “To the victor belong the spoils.”
- **Unappropriated control and corruption risk.** Wyden, Warren, and Whitehouse. Ownership stays Venezuelan on paper, but State can steer billions by coercing requests or paying favored firms, with no public audit and a six-month pause in AML enforcement. Murphy’s version is blunter: the oil is being taken at gunpoint and administered for 30 million people without a legitimate mandate.
- **A bargain that hides the last recipient.** De La Cruz. Trump designed the tap. Rodríguez accepted it. Washington decides when money moves. Caracas does not have to show who bought the cheap dollars. The country that needs power and hospitals is not at the table.
- **Preferential-dollar machine.** Ríos and Primero Justicia. More than 90 percent became FX allocations. In Venezuela’s system, one allocation at a preferential rate can mint a fortune. The government’s salary story does not appear in the U.S. ledger, and the U.S. banking story does not appear on Transparencia Soberana.
- **Gross-versus-net, not theft.** Velásquez, and one clause of De La Cruz’s thread. Export value at a reference price is not cash deposited after freight, diluent, discounts, and contractor take. The unpublished invoices are the missing record.
- **Parallel channels.** Times, as summarized by OregonLive and Es Con Usted. Chevron and North American Blue Energy Partners market some production outside this account, so $12.26 billion is not all of Venezuela’s oil income.
- **Occupation or abduction.** Al Jazeera’s November 12 story calls the January operation an abduction and says rights activists fear oil will crowd out democratic reform. I did not find a named activist’s quote on the report.
- **Alliance, not coercion.** @ipoolgian. The January 9 decree shielded the money for the Venezuelan state, PDVSA supplied the buyer list, and Washington returned 98.7 percent while leaving the interim government in place with no election date he accepts.

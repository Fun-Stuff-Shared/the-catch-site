## Claims to check

* **"Vance and Addison met on December 27 and discussed a post-capture sale."** Reuters's only positive source is one person who says Addison described the meeting. Addison and Vance's office deny it. Flight records show his Embraer and the vice-presidential plane at Bozeman the same day. Co-location is not a meeting. Addison's denial is specifically that he never told anyone a meeting happened.
* **"Vance set up the Venezuela contract."** What an official confirmed is a conversation at least a year earlier in which Vance called Addison trustworthy. DOE says the two never discussed Venezuelan oil sales. Those can both be true. They do not show Vance ordered the January 5 offer.
* **"The raid's real motive was to hand Vitol and Trafigura a monopoly."** That is the reading in the Molotov post and in Levine's "grift" line. Reuters does not say that. Its source describes a hypothetical question a week before the operation. Both companies told Reuters they had no advance knowledge.
* **"They still hold an exclusive, no-bid monopoly."** They received the first special licenses without a public tender. OFAC general licenses from February opened lifting and sale to established U.S. entities. Reuters reported in March that a general license covered any interested exporter. The KPMG report still has the two firms at 61 percent of custody-account deposits through September, and the Globe says the number of buyers rose while the two "continued to dominate." Dominance is not a legal monopoly.
* **"61 percent of Venezuela's oil money."** The 61 percent is their share of deposits into this custody account, not of all export revenue. Chevron and NABEP are outside it.
* **"The firms earned hundreds of millions."** That is Seng's per-barrel estimate times a national export total, not a company account. Vitol's reported company-wide margin is lower than Seng's 3.5 to 4 percent. The barrels are not all theirs.
* **"Addison gave $6 million" and "Addison gave $7 million."** Both are in circulation. Reuters says $7 million from Addison and his wife in 2024, of which $5 million went to MAGA, plus $300,000 to a Vance PAC in 2022. I did not check FEC.
* **"The operation killed up to 100 people."** That clause is in the Reuters lead. The text I read does not attach it to a named official tally. Trump and Vance said no Americans died.
* **Traders Union's headline treatment.** It says flight records and interviews "show" the meeting. Reuters presents the meeting as alleged and denied.

## Competing explanations

Each is the strongest form I found its holders actually giving.

* **Access, not expertise, chose the counterparty.** Held by Garcia, Levin, and the Reuters frame. Addison was a U.S. gasoline trader, not the regional chief, and Vitol itself told Reuters he could not negotiate the crude contract. He had given $300,000 to a Vance PAC and, with his wife, $7 million in 2024. A person who heard his account says that a week before the raid he told Vance that Vitol could sell the crude fastest. Wright called him two days after the raid. By January 9 the licenses existed. In March he left Vitol. By July he chaired Wright's energy-policy advisory board. Garcia's version adds the possibility that insiders had warning of the military action.
* **The meeting did not happen, and the call was ordinary.** Held by Addison and, on the meeting, by Vance's office. Addison's strongest line is that anyone who says he described such a meeting is inventing it. DOE's strongest line is that Vitol was picked because it is the largest trader, and that Vance and Wright never discussed Venezuelan oil sales. The planes at one airport, and homes near the same club, explain proximity without a pre-raid commercial meeting.
* **The traders were the only people who could move quarantined barrels before storage shut in production.** Held by Rubio and Wright in January. Sanctions were finally being enforced, oil was stuck on ships and onshore, China had been buying at a steep discount, and trading houses that had marketed Venezuelan crude before 2019 could put tankers on berth in days. Rubio called it a short-term fix. Holtum said a cargo would load within a week. Later general licenses are what this account predicts if the duopoly was a bridge, not the design.
* **This is custody of Venezuelan money, returned at once, so it cannot be stolen or attached.** Held by Rubio and by Wright's "we're not taking their oil." The KPMG figures are the evidence they would use: $12.26 billion in, about $11.7 billion back out for the currency auctions, $170 million left on September 30, every transfer signed by State and Treasury. Rubio's addition is that a U.S. bank account would have been seized by creditors of the old expropriations, so Qatar was a shield, not a slush fund.
* **It is an indefinite American seizure.** Held by Murphy, by Public Citizen, and by critics Wright answered in Caracas. The strongest form: the oil was taken at gunpoint, sold by U.S.-chosen traders, parked first in Qatar, and spent only with U.S. permission, while the Constitution of Venezuela says the resource belongs to Venezuelans. Trump's own January 9 lines, "we're taking back what was taken from us" and "you're not dealing with Venezuela at all," are what this account treats as the admission.
* **The point is hemisphere strategy, and the traders are incidental.** Held by Trump, Vance, and Rubio on January 9. Drugs, Cuban forces, Iranian ties, and Chinese purchases are the reason for the raid. Oil revenue is the lever that lets Washington do it without an occupation and without taxpayer money. Wright on January 11 said more oil is not a favor to American producers, because it pushes prices down.
* **The firms were chosen despite their records, which is the scandal.** Held by Public Citizen and by the January 29 reporting on the licenses. Vitol and Trafigura paid large FCPA and manipulation penalties, the Justice Department's foreign-bribery unit had been cut back, and the licenses still went out confidentially, covering more than oil and running into 2027, with payments into the Qatar account. This explains why critics think the selection was reckless. It does not, by itself, explain why Addison was the person Wright called.
* **A second, quieter deal is the real transfer of the resource.** Held in the later NABEP fight by Meeks and Garcia. North American Blue Energy Partners, owned by Betancourt, gave the United States 35 percent of its parent and the right to buy 20 percent of output at cost, covering on the order of a fifth of reserves. Vitol has a commercial relationship with NABEP and no long-term contract. On this account the January trader licenses were the offtake mechanism, and the ownership change is the NABEP contract.

### 1. The reason Congress interviewed the defendants is incompletely traced.

On June 7, 2023, House Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan specifically sought Benavides's testimony over allegations that the FBI's August 2020 defensive briefings of Senators Chuck Grassley and Ron Johnson, followed by leaks, had undermined their investigation of Hunter Biden's foreign financial dealings. The senators alleged that the briefings enabled their investigation to be portrayed as Russian disinformation.

The prosecution's own affidavit establishes another important dimension: Benavides's September 2023 interview was conducted as part of the committee's investigation into alleged government pressure on social media companies to censor speech.

**Why it changes the story:** The prosecution concerns false statements allegedly made during congressional investigations of suspected FBI misconduct, including the treatment of information about Hunter Biden and the 2020 election. The laptop was not the entire subject of the charges, but it was central to the controversy that prompted congressional scrutiny. The brief's distinction between the charges and the laptop controversy is legally sound but historically incomplete.

### 2. The government's affidavit contains stronger evidence for the legitimate-coordination explanation than the brief acknowledges.

Paragraph 10 describes an August 14, 2020, meeting attended by senior Baltimore FBI officials, case agents and three federal prosecutors from Delaware. Floris and Benavides presented classified information. According to the affidavit, the investigative team raised no objection to the proposed defensive briefings and asked the task force to conduct additional intelligence checks on Hunter Biden and his companies.

**Why it changes the story:** The investigators and prosecutors responsible for the underlying criminal case were not merely aware of task force involvement; they requested further assistance. That substantially strengthens the alternative that the defendants regarded their activities as authorized intelligence coordination rather than participation in the criminal investigation itself.

It does not resolve the allegations that they denied conversations or knowledge they actually possessed. But it changes the evidentiary significance of the contact prosecutors cite.

Source: [Criminal affidavit, paragraphs 8–11](https://www.justice.gov/usao-dc/media/1468056/dl?inline=). [Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-dc/media/1468056/dl?inline=)

### 3. The judge questioned a required element of the offense on October 23.

At the defendants' initial appearance, Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui questioned whether their alleged false statements materially affected the congressional investigation.

The record includes the government's materiality theory and a congressional staffer's assertion that the answers impeded investigative follow-up. It includes the judge's concerns about the case's age and the unusual use of a complaint, but not his separate concern about materiality.

**Why it changes the story:** Materiality is an element prosecutors must establish under 18 U.S.C. § 1001. The court's questions therefore reached the substance of the charges, not merely their timing or procedural handling.

The same courtroom report confirms both defendants were released after their initial appearance. The record currently treats their release status as unknown; the specific conditions remain unverified.

### 4. The task force's original purpose and an independent investigation of its activities are missing from the lineage.

The Justice Department inspector general's July 2024 report establishes that the FBI created the Foreign Influence Task Force in **2017**, specifically in response to Russian interference in the **2016 presidential election**. The program expanded after the 2018 midterms to address other foreign governments.

The inspector general found effective coordination among the relevant Justice Department components. It also found that the FBI and DOJ lacked specific guidance governing information-sharing with social media companies until February 2024, creating potential First Amendment risks.

The evaluation did **not** resolve allegations involved in the separate litigation over government pressure on social media platforms.

**Why it changes the story:** These findings establish the task force's original national-security rationale while identifying a documented institutional problem with its operations. Both matter when evaluating later claims that its work was politically weaponized. The affidavit's description of oversight and deconfliction does not substitute for these independent findings.

Source: [DOJ inspector general report 24-080, July 2024](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/reports/24-080.pdf). [DOJ Inspector General](https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/reports/24-080.pdf)

### 5. The original documents behind the 2026 Round River allegations are absent.

The record relies on Patel's August 2026 statement and the October affidavit for its explanation of Round River. But the August declassification included underlying ODNI materials.

Those materials contain two consequential details:

- A declassified spreadsheet classified **Grassley, Johnson, Jim Jordan and other political figures as “conduits”** in the reporting being assessed, while categorizing Joe and Hunter Biden as targets.
- An ODNI presentation asserted that Round River had **not corroborated any allegations** associated with the so-called Ukraine Narrative.

**Why it changes the story:** These documents illuminate the dispute underlying the renewed FBI review: how intelligence reporting about the Bidens was categorized, evaluated and potentially used in defensive briefings involving lawmakers.

They also impose an important evidentiary limit. The classification of lawmakers and the description of the project do not, by themselves, establish wrongdoing by Floris or Benavides. Nor does the ODNI assessment establish that the underlying allegations were true.

The distinction between what the declassified documents show and what investigators suspect is central to understanding why the task force came under renewed scrutiny.

### 6. A significant intermediate step in the investigation predates the October 2026 witness interviews.

On **April 10, 2025**, Grassley and Johnson formally demanded that FBI Director Kash Patel provide communications concerning the laptop, specifically naming **Bradley Benavides**, Laura Dehmlow and other FBI personnel.

The request followed the public release of October 2020 FBI chat messages earlier that month. The senators said the records raised questions about the FBI's handling of the laptop.

**Why it changes the story:** The brief's chronology jumps from the 2023 interviews to the 2026 investigation without this documented effort to obtain the same category of communications prosecutors now cite.

It establishes that scrutiny of Benavides's contemporaneous records was specific and public well before the October 2026 interviews. It does not establish when prosecutors obtained particular documents or witness accounts, but it further limits how much explanatory weight the *recency* of the interviews can bear in the matrix's new-evidence hypothesis.

### 7. The election-timing question lacks the governing Justice Department policy.

The brief relies on AP's characterization of Justice Department election-period practice. The primary rule is **Justice Manual § 9-85.500**.

It prohibits prosecutors and agents from selecting the timing of investigative actions, charges or statements **for the purpose of affecting an election or benefiting or disadvantaging a candidate or party**. Actions likely to raise an issue, or the perception of one, under that rule require consultation with the Public Integrity Section.

The rule does **not** establish a categorical prohibition on filing charges within 60 days of an election.

**Why it changes the story:** The significance of the October 23 announcement is not determined solely by its proximity to the November 3 election. The governing questions concern the reason for the timing and the required internal review.

The public record does not establish whether that consultation occurred or what prosecutors' timing rationale was. Consequently, the rule neither establishes nor disproves the defense's allegation of political timing.

Source: [DOJ Justice Manual § 9-85.500](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-85000-protection-government-integrity). [Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-85000-protection-government-integrity)

Next I’ll pull the primary pages and the officials who actually spoke.The one-day loss was a market repricing, not an agency order. The figure closest to a “total U.S. market” loss is about **$3 trillion in S&P 500 company market value** on Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026. That matches the number President Trump later used. It is not an official government count, and other indexes print different dollar totals that should not be added together.

**What the tape shows.** Wednesday, Nov. 4, the indexes rose: the Dow by 1,569 points, the Nasdaq by 1.44 percent, the S&P 500 by 1.6 percent to 7,688.62. Chip and data-center stocks were already falling under that rally. Thursday they led a broad selloff. CNN’s page, which I opened, gives the Nasdaq Composite **−5.52 percent**, in a correction (more than 10 percent under its Oct. 6 high), the Dow **−1,833 points**, Nvidia **−9.3 percent**, a chip index **−12.3 percent**, Tesla **−11 percent**, SpaceX **−9 percent**, the Nasdaq 100 **−6.36 percent**, CoreWeave about **−10 percent**, and Nebius about **−15 percent**. Morningstar’s U.S. total-market index fell **4.35 percent**. No Wilshire 5000 dollar figure turned up.

**What moved it.** The selling was concentrated in chips, neoclouds, and data-center operators. The reason given that day, by Reuters, CNN, NPR, and the Associated Press, was fear that Democratic wins would slow the AI data-center buildout: a Texas governor-elect who had promised a moratorium, local ballot limits, and a fresh call for a national moratorium. Qualcomm’s weak outlook and an AI trade that was already under strain (CoreWeave credit spreads, a chip index already about 30 percent off its high) were in the same session. No federal rule changed on Nov. 5. Democrats do not take Congress until Jan. 3, 2027. Gina Hinojosa does not take office until Jan. 19.

## Officials who acted

Nobody at the Fed, Treasury, SEC, or the exchanges ordered this decline. The official acts markets were pricing were earlier, and mostly state and local.

- **Greg Abbott, then Governor of Texas (Republican).** Starting in August 2026 he paused new data-center grid connections for an audit expected to run into December, and he paused Texas Commission on Environmental Quality air permits. He did not issue Hinojosa’s formal moratorium. The Texas Tribune, which I opened, says he had called Texas the “epicenter of AI development” in November 2025, then reversed course. [Texas Tribune, Nov. 4](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/04/data-center-texas-pause-gina-hinojosa-governor-policies/).
- **Gina Hinojosa, Texas governor-elect (Democrat).** She has not taken office and, in the coverage through Nov. 15, had not issued a moratorium. Her campaign pledge is what traded. Opened, Texas Tribune, quoting her Oct. 22 video on X (I did not find the status URL): “When I am governor I will issue a moratorium — a ban — on all data center approvals until laws can be passed by the Legislature to protect Texans.” She also said the pause should last until lawmakers pass “real protections so Texans can have a seat at the table to decide if and how data centers come into their communities.” USA Today, which I could not open, reported her telling CNN on Nov. 5: “What has happened is data centers have moved into mostly rural communities, and there are no rules and laws to protect Texans and Texans are being taken advantage of,” and “we need to stop what we are doing with data centers until we have those protections.” [Search snippet of that USA Today story](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/05/texas-is-done-with-data-centers-until-further-notice-new-governor-says/92634485007/).
- **Local voters, Nov. 3.** Ballotpedia’s Nov. 6 write-up, which I did not open in full but whose text is in the search result: voters favored limiting data-center development in **40 of 41** local measures that day. The exception was Harwood, North Dakota, which rejected extra review. Approved items included five full bans, twelve partial bans, and three six-month moratoriums in Maine. Some measures were advisory. [Ballotpedia News, Nov. 6](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/11/06/voters-favored-limiting-data-center-development-in-40-of-41-local-ballot-measures-on-nov-3/). A later Ballotpedia page, fetched only as a snippet, said that as of Nov. 14 voters had decided **53** measures in 2026 and favored limits in **52**, and that **46 of 47** Nov. 3 contests went that way. The count grew as more results were included. [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Data_center-related_ballot_measures,_2026).
- **Joan Huffman, Republican Texas state senator, chair of Senate Finance, and 23 of 31 senators as joint authors.** On Nov. 9, the first day of filing for the session that starts Jan. 12, she filed SB 293 to repeal the data-center sales-tax exemptions. I opened the press release: “Texas should welcome investment. It should not write a blank check.” “When this exemption was created in 2013, the cost to taxpayers was about $14.6 million in the first biennium. The Comptroller now estimates forgone sales tax revenue in the range of $3.3 billion for the coming biennium.” “No tax exemption should run on autopilot.” Existing exemptions would expire on the effective date. [Senate press release, PDF](https://www.senate.texas.gov/members/d17/press/en/p20261109a.pdf).
- **Donald Trump, President.** He did not issue a market order. He supplied the political explanation. See Elected officials. I did not open Truth Social.

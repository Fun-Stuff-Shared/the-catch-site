**C1 — [inactive-why] — Scope mismatch (checked strictly)**

**Claim:** “Under Mississippi law, a voter becomes inactive when county election commissioners mail the voter a confirmation notice asking them to confirm their current address. The law requires a notice when records indicate the voter has moved (Postal Service change-of-address data, registration in another state, or other reliable information) or when the voter has not voted or updated their registration over a full presidential cycle.”

**Record:** `hb1310-2023`, Section 23-15-152(2):

> “No registered voter shall be sent a confirmation notice under paragraph (c) of this subsection if he or she has been sent a confirmation notice for those same reasons within the last six (6) years.”

Section 23-15-152(6) also states:

> “No systematic list maintenance shall occur during the ninety (90) days immediately preceding a federal primary or general election which is limited to moving a voter to inactive status in subsection (2) of this section or purged status in subsection (5) of this section.” records(20261120-053758)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Mississippi law unconditionally requires a confirmation notice whenever a voter has failed to vote or update their registration over the specified presidential-election period. The law explicitly prohibits sending another notice for that reason if one was sent within the previous six years. It also restricts systematic changes to inactive status during the 90 days preceding a federal primary or general election.

The listed reasons for sending notices and the relationship between mailing a notice and inactive status are otherwise supported. The short form does not introduce an additional error.

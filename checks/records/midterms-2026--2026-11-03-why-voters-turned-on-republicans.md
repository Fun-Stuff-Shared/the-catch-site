## The explanations

Question 1: why did voters turn against Republicans? Each answer as its camp puts it, what should turn up if it is true, and what should not.

- A. The Iran war (JD Vance: "The reason we lost is the war." [vance-x-post-2026-11-04: "The reason we lost is the war."]). If true: the war ranks at or near the top of what voters called crucial, and war opponents vote Democratic. Should not find: the war well behind other factors.
- B. Gas and prices (Trump: "I don't think it's the war, Bret, I think it's the gasoline price" [fox-trump-election-night: "I don't think it's the war, Bret, I think it's the gasoline price"]; Ted Cruz: "Food is too high, gas is too high" [cruz-x-post-2026-11-04: "Food is too high, gas is too high, diesel is too high, insurance is too high, life is too high."]). If true: cost of living rated crucial or major by most voters; voters hurt by inflation vote Democratic. Should not find: price-hurt voters split evenly.
- C. Tariffs (Cruz: "Tariffs have made everything too expensive" [cruz-x-post-2026-11-04: "Tariffs have made everything too expensive, and the Iran war has dragged on too long."]; Phillip Magness: "the war is not what caused the blue wave" [dispatch-magness-tariffs: "But the war is not what caused the blue wave."]; against: Peter Navarro, tariffs "have played no role" [washsun-navarro-moore: "have played no role in the inflation and affordability issue"]). If true: most voters say tariffs hurt. Should not find: voters crediting tariffs or blaming prices on outside factors.
- D. Affordability and corruption (Hakeem Jeffries: "We ran on affordability. We won on affordability." [aljazeera-jeffries: "We ran on affordability. We won on affordability."]; Chuck Schumer: "costs, chaos, and corruption" [schumer-floor-2026-11-09: "concerns about costs, chaos, and corruption"]). If true: corruption and cost of living rated crucial by many; Democrats preferred on them. Should not find: no Democratic edge on those issues.
- E. A referendum on Trump (CBS: "This election was clearly a referendum on Mr. Trump" [cbs-exit-poll-final: "This election was clearly a referendum on Mr. Trump and largely a rejection of his policies."]; against, Trump: "I wasn't on the ballot" [fox-trump-election-night: "And I wasn't on the ballot. If I was on the ballot, they would have gotten out,"]). If true: many vote to oppose him; approval tracks the House vote. Should not find: Trump not a factor for most voters.
- F. Weak candidates (Trump: "They didn't like the candidates" [fox-trump-election-night: "They didn't like the candidates and some of the candidates that I endorsed fought me"]). If true: voters doubted the losing Republicans' character or experience. Should not find: the same swing in races with well-regarded Republicans, or in the national House vote.
- G. Turnout versus switching. Stay-home camp: Brookings, "Discontented Republicans didn't abandon their party in 2026; they stayed home" [brookings-how-democrats-won: "Discontented Republicans didn’t abandon their party in 2026; they stayed home"]; Trump, "If I was on the ballot, they would have gotten out" [fox-trump-election-night: "If I was on the ballot, they would have gotten out,"]. Switching camp: Vance, "the main problem is not turnout" [vance-x-post-2026-11-04: "But the main problem is not turnout. The main problem is that many people who voted for Trump/Vance in 2024 voted for Democrats in 2026."]. If switching: a real share of 2024 Trump voters votes Democratic; should not find only a few percent. If staying home: the electorate is less Republican than 2024's; should not find an electorate as Republican as 2024's.
- H. Redistricting and the voting-rights ruling (USA Today: "exit polling showing the Supreme Court's voting rights decision, GOP redistricting and the economy were major factors" [usatoday-black-voters: "with exit polling showing the Supreme Court’s voting rights decision, GOP redistricting and the economy were major factors."]). If true: the swing concentrated where lines were redrawn. Should not find: the same swing in statewide races, which have no districts.
- I. The usual midterm swing (the Post: midterms "almost always" swing against the president's party [wapo-voting-groups: "Midterm turnout is lower than in presidential election years and almost always swings against the party of the sitting president."]). If true: a loss within the historical range for a president this unpopular. Should not find: a loss far beyond what history shows at that approval.
- J. Democracy and overreach (Schumer's "chaos"; voters naming the state of democracy). If true: democracy a top issue and its voters break Democratic, including some 2024 Trump voters. Should not find: its voters already Democrats.
- K. Immigration enforcement "gone too far". If true: majority says too far and votes Democratic; enforcement-focused voters do too. Should not find: Republicans still preferred on immigration.
- L. Jobs and a weakening economy, apart from prices. If true: job worries measured separately and voting Democratic. Should not find: voters calling the economy good.
- M. Health costs (Democrats ran on the expired ACA credits and Medicaid cuts). If true: voters say Trump's policies raised their health costs and favor Democrats on health care.
- N. A rigged or irregular count (Trump, Nov. 9: "Does anybody really believe that this 'Election' was honest" [usatoday-trump-doubts-2026-11-09: "Does anybody really believe that this 'Election' was honest"]). If true: credible irregularities named by officials, court challenges. Should not find: officials reporting no problems and no court wave.
- O. Party loyalty (the largest open-ended answer in YouGov's post-election poll). If true: most votes did not move, and the side whose voters stayed more loyal gained. Should not find: equal loyalty on both sides.
- P. Bad messaging (Trump, Nov. 8: "I think it was bad messaging." [cbs-60-minutes-2026-11-08: "I think it was bad messaging."]). If true: voters unaware of the administration's record, or a poll showing the message, not conditions, moved them. Should not find: nothing a public poll could show, unless one asked about awareness of the record.
- R. Abortion rights (KFF: "abortion motivated Democratic voters to turn out more so than Republicans" in recent elections [kff-abortion-ballot-2026: "This pattern is consistent with recent elections in which abortion motivated Democratic voters to turn out more so than Republicans."]). If true: abortion-rights voters turn out and vote Democratic where measures were on the ballot. Should not find: the measures drawing both sides, or ticket-splitting. Verdict: cannot be tested as a national cause.
- Q. Israel and "Put America First" (two of the four answers Vance says a pollster he cites heard [vance-x-post-2026-11-04: "3) Put America First"] [vance-x-post-2026-11-04: "4) Change our relationship to Israel"]). If true: many voters call U.S. support for Israel too strong and those voters include defecting Trump voters. Should not find: the view held mostly by Democrats already.

Verdicts, one per answer:
- A. Iran war: partly supported. Fourth of five as "crucial" and 2% in voters' own words, but in a non-probability poll 44% named it a top cause of rising costs, and a probability sample put prices ahead of the war's goals. It mostly reached voters through prices.
- B. Gas and prices: supported. Cannot be separated from E: the voters who name prices blame Trump's policies for them.
- C. Tariffs: supported as voters' judgment (66% hurt the national economy). Navarro's "no role" is contradicted by how voters judged them, not by an economic test.
- D. Affordability and corruption: partly supported. The two most-cited crucial factors, but the Democratic edge on them was narrow and 52% viewed the party unfavorably; 62% said Trump puts his own interests first.
- E. Referendum on Trump: supported. 44% voted to oppose him, a plurality; 16% to support him; approval 37%; second in voters' own words. Inseparable from B.
- F. Weak candidates: partly supported. Voters doubted Paxton and Marshall, 7% named candidate qualities, half said Trump's rallies hurt his candidates; the national House vote swung everywhere.
- G. Turnout versus switching: both, leaning toward staying home. In the one post-election survey that counts nonvoters, 6% of Trump's 2024 voters switched; among non-MAGA Republicans 34% did not vote and 8% switched. Self-reports. The Voter Poll and NORC disagree on 2024 nonvoters (4% against about 15%).
- H. Redistricting: not supported as a cause of Democratic gains; most redraws failed and Republicans won two redrawn seats. The mobilization version cannot be tested.
- I. Midterm swing: supported for direction; the size matches losses under the least popular presidents in the table.
- J. Democracy: cannot be tested with what is public. Second-ranked issue, voters D 79-20, but no public table shows how those voters voted in 2024.
- K. Immigration: partly supported. 58% said "too far" and Trump said his policy "might be too tough", but enforcement-focused voters went Republican.
- L. Jobs: cannot be tested with what is public.
- M. Health costs: partly supported, with a measured 58% premium rise behind it.
- N. Rigged count: not supported. Trump raised it Nov. 9; officials raised no major concerns and there was no court wave.
- O. Party loyalty: supported as the reason most votes did not move; Republicans were less loyal (64%) than Democrats (78%).
- P. Bad messaging: cannot be tested. No public poll asked whether voters knew the administration's economic record.
- Q. Israel: partly supported (51% too strong, but mostly Democratic voters; nothing ties it to switching). "Put America First": cannot be tested.

Vance's Nov. 4 checks: Florida's Voter Poll party ID among voters (R 41, D 30) roughly matches his "9 points more Republican"; "a couple points worse than 2024" cannot be tested like-for-like with public data, so it is not a catch. His pollster's generic ballot of 13 matches the House vote (about 12, expected 13). Trump's Nov. 3 words, which Vance cites, named the gasoline price, not the war, and added "a part of it was Iran" [fox-trump-election-night: "I think a part of it was Iran."].

The result turns on two rows: the five "crucial" shares (A against B and D), and the 2024-vote rows (G). It would change if the final weighted file moved the corruption, cost-of-living and Iran shares by several points, or if voter-file studies show recalled 2024 vote misstates the electorate.

Question 2 (the catch): do the reports say what the polls measured?
- The Voter Poll is one survey sold under seven names [ssrs-voter-poll-methods: "ABC, the Associated Press (AP), CBS, CNN, Fox News Media, MSNOW, and NBC."]; NBC's version is the same data [nbc-exit-poll-how-it-works: "The companies receive the same data, and NBC News then independently analyzes and reports on the exit poll results."]. Branding alone is not an error.
- USA Today's Kansas list (below) does not match the Kansas poll. The Catch.
- Group shifts from 2024 compare the new 2026 poll with different 2024 surveys. Each outlet's starting point comes from a real 2024 survey, so it is not an error by either; told in Who moved as why swing figures differ, not as a catch.
- The Post's county analysis is not a voter survey [wapo-voting-groups: "These figures represent aggregated population demographics, not individual voter preferences."]. Background.

## The story

1. Democrats took the House and Senate on Nov. 3, and in the Voter Poll, the survey of about 100,000 voters that seven news organizations share, government corruption and the cost of living were the issues voters most often called crucial.
2. Republicans lost voters both ways, but in the one post-election survey that counts nonvoters, by voters' own account more of Trump's 2024 voters stayed home than switched: 65% voted Republican, 6% Democratic and 24% did not vote [economist-yougov-2026-11-06-crosstabs: "Yes 74% 100% 100% 13% 87% 76% 82% 69% 76% 79% 83% 66% 73%"]; early returns in 13 states point the same way, except in Texas [stateline-turnout-2026-11-10: "Bronner estimated 75% of Democratic gains came from a turnout gap and about 20% from Republicans switching sides and voting for Democrats."].
3. Gasoline had risen from $2.94 a gallon on Feb. 23 to $4.15 on Nov. 2, 79% of voters said inflation had caused their family hardship, and 37% approved of Trump.
4. Vance says the war lost it and Trump has said gas prices, bad candidates and bad messaging; voters called the war crucial less often than corruption or prices, but many named it as a cause of the prices, and the polls cannot separate anger at prices from anger at Trump.
5. Democrats take Congress on Jan. 3 with majorities short of the Senate's 60 votes and of a veto override, facing a public that, in a Reuters/Ipsos poll, wants the war ended (71%) and the tariffs repealed (62%).

## The plan

Headline: Voters blamed Trump for high prices, and many of his 2024 voters stayed home (the earlier headline described how the question was worded, not what happened)

What happened (sentence 1):
1. What happened? Democrats won both chambers [ap-uncalled-races-2026-11-10: "With 255 seats in the House, Democrats will have their largest majority elected to the chamber since the 2008"].
2. What did voters say? The five "crucial" shares, as a bar chart: corruption 55, cost of living 49, immigration 36, Iran 34, AI 23 (rows above).
3. Whose poll? One survey, seven sponsors [ssrs-voter-poll-methods: "ABC, the Associated Press (AP), CBS, CNN, Fox News Media, MSNOW, and NBC."]; a second, NORC's for the Post, agrees on approval [wapo-norc-ten-factors: "The poll of voters found his approval at 39 percent positive and 61 percent negative"].
4. Trump on voters' minds: 44% opposed, 16% supported, 40% not a factor.

Who moved (sentence 2): opens on "who changed sides, and who stayed home?"
1. YouGov, the survey that counts nonvoters: Trump 2024 voters 65% R, 6% D, the rest mostly did not vote; Republicans 64% loyal, Democrats 78%. Self-reports.
2. The Voter Poll, voters only: Trump 2024 voters D 7; composition 49/44 against Trump's 49.8-48.3 win. NORC: 2024 nonvoters about 15% of voters, against the Voter Poll's 4%.
3. Groups chart: 2026 House vote by gender, age, race, education, income and area, from the Voter Poll; no 2024 comparison (like-for-like not confirmed).
4. Places chart: the Post's county returns (AP, 8:30 a.m. Nov. 4), 2026 Senate margin against 2024 presidential margin in the same counties, by county type and by state. Captioned as places, not voters.

Why now (sentence 3): gasoline from EIA [eia-gasoline-weekly: "2026-02/23: 2.937"] [eia-gasoline-weekly: "2026-11/02: 4.153"]; war began in February [ap-trump-blame: "war in Iran that he started with Israel in February"]; hardship 79%; approval 37%.

The sides (sentence 4): Vance, Trump, Cruz, Navarro, Jeffries and Schumer in their words; quote cards. Vance's "The president himself said so last night" beside Trump's Nov. 3 words, dated. Trump's Nov. 8 "bad messaging".

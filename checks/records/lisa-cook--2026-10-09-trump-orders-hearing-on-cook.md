## The story

President Trump has ordered Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook to a closed White House hearing on November 5, where three of his own officials will question her about mortgage-fraud allegations and recommend whether he has cause to fire her.
Cook, who sets interest rates on the Fed's board and whose term runs to 2038, denies the allegation that she claimed two homes as her main residence in 2021, and stays in her job meanwhile.
Trump tried to fire her last year without hearing her side, the Supreme Court blocked that in June but said he could try again, and this hearing is the chance to respond the Court said she was owed.
Trump says she cannot be trusted and is "an automatic no vote"; her lawyers say it was an honest paperwork mistake, not grounds to remove her, and doubt the hearing is genuine.
After the hearing Cook can answer in writing by November 10, the committee then recommends, Trump decides with no deadline, and a firing would go back to the courts.

## The sides

The administration: deceit or gross negligence by a financial regulator in her own financial transactions is cause; signing two principal-residence promises two weeks apart cannot have been honest, and at minimum was grossly negligent. [scotus-oral-argument-2026-01-21: "financial regulator in financial transactions"] [wh-scavino-letter-2026-08-05: "a jury could find that you intended to defraud these institutions for your own benefit, but at a minimum, this conduct was grossly negligent"] [scotus-oral-argument-2026-01-21: "The American people should not have their"]
Cook: an inadvertent oversight on a lender's form; she asked about a vacation-home loan and called Atlanta a "2nd Home" elsewhere; she never rented it; nothing shows she got a better rate; the conduct was private and before office; Trump and five cabinet members reportedly did the same. [lowell-response-2026-08-26: "Governor Cook confirmed that her Atlanta property was a “2nd Home.”"] [lowell-response-2026-08-26: "neither the Letter nor Director Pulte’s Referral declares that Governor Cook actually received a preferential interest rate on any property"] [lowell-response-2026-08-26: "The pre-office conduct at issue, all of which took place in Governor Cook’s private capacity"] [lowell-response-2026-08-26: "Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Attorney General Todd Blanche have both entered into “primary” residence mortgages for multiple homes."]
The Court's frame: cause is a "substantial threshold," weighed by seriousness and nexus to the job; whether she was grossly negligent is "an open question." [scotus-trump-v-cook-2026-06-29: "Whether “cause” for removal exists in any given situation will depend, at least in part, on the seriousness of the alleged misconduct, and the extent of any nexus that may exist to the Governor’s professional duties."] [scotus-trump-v-cook-2026-06-29: "It at least remains an open question what precisely happened here, and indeed whether Cook committed “gross negligence,” let alone “deceitful and potentially criminal conduct,” as the President’s letter alleges."]
Agreed: she signed a principal-residence covenant on both homes; she is owed notice and a chance to respond; the dispute is whether that is cause. [lowell-response-2026-08-26: "Governor Cook’s signing of a “primary” mortgage agreement"] [scotus-trump-v-cook-2026-06-29: "Only after Cook has had the opportunity to respond to the charges made against her"]

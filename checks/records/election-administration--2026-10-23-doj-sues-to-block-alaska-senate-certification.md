## The story

The Justice Department has asked a federal judge to stop Alaska certifying its Senate result if Sen. Dan S. Sullivan loses but would have won had every vote for a second Dan Sullivan been his, until an investigation finds whom those voters meant.
The suit is against Alaska's own election officials, who tried to remove the second Sullivan in June before the state's courts put him back on the ballot.
President Trump says he ordered it, a day after saying he was looking at "powerful legal options" to remove the other Sullivan from the ballot, and seven weeks after telling Attorney General Todd Blanche he thought there was a legal case.
The department says a voter-registration law's aim of protecting "the integrity of the electoral process" requires the hold; Murkowski, Peltola and election lawyers say that clause gives no such power and the ballot already tells the two men apart.
The suit matters only if the ranked-choice count, due Nov. 18 with certification targeted for Nov. 25, leaves Sullivan short in a way the other Sullivan's votes would reverse.

## The event

On Oct. 22, 2026, the United States sued the State of Alaska, its elections director Carol Beecher and its election and ballot-review boards in federal court in Anchorage [doj-complaint-2026-10-22: "Case 3:26-cv-00376-SLG Document 1 Filed 10/22/26"] [doj-complaint-2026-10-22: "State of Alaska; Carol Beecher, in her official"]. The complaint, signed by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward [doj-complaint-2026-10-22: "Stanley E. Woodward, Jr. Associate Attorney General"], asks the court to declare that certifying a Senate result "impacted by voter confusion" would violate the National Voter Registration Act and to bar certification of such a result pending an investigation [doj-complaint-2026-10-22: "Declare that certification of any result that may be impacted by voter confusion, as defined above, would violate Defendants’ duties under the NVRA to “protect the integrity of the electoral process.”"]. On Oct. 23 President Trump said he ordered it [cnn-sues-2026-10-23: "Well, I’m the one that said ‘do it,’"].

- Mary Peltola, Democrat, former U.S. representative.

- Alaska's election officials, the defendants.

### C1 — Scope mismatch

**Claim:** “Reuters, NBC, UPI, AP and PBS described the suit as blocking certification if Sullivan loses, if a Democrat wins, or if Daniel J. Sullivan stays on the ballot, and the Times's headline said the department ‘Seeks to Block Alaska Vote if Republican Loses.’”

**Records and exact words:**

- **Reuters:** “sued to block Alaska's US Senate race certification if the Republican party loses.” records(20261024-070511)
- **NBC:** “if: (1) Senator Sullivan does not win the election; and (2) had all votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan been cast instead for Senator Sullivan and Alaska’s rank-choice process played out accordingly, Senator Sullivan would have won the election.” records(20261024-070511)
- **UPI:** “preemptively calling on the courts to block the certification of Alaska's upcoming Senate race if the Democratic candidate wins.” records(20261024-070511)
- **AP:** “demands that the state be blocked from certifying a Peltola win if Daniel J. remains on the ballot.” records(20261024-070511)
- **PBS:** “demanding that the state be blocked from certifying a Peltola win if the other Sullivan remains on the ballot.” records(20261024-070511)
- **New York Times:** “On Trump’s Order, Justice Dept. Seeks to Block Alaska Vote if Republican Loses.” records(20261024-070511)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:**

The phrase “or if Daniel J. Sullivan stays on the ballot” presents his continued presence as an independent condition sufficient to trigger blocked certification. AP and PBS instead describe two simultaneous conditions: Peltola wins **and** Daniel J. Sullivan remains on the ballot.

NBC also quotes the lawsuit's narrower proposed test: the incumbent must lose **and** reallocating Daniel J. Sullivan's votes to the incumbent through ranked-choice tabulation must reverse the outcome.

The claim accurately reproduces the Times headline and the broader Reuters and UPI characterizations. The problem is its aggregation of distinct descriptions into seemingly interchangeable conditions, including one condition neither AP nor PBS states independently.

Five claims have support or scope problems in the supplied records.

1. **C24 — [january-rule]**

   **Claim:** “A Commerce rule effective January 15, 2026, moved exports of the H200 and AMD's MI325X to China from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review, if the exporter certifies conditions including U.S. supply, a cap of half of U.S. shipments, and third-party testing in the United States.”

   **Record:** `fr-h200-rule-2026-01-15` states that “the aggregate TPP of ‘advanced-node integrate[d] circuits’ exported to China or Macau will be no more than 50 percent of the aggregate TPP shipped to customers in the United States.” The rule's preamble uses shipment-based shorthand, but its operative certification requirement measures aggregate total processing performance (TPP). 

   **What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the regulation imposes a straightforward numerical cap of one chip exported to China for every two shipped to U.S. customers. The binding calculation concerns aggregate computing performance, which need not equal the number of chips.

2. **C37 — [hopper-2-4b], short form**

   **Claim:** “Nvidia shipped $2.4 billion of Hopper data center products, the family that includes the H200, to China in the quarter that ended October 25.”

   **Short form:** “Aug.-Oct.: $2.4 billion of Hopper chips to China”

   **Record:** `nvidia-cfo-q3-2026-11-17` states: “Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were $2.4 billion.” 

   **What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Nvidia identified $2.4 billion specifically in individual Hopper chips. The disclosure concerns the broader category of Hopper data center products, without isolating chip-only shipments. The full claim holds; the short form is more specific than the record.

3. **C42 — [amd-licenses]**

   **Claim:** “AMD says the U.S. granted it licenses in February 2026 to ship MI325 chips to certain Chinese customers, and said in August it did not yet know whether China would allow the chips in; its November filing mentions the licenses but reports no MI325 shipments to China.”

   **Record:** `amd-10q-2026-11-04` states: “During the first half of 2026, the U.S. government granted us some export licenses authorizing us to ship our AMD Instinct MI325 products to certain China-based customers.”

   It continues: “Any MI325 products shipped to China are required by the terms of the licenses to first undergo an inspection process in the United States.” 

   **What a reader would wrongly believe:** That AMD affirmatively reported zero MI325 shipments to China. The November filing does not disclose an MI325 shipment quantity or establish that none occurred. Its silence cannot establish a zero-shipment finding. The February licensing and August uncertainty portions are supported.

4. **C45 — [three-listed-june]**

   **Claim:** “The Pentagon formally added Alibaba, Baidu and BYD to its list of Chinese military companies on June 8, 2026.”

   **Short form:** “June 8: Alibaba, Baidu, BYD listed”

   **Records:** `techcrunch-1260h-2026-06-08` states: “The Pentagon has added Alibaba, Baidu, EV-maker BYD, and buzzy robotics company Unitree to a list of entities it says support the Chinese military.” 

   `fr-1260h-2026-06-10` states: “This PDF is FR Doc. 2026-11571 as it appeared on Public Inspection on 06/08/2026 at 12:30 pm.”

   The formal Federal Register publication is dated June 10, 2026. The June 8 timestamp establishes public-inspection availability, not that the formal designation occurred on that precise date. 

   **What a reader would wrongly believe:** That June 8 is the established date of formal designation or Federal Register publication. The records establish that the list was publicly available by June 8 and formally published June 10, without identifying the precise date the Pentagon made its designation determination.

5. **C58 — [hearings-planned]**

   **Claim:** “Meeks wants public hearings with Lutnick and Kessler, and the committee plans hearings with them next month, the Times reports; no date is set.”

   **Record:** `meeks-remarks-2026-11-19` states: “That must include public hearings with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and BIS Undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler.”

   `businesstimes-nyt-2026-11-20` states: “The committee plans to hold hearings next month with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and his deputy Jeff Kessler, who has had a special role in approving license applications.” 

   **What a reader would wrongly believe:** That it has been established that the committee has not scheduled a particular hearing date. The record supplies no specific date, but does not establish that no date has been set. The distinction matters because an unreported schedule is not necessarily an undetermined schedule.

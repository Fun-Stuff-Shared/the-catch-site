**C1 — Unsupported attribution of birthplace information to registrants.**

**Claim:** “In the same sample, 38.9 percent of the records that meaningfully reported a birthplace listed the United States, which the county calls strong evidence of citizenship; counting all the sampled voters, the county's brief puts it at 25.8 percent and calls a U.S. birthplace ‘a strong indicator of citizenship status.’”

**Record flores-decl-2026-10-20:** “of the voters where birthplace was meaningfully reported, 38.9% indicate that they were born in the United States, which is strong evidence of citizenship.” records

**Record logan-opp-2026-10-20:** “25.8% of the sampled voters record a place of birth in the United States, a strong indicator of citizenship status.” The brief also specifies that these figures describe the composition of the records, not individual citizenship determinations. records

**Finding:** Both percentages, their distinct denominators, the attribution to Los Angeles County, and the short form are supported.

However, the claim's stated scope specifies that “birthplace is the registrant's own entry.” Neither record establishes who supplied or entered the birthplace information.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the County established the birthplace information was self-reported by registrants. The records establish only that birthplace information appeared in the sampled voter records.

### Finding 1 — C3 [no-record-billions-to-us]

**Claim:** “Neither the report nor the White House's terms for the NABEP deal record any payment of Venezuela's oil money to the U.S. government: the account's billions are Venezuela's property in U.S. custody and were paid out to Venezuela, and the NABEP stake and oil rights carry no recorded payment.”

**Record:** `state-custody-report-2026-11-12`

The report states:

> “Of the total amount, $12.11 billion (98.7%) was disbursed from the Venezuela Custody Account, while $168.56 million remained in custody as of September 30, 2026.” records(20261113-203332)

It also states:

> “In addition to foreign exchange interventions, authorized funds may be disbursed on behalf of the interim Government of Venezuela directly to vendors and service providers supporting governmental purposes, critical goods, services, and operational needs. Between March 6, 2026 and September 30, 2026, $133.11 million has been disbursed through vendor payments.” records(20261113-203332)

The vendor ledger identifies recipients including KPMG LLP, Greenberg Traurig LLP and other service providers. records(20261113-203332)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the billions were simply paid out to Venezuela, rather than distributed through multiple channels that included direct payments to third-party vendors, with some funds still held in custody.

The claim's central distinction between Venezuelan sovereign assets and U.S. government revenue is supported. Its description of the funds' disposition is broader than the report supports.

### Finding 2 — C10 [rios-question]

**Claim:** “The Venezuelan analyst Ricardo Ríos noted that more than 90% of the money went to sell dollars in Venezuela and asked who the main buyers of those preferential-rate dollars were.”

**Record:** `x-rios-2026-11-13`

Ríos wrote:

> “Más del 90% se usó para liquidar divisas en el mercado interno.”

He then asked:

> “¿Quienes fueron los principales beneficiarios de wsa asignación de divisas a precio preferencial?”

The second passage asks who the principal **beneficiaries** of the preferential-rate foreign-exchange allocation were, not specifically who purchased the dollars. records(20261113-203332)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Ríos specifically questioned the identity of the main purchasers of preferential-rate dollars. His question was broader, concerning who benefited from the allocation, which need not be limited to direct buyers.

The distinction matters because beneficiaries of a preferential foreign-exchange system are not necessarily identical to the purchasers receiving the currency.

### C14 — gl46-opened

**Claim:** “On January 29, the Treasury issued General License 46, letting any established U.S. firm market Venezuelan oil, with payments into the U.S.-controlled account.”

**Record:** `fr-gl46-2026-05-07`

The license states:

> “Any monetary payment to a blocked person is made into the Foreign Government Deposit Funds, as specified in Executive Order 14373 of January 9, 2026, or any other account as instructed by the U.S. Department of the Treasury.”

records(20261113-002424)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That General License 46 required all payments associated with authorized Venezuelan oil transactions to enter the same U.S.-controlled account. The license applies this condition specifically to monetary payments to blocked persons and expressly permits alternative accounts designated by Treasury. The claim presents one permitted payment route as the exclusive arrangement.

### C25 — vance-campaign-direct

**Claim:** “Addison's direct gift to Vance's campaign was $10,800 to Ohioans for JD, a joint fundraising committee, of which $2,900 twice went to JD Vance for Senate.”

**Record:** `fec-addison-2022`

The FEC records identify three allocations:

> “OHIOANS FOR JD	C00783175	itemized	10800.00”

> “WORKING FOR OHIO	C00783167	memo	5000.00”

> “JD VANCE FOR SENATE INC.	C00783142	memo	2900.00”

The final $2,900 entry appears twice. records(20261113-002424)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Addison contributed $10,800 directly to Vance's official Senate campaign. The $10,800 contribution went to a joint fundraising committee. Its reported allocations included $5,800 to JD Vance for Senate and $5,000 to Working for Ohio. Although the claim identifies the joint committee and the two $2,900 allocations, its opening characterization treats the entire $10,800 as a direct campaign contribution.

### C31 — primerica-field

**Claim:** “The filings carrying the $5 million and the Trump 47 gifts list the Vitol household's address, the middle initial C and, in the same filings, Shannon V. Addison, but give the employer and title of a different man, Primerica's non-executive chairman John A. Addison Jr., who files from Georgia.”

**Records:** `sec-primerica-proxy-2015`, `fec-addison-2024`, `fec-addison-primerica-ga`

Primerica's SEC proxy statement identifies John A. Addison Jr.'s position:

> “Mr. Addison has served as the non-executive Chairman of Primerica Distribution since April 2015”

The same document identifies the chairman of Primerica's corporate board separately:

> “Mr. R. Williams has served as non-executive Chairman of the Board since April 2015.”

records(20261113-002424)

The relevant FEC entries report:

> “PRIMERICA DISTRIBUTION	NON-EXECUTIVE CHAIRMAN”

records(20261113-002424)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That John A. Addison Jr. was the non-executive chairman of Primerica Inc.'s corporate board. The SEC document identifies him as non-executive chairman of Primerica Distribution, a different position. The corporate board chair was D. Richard Williams. The distinction matters because the claim uses the position to distinguish two individuals with similar names.

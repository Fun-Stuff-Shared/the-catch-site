## Claims to check

- **“Nobody’s been killed in the last five months” and “we haven’t lost one soldier” in five or six months.** Contradicted by the department’s own July announcements. Four soldiers were killed July 17–19 in the Jordan attack and the Erbil detonation. Edwards died July 1. Two named deaths followed in September, and two unnamed airmen in October. Hostile deaths alone are enough. The non-hostile deaths are inside the same window if the claim is “nobody.”
- **The running total is 18.** That was a figure Trump and, for a time, DCAS used. DCAS was reported at 21 by October 10 and at 23 by October 30–November 9. On November 11 he was still saying 18.
- **“18” includes Venezuela, and nobody was killed in Venezuela.** Both sentences are in the CBS transcript. They cannot both be a count of Iran-war deaths. I did not find a Pentagon Venezuela death in this material.
- **Short clips show him saying nobody died and stopping there.** Snopes says that attribution is real and incomplete. The next lines are the 18 and “Eighteen is too much.”
- **People.com’s date.** The page says he “falsely claimed on Thursday, Nov. 11.” November 11, 2026, was Wednesday, the Arlington speech, where he said 18 had died. The Hewitt sentence was Thursday, November 12. https://people.com/trump-says-no-u-s-soldiers-have-been-killed-in-iran-war-in-the-last-5-months-despite-multiple-deaths-since-july-12253156
- **“Four in July.”** Mediaite’s November 12 story says “including four in July.” The Post’s November 13 deck counts five July deaths: three at the Jordan base, one at Erbil, and the Navy captain on July 1. Feehan’s death date is July 18 from the July 17 attack, so “three killed on July 17” is also incomplete.
- **Hegseth’s “complete LIE.”** The Post story he attacked, on September 18, said internal figures were at least 22 against a public 18, and that not every extra death was a combat death. On October 9 the public toll moved to 21 by adding noncombat deaths. His sentence was about that September story. It is not a later defense of the November five-month claim. X has attached a community note to his post pointing at the later increase.
- **“Temporary data disruption.”** Parnell said that on July 24. By July 27 the Post reported the four July deaths had been put back in a separate category, not restored to Operation Epic Fury. https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/07/27/pentagon-relabels-us-casualties-caused-by-iran-amid-scrutiny-wartime-transparency/
- **A September suicide.** October stories attribute that word to an unnamed official. The later named September Navy death, Ramirez-Galvan, is described on Military.com only as “found deceased,” with an investigation. Those may be the same case. I did not find a page that settles the cause.
- **Wilkerson’s cause.** The Army told Military.com “non-combat related.” Her brother told The Intercept it was a “coronary issue.” Those are different speakers.
- **McCollum’s “three times, the House has voted to stop this unlawful war.”** Her sentence. I did not read the roll calls.

## Competing explanations

Each is the strongest form I could find, with who holds it.

- **It is not a war, because the recent months are quiet and the total is small beside Vietnam, Korea, Iraq, and Afghanistan.** Trump, November 6–12. On Hewitt he adds that the sharp fighting has been three weeks, not seven months, and that the United States controls the Gulf. On November 11 he still counted “18 beautiful souls,” so this version denies recent deaths while admitting a total.
- **He said both things, and the viral clips cut the second.** Snopes, November 11. The words “nobody has died” and “we haven’t lost one soldier” are on the CBS transcript. So is “18 people killed” and “Eighteen is too much.” Snopes says the DNC declined to explain the edit. This account does not say the five-month sentence is true. Snopes says the July Arabian Sea death makes it false.
- **The five-month sentence is false, and he said it after honoring the dead.** Washington Post, November 13; Military Times; the July 22 Pentagon release. The strongest evidence is the four July 17–19 deaths, which are enemy-action deaths inside the window, plus Edwards on July 1. The Post’s summary count is 23, with 11 from enemy fire and 12 non-hostile. Daily Beast adds that Trump was at the July 22 Dover transfer for Feehan, Gonzales, Rampersad, and Swinton. https://www.thedailybeast.com/trump-pushes-bogus-claim-about-us-troop-deaths
- **Only combat deaths should count, and the books were split when Epic Fury was declared over.** This is the department’s category system, stated by a Pentagon official to Military Times on November 4. Overseas Operations starts July 7. Snopes’s reading puts Edwards, a non-hostile July 1 mishap, in Epic Fury, and the four July 17–19 deaths in the later category. I did not find Hegseth, Parnell, or Trump saying the November 12 sentence is true because of that split. The split does not remove the four hostile July deaths from the five-month window. It changes which label they carry.
- **The public toll was wrong because of a glitch, and reports of hidden deaths were a lie.** Parnell and Valdez, July 24, and Hegseth, September 18. The strongest form of Hegseth’s denial, reported by the Daily Wire from an OAN interview and not opened by me: “Even if we wanted to attempt to hide a combat death, we never would and we never could.” The later DCAS increases were noncombat names the Post had already flagged, which fits a narrow reading of “combat death.” It does not fit the July 17 hostile deaths, which the department had already announced.
- **The department is managing the toll by renaming the war.** Intercept, Houlahan’s July 27 letter, Smith and Houlahan on October 9, Schumer on November 9. Their form: deaths and wounds were removed from Epic Fury after a declared end, then reappeared under a bland “Overseas Operations” label, and noncombat deaths in the same theater were left out until Congress and reporters pressed. The Intercept’s November 13 suit says the public still does not have the records that would show how the counts were changed. Democracy Forward’s release still says “at least 21” dead, citing an October 12 story, which is behind the 23 already on DCAS by the filing date.
- **The number moves because non-hostile deaths are real war deaths and were added late.** NYT on October 10, Military.com’s November 11 list, Military Times on November 4. Davius in March, the September Navy death, Wilkerson, and the two October airmen are in this group. A defender of Trump can say he meant killed in action. That defense still fails on Gonzales, Rampersad, Feehan, and Swinton.
- **He is repeating a stale number he has already treated as the whole war.** Trump corrected Lutnick to 18 on September 3 and was still at 18 on November 11 and November 12, after DCAS had moved to 21 and then 23. This explains the “18.” It does not explain “nobody” over five months, because several of the deaths inside those five months were already public in July, and he attended the transfer.
- **“Nobody has died” was about murders in Washington, and the Iran line is separate.** The CBS transcript puts “nobody has died” immediately after “we haven’t had a murder in a long time,” then turns to Iran in the next sentence. Snopes treats the Iran reading as the one he continues. Both readings are available from the same words. The following sentence, “we haven’t lost one soldier,” is about Iran on the page.
- **The war is being slowed for the governments that invest here, not because of casualties.** Trump to Stephen A. Smith, November 12. This is his account of duration. It is not an account of the death claim. Raw Story’s headline joins the two. The Factbase transcript supports the investor sentence and does not show him repeating “nobody’s been killed” to Smith.
- **He knows and does not care.** Daily Beast quotes an X user, Suzie Rizzio: “He couldn’t care less about them because, in his eyes, they shouldn’t have gotten themselves killed.” That is a motive claim. The page does not show evidence for it beyond his having been at Dover and later saying nobody had been killed.

What I could not find: a White House or Pentagon statement on November 12–15 answering the Post; any Republican member defending the five-month sentence; the two October airmen’s names; a DCAS page opened directly; Cao’s X post; the Intercept complaint PDF; and a medical-examiner finding that states Swinton’s hostile or non-hostile code.

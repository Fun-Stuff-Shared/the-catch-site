## 1. Weak provenance: the central vacation-home document is not what the record calls it

The May 28, 2021 document supporting Cook’s defense is identified throughout the brief as a lender-issued **loan estimate**. The original document is available as Exhibit F to her lawyers’ November 17, 2025 submission. It is titled **“Bank-Fund Staff Federal Credit Union — Rate Search Results”** and explicitly instructs the customer to obtain an official Loan Estimate before choosing a loan.

It also quotes terms for a **15-year mortgage**, whereas Cook ultimately obtained a **30-year mortgage** on the Atlanta property.

The document establishes that a preliminary rate search identified the property as a vacation home. By itself, it does not establish that the lender approved the executed mortgage on that basis, or that the lender received and considered this information during underwriting. Cook’s lawyers say the document was provided to the lender, but that is a separate assertion.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence offered against an inference of intentional deception. Misidentifying it as a formal loan estimate gives that evidence more weight than the underlying document independently supports. The distinction does not establish fraud; it establishes a limit on the exculpatory evidence.

## 3. Missing primary record and allegation scope: Cook answered both criminal referrals in November 2025

The record omits Cook’s lawyers’ **November 17, 2025 response to Attorney General Pam Bondi and Justice Department official Ed Martin**. Unlike the later August 2026 White House response, this 54-page submission addresses both of Pulte’s criminal referrals and includes underlying mortgage, security-vetting and financial-disclosure documents.

The second referral alleged inconsistencies involving Cook’s Cambridge condominium and federal disclosures made **during her tenure as a governor**. It also challenged how the Ann Arbor property was characterized after Cook began renting it out.

Cook’s November response disputes those allegations. Her lawyers said she refinanced the Cambridge property as a second home in 2021, that the lender approved the accompanying rider, and that government vetting forms disclosed its second-home and rental use. They also explained that Ann Arbor rentals began after she moved for her Fed duties.

**Why it matters:** The broader criminal-referral history includes allegations concerning disclosures made while Cook was in office, not solely mortgage documents signed before her appointment. That distinction bears directly on the legal argument that pre-appointment conduct cannot constitute removal cause.

**The boundary is important:** The August 2026 removal notice relied on the original Michigan and Georgia allegations. The October 2026 memo does not establish that the committee will consider the Cambridge referral or any alleged in-office misconduct. Those additional allegations must not be treated as established charges in the November hearing.

The November letter is also the available **primary source for Cook’s response to the full criminal-referral history**, rather than an account reconstructed from subsequent news coverage.

## 4. Missing lineage: how Pulte generated the allegations is contested independently of their substance

An October 6, 2025 Reuters investigation found that Pulte sent criminal referrals directly to the Justice Department **without using the FHFA inspector general**, bypassing the established channel intended to provide independence from politically appointed agency leadership.

Reuters reported that Pulte used this route for allegations involving Cook, New York Attorney General Letitia James and Senator Adam Schiff. Pulte also publicly announced accusations before the usual independent investigative process had occurred.

Pulte did not respond to Reuters’ questions for that investigation. Trump defended the broader prosecutions as a pursuit of justice rather than revenge. The investigation did not establish that the referrals were legally invalid or that their underlying allegations were false.

**Why it matters:** The record presently identifies the criminal referral as the event that started the removal effort, without accounting for the independently documented dispute about **how the referral was produced**. That changes the evidentiary significance of the referral and supplies a factual basis for examining Cook’s selective-enforcement allegation beyond her lawyers’ comparisons with cabinet members’ mortgages.

The later GAO inquiry already held in the brief is part of this oversight history, but it does not substitute for the earlier documented departure from the inspector-general process.

## 5. Missing stakes: Trump already chairs the Fed through his appointee, and Cook has responsibilities beyond rate votes

On May 22, 2026, **Kevin Warsh became Federal Reserve chair following Trump’s nomination and Senate confirmation**. Alongside Michelle Bowman and Christopher Waller, he is one of three sitting governors initially appointed by Trump. Replacing Cook would give Trump **four of the seven board seats occupied by his appointees**, although appointment does not establish that those governors would vote as he wishes.

The brief also does not account for Cook’s role as **chair of the Board’s Committee on Financial Stability** or her participation in banking and regulatory oversight. Her removal would affect those responsibilities, not merely her place among the 12 interest-rate voters.

There is also a relevant subsequent Federal Reserve record: minutes released October 7 show that **most participants at the September meeting expected another rate increase would likely be appropriate by year-end**. That places the disagreement over interest rates across a broader group of policymakers under a Trump-appointed chair, rather than identifying Cook as the obstacle to lower rates.

**Why it matters:** The potential consequences are substantially larger than a vacancy in one policymaking seat. They include a possible change in the board’s appointment balance and leadership of financial-stability oversight. At the same time, the September minutes weaken any inference that removing Cook alone would reverse the Fed’s rate decisions.

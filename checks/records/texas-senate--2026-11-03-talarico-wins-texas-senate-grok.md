## Competing explanations

Each is the version its holders give, including ones with little support.

- **The voters beat the money.** Talarico, November 3–4. The richest donor in the race tried to buy a senator and lost to a "politics of love." He says Musk wanted land, water, and tax treatment for data centers. Held by Talarico and, in the victory-night framing, his campaign.
- **This was a national repudiation of prices and the Iran war.** Cruz, November 4. Tariffs made "everything too expensive," the war "dragged on too long," and the midterms were a "Watergate-level repudiation," with Texas part of a loss of both chambers. He still says he will work with Talarico on Texas projects and fight him on taxes, the border, crime, and "our children."
- **It was not about Trump.** Trump, November 4, as reported by LiveNOW. Democrats "fought hard," but "I don't view it as a hit on me," and the GOP would have won "IN A LANDSLIDE" had he been on the ballot. For Abbott, a separate claim: he lost by becoming a "RINO."
- **Cornyn and Rove cost Paxton the race.** Trump, October 18. They were "WEAK and INEFFECTIVE Political Hacks" who would not close ranks after the runoff.
- **Nominating Paxton cost Republicans a seat they could have held.** The account given by national Republicans before the runoff, and by MS NOW's write-up after. Senate leaders wanted Cornyn as the stronger general-election candidate. Trump endorsed Paxton as a "true MAGA warrior." Paxton carried the 2023 impeachment (he was acquitted by the Texas Senate), a securities-fraud case, and a divorce fight into the general election. Held in this form by the pre-runoff leadership argument reported by AP, and after the fact by outlets such as MS NOW: "Texas Republicans chose Ken Paxton over Sen.

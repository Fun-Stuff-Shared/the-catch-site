I'll gather primary records and public statements on the October 22 stay in Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy, then separate verbatim quotes from summaries.The October 22 order put Secretary of State David Scanlan’s broader student-ID ban back in force for November 3. It did not decide whether House Bill 323 is constitutional, and it did not publish a vote. Only Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented. The public Supreme Court docket shows no filing after the stay.

What the order actually did: it stayed the October 2 and October 5 preliminary injunctions in *Coalition for Open Democracy v. Scanlan*, No. 1:26-cv-647. Those orders had left House Bill 323 in place and blocked only Scanlan’s April 23, 2026 directive, to the extent it told officials not to consider a student ID even as backup evidence under the safety-valve statutes. Verified on the order text at Cornell LII and on the docket: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/26A512 and https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26a512.html. The opinion PDF linked from that docket is https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a512_6j37.pdf.

I did not find a public statement on this order from Gov. Kelly Ayotte, Rep. Chris Pappas, former Sen. John Sununu, Sens. Jeanne Shaheen or Maggie Hassan, or from Congress.

## Officials who acted

- **U.S. Supreme Court, unsigned order, October 22, 2026.** Verbatim, verified on the Cornell page: “This Court has repeatedly emphasized that lower federal courts should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election.” *Republican National Committee v. Democratic National Committee*, 589 U.S. 423, 424 (2020). “Here, the District Court did not point to anything that would justify deviating from that principle.” The stay runs through the First Circuit appeal and any timely certiorari petition. It ends automatically if certiorari is denied. https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/26A512

- **Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson, dissent, October 22, 2026.** Verbatim, same page: the *Purcell* principle “arose in a particular context, and this Court has yet to fully define its limits.” The district court “issued a targeted order” so that the secretary would “conform his public statements to the letter of state law.” “The District Court’s injunction was reasonably designed to address alleged misinformation, not to change the law.” “When a federal court determines that material misrepresentations are being made about a State’s election rules, an injunction to prohibit that conduct does not *contribute* to public confusion, it *prevents* that very problem.” “I respectfully dissent because—even, and perhaps especially, when an election is looming—I would not disturb a clearly justifiable federal-court order issued to ensure that the public receives accurate information about a State’s electoral processes.” Her footnote says the deputy secretary “conceded that he did not see any factual reason why a student ID could not be reasonable evidence of a prospective voter’s identity under the safety valve,” quoting the stay appendix. The order does not say how any other justice voted.

- **U.S. District Judge Joseph Laplante, District of New Hampshire, October 2 and October 5, 2026.** He denied an injunction against RSA 659:13 as amended by HB 323 and enjoined the April 23 press release and guidance only so far as it barred officials from considering student IDs under RSA 654:12 and RSA 659:13’s safety valve. He ordered revised guidance that student IDs are no longer expressly valid, but “may” be considered with other evidence. Verified in the appendix filed with the Court: https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A512/429592/20261015080505311_Appendix%20-%20Final.pdf. The New Hampshire Union Leader, October 5, quotes him: “The Legislature decided and has provided that a student ID is no longer, on its own, a expressly valid form of identification.” “It does not follow, and the state has not explained why it should follow, that it also decided that a student ID also may not constitute evidence a polling station official may rely on to establish voter identity when no other form of ID is available at all.” https://www.unionleader.com/news/politics/judge-allows-student-ids-as-voter-id-evidence-41aaff05. The Center Square quotes the same order: “The State has offered no independent justification for that broader prohibition” and “even a minimal burden on the right to vote must be justified by the State’s interests.” https://www.thecentersquare.com/new_hampshire/article_6232f033-29dc-4fb9-b4b4-f82fdc4d9bab.html. ## States

- **New Hampshire, the only state in the order.** HB 323 removed student IDs from the list of photo IDs that are expressly valid to obtain a ballot. Scanlan says SB 223 then inserted “only” before the government-issued list in RSA 659:13. RSA 654:12(I)(d)(4) still allows “any other evidence that reasonably establishes that it is more likely than not that the person is who they claim to be.” The fight is whether a student ID can be part of that other evidence. Scanlan’s October 23 guidance says it cannot, for registration, absentee voting, or election-day check-in, same as the September 8 primary.

## Stated reasons

- **The Court, October 22:** lower federal courts should ordinarily not change election rules on the eve of an election, and the district court gave nothing that justified a departure. It cites *RNC v. DNC* (2020) and *People Not Politicians v. Onder* (September 25, 2026). It does not mention fraud, students, or the Senate race.

- **Jackson:** the injunction corrected misleading official guidance and served *Purcell* by reducing confusion. She would not disturb it.

- **Formella, October 22:** election integrity, applying the statutes “as written,” and avoiding last-minute uncertainty.

- **Scanlan, October 6:** HB 323 and SB 223 show legislative intent that student IDs are not reasonable evidence of identity. A split rule, usable to register but not to get a ballot, “would make no sense.” Case-by-case supervisor decisions risk unequal treatment. A late change risks confusion and disenfranchisement.

- **Scanlan’s application, as reported:** voters might bring only a student ID, which would not be enough even under Laplante. Undoing months of training, posters, ads, and website changes this close to the election is the core of *Purcell*.

- **McFarlane:** colleges lack government-level controls. The bill applies one standard and supports public confidence.

- **Berry:** students already show government ID for school, alcohol, and firearms. Equality means no special class. Judges should not change rules this close to the election.

- **Gruters:** the suit is an “eleventh hour” attack on voter ID and a “power grab.”

- **Adams:** pre-election rule changes are the worst time. The primary ran under the stricter rule.

- **O’Brien:** the suit enables undetected fraud.

- **Plaintiffs:** the directive exceeds the statute, burdens the right to vote, and discriminates against young voters. A stay now reverses guidance already posted and will turn people away. They say the law is unconstitutional. Laplante did not enjoin the statute at the preliminary stage.

- **Tentarelli:** confusion suppresses voting, and the DMV cannot issue voting-only IDs fast enough for this election.

- **Commentators on X:** student IDs do not prove citizenship or domicile. Some add that noncitizens can hold them. The Court did not say that.

## Claims to check

- **“The Court reinstated the law” or “upheld the ban.”** Formella’s release uses “reinstating New Hampshire’s law.” The order stays two injunctions. Those injunctions did not block HB 323. What returned is Scanlan’s April directive, including the safety-valve ban. HB 323 had remained in effect for the September 8 primary and, by Laplante’s order, for the general election as well.

- **“Student IDs cannot be used to vote” and “fully ban student IDs for voting.”** The secretary’s October 23 release says they cannot be used to prove identity for registration, absentee voting, or check-in. It does not say a student cannot vote with another accepted ID. Before the stay, Laplante had not restored student IDs as standalone ID. They could be considered only with other evidence.

- **“8–1” and “even the liberal justices voted” to keep student IDs off the ballot.** Daugherty, Fournier, and Eagleman state this as fact. The order says “Justice Jackson dissenting” and publishes no other votes. Silence is not a recorded vote. https://x.com/EricLDaugh/status/2113397322335785239

- **“A federal judge tried to water down” or overturn HB 323 weeks before the election.** Mila Joy, October 23: “A federal judge tried to water it down.” https://x.com/Milajoy/status/2113701655153868907. Laplante wrote that he was not enjoining the statute. He blocked the secretary’s broader reading.

- **“The First Circuit upheld” Laplante.** Democracy Docket’s October 23 article says that. The public record I verified is a denial of a stay, not an opinion on the merits.

- **“The Court ruled that student IDs are invalid” or decided constitutionality.** The order is a stay pending appeal. Formella’s release says it does not resolve the merits. Jackson’s dissent is about *Purcell* and accurate information, not a holding that the statute is valid.

- **“Noncitizens can get student ID,” offered as what the case is about.** Daugherty and the Election Integrity Network. HB 323 and the directive are about proof of identity. New Hampshire still requires separate proof of citizenship, age, and domicile to register. A May ruling on HB 1569, a different statute about citizenship affidavits, is a different case. The Dartmouth describes that May ruling. https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2026/08/flavin-activist-group-sues-new-hampshire-to-block-ban-on-use-of-student-ids-to-obtain-ballot

- **“Near-unanimous ruling.”** A headline shared on X. The Court did not say that.

- **Proof-of-citizenship case trackers.** At least one tracker titles 26A512 as a proof-of-citizenship application. The docket title is Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy, an application to stay the student-ID injunctions.

- **Headlines that the Court “banned student IDs.”** The Court stayed a federal injunction. The ban is state law plus the secretary’s directive.

## Competing explanations

Each is the strongest form I found, with who holds it.

- **Timing, nothing more.** The Court’s own text. Federal courts should not change election rules this close to an election, and this record had no reason to depart. Holders: the unsigned order. Scanlan and Formella add that the October injunction upset months of training and public notice and would confuse officials and voters. Adams: the stricter rule already ran in the September 8 primary.

- **The secretary was right about state law.** Student IDs were removed from the express list, and SB 223’s “only” means they also cannot be “other evidence.” A contrary reading lets someone register with a card they cannot use to obtain a ballot, and it leaves each town’s supervisors to improvise. Holders: Scanlan’s October 6 statement. Berry: the Legislature, not a judge, sets that rule, and it treats students like everyone else. McFarlane: one standard, because schools do not issue ID the way governments do.

- **The directive went past the statute, and the stay restores a misstatement.** HB 323 took student IDs off the automatic list and left the safety valve alone. The deputy secretary saw no factual reason a student ID could not be reasonable evidence. The injunction forced the website to match the statute. Staying it reimposes the misstatement twelve days before the election, which is the confusion *Purcell* is meant to stop. Holders: Laplante’s order, Jackson’s dissent, and the plaintiffs’ October 19 brief and October 22 statement. Tentarelli: the registration extension was an assumption the bill did not spell out.

- **The law itself is an unconstitutional burden and age discrimination, aimed at young voters in a close Senate race.** Students are less likely to have a license or passport with them, a state ID costs money and a DMV trip, and young voters lean Democratic. The stay matters because Sununu and Pappas are close and the election is November 3. Holders: the complaint, as described by Free Speech For People and Open Democracy. Zink: if you are 18 and a citizen, the ID is only proof of identity. Democracy Docket and Bloomberg frame the practical stakes around the Senate race. Bloomberg does not quote a party official making that claim. I did not find Ayotte, Scanlan, or the RNC saying the purpose was to affect that race.

- **Election integrity against noncitizen or out-of-state voting.** A campus card shows enrollment, not citizenship or New Hampshire residence. Out-of-state students should vote absentee at home. Allowing the card invites double voting. Holders: O’Brien, to the point of “undetected fraud.” Adams, raising “out-of-state students on Mass driver’s licenses.” Election Integrity Network, Daugherty, Mannarino, and many replies on X. The Court’s order does not adopt this account. Plaintiffs say the state never identified a security problem specific to student IDs as compared with other non-government evidence the safety valve still allows. That point is in USA Today’s account of the coalition’s argument: https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/10/22/supreme-court-student-id-vote-republican-new-hampshire/92335101007/

- **A partisan Supreme Court intervention in the shadow docket.** The same Court that is loose about corporate election spending is strict about a student card. The unsigned order, with only Jackson writing, is the method. Holders: D’Arrigo’s post, and a Common Dreams line reposted by NewsGram: “The Supreme Court thinks corporations can buy our elections but a college kid can’t use a student ID to vote.” https://x.com/DarrigoMelanie/status/2113409429181960588 and https://x.com/NewsGramHQ/status/2113853713030975694. I did not open the Common Dreams article. This reads the silence of eight justices as a vote. The order does not say that.

- **Judicial overreach, and Jackson is the outlier.** A single judge rewrote a clear statute days before voting, and Jackson would rather have federal courts editing state guidance during an election. Holders: Berry’s “black robes” comment, Fournier, Eagleman, and Turley’s claim that Jackson ignored why *Purcell* exists. Turley quotes her. He does not claim a published 8–1 vote. The others do.

- **Ordinary administration, narrow and temporary.** The law was never blocked. The stay lasts only while the appeal proceeds. Students can still vote with a government photo ID, and the state is telling them how. Holders: the order’s own limits, Formella’s “does not resolve the merits,” Manchester Ink Link’s practical summary, and Scanlan’s October 23 guides. https://manchester.inklink.news/supreme-court-puts-nh-student-id-ban-back-in-place-for-nov-3-election/

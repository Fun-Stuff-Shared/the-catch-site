### C1 — 120-day exception is broader and more specific than stated

**Claim words:** “once 120 days have passed since they reported it inside the company.”

**Record:** `doj-whip-terms-2026-10-20`

**Exact words:**
> “at least 120 days have elapsed since they provided the information to the relevant entity’s audit committee, chief legal officer, chief compliance officer (or their equivalents), or their supervisor, or since they received the information, if they received it under circumstances indicating that the entity’s audit committee, chief legal officer, chief compliance officer (or their equivalents), or their supervisor was already aware of the information.”

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That any internal report starts the 120-day clock and that making an internal report is necessary. The terms identify specific recipients and separately allow the clock to begin when the person received the information, provided the designated personnel were already aware of it.

### C2 — An assessment consideration is presented as a mandatory condition

**Claim words:** “a tip must carry original information ... specific enough to lead the department to open, reopen or widen an investigation that produces charges.”

**Record:** `doj-whip-terms-2026-10-20`

**Exact words:**
> “In assessing whether an individual’s submission of original information led to criminal charges, the Department will consider whether:”

> “The individual provided the Department with original information that was sufficiently specific, credible, and timely to cause the Department to open an investigation, reopen an investigation that the Department had closed, or to inquire about different conduct as part of an ongoing investigation, and that original information led the Department to obtain criminal charges.”

The record separately states:
> “The original information must lead to criminal charges involving one or more of the programmatic categories outlined above.”

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That causing an investigation to open, reopen, or expand is itself an absolute eligibility requirement. The terms expressly require the information to lead to criminal charges but describe those investigative consequences as considerations in assessing whether the information led to charges, rather than an independently stated mandatory condition.

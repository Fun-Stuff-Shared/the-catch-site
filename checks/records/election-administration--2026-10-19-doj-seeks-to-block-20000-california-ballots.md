## The story

The Justice Department asked a federal appeals court on Oct. 23 to make California set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 voters it calls likely noncitizens, before the trial judge, who has a hearing set for Oct. 27, had ruled, and the same day asked registrars in at least 18 counties to commit within 48 hours to sorting citizens from noncitizens on their own lists and to set aside the ballots of anyone whose citizenship cannot be verified. The list matches an August 2025 copy of California's voter roll to immigration records, and in a 200-name Los Angeles County sample more than half had canceled or inactive registrations and were mailed no ballot. DOJ says California has refused since July to check citizenship and that ballots counted now can never be pulled back. Its officials have called the 20,000 "verified" noncitizens, while its sworn filings say "highly likely," and California says the order would hold citizens' ballots with no deadline. The Ninth Circuit set a Saturday deadline for California's answer, and Judge Carter hears the request on Oct. 27.

## Why: the stated reasons

- Blanche: "State-facilitated voter fraud is unacceptable" [doj-release-2026-10-15: "State-facilitated voter fraud is unacceptable in the United States of America"].
- Essayli: California refused to remove noncitizens or sequester ballots [doj-release-2026-10-15: "California election officials were given an opportunity to remove noncitizens from their voter rolls and to sequester ballots returned from suspected ineligible voters. They refused"].
- Essayli on timing: investigators "did not know exactly which voters would receive ballots until they were sent" [fox-essayli-2026-10-22: "investigators did not know exactly which voters would receive ballots until they were sent"].
- DOJ on urgency: harm is "impossible to remedy" once ballots are counted [ca9-motion-2026-10-23: "Once illegally cast ballots are counted and commingled with lawful votes, the resulting harm is impossible to remedy"].
- McCotter on the 28: "California refused to look at that list" [doj-release-2026-10-19: "California refused to look at that list"].

## The sides

DOJ: federal law bars noncitizen voting, and a state cannot knowingly facilitate it, so counting listed people's ballots is preempted as applied [doj-complaint-2026-10-15: "Violation of the Supremacy Clause"]; it asks only to segregate, not to remove anyone [doj-expedite-request-2026-10-21: "The United States does not seek to remove voters from the rolls"]; the list is a floor [ca9-motion-2026-10-23: "The list of over 20,000 individuals delivered to California represents the floor"].

California: the suit "borders on the absurd" [ca-state-opp-2026-10-20: "borders on the absurd"]; the order would violate the NVRA quiet period [ca-state-opp-2026-10-20: "Plaintiff’s requested injunction would violate the NVRA’s “quiet period”"]; state law already lets mail ballots be challenged on citizenship [ca-state-opp-2026-10-20: "State law permits challenges to mail ballots related to “the voter’s qualifications to vote,” including citizenship"]; there is no guarantee held ballots are counted in time [ca-state-opp-2026-10-20: "with absolutely no guarantee that their rights will be restored in time for their votes to be counted"]. It has answered grand-jury subpoenas for about 300 voters [ca-state-opp-2026-10-20: "Since August 2026, the Secretary’s Office has received federal grand jury subpoenas for voter records for approximately 300 individuals"].

LA County: it cannot withhold ballots on unverified allegations [logan-statement-2026-10-15: "We cannot withhold or refuse to count ballots based solely on unverified allegations"]; the list is unreliable [flores-decl-2026-10-20: "it indicates that the October List is unreliable"].

Agreed: once a ballot leaves its envelope it cannot be tied to the voter (DOJ quotes the county saying so) [doj-expedite-request-2026-10-21: "There is no un-ringing the bell, which is"]; DOJ's list is of likely, not confirmed, noncitizens in DOJ's own court papers [bartell-decl-2026-10-21: "are highly likely to be noncitizen aliens"].

The county's status percentages conflict between its own filings. Bhullar's sworn declaration gives 42.9 percent canceled, 42.4 active and 4.6 inactive, which sums to 89.9 [bhullar-decl-2026-10-20: "42.9% of the list sampled had canceled voter registration records"]; the county's motion to dismiss, citing the same paragraph, gives 14.6 inactive, which sums to 99.9 [logan-mtd-2026-10-21: "met all legal requirements for voter registration; and 14.6% are considered"]. Neither fits the counts cleanly: 42.4 percent of 200 is about 85 active records against 86 mailed ballots, and 86 + 112 is 198 of 200. The documents do not settle it, so the page uses only the counts (86 mailed, 112 not) and the birthplace and voting-history figures, which do not depend on the status split.

1. **The five-sentence story states the wrong numerical trigger.** It says certification would be blocked if Sen. Sullivan loses by less than the votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan. Paragraph 42 requires something different: reallocating every Daniel J. Sullivan vote to the incumbent and rerunning the ranked-choice tabulation must produce an incumbent victory. Because votes for the other Sullivan may otherwise transfer to Peltola, this is not equivalent to comparing the final margin with his vote total. A margin larger than that total could still satisfy the complaint's test. This changes which election outcomes fall within the requested relief. **Source:** [DOJ complaint, paragraph 42, page 13](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/1-2026-10-22-Complaint.pdf).

2. **The primary results misidentify the fourth-place candidate and omit the withdrawal that produced November's ballot.** David Leslie, a Democrat, finished fourth with 1,850 votes (1.1%). Gerald Heikes finished fifth with 1,741 votes (about 1.0%). Leslie withdrew by the August 31 deadline, allowing Heikes to advance. The complaint's attribution of 1.1% to Heikes is therefore wrong, and the brief reproduces it. This matters because the withdrawal—not the original top-four result—explains why Heikes appears on the general-election ballot. It also establishes another substantive numerical error in the DOJ's primary account. **Source:** [Certified primary results and candidate withdrawal](https://ballotpedia.org/United_States_Senate_election_in_Alaska%2C_2026).

3. **Question 3 incorrectly treats the absence of a Republican ballot label as evidence that the complaint's reference to three Republican candidates is false.** Daniel J. Sullivan filed as a Republican. At a July 22 legislative hearing, Elections Director Carol Beecher acknowledged that the division deliberately omitted his Republican affiliation while identifying the incumbent as a Republican. The number of printed party labels therefore cannot establish the number of Republican-affiliated candidates. His subsequently reported change in registration requires separate verification. This invalidates the matrix's stated reason for ruling out that part of the DOJ's position. **Sources:** [Alaska Public Media, July 22 hearing](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-07-24/alaska-elections-division-director-defends-disqualifying-u-s-senate-candidate); [DOJ complaint](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/1-2026-10-22-Complaint.pdf).

The Press itself identifies strategic voting as an unresolved explanation.

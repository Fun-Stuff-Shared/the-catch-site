## The story

On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil from government emergency stocks over four months, diesel first.
Farmers, truckers and drivers have watched diesel go from $3.81 to $6.20 a gallon in the U.S. since the war with Iran began in February.
Washington, with diesel at a record in September, had pressed Europe to release its stocks and weighed banning U.S. diesel exports.
The White House calls it 100 million barrels of diesel Trump negotiated from Europe, but it is the part of a release promised in March that governments had not yet delivered.
The energy agency's board meets next week to decide who releases what, and how much of it is diesel.

## The plan

1. On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil from government emergency stocks over four months, diesel first.
   - What exactly did they agree? 100 million barrels through the energy agency, starting at once, over four months, with a large diesel release in the first 20 days. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days"]
   - What is the energy agency? The International Energy Agency, whose 32 member countries hold emergency stocks and release them together. [doe-spr-release-2026-03-11: "32 member nations of the International Energy Agency"]
   - Oil or diesel? Both, in an unstated mix: the statement gives no split. [reuters-iea-board-2026-10-06: "They did not provide a breakdown of the volumes of crude, diesel and other products to be released, however, or say which countries would participate."]

## The event

On October 2, 2026, the G7 leaders agreed, "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled," to "a coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners." [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled, we will implement our commitments with a coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days"]

The sentence before it asks the agency to monitor the March commitments. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "The G7 requests the IEA to monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments. In this regard"]

The leaders also agreed to coordinate refinery maintenance, run refineries harder where feasible, and refrain from export restrictions on energy among themselves. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "We will coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries to prevent simultaneous capacity shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible"] [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries"]

The French presidency convened the video call; its readout says up to 100 million barrels of refined products, including diesel, and crude within four months. [elysee-g7-readout-2026-10-02: "produits raffinés, notamment de diesel et de pétrole brut, jusqu'à 100 millions de barils sous 4 mois"]

Hours later Trump said he would not authorize an export ban on diesel. [reuters-no-export-ban-2026-10-02: "he will not authorize an export ban on diesel, hours after G7 countries agreed to release diesel and crude oil from their emergency reserves."]

On October 7 the energy agency's member governments backed finishing the March release as soon as possible and putting diesel first. [iea-member-meeting-2026-10-07: "IEA Member governments expressed support for accelerating the oil stock releases announced in the Collective Action of March 2026 with a view to completing them as soon as possible"]

[iea-g7-meeting-2026-10-02: "refined-product flows remain severely constrained, with Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries exacerbating the situation for diesel"]

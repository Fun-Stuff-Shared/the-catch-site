## The story

On October 2 the G7 leaders agreed to release 100 million barrels of emergency oil over four months, with diesel first.
The barrels are meant to come from what governments, mostly in Europe, promised in March and had not yet released.
Diesel has run short since the Iran war choked Gulf fuel exports and Ukrainian strikes cut Russia's, and Washington pushed Europe with talk of a U.S. diesel export ban.
The White House calls it 100 million barrels of new European diesel, while the energy agency and Germany say it finishes the March pledge with no new barrels.
The agency's board meets on October 14 and 15 to work out who releases what.

## Outline

1. **The news.** The G7 agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels over four months, diesel first, "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled." On October 7 the energy agency said finishing the March release would bring about 100 million barrels to market, and member governments backed finishing it fast and putting diesel first. An internal European document, as Euronews reports it, says the 100 million can be covered inside the March action if everyone delivers; Germany says it will release no additional quantities. Diesel was $6.199 a gallon on October 5, down from a record $6.529 on September 21 and up from $3.897 on March 2.
2. **The story in order: whose barrels.** March: 32 agency members pledge 400 million barrels; the U.S. share is 172 million. The U.S. lends its share to companies in exchanges; awards add up to about 133.5 million barrels and the reserve's stock has fallen by 132.5 million since March 20; the September 29 offer of up to 40 million covers the rest of the 172 million (computed: 172 minus 133.5 is 38.5). By October 2 about 325 million of the 400 million had been released. Some countries released more than they pledged; the stocks pledged and not released come to about 100 million. Germany had released 600,000 of 2.65 million tonnes; France had about 8 million barrels left; Italy and Japan say they have met their pledges; the European document lists Greece, Italy and Portugal as done and France, Germany, the Netherlands and Spain as under assessment, with about 50 million barrels pending at EU level. "Commitments that have already been fulfilled" is the G7's way of counting what was released; Germany's ministry reads the sentence as the March obligations implemented through the 100 million.
3. **Why now.** The war that began when the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran in late February choked shipping through the Strait of Hormuz; Saudi Arabia and the UAE rerouted what they could through Yanbu on the Red Sea and Fujairah, until Houthi attacks and an attack on the pipeline to Yanbu cut those routes. Crude flows recovered more than fuel flows: Gulf diesel exports were a quarter of pre-war in August, and Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries plus Russia's export ban took most Russian fuel off the market; China suspended its refiners' fuel exports for October (four sources to Reuters). Europe buys about half its diesel imports from the U.S. Washington, with diesel at a record before the midterms, weighed an export ban and told France and Germany to release diesel. Each actor's stated reason, attributed.
4. **The sides.** Washington: Europe released "only a fraction," the U.S. is delivering; Trump says Europe agreed to a massive diesel release and the ban was never really on the table. Europe: Germany says the market did not take its barrels; the Commission rejects any ban; Macron says no threat was made; the EU will release more only after the agency assesses supply risk. What both agree on: the release goes through the agency and starts with diesel.
5. **The catch.** The 100 million is not 100 million new barrels: on the agency's own numbers it is about the size of what was pledged in March and never released. The White House's "100 million barrels of refined diesel" and Wright's "the majority diesel" are not in the G7 text, which gives no split. Whether an export-ban threat was made rests on unnamed sources against two presidents' denials; what Washington asked for is reported as 100 million barrels of diesel in 20 days, and as 120 million in six months.
6. **What is next.** The agency's board on October 14 and 15; tenders over the next two weeks; the agency's follow-up report "before 20 days"; the U.S. award on the October 6 bids; France's 10 million barrels to French distributors; the House export-ban bills sit in committee. Supply is still at risk: on October 6 Hormuz transits fell to a two-month low after a week of tanker attacks, and on October 8 a hurricane shut in a quarter of U.S. Gulf oil output and Brent rose about 4 percent. The board decision will settle who releases what; it will not settle whether prices fall.
7. **Dig deeper.** The joint statement, the French readout, the agency statements, the DOE awards, the EIA tables, the bills, the tax order.
8. **Background held.** How France sells its 10 million barrels (to distributors at the pre-crisis purchase price, through SAGESS, with weekly checks); the Rollins interview and the post that overstated it; the tax order; Ohio's tax holiday; Russia's Novak; the industry letter; prices of October 2.

## The event

On October 2, 2026, the G7 leaders agreed, "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled," to "a coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners." [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled, we will implement our commitments with a coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days"]

The sentence before it asks the agency to monitor the March commitments. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "The G7 requests the IEA to monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments. In this regard"]

The leaders also agreed to coordinate refinery maintenance, run refineries harder where feasible, and refrain from export restrictions on energy among themselves. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "We will coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries to prevent simultaneous capacity shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible"] [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries"]

The French presidency convened the video call; its readout says up to 100 million barrels of refined products, including diesel, and crude within four months. [elysee-g7-readout-2026-10-02: "produits raffinés, notamment de diesel et de pétrole brut, jusqu'à 100 millions de barils sous 4 mois"]

Hours later Trump said he would not authorize an export ban on diesel. [reuters-no-export-ban-2026-10-02: "he will not authorize an export ban on diesel, hours after G7 countries agreed to release diesel and crude oil from their emergency reserves."]

On October 7 the energy agency's member governments backed finishing the March release as soon as possible and putting diesel first. [iea-member-meeting-2026-10-07: "IEA Member governments expressed support for accelerating the oil stock releases announced in the Collective Action of March 2026 with a view to completing them as soon as possible"]

## Why: the stated reasons

- **The G7 leaders**: to stabilize supplies and "shield households and businesses from price shocks." [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "shield households and businesses from price shocks"] They condemn Iran's attacks and call for restored navigation through Hormuz. [govuk-g7-statement-2026-10-02: "immediate and full restoration of navigational rights and principles in the Strait of Hormuz"]
- **The energy agency, October 2 (Birol)**: crude exports from the Middle East have recovered, but "refined-product flows remain severely constrained, with Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries exacerbating the situation for diesel." [iea-g7-meeting-2026-10-02: "refined-product flows remain severely constrained, with Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries exacerbating the situation for diesel"]
- **The energy agency, September report**: product prices rose far more than crude; Gulf product exports were nearly 60 percent below February; Russia's product exports nearly halted after Ukrainian attacks. [iea-omr-2026-09: "the rise in both crude futures and physical prices pales in comparison with those for refined products, where market tightness is now most acute"] [iea-omr-2026-09: "refined product and LPG exports remain nearly 60%, or 3.7 mb/d, less than in February"] [iea-omr-2026-09: "Disruptions to Russia’s refining system and a near-halt to product exports following intensified Ukrainian attacks have compounded these losses"] US diesel 94 percent above pre-war in early September, Brent 45 percent. [iea-omr-2026-09: "94% above pre-war levels"] [iea-omr-2026-09: "45% above pre-war levels"]
- **The agency, October 7**: diesel first "given the current tightness in diesel markets." [iea-member-meeting-2026-10-07: "given the current tightness in diesel markets"]
- **Wright, September 29**: the U.S. and Japan are delivering; several European countries released "only a fraction." [doe-spr-exchange-2026-09-29: "While the United States and Japan are delivering on their commitments, several European member countries have released only a fraction of the crude oil and petroleum products they pledged."] Wright, September 30: supplies are "tight" because of lost exports from Russia, the Middle East and China. - **Bessent, October 1**: Europe should accelerate its existing commitments and add supplies. [bessent-post-2026-10-01: "Our European partners should accelerate delivery on their existing commitments and make additional supplies immediately available"]
- **Trump, September 30**: an export ban might help diesel "but it might raise the price of other things."

### C9 — Purge timing and eligibility

**Claim:** “A voter who neither answers the notice nor votes through the second federal general election after it is moved to purged status.”

**Record:** `hb1310-2023`, Section 23-15-152(4)–(5):

> “A registered voter ‘fails to respond to the confirmation notice’ if the voter ... fails to:
> (a) Respond to the confirmation notice; or
> (b) Update the elector's registration information.”

The law also states:

> “A registered voter who votes at least once in any election in the registered voter's county or municipality of registration ... or who is active or reserve military or who serves on jury duty or responds to a summons for jury duty shall not be purged.”

It requires failure to respond or update registration, together with failure to vote, before removal under this procedure. records(20261119-234504)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That failing to return the confirmation notice and not voting through the second federal general election are sufficient conditions for purging. The claim omits registration updates and statutory protections for military personnel and jury service.

### C54 — Purge eligibility broadened beyond the record

**Claim:** “Voters made inactive before the 2024 presidential election who have not voted since can now be purged.”

**Record:** `mt-2026-11-06` states:

> “Any Lafayette County voters in this experiment who were inactivated before the 2024 presidential election and who have not voted since then are now on the board to be purged.” 

The underlying law, `hb1310-2023`, requires failure to respond or update registration and failure to vote during the prescribed period. It also establishes exceptions involving military service and jury duty. records(20261119-234504)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That every voter made inactive before the 2024 presidential election who has not voted since is now legally eligible for purging.

The reporting concerns voters in the Lafayette County experiment. Although the statutory timing applies statewide, the claim omits additional conditions and exceptions that determine whether an individual registration may be purged. Being made inactive before the 2024 election and not voting afterward are not sufficient by themselves.

## Stated reasons

- **Why detonate.** EPA notice, verified: recrystallization raised sensitivity to friction and shock; moving the containers risks an accidental detonation; the secretary of the Army chose in-place detonation after looking at alternatives; the permit is to address "an imminent and substantial endangerment." Army Q&A, verified: destroying it in place is "the only responsible option." Telle, verified: cold would "risk further destabilization," so Monday is "the latest possible moment" before a forecast temperature drop. Barati, via Radio Iowa: the material crystallized after storage tanks were too dry.
- **Why delay three days rather than cancel.** Telle, verified: public safety, and time to "actively engage with state and local officials" before that temperature drop. Grassley, verified: "to further educate the public."
- **Why the emergency permit.** EPA, verified: the waste is stored where the existing RCRA permit does not authorize treatment.

## Claims to check

- **"The Army immediately told the public in March."** Telle’s post says that. The governor’s office says it learned on November 16. The dated public advisory is November 15. An open house was October 15, and the date was still "November," not November 20. Both can be partly true: local contact in October, statewide notice in mid-November. The word "immediately" is what the community note disputes.
- **"Rinse it off and it’s gone."** That is Barati to Radio Iowa, about property, not a line in the written advisory. The advisory says wet-clean surfaces and wash the car. Thorne’s point is that hosing moves lead into soil or water. The Intercept headline "Just Rinse It Off" is the outlet’s gloss.
- **"About 10,000 pounds" and "11,000 pounds."** Primary figures in documents I opened are 9,700 (lawmakers) and about 9,500 (Earthjustice). Mother Jones’s 11,000 is higher than those.
- **"The plume will coat Chicago" or "a hundred-mile swath" as a settled fact.** Maps were weather-dependent and were to be updated 48 hours, 6 hours, and hourly before the blast. Illinois EPA said Mercer County could be affected and that the maps did not state the conditions modeled. A 100-mile figure is in advocacy and news summaries of modeling, not in the advisory text I opened.
- **"Five days’ notice" as the whole record.** Five days is the gap from the November 15 advisory to November 20. The October 15 open house told Middletown a November detonation was coming, without the date or the regional plume maps.
- **"The Army forgot to warn people" (Mother Jones headline).** The plant published an October open house, a November 15 advisory, maps, and a shelter plan. Critics’ documented complaint is late and thin notice outside Middletown, especially to Illinois and to the governor.
- **Eric Sorensen’s party.** Duckworth’s release says Democrat. A CBS list called him a Republican.
- **"Hegseth’s Pentagon" ordered a bioweapons test.** I found no Hegseth statement on this event. The acting official who postponed it is Telle. Lead azide is a primary explosive used in detonators, which the Army Q&A states. It is not described as a biological weapon in any official record I opened.

## Competing explanations

- **Army and EPA account, the one in the permit and the Q&A.** A surveyed batch, stored wet, dried out and recrystallized. By March 2026 it was too sensitive to move or open. A national expert panel and the secretary of the Army chose two controlled detonations inside earth-covered concrete magazines, with sand barriers, wet soil, and misters, under an emergency RCRA permit, as the lowest risk compared with an unplanned blast in cold weather. Lead in the dust is real but, Barati says, a brief low dose, and rain or hosing deals with residue. Held by Telle, Nosse, Barati, and the EPA notice.

- **Least-bad blast, indefensible process.** Detonation may be the only safe disposal, but the Army and EPA did not show the alternatives, skipped comment, and gave the region days rather than months. Held by Sarah Green, Michael Schmidt, Rob Sand, the November 19 letter, Grassley on communication, Reynolds on notice, and Hinson.

- **Storage failure turned into a public-health event.** The Army let 68 drums degrade for about 20 years, then chose open detonation instead of a safer treatment the National Academy of Sciences has already described for munitions disposal. Held by Thien Chau and Earthjustice. Taylor adds that the action is reckless without an environmental and health impact statement.

- **The lead does not disappear.** Detonation may destroy the explosive, but lead is an element, so the dust settles on soil, farms, and water. Hosing relocates it. Held by Thorne, and in the Register’s paraphrase of Cwiertny. A Register opinion, which I did not open, makes the same point from the CDC’s position that no safe level of lead in a child’s blood has been identified.

- **Cold is the deadline, so delay has a hard stop.** Waiting past the forecast temperature drop raises the chance of an unplanned explosion. Held by Telle and by Barati’s "it could self detonate."

- **Monday is worse because the weekend rain is lost.** One X user argued that postponing past weekend rain leaves dry ground so dust sits longer. https://x.com/bbgm21e8/status/2123707090564944287. That is a forecast argument, not an official finding. Barati had told KCRG, in a report I did not open, that Saturday and Sunday rain "should alleviate" microgram-level lead.

- **Out-of-the-box technical alternative still exists.** Zach Lahn, partial post above, says people familiar with the material, including officials in Washington, think something other than detonation should be tried first. He does not name the method in the text I saw.

- **Bioweapons test, or a mineral land grab.** Posts from one account call the advisory "Bio-Weapons Testing" and tie it to a claim of precious metals under northeast Iowa. https://x.com/MSuckherbi55312/status/2123710912078954513. Nothing in the Army, EPA, or court record I opened supports either claim. Northeast Iowa is also not Middletown.

- **Ideological neglect at the Pentagon.** Occupy Democrats argues the short notice reflects Hegseth’s personnel purge. https://x.com/OccupyDemocrats/status/2123656208867369263. That is an inference. The records I opened show a March discovery, an October local meeting, a November 13 permit, and a November 15 regional advisory. They do not mention Hegseth.

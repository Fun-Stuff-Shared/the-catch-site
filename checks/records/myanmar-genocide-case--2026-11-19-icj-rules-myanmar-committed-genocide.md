## The story

1. On Nov. 19 the International Court of Justice, the UN's court for disputes between countries, ruled 9 votes to 6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017.
2. It found Myanmar's security forces killed at least 10,000 Rohingya and drove more than 700,000 into Bangladesh, intending to destroy the roughly 815,000 who lived in northern Rakhine State.
3. The ruling decides the case The Gambia brought in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention, leaving only the amount of compensation for a later phase.
4. The six dissenting judges agreed soldiers killed and gravely harmed Rohingya in three villages but said the evidence did not show that pattern across northern Rakhine or prove intent to destroy the group, and Myanmar's government called the ruling unfair.
5. Myanmar must investigate and, where warranted, prosecute those responsible, let the Rohingya return and report back within two years so the court can set compensation, but the court has no way to enforce that beyond the UN Security Council.

## The event

On 19 November 2026 the International Court of Justice, the UN's court for disputes between states, ruled in The Gambia v. Myanmar that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya [icj-myanmar-press-release-2026-11-19: "The Court
found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine State
during the 2017 “clearance operations”"]. The vote on that finding was nine to six [icj-myanmar-press-release-2026-11-19: "By nine votes to six"]. The court also found Myanmar failed to prevent genocide by people acting alongside its forces (nine to six), failed to punish it (eleven to four), failed to legislate (fourteen to one), and breached the court's 2020 order to preserve evidence (eleven to four); it found Myanmar did not directly and publicly incite genocide [icj-myanmar-press-release-2026-11-19: "However, the Court found that Myanmar did not directly and publicly incite the commission of genocide by private persons"]. The judgment is final and cannot be appealed [icj-myanmar-press-release-2026-11-19: "which is final, without appeal and binding on the Parties"].

## The people it happens to

The Rohingya are a mostly Muslim people of Rakhine State, in western Myanmar, whom the court found to be a distinct ethnic group [icj-myanmar-summary-2026-11-19: "the Rohingya of Myanmar constitute a distinct ethnic group"]. The court counted about 815,000 Rohingya living in northern Rakhine before 2017 as the part of the group targeted [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "approximately 815,000 Rohingya who lived in northern Rakhine State prior to the 2017"]. It accepted a UN estimate of at least 10,000 dead [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "The Court considers that the FFM’s estimate of at least 10,000 deaths is supported by the record"], and records that over 700,000 fled in 2017 [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "over 700,000 fled in 2017"]. It found mass rape, attacks on children, and shooting at people as they fled [icj-myanmar-summary-2026-11-19: "the Myanmar security forces systematically shot at Rohingya as they were fleeing"], and the razing of villages that were then built over [icj-myanmar-summary-2026-11-19: "a deliberate, organized process of
destroying Rohingya villages"]. About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees now live in Bangladesh [ap-icj-myanmar-genocide-2026-11-19: "About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees now live in Bangladesh"].

- The Gambia brought the case with the backing of the 57-member Organisation of Islamic Cooperation [aljazeera-what-ruling-means-2026-11-19: "was backed by the 57-member Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC)"]; Myanmar argued it was suing as the OIC's "proxy", and the court held that seeking others' support did not bar the case [icj-myanmar-order-2020-01-23: "the fact that The Gambia may have sought and obtained the support of other States or international organizations in its endeavour to seise the Court does not preclude the existence between the Parties of a dispute"].
- The Gambia's stated reason for suing: the Convention lets any party bring a case [nyt-icj-myanmar-genocide-2026-11-19: "international law gives countries the ability to act against genocide anywhere in the world"]. The court held that The Gambia may claim reparation in the Rohingya's interest, not its own [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "while The Gambia cannot seek reparation for itself"].
- Why the date: set by the court on 3 November (above).

## The sides

- The Gambia and the majority: intent to destroy is the only reasonable inference [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "the only
reasonable inference that can explain Myanmar’s actions is an intention to destroy a substantial part
of the protected group"]. The majority tested and rejected Myanmar's three alternatives [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "the Court cannot conclude, from all the evidence taken together, that an intent to carry out counter-insurgency operations, an intent to forcibly displace or an intent to punish the Rohingya population are reasonable inferences to be drawn"].
- Myanmar: its civilian government under Aung San Suu Kyi first defended the military at the court in 2019 (above); after the 2021 coup the opposition National Unity Government said it "respects the authority" of the court [dw-icj-myanmar-2026-11-19: "respects the authority"]. Its case: it fought insurgents; it denied the facts [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "Myanmar largely denies The Gambia’s account"].
- The six dissenting judges (Vice-President Sebutinde; Judges Tomka, Abraham, Xue, Nolte; Judge ad hoc Kress): intent not proven [icj-myanmar-joint-dissent-2026-11-19: "Our conclusion is that the commission of genocide by Myanmar has not been established"]; the majority departed from the 2015 test [icj-myanmar-joint-dissent-2026-11-19: "We do not see a compelling reason to depart from this jurisprudence"].
- Agreed by both sides of the bench: the Rohingya are a protected group, the 815,000 are a substantial part, and serious crimes were committed [icj-myanmar-joint-dissent-2026-11-19: "We have no doubt that Myanmar’s security forces committed serious human rights"]. The fight is over intent and the test for inferring it.

The record: in 2007 [icj-bosnia-press-release-2007-02-26: "Finds that Serbia has violated the obligation to prevent genocide"]. Difference: Serbia was found in breach of the Convention in 2007; what is first is a finding that a state itself committed genocide, as AP put it [ap-icj-myanmar-genocide-2026-11-19: "The decision marks the first time the court has ruled that a state committed genocide."].
- Al Jazeera: [aljazeera-convicts-2026-11-19: "UN top court convicts Myanmar of ‘genocidal intent’ against Rohingya"] and [aljazeera-world-reacts-2026-11-19: "World reacts to ICJ finding Myanmar guilty of genocide against Rohingya"]; UPI: [upi-icj-myanmar-genocide-2026-11-19: "Myanmar found guilty of genocide against Rohingya"]; the Guardian's analysis: [guardian-analysis-2026-11-19: "the first country in history to be found guilty of committing genocide"]; DW: [dw-icj-myanmar-2026-11-19: "The judges also found the country guilty of a series of other more specific violations"]. The record: [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "They are not of a criminal nature"]. Difference: no one was convicted; the court found the state responsible and left individuals to prosecution.

## What is next

- Myanmar's report on restitution and compensation is due within two years; the court will then set the amount in a later phase [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "Myanmar shall, within a period of two years of the delivery of the present Judgment, transmit to the Court a report setting out the measures of restitution that it has adopted"].
- Ordered now: investigate and if warranted prosecute; legislate; guarantees of non-repetition; restitution including return [icj-myanmar-summary-2026-11-19: "allow and facilitate the safe, voluntary and dignified return"]; compensation [icj-myanmar-press-release-2026-11-19: "to compensate the Rohingya group and its members for the damage caused"].
- Not ordered: citizenship for all Rohingya [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "ordering the provision of citizenship for all members of the Rohingya group is beyond the scope"].
- The six-monthly reports under the 2020 order end [icj-myanmar-judgment-2026-11-19: "Myanmar is no longer required to submit such reports"].
- Enforcement: the court has none of its own [ap-icj-myanmar-genocide-2026-11-19: "it has no way to enforce it beyond reporting noncompliance to the U.N. Security Council"].
- Enforcement path: [un-charter-chapter-14: "the other party may have recourse to the Security Council"]; veto [un-charter-chapter-5: "including the concurring votes of the permanent members"].
- Britain asked for a Security Council session; no date found as of 20 November [uk-fcdo-miliband-2026-11-19: "calling urgently for a UN Security Council session to discuss the judgment"].
- Separately, the ICC application against Min Aung Hlaing (deportation and persecution, not genocide) awaits a public decision [icc-bangladesh-myanmar-page: "bears criminal responsibility for the crimes against humanity of deportation and persecution of the Rohingya"].
- South Africa's genocide case against Israel is years from completion [guardian-us-eu-reaction-2026-11-20: "That case, which the US has pushed back on, is years from completion."].

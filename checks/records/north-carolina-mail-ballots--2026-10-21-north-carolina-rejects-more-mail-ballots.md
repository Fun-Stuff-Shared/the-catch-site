## The story

North Carolina is rejecting a larger share of mail ballots this fall after the State Board of Elections told counties on Sept. 4 to spoil any ballot that arrives with its inner envelope unsealed: 3.1% through Oct. 20, against 2.6% at the same point in 2022, the Board says.
In Wake and Mecklenburg counties, home to Raleigh and Charlotte, about 400 ballots had been rejected for an unsealed inner envelope by Oct. 21; those voters are mailed a new ballot or can vote in person, and in Mecklenburg 60 of 259 had.
A Republican lawsuit made the Board reconsider its 2024 ruling that one sealed envelope was enough, the Board's new Republican majority reversed it in February, and a federal judge refused on Oct. 19 to block the rule because voting had already begun.
Gov. Josh Stein says the rate is twice 2022's, but his figure rests on a volunteer tally from 27 counties that its publisher first compared with the state's 2022 rate and then with the national one; the Board's own counts show a half-point rise.
Ballots must arrive by 7:30 p.m. on Nov. 3, voters whose ballots are caught after Oct. 30 must vote in person, and the lawsuit goes on.

What happened (sentence 1; the event, what it means, where it stands):

A ballot whose inner envelope is unsealed is spoiled, even inside a sealed outer envelope; in 2024 one sealed envelope was enough [ncsbe-numbered-memo-2021-03-2026-09-04: "The ballot is inside an unsealed container-return envelope when it is received, regardless of whether it is in a sealed outer return envelope."].

## Why: the stated reasons

The Board: the statute requires a sealed envelope [ncsbe-numbered-memo-2021-03-2026-09-04: "Ballots must be “sealed in the container-return envelope.” N.C.G.S. § 163-230.1(d)"]; chain of custody [schroeder-opinion-2026-10-19: "The State Board reasoned that “the same chain of custody concerns could be present” with an unsealed container-return envelope as when the outer envelope is not sealed."]; Tyson: "They're worried about chain of custody issue like this ballot was tampered with" [abc11-2026-10-21: "They're worried about chain of custody issue like this ballot was tampered with or this ballot looks like there was something that happened to it on the way from the absentee voter to the Board of Elections,"]. Republican board member Stacy Eggers on Sept. 4: "The voter experience in casting their ballots will not change" [abc11-2026-09-04: "The voter experience in casting their ballots will not change,"]. Democratic member Siobhan O'Duffy Millen: an "end run around the process" [abc11-2026-09-04: "Now, to my mind, we're doing an end run around the process that we said we wanted to do and revising the numbered memo,"].

## The sides

The Board and the Republican intervenors: the rule follows the statute and the voter keeps every way to vote [ncsbe-hayes-statement-2026-10-20: "North Carolina voters can be assured that this ruling does not change how they cast their ballots or the instructions they have already received."]. Stein and the challengers: late changes cost votes [abc11-2026-09-04: "Today the State Board of Elections approved new ways for mail-in ballots to be rejected on technicalities"]. Both say the rate is up; they differ on by how much and on whether a reissued ballot is a lost vote.

## What is next

Mail ballots due 7:30 p.m. Nov. 3; early voting through 3 p.m. Oct. 31; ballots spoiled on or after Oct. 31 cannot be reissued by mail. The lawsuit continues, with answers due Dec. 1 [docket-1-26-cv-965: "Date of Last Known Filing: Oct. 19, 2026"]. The Board expects the final rate slightly above 2022's 2.2%. The formal rules await the General Assembly after the election.

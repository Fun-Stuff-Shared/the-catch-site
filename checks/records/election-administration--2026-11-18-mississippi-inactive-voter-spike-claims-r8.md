**C1 — [inactive-why]**

**Claim:** “Under Mississippi law, a voter becomes inactive when county election commissioners mail the voter a confirmation notice asking them to confirm their current address, which the commissioners send when they have reliable information that the voter has moved.”

**Record: hb1310-2023**, Section 23-15-152(2), states:

> “The election commissioners shall send a confirmation notice to the following:”

Subsection (c):

> “A registered voter who has failed to vote at least once in any election or update his or her registration during a period that begins in the year of a presidential preference primary and extends until the next general election for President of the United States that does not occur in the same year as the beginning of the period”

Subsection (d):

> “A registered voter if the registrar or election commissioners have received reliable information that he or she has moved within or outside of the state.”

Subsection (3):

> “The county election commissioners shall place any registered voter who has been sent a confirmation notice on inactive status in the Statewide Elections Management System.” records(20261120-053636)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That inactive status results from a confirmation notice triggered by reliable evidence that the voter moved. The statute also requires notices for voters who have neither voted nor updated their registration during the specified period, regardless of whether officials have evidence of relocation. Postal change-of-address information and notification of registration in another state are additional triggers.

The claim accurately describes one statutory trigger but presents it as the explanation for inactive status generally. That distinction is material to a story examining a spike in inactive registrations.

The short form accurately identifies the notice as the trigger for inactive status and does not independently introduce the same problem.

## Experts and commentators

**Rick Hasen, UCLA law professor, quoted by the Los Angeles Times on October 14** and carried on Election Law Blog. He said he is “not aware of anything like this ever happening this close to an election,” that the National Voter Registration Act “prevents systematic purges of voters 90 days before the election,” and that whether that rule covers removal of noncitizens “is currently before the U.S. Supreme Court, in an Arizona case the high court will hear in December.” He called the demand “legally questionable” and said inaccurate lists in other states could send the registrar “on a wild goose chase.” https://electionlawblog.org/2026/justice-dept-demands-california-prove-citizenship-for-lists-of-voters-or-withhold-ballots/

**Hasen, quoted by CNN on October 15:** “The federal complaint filed today is full of language about the supremacy clause and federal ‘preemption.’ But federal law does not preempt the state’s right to run its election and judge the qualifications of its voters.” CNN also says he expected the suit to “go nowhere.” https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/15/politics/trump-doj-sues-california-non-citizens-voting

**Hasen, to the Associated Press, quoted on Election Law Blog,** on the October 16 criminal warning: “It is deplorable that rather than work cooperatively and assist states in their election administration responsibilities, the federal government is making escalating threats and unreasonable demands beyond its power in the middle of an ongoing election.” https://electionlawblog.org/author/rhasen/

**Hasen, October 22–23, in his own posts.** He called the appeal of a not-yet-issued order irregular, wrote “I’m not aware of any case where a party, much less the DOJ, sought to appeal a district court’s failure to quickly enough schedule a hearing on a TRO when a hearing had already been set,” and said he expects the Ninth Circuit to deny it, or that the department may try to reach the Supreme Court if the circuit is slow. ## Stated reasons

- **Mullin, July and October 15:** only citizens should elect federal leaders; even one noncitizen vote “cancels out the ballot of an American citizen.” DHS compared public voter files with immigration records.
- **Blanche, October 15:** “State-facilitated voter fraud” from mailing ballots to people the government calls likely noncitizens, and a refusal to work with the federal review.
- **McCotter, October 15 and 19:** 18 U.S.C. § 611 makes noncitizen voting in a federal election a crime; California is “knowingly allowing” it by not sequestering ballots; the 28 charges show what that refusal risks.
- **Essayli:** California will not verify citizenship beyond the voter’s own attestation, so the federal government did the match itself. He says sequestration is not a purge. He also says officials who knowingly count those ballots can be aiders and abettors.
- **The complaint, as quoted by CBS:** “The Federal Government asked California to work collaboratively to identify any noncitizens registered to vote in the State. California made no serious effort to do so.” And: “This Court should enjoin California from presently tabulating vote-by-mail ballots from likely noncitizens in the upcoming federal election until the voter’s citizenship status can be determined.” https://www.cbsnews.com/news/justice-department-california-lawsuit-mail-ballots-noncitizens/
- **Weber and Logan:** they were given no methodology, no underlying evidence, and, at first, no list the Secretary of State could use. They say they have no authority to hold a registered voter’s ballot because a third party asserts noncitizenship, and that the demand arrived after ballots were already out.
- **Bonta:** the suit rests on possibilities, not proof of an unlawful ballot, and it asks a court to rewrite election administration during an election.

## Claims to check

- **“20,000 confirmed noncitizens” versus “likely.”** Essayli wrote on October 16 that the 20,000 were “confirmed by a review of federal databases,” and on October 20 that the government had “verified” at least 20,000. The October 15 press release, the complaint language Democracy Docket catalogs, the TRO papers, and McCotter’s “we’re not perfect” line use “likely,” “highly likely,” “suspected,” or “reasonably believe.” The October 13 letter, per Democracy Docket, also asked the state to check “whether any citizens are listed.”
- **“California refused to look.”** McCotter said that on October 19. Weber’s office says it was not given the list. Logan says the county did not open the thumb drive, then did respond in writing on the morning of October 15. By October 20 the county had reviewed a 200-record sample. “Refused to sequester” is what the county admits. “Refused to look” does not match the later sample.
- **The list is an Los Angeles list.** Logan said on October 15 that it covered people “throughout California.” The county later counted 7,035 Los Angeles addresses out of more than 20,000.
- **All of them were mailed a live ballot.** Essayli says they were. Flores says 112 of 200 sampled Los Angeles records were canceled or inactive, so no general-election ballot was mailed.
- **190,832 fell to 20,000 because the first number was wrong.** Bonta said that to the AP, as Newsweek reports it. DHS’s October 16 post says the opposite: 190,832 was the preliminary pool, and 20,000 is the part manually reviewed so far. Both cannot be the department’s position. Weber’s October 14 release still calls the July figure unsubstantiated because the methodology was never produced.
- **The 28 arrests prove the other 19,972 are noncitizens.** Essayli said the arrests prove the list is accurate. The department’s own criminal release calls the 28 “likely noncitizen registrants” on a larger list, and McCotter said some names may turn out to be citizens. Twenty-eight charged people, all still only accused, do not establish the status of the rest.
- **“The crime is occurring in real time.”** That is Essayli’s interview line, as Fox reports it. The county sample found that most sampled records had no voting history, and some listed a U.S. birthplace. Returned ballots from people on the list are not the same thing as ballots already counted from confirmed noncitizens.
- **Judge Carter denied the TRO.** The department’s phrase is “constructive denial,” meaning he had not ruled by its noon deadline. His October 23 order still sets a hearing and says he will rule promptly after October 27. There is no denial order in the sources I opened.
- **One court PDF header shortens the district number to 2:26-cv-1205.** The docket number used by the court, PacerMonitor, and the parties is 2:26-cv-12025.
- **The Secretary of State’s October 14 release spells the DHS secretary “Mullen.”** The July 16 letter and the October 15 DOJ release use “Mullin.”

## Competing explanations

Each is the strongest form I found from the people who hold it.

**The Justice Department and DHS.** California mails a ballot to every registrant and will not check citizenship against federal immigration records. A multi-agency match, followed by manual review, found more than 20,000 likely noncitizens still registered. The July figure of up to 190,832 was the unverified pool, not a retracted claim. Sequestering ballots is ordinary election administration, not a purge. Twenty-eight green-card holders on the list have now been charged, which shows the match finds real noncitizens who registered and, in many of those cases, voted. Counting the rest would dilute citizens and violate 18 U.S.C. § 611. Holders: Blanche, Mullin, McCotter, Essayli, the DHS account, and the complaint.

**California and Los Angeles County.** The list is an unverified third-party match, delivered mid-election, partly aimed at the wrong county, and already shown to include inactive registrations and people whose records say they were born in the United States. State law tells officials how to test eligibility. A criminal ban on noncitizen voting does not let the federal executive order a state to hold registered voters’ ballots. The suit is a political stunt that risks disenfranchising citizens. Holders: Weber, Bonta, Logan, Flores, and the state’s October 20 brief.

**Election-law critics of the suit.** Federal law does not let the executive displace state election administration on a bulk list inside the final weeks, the NVRA’s 90-day limit is at least arguably implicated, similar federal lists have been wrong, and appealing a judge’s silence is not an appeal of an order. The likely audience is public doubt about the midterms, not a remedy the court will grant. Holders: Hasen, and the “long shot” framing in CNN’s account of legal experts. Levitt and Trepel add that the criminal threat against officials who follow state counting rules is legally empty.

**A post-election placeholder.** The suit was filed late on purpose, after ballots were out, so that if results are close the unsegregated 20,000 can be cited to challenge outcomes, intimidate officials, or justify a federal presence around voting. Holders: Berwick and Marsden in the Chronicle op-ed. Hasen has also written that the department may be building a path to the Supreme Court because it expects to lose before Carter.

**Mistaken registration, not a scheme.** The people charged are lawful permanent residents, and several accounts describe Real ID, canvassers, or poll workers as the reason they thought they could vote. Noncitizen voting that does happen is often a green-card holder misunderstanding the rule, and 28 cases among more than 20 million registrants are isolated crimes, not a stolen election. Holders: NPR’s summary of the research literature, Hasen on accidental DMV registration, Shah’s statement to KCRA, and Bonta’s “drop in the bucket” line. Essayli rejects this as an excuse for not checking the list.

**The number is a floor, and officials are hiding a larger problem.** Twenty thousand is only what could be manually reviewed without the state’s help. California will not open its rolls or check the names because the real total is higher. The 28 prosecutions are what could be charged in a week, the way traffic stops do not capture every speeder. Holders: Essayli on X and in the Fox interview. A further version, which I did not verify against a filing, is El País’s report of another 17,000 names.

**Commercial or database matching is producing false hits, as it has before.** The July number came from commercial data and DHS holdings, the department would not show its method, and other states have seen citizens flagged by the same kind of match. The Los Angeles sample is the first public look at this list and already shows inactive records and U.S. births. Holders: Weber, American Oversight’s account of the July figure, Reichlin-Melnick, and Hasen’s comparison with lists in other states.

What I could not find, and so am not treating as fact: the text of Judge Carter’s order beyond the CourtListener extract; the Ninth Circuit clerk’s order itself; the names of the proposed intervenors; a congressional vote on this list; an ACLU, Brennan Center, or RNC statement on this specific suit; and Trump’s jail post in his own words.

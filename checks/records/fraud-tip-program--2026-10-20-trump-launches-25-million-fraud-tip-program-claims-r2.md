### Findings

**C3 — [terms-charges-defined] — Charging trigger is broader than the terms support**

**Claim:** “Under the terms, a tip qualifies when it leads the Justice Department to file criminal charges, and no reward is paid while the charges are sealed unless the department decides otherwise.”

**Record:** `doj-whip-terms-2026-10-20`

> “Criminal charges means the Department obtained and unsealed an indictment, information, or criminal complaint.”

> “No reward is payable while the relevant criminal charge or charges remain under seal or otherwise non-public, unless the Department determines in its sole discretion that such a reward should be paid prior to unsealing.” records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That filing criminal charges is sufficient to satisfy the program's eligibility trigger. The terms define qualifying criminal charges as obtained **and unsealed**. The separate discretionary exception for payments before unsealing does not eliminate that distinction.

The short form, “Trigger: criminal charges,” also omits the unsealing requirement.

**C5 — [faq-staff-review] — Review arrangement generalized beyond portal submissions**

**Claim:** “Tips are reviewed by staff under the head of the Justice Department's fraud division, according to its FAQ.”

**Record:** `doj-whip-faqs-2026-10-20`

> “Whistleblower submissions made through the online portal are reviewed by staff under the direction of the Assistant Attorney General for the National Fraud Enforcement Division.” records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the FAQ establishes this review arrangement for every tip received by the program. It establishes the arrangement specifically for submissions made through the online portal. The department also permits consideration of tips submitted through other channels, but the cited passage does not establish their review arrangements.

**C61 — [treasury-routes-tips] — Announced processing procedure stated as established operation**

**Claim:** “Treasury reviews, sorts, de-duplicates and refers tips to the agency, inspector general or law enforcement office it picks.”

**Record:** `treasury-intake-notice-2026-10-22`

The Federal Register notice describes the collection as follows:

> “The Department of the Treasury is launching a government-wide intake and referral portal”

It states:

> “Treasury will collect information necessary to receive, review, triage, categorize, de-duplicate, and refer allegations to the appropriate Federal agency, inspector general, law enforcement organization, or other authorized entity for review and potential action.”

The notice describes the processing procedure in prospective terms. It also identifies the publication as a notice of information collection and request for comment. records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Treasury had already established and was carrying out every described processing step as of October 24. The notice establishes Treasury's intended responsibilities and procedures, not that all those procedures were already being performed. The reporting portal separately confirms that reports go to Treasury for review, but it does not independently establish that every described step, including de-duplication, was operational.

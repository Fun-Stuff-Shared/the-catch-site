## The explanations

Question 1: Is Trump's November 12 statement that nobody has been killed in the last five months true, and what was he counting? His window, in his words, is five months back from November 12, so from about June 12. His November 8 version said "six months, five months, six months"; the six-month window (from about May 8) adds no death, since the Epic Fury table shows no death in May or June [dcas-epic-fury-2026-11-13: "MARCH 2026: hostile deaths 7"].

- A. True as he means it: no U.S. troop has been killed by the enemy in five months (Trump). If true: no Pentagon "killed in action" record dated after about June 12. Should not find: hostile deaths from July to November.
- B. True of the war as the administration books it: Operation Epic Fury is over, and deaths after July 7 belong to "overseas operations", not to the Iran war (the Pentagon's accounting; Trump's July 10 notice to Congress that hostilities had resumed). If true: the department treats post-July 7 deaths as outside the Iran war and does not tie them to Iran. Should not find: Pentagon records attributing those deaths to Iran or counting them with the war's dead.
- C. Literally true: no service member died at all in the window. Should not find: any death in the Pentagon's tables between June 12 and November 12.
- D. Loose but roughly right: the last combat death was so long ago that "five months" is fair shorthand for "a long time". If true: the last killed-in-action death falls near or before mid-June.
- E. False: the Pentagon has announced deaths inside the window, including troops killed by enemy fire (the Washington Post).
- F. True in that the July deaths came from Iran-backed militias, not Iran, so they are not the Iran war's dead (raised by the orchestrator; no outlet or official was found putting it forward). If true: the Pentagon or Central Command attributes the July attacks to proxies. Should not find: either calling the attackers Iranian.

## The story

1. On November 12, Trump told radio host Hugh Hewitt that in the war with Iran "nobody's been killed in the last five months."
2. The Pentagon's own records list nine U.S. service members who died in those five months, four of them killed in action by Iranian missiles and a drone in Jordan and Iraq on July 17 to 19.
3. He said it while arguing the war had cost few American lives, nine days after telling the Wall Street Journal the war was partly to blame for Republican midterm losses.
4. The Pentagon counts deaths since July 7 on a separate "Overseas Operations" page, but its own releases call the four killed in action in Iranian attacks, and Trump met their coffins at Dover on July 22.
5. The Intercept sued the Pentagon on November 13 for its records on how the casualty count has changed.

## The event

On Thursday, November 12, 2026, Trump called in to Hugh Hewitt's show on the Salem News Channel. Asked whether he would hesitate to use force against Iran, he said: "Nobody’s been killed in the last five months" and "We haven’t lost a soldier in five months, and we control the Gulf" [hewitt-trump-interview-2026-11-12: "We haven’t lost a soldier in five months, and we control the Gulf."]. ## Why: the stated reasons

The setting: on election night, November 3, Trump told the Wall Street Journal the war was partly to blame for Republican losses [wsj-trump-iran-midterms-2026-11-04: "“Iran was a piece of it,” he said"]. In the week after, he defended the war in the Douthat interview, to reporters, and to Hewitt.

Trump's stated point in both interviews is that the war has been cheap in American lives next to past wars and Russia's: "You know, in Russia, they’re losing 25,000 people a month" [hewitt-trump-interview-2026-11-12: "in Russia, they’re losing 25,000 people a month between Russia and Ukraine, mostly soldiers"]; "This war, seven months, 18 people killed" [cbs-douthat-trump-transcript-2026-11-08: "This war, seven months, 18 people killed. That includes Venezuela, 18."]. The Pentagon's stated reason for the separate category: Epic Fury "has concluded" [ap-new-category-2026-07-28: "because Operation Epic Fury has concluded, the military has decided to classify casualties tied to the latest strikes in the new category"].

## The sides

Both sides agree on the dates and names of the dead; the Pentagon's own records are the source on both.

## What coverage says that the records do not

- Mediaite says 23 have "been killed". Twelve of the 23 are non-hostile deaths (crash, illness, suicide, pending) [wapo-two-deaths-2026-10-29: "In all, there have been 11 deaths from enemy fire and 12 nonhostile deaths, according to DCAS."]; "killed" fits the 11 hostile deaths.

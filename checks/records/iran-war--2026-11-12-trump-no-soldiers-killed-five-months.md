## The explanations

Two questions, kept apart after review round 1: whether the statement is true, and whether any way of counting makes a narrower version of it true. Who killed the soldiers and which operation the Pentagon files them under are different facts, and the sources differ on the second.

## The story

1. On November 12, Trump told radio host Hugh Hewitt that in the war with Iran "nobody's been killed in the last five months."
2. The Pentagon announced four soldiers killed in action on July 17 to 19, three in an attack on a base in Jordan and one in Iraq, and Trump went to Dover on July 22 to meet their coffins.
3. He made the claim while arguing the war had cost few American lives, nine days after telling the Wall Street Journal it was partly to blame for Republican midterm losses.
4. The Pentagon files every death since July 7 on a separate "Overseas Operations" page, not under Operation Epic Fury, which it says ended in May, and only on the Epic Fury page has no one been killed by enemy action since March; Trump himself counted the July dead in September among "18 people" "killed in Iran".
5. The Intercept sued the Pentagon on November 13 for its records on how it has counted the war's dead.

## Why: the stated reasons

The setting: on election night, November 3, Trump told the Wall Street Journal the war was partly to blame for Republican losses [wsj-trump-iran-midterms-2026-11-04: "“Iran was a piece of it,” he said"]. In the week after, he defended the war in the Douthat interview, to reporters, and to Hewitt.

Trump's stated point in both interviews is that the war has been cheap in American lives next to past wars and Russia's: "You know, in Russia, they’re losing 25,000 people a month" [hewitt-trump-interview-2026-11-12: "in Russia, they’re losing 25,000 people a month between Russia and Ukraine, mostly soldiers"]; "This war, seven months, 18 people killed" [cbs-douthat-trump-transcript-2026-11-08: "This war, seven months, 18 people killed.

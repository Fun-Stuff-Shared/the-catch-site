## The story

On Nov. 19, Mississippi's Democratic lawmakers asked the Legislature to investigate why more than 230,000 voters were listed as inactive before the Nov. 3 election. Inactive voters stay registered but can vote only by affidavit ballot, which county officials accept or reject later, and about a quarter of this year's were rejected. The list grew by about 60,000 in the two years after the 2024 election, about four times the largest rise between a presidential election and the next midterm in federal counts back to 2016, after the secretary of state began sending every county address flags drawn from Experian credit data. Secretary of State Michael Watson says the 230,000 built up over years under a law that requires clean rolls, Democrats say the credit data flagged eligible voters, and Experian says its data was never meant to validate voter records. No hearing is set, and Democrats plan a bill in January to let flagged voters answer before they lose active status.

## What coverage says that the records do not

- UPI: "Michael Watson, flagged 230,000 voter registrations as inactive" [upi-2026-11-04: "flagged 230,000 voter registrations as inactive"]. The 230,000 is everyone in inactive status, built over years; the office's 2025 lists flagged about 113,000 [sos-column-2026-11-16: "flagged approximately 113,000 individuals"].
- WAPT: Watson "used data from the credit reporting company Experian, which listed 230,000 registered Mississippi voters as inactive" [wapt-caucus-2026-11-19: "which listed 230,000 registered Mississippi voters as inactive"]. Experian listed no one; the 230,000 is the whole inactive list, and the Experian-era lists flagged about 113,000 with Postal Service data included.
- AP: "an increase of nearly 60,000" [ap-2026-11-19: "an increase of nearly 60,000 since the presidential election two years ago"]. On the federal series the rise is about 64,000; AP's 2022 figure of 185,000 differs from EAVS's 159,292.
- AP: "Experian has not provided services to Mississippi since early 2025" [ap-2026-11-19: "Experian has not provided services to Mississippi since early 2025"]. Not a contradiction of the July 2025 rollout: the data was bought once and used through 2026 [wlox-2026-11-15: "We uploaded the rest of it to finish it up in 2026,"].
- Watson to the Clarion Ledger, as the paper reports it: voters "would only be marked as inactive if they didn't reply" [clarion-2026-11-18: "voters would only be marked as inactive if they"]. The statute and his own office's release put voters on inactive status when the card is sent [sos-experian-release-2025-07-21: "will be sent a confirmation card and then placed on “Inactive” status"].
- Watson to WLOX: "The process didn't change" [wlox-2026-11-15: "The process didn’t change."]. His own column called the commercial data "the first of its kind in Mississippi" [sos-column-2025-02-19: "Accessing commercial data, like Experian’s, is the first of its kind in Mississippi."].

## The story

The Justice Department launched a program on October 20 that pays people $10,000 to $30,000 when their tip about health care or benefits fraud leads to criminal charges, with $25 million from the Treasury.
In the Oval Office the same day, President Trump described something else: a 1% commission on recovered money with no limit and no charges needed, decided by him as "the arbitrator."
The program follows an October 1 directive from the department's new fraud division and is pitched as a faster alternative to whistleblower programs that pay only after a case ends.
The written terms say the department alone decides, in its sole discretion, and four days later they still say $10,000 to $30,000; critics say a cash bounty with the president as arbiter invites politically targeted tips.
Nothing has changed the terms yet, and the $10,000 for YouTuber Nick Shirley, whose video predates the program, has not been explained under them.

## The event

On October 20, 2026, the Justice Department's National Fraud Enforcement Division announced the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program [doj-release-2026-10-20: "the first ever whistleblower rewards program that pays an up-front award for tips leading to criminal charges"]. Tips go through a White House portal [doj-release-2026-10-20: "Members of the public can report fraud by going to america.gov/fraud."], which forwards them through Treasury [america-gov-fraud-2026-10-24: "It goes straight from your browser to the Treasury Department. America.gov keeps nothing."]. Trump and Vance announced it in the Oval Office that afternoon [factbase-transcript-2026-10-20: "So, today, I'm thrilled to announce the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program"].

- Agreed: rewarding whistleblowers is not itself in dispute; the fight is over the president's role and which tips get pursued.

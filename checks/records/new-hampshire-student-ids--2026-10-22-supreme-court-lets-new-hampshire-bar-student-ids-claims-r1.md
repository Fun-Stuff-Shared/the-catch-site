## Findings

### C4 — [stay-duration] Short form misstates how long the stay can last

**Claim:** “The pause lasts through the state's appeal in the First Circuit and any timely petition to the Supreme Court: it ends automatically if the Court declines to hear the case, and if the Court takes it, it lasts until the Court's judgment.”

**Short form:** “Lasts until the appeal ends”

**Record:** `scotus-26a512-order-2026-10-22`

**Exact words:**
> “are stayed pending the disposition of the appeal in the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit and disposition of a petition for a writ of certiorari, if such a writ is timely sought.”

> “In the event certiorari is granted, the stay shall terminate upon the sending down of the judgment of this Court.”

records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form suggests the stay necessarily expires when the First Circuit appeal ends. The Supreme Court's order allows it to continue through subsequent Supreme Court proceedings if a timely petition is filed. The full claim holds; the short form does not.

### C11 — [april-directive] Short form expands the prohibition beyond the record

**Claim:** “On April 23, Secretary of State David Scanlan's office announced that from June 2 student IDs could not be used as proof of ID to register, to request an absentee ballot, or to check in to vote.”

**Short form:** “Apr. 23: Secretary bars student IDs for any use”

**Record:** `sos-release-2026-04-23`

**Exact words:**
> “Effective June 2, 2026, student IDs cannot be used as proof of ID when registering to vote, requesting an absentee ballot, or checking in to vote in an election.”

records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form suggests student IDs were prohibited for every purpose, rather than the three election-related identity-verification uses identified in the announcement. The full claim holds; the short form exceeds its scope.

### C17 — [state-records-route] Required training is presented as completed training

**Claim:** “State records can also prove who a voter is: with the voter's permission, officials can confirm identity at registration from New Hampshire motor-vehicle or vital records, and supervisors are trained to use registration data to satisfy the ID requirement at the polls.”

**Record:** `rsa-659-13`

**Exact words:**
> “The secretary of state shall provide training for supervisors of the checklist on how the nonpublic data in the statewide centralized voter registration database may be used to satisfy voter identification requirements.”

records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The claim says supervisors have received training in using registration data to verify voter identity. The cited statute establishes a requirement that the Secretary provide training, not evidence that the training has occurred.

The separate registration record supports the availability of searches to help establish qualifications, but it does not establish that supervisors have completed the training described in this claim.

### C49 — [catch-8-1] The record does not establish that the post was widely shared

**Claim:** “A widely shared post called the order 8-1; the order records only Justice Jackson's dissent and no other votes.”

**Records:** `daugherty-x-2026-10-22` and `scotus-docket-26a512`

**Exact words — X post:**
> “🚨 BREAKING — WIN AT SCOTUS: US Supreme Court 8-1 GREEN LIGHTS New Hampshire's ban on using "student ID" as valid identification for voting in elections 👏🏻”

**Exact words — Supreme Court docket:**
> “Justice Jackson dissenting.”

records records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The record establishes that Eric Daugherty published a post characterizing the decision as 8–1. It contains no repost counts, engagement figures, or other evidence establishing that the post was widely shared.

The remainder of the claim holds: the Supreme Court's order identifies Jackson's dissent without disclosing the other justices' individual votes.

### C1 — Short form misstates when Trump addressed the U.S. attorney

**Claim:** At an Oct. 13 rally in Wasilla, Trump said he had told Alaska's U.S. attorney, "I hope you're going to make sure there's no cheating," adding that "any place you have rank(ed) choice, you have damn bad cheating."

**Short form:** Oct. 13: Trump to the U.S. attorney, "no cheating"

**Record:** `beacon-sues-2026-10-23`

**Exact words:**
> Alaska U.S. Attorney Michael Heyman was at Trump’s rally in Wasilla this month.

> “I told him — I just shook his hand, took a picture — I said I hope you’re going to make sure there’s no cheating, because any place you have rank(ed) choice, you have damn bad cheating. And we can’t let that happen. So, we’re going to defend the integrity of Alaska’s elections,” Trump said at the Oct. 13 event.

records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form suggests Trump directly addressed the U.S. attorney with those words on Oct. 13. The record establishes that Trump recounted an earlier conversation during his Oct. 13 rally. It does not establish when that conversation occurred.

The full claim is supported. The discrepancy is confined to the short form.

### C2 — Ranked-choice explanation overstates which ballots count and how a majority is determined

**Claim:** Alaska's general election is counted by ranked choice: each ballot counts for its highest-ranked remaining candidate, a candidate ranked first on more than half the active ballots wins, and otherwise the last-place candidate is defeated and those ballots move to each voter's next-ranked remaining candidate.

**Record:** `doj-complaint-2026-10-22`

**Exact words:**
> The election board shall initially tabulate each validly cast ballot as one vote for the highest-ranked continuing candidate on that ballot or as an inactive ballot. If a candidate is highest ranked on more than one-half of the active ballots, that candidate is elected and the tabulation is complete.

> Otherwise, “votes cast for the defeated candidate shall cease counting for the defeated candidate and shall be added to the totals of each ballot’s next-highest-ranked continuing candidate.”

records

The sample ballot also states:

> Rank as many or few candidates as you like.

records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The explanation suggests every ballot continues counting for a remaining candidate. The record expressly permits inactive ballots, including when no ranked candidate remains.

The phrase "a candidate ranked first on more than half the active ballots wins" also obscures that the majority threshold applies to the highest-ranked *continuing* candidate, including votes transferred from eliminated candidates. A winner need not have been originally ranked first on a majority of ballots.

The general description of elimination and transfer is supported, but these distinctions affect how readers understand the counting process.

**C3 and C4 hold.** Both accurately reflect their cited Associated Press records, including the attribution of the legal assessment to experts rather than presenting that assessment as an established outcome.

## Claims to check

- **“The FAA confirmed an L3Harris update.”** CBS attributes that to sources. Duffy and @FAANews have not said L3Harris, FTI, or an update. L3Harris had not commented when CBS published.
- **“It started at Midway because of routers.”** That is an airline source telling CNN what the FAA told airlines. Chicago had an earlier “equipment outage” ground stop. NBC Chicago said it was unclear whether that local stop and the national stop were the same event.
- **“Trump said it was not a cyberattack.”** Mullin said Trump was briefed and that there was no indication of an attack. I did not find Trump’s words. MS NOW said he had not addressed it publicly shortly before 6 p.m.
- **“Only the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11” versus “the third.”** Outlets use both. The prior one they agree on is January 2023, the NOTAM system. Some writers count 9/11 plus 2023 plus today as three events and then call today the second since 9/11. Both phrasings appear; they are not the same claim.
- **Airborne totals of 3,100 and 4,042.** Both are attributed to Flightradar24. They are not the same snapshot.
- **FlightAware totals from about 7,000 to more than 13,000 to 21,504.** Different clocks, and the largest figures appear to include the whole day’s weather delays. NPR noted nearly 7,000 delays on Thursday, before this stop.
- **A National Weather Service radar outage.** CNN, on the page I opened: NOAA communications director Chris Vaccaro said there is no known NWS radar or communications outage. An outdated Anchorage page had said there was.
- **“Controllers were laid off and that caused this.”** No official has said that. It is a social-media claim. Reuters describes chronic controller shortages as background strain, not as Friday’s trigger.
- **“Verizon’s FENS cutover took the system down.”** Verizon’s circulated statement says its network is fully functioning and it is not aware of an air-traffic issue. I found no official tying Friday to the FENS transition.

## Competing explanations

Each in the form its holders actually use. None is an FAA finding except the first.

1. **Temporary telecommunications outage at the centers.** Held by the FAA, Duffy, and Mullin. Radios, and in airline accounts radar and handoff automation, failed widely enough that departures were unsafe. Mullin: not a nefarious attack “as of yet,” and “We’re not ruling out anything.”
2. **L3Harris FTI update rebooted and crashed.** Held by “multiple sources” in CBS’s story, then repeated by market-news accounts. The private network that connects the air-traffic system, operated by L3Harris under the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure contract, was updated, rebooted, crashed, and the communications outage followed. L3Harris has not confirmed it. This is the most specific cause in a major newsroom, and it is still unsourced in public.
3. **Network routers, beginning at Chicago Midway and cascading.** Held by an airline source on CNN, describing what the FAA told airlines. Compatible with the L3Harris account if the routers sit on that network, but CNN’s source did not name L3Harris.
4. **A software update, under FBI inquiry, and not a crime.** Held by a person familiar with the matter, via Bloomberg. The FBI does not believe it was a cyberattack or criminality. Whether a bad update is the cause is an inquiry, not a conclusion. A New York Post source said the stop “was potentially caused by an upgrade to new software.” That source did not name the software.
5. **SMART, the new AI traffic tool.** Held as an open question by Mullin (“We’re looking at that”; “You just can’t rule out anything”), and as a timing argument online: the FAA said on November 10 it would use SMART in all 22 centers, three days before those centers lost communications. The vendor denies it outright. Ars Technica, in a search extract of a November 13 story, quotes the FAA’s November 10 description: after a September rollout in the Washington region, SMART would be used “in the airspace of all 22 centers” to reduce delays and fuel burn. GeekWire says SMART is a 12-year, $875 million Air Space Intelligence contract and that lawmakers asked in October to pause it in the Washington area. I did not open that October letter, so I am not quoting it.
6. **Cyberattack or terrorism.** Held widely on social media in the first hour. Rejected, for now, by Mullin, by a DOJ source speaking to MS NOW, by two law-enforcement officials speaking to CNN, by a Bloomberg person familiar with the FBI view, and by a senior security source on CNN who said there was still no indication of an attack while cyber teams kept looking. Mullin’s “not ruling anything out” is the remaining official hedge.
7. **The Microsoft 365 outage that began November 12.** Held by people connecting two outages on successive days, including pilots speculating on ATC audio, according to a Stars and Stripes/Los Angeles Times account. Mullin: “I don’t see any reason to connect those two at all. Those are separate issues.” I did not open Microsoft’s own status post.
8. **Verizon or the FENS transition.** FENS is the replacement for the aging FAA network. A September 2026 trade write-up, not a Friday statement, says Verizon was selected FENS prime in spring 2023 and AT&T was named lead in August 2026, with completion aimed at the end of 2028. https://saallianceair.com/article/faa-fens-network-modernization-2028-deadline-what-travelers-should-watch Friday’s holders of a Verizon theory are mostly inference from “telecommunications.” Verizon’s statement denies any known air-traffic issue on its network. A retired FAA technician, @CD_Kramer, argued the real story is that FTI should already have been replaced by FENS and that Congress never funded the infrastructure. That is a policy claim about why the network is fragile, not a claim that Friday’s cutover failed. https://x.com/CD_Kramer/status/2121370871390482778
9. **Staffing cuts.** Held on social media as the cause. No official, airline, or union statement I found blames Friday’s stop on layoffs or on a controller shortage. Shortages are the long-running context Reuters cites, alongside aging equipment.
10. **Aging infrastructure, no single trigger yet.** Held by Reuters as the frame, and by commentators such as Gunther Eagleman: if it was not a hack, the system is old enough to shut the country down. Duffy’s public position is still “technicians are investigating.”

What is established tonight is the order, the time, the official label “telecommunications outage,” and that departures were still stopped past 7:30 p.m. ET with a written window to 11 p.m. What is not established is the root cause. The L3Harris-update account and the Midway-router account are the two detailed stories in circulation, and both still rest on unnamed sources.

## The story

The Supreme Court on October 22 paused a federal judge's order, so New Hampshire can again tell poll workers not to consider a student ID at all, even as backup proof of who a voter is. On November 3 a college ID counts for nothing, so a student without a government photo ID needs an election official who knows them or a record the state already holds, such as a New Hampshire motor-vehicle record. The Court said federal courts should not change election rules on the eve of an election, and the judge ruled 32 days before this one. New Hampshire says the legislature meant to end student IDs for voting; the challengers and Justice Jackson say the law only took them off the automatic list and the Secretary went further. The pause is not a ruling on the law, it lasts through the state's appeal and any trip back to the Supreme Court, and the case is headed for trial.

## Why: the stated reasons

- The Court: lower federal courts should ordinarily not alter election rules on the eve of an election [scotus-26a512-order-2026-10-22: "This Court has repeatedly emphasized that lower federal courts should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election."].
- The Secretary, Oct. 6: the legislature intended no student IDs; a split rule would make no sense [cod-supplemental-appendix-2026-10-19: "The court’s interpretation defies common sense and defeats the clear legislative intent of HB 323 and SB 223"].
- The state's application: late re-education is impossible [nh-stay-application-2026-10-15: "Re-educating local election officials and the public in a short time is practically impossible"].
- The bill's sponsors, as recorded by the court: student IDs may be held long and lack expiration dates [dnh-orders-appendix-2026: "First, lawmakers argued that student IDs are unreliable because students may hold onto them for long periods and they sometimes lack expiration dates."]. Rep. Donald McFarlane, the sponsor, to AP: colleges lack government controls [pbs-ap-nh-student-ids-2026-10-22: "said he sponsored the bill to eliminate student IDs for voting because colleges don't have the same controls when issuing those cards as government agencies."].
- A companion bill's sponsor, Sen. Sullivan, cited out-of-state students; the state disavowed that reason in court [dnh-orders-appendix-2026: "The Assistant Attorney General representing the State “disavow[ed]” this rationale in court."].

## The sides

The state: the Secretary's guidance is the state's election rules and Purcell bars late change even if the judge's reading is plausible [nh-stay-reply-2026-10-20: "But even if the district court based its order on a plausible construction of state law, Purcell dictates that it should have stayed its hand."].

## What is next

The First Circuit appeal, No. 26-2132, is pending [nh-stay-application-2026-10-15: "On October 14, the First Circuit denied that motion in a 2-1 decision, with Judge Dunlap indicating that he would have granted a stay."]. The stay ends automatically if certiorari is denied, and otherwise when the Court's judgment issues [scotus-26a512-order-2026-10-22: "Should certiorari be denied, this stay shall terminate automatically."]. The district court anticipates a trial on the merits [dnh-orders-appendix-2026: "Counsel for both parties would be wise, when this case moves from the preliminary stage to a final trial on the merits, to seek to develop a more fulsome factual record."]. The challengers say they will keep litigating [fsfp-statement-2026-10-22: "We will continue to vigorously litigate this case in the defense of the fundamental right to vote of young voters across New Hampshire."]. The general election is November 3, with no early in-person voting [cod-opposition-2026-10-19: "New Hampshire has no early in-person voting. The general election is November 3."].

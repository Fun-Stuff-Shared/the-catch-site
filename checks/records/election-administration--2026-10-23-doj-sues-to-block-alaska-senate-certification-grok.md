## Officials who acted

- **United States, through Associate Attorney General Stanley E. Woodward, Jr. and Assistant U.S. Attorney E. Bryan Wilson**, filed *United States v. State of Alaska*, D. Alaska, **3:26-cv-00376-SLG**, **Oct. 22, 2026, 4:49 p.m. AKDT**. Complaint for declaratory and injunctive relief against the State of Alaska; **Carol Beecher**, Director of the Division of Elections; the Election Boards; and the State Ballot Counting Review Board. [CourtListener complaint](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75009254/1/united-states-v-state-of-alaska/); [docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75009254/united-states-v-state-of-alaska/); [PDF](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.akd.80263/gov.uscourts.akd.80263.1.0_2.pdf).
  - Verbatim, ¶2: “Daniel J. Sullivan decided to run for Senator in 2026 as a Republican with the intention of misleading supporters of Senator Sullivan into voting for him. The sole purpose of his candidacy is to benefit the Peltola campaign by reducing the number of votes for Senator Sullivan.”
  - Verbatim, ¶5: “The United States accordingly brings this lawsuit to protect the integrity of the 2026 Senate election in Alaska.”
  - Verbatim, ¶41–42: “Given the substantial likelihood that the inclusion of Daniel J. Sullivan will confuse or mislead voters looking to cast a vote for Senator Sullivan, and thus distort the will of Alaskan voters, there is a substantial likelihood that a certification of the election will reflect an electoral process that lacked integrity.” Relief is sought “if: (1) Senator Sullivan does not win the election; and (2) had all votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan been cast instead for Senator Sullivan and Alaska’s rank-choice process played out accordingly, Senator Sullivan would have won the election.”
  - Verbatim prayer ¶43–44: declare that certifying such a result “would violate Defendants’ duties under the NVRA to ‘protect the integrity of the electoral process.’ 52 U.S.C. § 20501(b)(3)”; and “[Defendants] from certifying any result that may be impacted by voter confusion … pending an investigation to determine whether votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan were meant to be cast for Senator Sullivan.” (¶44 is missing a verb; the docket PDF shows that gap.)
  - Legal hook, ¶36–37: Attorney General civil-action power under **52 U.S.C. § 20510(a)** to carry out NVRA Chapter 205, plus the NVRA purpose clause **§ 20501(b)(3)**.
  - Assigned to **Judge Sharon Louise Gleason** (Obama appointee). Cause listed as 28 U.S.C. § 1331; nature of suit 441 voting. [Docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75009254/united-states-v-state-of-alaska/).

## Claims to check

1. **“DOJ is blocking any Peltola win.”** Complaint’s test is two-part: senator loses **and** reallocating all Daniel J. first-choice votes (plus RCV) would make the senator the winner. Broader headlines (some AP/Seattle Times phrasing; Mother Jones “If Peltola wins”; Beacon photo caption “prohibit anyone but Sullivan”) overshoot the prayer. ## Competing explanations

Each in the strongest form its holders give.

1. **Federal integrity / spoiler theory (DOJ, Trump, Beecher’s June letter, GOP complainants).** Daniel J. Sullivan is not a good-faith candidate. He copied the senator’s short name, party, and site look to siphon Republican votes for Peltola. NVRA’s integrity purpose, plus Beecher’s finding, lets the United States stop certification of a tainted close loss and investigate voter intent. Trump: Democrats planted a fake; he ordered the suit. Strongest texts: complaint ¶2, 40–44; Beecher letter; Trump “I’m the one that said do it.”

2. **Preemptive power play to hold the Senate (Peltola, Child, Croft, Whitehouse, Doggett, Schumer, Pressley, Hovland).** With polls tight and the Senate in play, Trump is using DOJ to telegraph that a Democratic win will not be certified. It is “a despicable political threat to overturn an election” (Child) and “corruption” (Peltola). Timing 11 days out, Trump’s credit, and Blanche’s same-day refusal to rule out certification fights are the evidence.

3. **Legally empty NVRA-preamble case (Muller, Hasen, Becker, Gupta, Levitt, Diaz, Ginsberg, Olson).** Even if name confusion is real, § 20501(b)(3) creates no duty and no ballot-access or certification power. States run elections. Alaska courts already kept the candidate on. Secret ballots cannot be re-interviewed. The missing verb in ¶44 is of a piece with the theory. Strongest: Muller “one of the worst”; Hasen “preamble … gives no power”; Becker “absolutely no authority.”

4. **Real confusion, wrong lawsuit and wrong time (Morley, partly Ginsberg).** Sham-candidate worry is legitimate. This complaint is still frivolous because it is late, because NVRA does not authorize it, and because the Senate, not DOJ, is the judge of Senate elections if the margin is inside Daniel J. Sullivan’s pile.

5. **First Amendment / ballot-access rights (Somin, Dunbar, Gray, Daniel J. Sullivan).** Qualified citizens may run. Suppressing a candidate for possible confusion is unconstitutional. State courts already said there is no “good-faith” extra qualification. Daniel J.: patriotic challenge to the incumbent, not a plant.

6. **Friendly fire / state already fixed it (Murkowski, Muller “friendly fire”).** Alaska Republicans run elections. They labeled the incumbent and stripped the other man’s party. Voters do not need Washington’s help. Filing against Beecher after she tried to remove the man, and after the state supreme court reversed her, sues the state’s own officials.

7. **RCV already solves the named harm (Muller, Beacon analysis, Dupree replies).** A voter who wants the senator can rank both Sullivans. Confused first-choice votes for Daniel J. can transfer. Anchorage Press review (Beacon) said Daniel J.’s primary support clustered in left-leaning districts, which undercuts the “confused Republicans” story.

8. **Senate as backup venue (Morley).** If Peltola’s margin is smaller than Daniel J.’s votes, contest the seat in the Senate (1975 New Hampshire; 1924 Iowa analogies), not in this NVRA case.

9. **Minority / less-documented: DOJ line attorneys were overruled.** Somin: maybe “pressure from on high.” Ginsberg: “a client who is demanding it.” Fits Trump’s Friday claim. No internal memo found.

10. **Minority: criminal conspiracy still in the background.** NBC reported in July that federal and state investigators were looking at a conspiracy. Trump and officials hinted at a criminal case (Beacon). Thursday’s filing is civil, does not name Daniel J. as a defendant, and does not charge a crime. Status of any criminal inquiry: not found.

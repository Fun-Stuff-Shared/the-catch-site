### 6. The record overstates the confidentiality protection for tipsters and leaves out a distinct risk.

**What is wrong:** Q5 describes the terms as protecting a tipster's identity. The full rules qualify that protection substantially. DOJ can disclose identifying information when required in proceedings and can share it with other enforcement agencies for specified purposes. A tipster may have to testify or produce evidence.

Anonymous submissions must be made through an attorney, and DOJ must verify a recipient's identity before paying an award.

**Why it changes the story:** The record considers the risks to people *named in tips* but omits a serious risk to people *submitting them*. A public-facing reward program may require a participant to become an identifiable cooperating witness, potentially exposing that person to retaliation or other consequences.

This is not hypothetical speculation about how officials might misuse discretion; it is an obligation and limitation stated in the program's own terms. It materially qualifies the impression that whistleblower confidentiality is assured.

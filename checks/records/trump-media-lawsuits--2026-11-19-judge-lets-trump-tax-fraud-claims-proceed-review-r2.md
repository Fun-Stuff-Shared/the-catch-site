### 1. The record leaves unverified a central factual premise in Trump's complaint: that the alleged tax fraud was never investigated.

On October 2, 2018, New York's Department of Taxation and Finance publicly confirmed that it was reviewing the allegations in the Times investigation and pursuing appropriate investigative avenues. Bloomberg reported that state tax authorities had opened an investigation.

This matters because Trump's second amended complaint alleges that neither he nor his father had ever been investigated for tax fraud. The state's announcement directly challenges that premise, which the brief leaves as an unadmitted lead.

The announcement does **not** establish that prosecutors opened a criminal case, that tax fraud occurred, or that the review produced an enforcement action. Those distinctions remain unresolved. But the absence of criminal charges is also not conclusive evidence against the underlying allegations: the original 2018 investigation reported tax experts' assessment that criminal prosecution for the decades-old transactions was likely time-barred.

### 2. Trump's original, substantive defense to the tax allegations is missing from the lineage.

In October 2018, Trump's attorney Charles Harder did considerably more than point to the absence of criminal charges. He categorically denied fraud and tax evasion, asserted that Trump had virtually no involvement in the disputed transactions, and said licensed attorneys, accountants and appraisers had handled them. He further maintained that tax authorities examined the filings, required minor adjustments and approved them.

These were assertions by Trump's lawyer, not independently established findings. They nevertheless constitute a materially different defense from the one principally preserved in the brief.

This changes the understanding of the dispute's origins. Trump's position was not simply that no prosecution established a crime; it was that the transactions were lawful, professionally supervised and largely outside his personal control. The record does not test that stronger contemporaneous account against the reporters' evidence.

### 3. The record does not distinguish the book's research from the earlier investigation's research.

The defense evidence preserved in the brief emphasizes approximately 100,000 pages examined for the 2018 investigation and expert quotations from that article.

But in February 2024, Craig and Buettner gave a firsthand account of their subsequent work on *Lucky Loser*. They said they had begun the book in 2020, interviewed hundreds of additional people and obtained extensive new documents from different periods of Trump's life. Penguin Random House separately describes the book as drawing on more than 20 years of confidential Trump tax information, including his tax returns.

This is material to the judge's central reasoning. The ruling turns on an inference that the authors made complex accusations from incomplete information without obtaining a necessary qualified opinion. The book's independently accumulated evidence is therefore a distinct part of the factual foundation that must be assessed.

Additional records and interviews do not themselves establish that the authors obtained the particular expert opinion Merryday considered necessary. But treating the 2018 investigation's documented source base as the effective measure of the 2024 book's research leaves that inference insufficiently tested.

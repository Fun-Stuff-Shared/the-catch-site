**The story in five sentences**

1. Early on October 3, Anna Yagi, 39, of Naha, was found strangled in a hotel room she had entered with a man about two hours earlier. Her wallet and backpack turned up near the hotel the same day.
2. That night police spotted Lance Cpl. Devin Jacob Ballard, 20, a Marine from Futenma, walking alone on Gate 2 Street in Okinawa City, 25 km away. He was arrested before dawn on October 4 on suspicion of robbery-murder and told police "I don't know." Prosecutors have up to 23 days from the arrest to decide whether to indict.
3. Because Japanese police caught him off base, he is in Japanese custody. The custody fight that shaped past cases, starting in 1995, never came up, and the U.S. ambassador says that shows "the SOFA is working."
4. Tokyo protested at every level within a day. The U.S. pledged cooperation, and on October 6 it ordered a 48-hour stand-down, an alcohol ban and a 30-day midnight curfew for every service member on Okinawa, but no apology. The new governor, elected three weeks earlier with the ruling party's backing, says he wants an apology if guilt is established.
5. The fight now is the one Okinawa has had for decades:
   - Okinawa says discipline measures have failed and the SOFA itself must change. Those measures include an apology and curfew in 2012, a curfew in 2016, the 2024 liberty order, joint patrols on the very street where he was found, and a forum.
   - Tokyo offers talks on "effective measures" and says only that the agreement is kept under review.

3. **The sides, in their own words.**
   - **Okinawa:** "Operational improvements have reached their limit" (prefecture, since 2003). It wants the SOFA reviewed, accountability for commanders, and an apology if he is found guilty.
   - **The U.S.:** The allegations don't represent Marine values. It is cooperating fully, the SOFA "is working," and it won't comment on an ongoing investigation, so no apology. It answers with a stand-down and a curfew.
   - **Tokyo:** Protest, discipline, and prevention. It fixes problems one at a time with supplementary agreements, not by reopening the treaty. Strengthening defenses and reducing Okinawa's burden must go "together." Inside the ruling party, the LDP's security chief asks, "should every case end with only a protest?"
   - **Ballard:** Denies it. No lawyer has spoken.
   - **All agree:** Japan has the case, the U.S. is cooperating, and prevention has to improve.
   - **Actually in dispute:** whether discipline measures can work at all, or whether the agreement itself must change.

4. **The catch.**
   - **Where and when he was arrested.** AP's first version and NPR said Naha's entertainment district, late Saturday. It was Okinawa City, before dawn Sunday. AP's corrected text is the one PBS carried.
   - **Cases are not people:**
     - 2025 had 101 cases involving 80 people, not "100+ arrests."
     - Since 1972 there have been 6,409 cases involving 6,285 people, in Okinawa. They were not "6,000 arrested in Japan."
     - The "80 in 2024, highest in 20 years" claim is right, if it counts people.
   - **"Denied the charges."** He hasn't been charged.
   - **"Stole her wallet and backpack."** That is the police suspicion; both were recovered the same day.
   - **Jurisdiction is not custody.** Being off duty gives Japan the case. Being arrested off base by Japanese police gives Japan the man.
   - **70% is land, not facilities.** Okinawa has 70.3% of exclusive-use area but 40.8% of the facilities. U.S. troops in Japan number about 60,000 (USFJ), not the 50,000–55,000 outlets gave.
   - **The family's statement.** Outlets quoted the grief and left out the request for privacy.

5. **What this won't settle.**
   - The indictment decision is due within 23 days.
   - The curfew runs from October 9 to about November 8, followed by a liberty-policy review.
   - The prefectural assembly votes on October 9, and Koja meets Takaichi on October 7.
   - Nothing here changes the custody rule for the next case where the U.S. holds the suspect first.

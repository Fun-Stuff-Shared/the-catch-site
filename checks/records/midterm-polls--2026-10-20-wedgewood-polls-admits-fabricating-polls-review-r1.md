The record is incomplete. The most consequential gaps concern a fabricated poll designed around a candidate’s scandal, evidence involving a related polling operation, the actual effects of the false numbers, and conclusions the evidence matrix treats as settled without sufficient support.

1. **A fabricated Maine poll did more than report an election margin.** On July 6, Wedgewood released a survey claiming that 75% of Maine voters wanted Democratic Senate candidate Graham Platner to withdraw if another scandal emerged. It also claimed that 65% of Harris voters favored withdrawal and that Troy Jackson would perform better against Susan Collins. Wedgewood said it had rushed the release because news was imminent; an allegation against Platner was reported that day. The Hill published a dedicated story, and Mediaite published one it later corrected. This is a major omission because the fabricated numbers purported to measure whether a candidate should remain in a race, not merely which party was ahead. It also supplies a potentially important test of the theory that Wedgewood was trying to help progressive candidates: this survey could instead have damaged one. **Sources:** [The Hill's July 7 report, preserved by AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/poll-majority-maine-voters-want-030326000.html); [Mediaite's corrected July 6 report](https://www.mediaite.com/politics/whopping-majority-of-maine-voters-said-theyd-ditch-graham-platner-over-another-scandal-right-before-another-scandal-broke/).

2. **The related SoCal Strategies investigation is a substantive development, not incidental background.** On October 22, FiftyPlusOne published a second investigation based partly on information from the source who raised concerns about Wedgewood. It reported doubts about some SoCal Strategies polls, an October 20 interview with a SoCal affiliate, and the subsequent disappearance of the firm's website, X account and Substack. FiftyPlusOne removed two disputed SoCal polls and stopped accepting new ones, while retaining older surveys. This changes the apparent scope of the case: investigators were examining potentially related activity beyond Wedgewood. It does **not** establish that SoCal's surveys were fabricated. **Source:** [FiftyPlusOne's October 22 SoCal investigation](https://blog.fiftyplusone.news/p/more-pollster-funny-business-uncovered-socal-strategies?action=share).

3. **The money-motive matrix omits independently checked trading patterns preceding SoCal poll releases.** BETINT examined 2,458 publicly visible trades from an account suspected of being associated with Wedgewood's operator. It identified bets placed three to nine hours before three SoCal Strategies survey releases, each anticipating the direction of the forthcoming poll. In one documented example, the account bet against Thomas Massie nine hours before SoCal published a poll putting his challenger ahead. The account holder's connection to Wedgewood remains unconfirmed, and the trades do not establish wrongdoing. But the matrix tests the betting hypothesis principally against the account's small **Wedgewood-related** profits. That excludes a materially different, evidence-backed possibility: benefiting from advance knowledge of polling releases associated with an earlier operation. **Source:** [BETINT's October 22 trading-record analysis](https://www.betint.net/p/kalshi-account-tied-to-fake-pollster).

4. **The Wisconsin market reaction was more substantial than the record's characterization conveys.** BETINT reconstructed David Crowley's contract falling from 23% to 13% within 15 minutes of Wedgewood's July 21 poll, with trading around **70 times its normal hourly pace** and approximately **$45,000 in new money** entering within a day. Kalshi instead described a temporary six-point effect on Crowley's price. Those measurements have not been reconciled. The missing volume evidence matters because it shows significant trading activity around the release even if no substantial profit by a suspected Wedgewood-associated account has been established. **Sources:** [BETINT's market analysis](https://www.betint.net/p/kalshi-account-tied-to-fake-pollster); [Kalshi's October 22 findings](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-wedgewood-fake-polling-did-not-meaningfully-impact-markets).

5. **The McMorrow comparison obscures what was distinctive about Wedgewood's fabricated result.** A legitimate Susquehanna survey also put McMorrow at 9%, but had **49% undecided**. Wedgewood assigned her the same 9% while claiming only **6% undecided**, portraying a far more settled race. Its invented general-election matchups also made her the weakest Democrat against Republican Mike Rogers. Consequently, the existence of other polls showing single-digit support does not adequately test whether Wedgewood helped create the perception that McMorrow's campaign had definitively collapsed. There is an additional provenance issue: MIRS says it declined to publish the earlier Mitchell poll after McMorrow challenged its open-link methodology. That dispute does not prove Mitchell's results were false, but it matters when those results are used to corroborate the campaign narrative. **Sources:** [Michigan Public Radio Network's comparison](https://www.wvpe.org/michigan-public-radio-network-news/2026-10-22/michigan-pollsters-react-to-fake-polling-scandal); [MIRS's account of the Mitchell dispute](https://home.mirs.news/post/wedgewood-admits-fabricating-polls-mcmorrow-their-experiment-damaged-campaign).

6. **A major aggregator's successful rejection of Wedgewood is absent from the vetting account.** RealClearPolitics says it excluded Wedgewood not only from its averages but also from the polls displayed on its site. Its polling director cited the firm's nonexistent track record, placeholder-style website and lack of identifiable staff. This is a meaningful counterexample to the claim that the firm could pass the industry's checks universally. It also distinguishes two forms of scrutiny that the matrix currently treats together: checking a source's explanations and identifying enough independently verifiable information to accept it at all. **Source:** [RealClearPolitics' October 22 account](https://www.realclearpolitics.com/articles/2026/10/22/why_realclearpolitics_didnt_fall_for_the_fake_polls.html).

7. **The earlier Median Strategies incident establishes a closely comparable precedent for the “social experiment” explanation.** On August 17, the Los Angeles Times reported that Median Strategies acknowledged fabricating a Los Angeles mayoral poll after repeated questions from the newspaper. Median described its operation as an experiment testing whether unverified polling could spread. Mayor Karen Bass had already promoted the fabricated result. This occurred while Wedgewood was operating and before its October admission. It does not demonstrate coordination or imitation, but it makes the recurrence of the same explanation after journalistic scrutiny material to evaluating Wedgewood's claimed purpose. **Source:** [Los Angeles Times, August 17](https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2026-08-17/poll-showing-bass-leading-raman-by-double-digits-was-bogus-company-says).

8. **The motive matrix rules out Wedgewood's experiment explanation on evidence that does not exclude it.** The Times and FiftyPlusOne asked questions; Wedgewood supplied false answers. That disproves the narrow assertion that nobody attempted vetting, but it does not disprove the possibility that someone intentionally submitted fabricated answers to test whether those checks would detect fabrication. Likewise, the timing of the October 20 admission strongly supports the investigators' suspicion of a cover story, but it does not establish that no experiment was originally intended. The record cannot distinguish a preplanned but deceptive test from a post-exposure justification merely by showing that Wedgewood lied to its reviewers. **Sources:** [FiftyPlusOne's investigation](https://blog.fiftyplusone.news/p/fiftyplusone-just-caught-a-pollster); [Silver's October 22 assessment](https://www.natesilver.net/p/fake-polls-are-a-real-problem).

9. **The partisan-bias matrix tests a broader allegation than the strongest evidence supports.** Silver's 0.6-point Democratic average is derived from comparable general-election matchups, with retrospective averages for some early polls and several races excluded for lack of a benchmark. His own analysis identifies unusually favorable numbers for particular progressive candidates and warns against drawing much from three examples. The absence of a consistent statewide Democratic bias therefore does not exclude **selective manipulation of particular primaries or candidates**. Conversely, a 0.6-point mean across a small, selectively comparable set does not by itself rule out the serious answer that there was no *meaningful systematic* partisan lean. The distinction changes which explanations can legitimately survive the matrix. **Source:** [Silver's analysis and methodological notes](https://www.natesilver.net/p/fake-polls-are-a-real-problem).

10. **The post-correction inventory misses other live displays of fabricated data.** Prediction Edge's Georgia Senate page continues to list Wedgewood's October 2–3 result among its latest polls, directly beside a polling average that it attributes to FiftyPlusOne. PollingSource still labels **23 Wedgewood polls as active** and displays the fabricated results without a fraud warning on the pollster page. It is not established that either site's current calculated averages actually include the data. Nevertheless, the continuing presentation of those results as polling evidence means USA Today's live blog is not the only located, unflagged exposure. This directly changes the correction and remaining-distribution account. **Sources:** [Prediction Edge's Georgia Senate page](https://www.predictionedge.com/elections/us-senate/georgia-race-winner-by-party/); [PollingSource's Wedgewood page](https://pollingsource.com/pollsters/wedgewood-polls).

11. **The FCC development lacks the legal threshold that determines whether an investigation could produce an enforcement action.** Beyond her October 21 response, Commissioner Anna Gomez sent an October 23 warning to the National Association of Broadcasters. She argued that the FCC's news-distortion policy requires evidence of intentional fabrication or distortion by a broadcaster, not merely reporting information later shown to be false. The broadcast-hoax regulation is narrower still: it addresses knowingly false reports about crimes or catastrophes that directly cause substantial public harm. Nothing in the record establishes that a licensed broadcaster knowingly aired fabricated Wedgewood numbers. This changes the significance of Representative Stephanie Bice's request: a request for investigation is not evidence of conduct meeting the FCC's enforcement standards. **Sources:** [Gomez's October 23 letter as reported by Radio World](https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/business-and-law/fccs-anna-gomez-sounds-alarm-over-election-polling-ahead-of-midterms); [47 CFR §73.1217](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/73.1217).

12. **The stated absence of forecast effects is stronger than the cited forecast evidence.** Silver reported that FLIPR's forecasts and averages were *basically* unchanged, but explicitly cautioned that concurrent releases of other polls confounded the comparison. State Navigate's own account found small changes, including a 1.1-point change in Nebraska. Decision Desk HQ's finding was narrower: removal changed no projected seat totals or chamber-control probabilities. These are not interchangeable measures. The evidence supports little measurable effect on the reported major forecast outcomes, not a demonstrated absence of any effect on forecasts or averages. That distinction matters because the story uses forecast stability to evaluate whether the fabricated numbers changed anything. **Source:** [Silver's October 22 account](https://www.natesilver.net/p/fake-polls-are-a-real-problem).

13. **The record does not substantiate its implied connection between money collected and fabricated polling services delivered.** Its verified financial evidence establishes a $500 Nebraska fundraiser and appeals for funding, but Nebraska is precisely the one survey Wedgewood claims to have conducted. Kalshi separately asserted that political organizations paid poll purveyors tens of thousands of dollars without supplying payers, transactions, dates or underlying records. Those statements cannot establish that Wedgewood received tens of thousands, sold fabricated polls, or retained money for surveys it never conducted. The distinction materially affects the competing explanations of financial fraud, paid political influence and an independently financed operation. **Sources:** [Kalshi's October 22 statement](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-wedgewood-fake-polling-did-not-meaningfully-impact-markets); [Silver's assessment of the unresolved crowdfunding question](https://www.natesilver.net/p/fake-polls-are-a-real-problem).

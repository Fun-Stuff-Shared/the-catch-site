### C1 — [below-threshold] — Scope overstated

**Claim:** “The new data-center control applies only where a data center gives such companies more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP; below that, this control requires no license, though BIS says an older control still applies when a China-headquartered company is the main (over 50 percent) remote user.”

**Short form:** “Below 12 million TPP: outside the new control”

**Record:** `fr-pi-2026-23529` — § 744.24(a)(1):

> “more than 12,000,000 ‘aggregate TPP’ either at one data center or through ‘linked data centers’”

The provision also covers data centers that are “providing or will provide” the specified access.

**Record:** `fr-pi-2026-23529` — § 744.24(a)(2):

> “BIS may inform persons, either individually by specific notice or through amendment to the EAR, that a license is required for the export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) of specified items to certain persons or destinations, because there is an unacceptable risk of use in, or diversion to, the activities specified in paragraph (a)(1) of this section.”

 records(20261118-021542)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:**

The categorical statement that computing below 12 million TPP falls outside the new control omits two material qualifications.

First, the threshold can be reached through linked data centers, even if an individual facility falls below it. It also applies to intended future access, not exclusively computing already being provided.

Second, § 744.24(a)(2) establishes an additional licensing mechanism based on unacceptable diversion risk. BIS can impose licensing requirements on specified transactions through individual notice or regulatory amendment without first establishing that the prohibited end use is already occurring.

Consequently, the claim and its short form overstate the certainty of the below-threshold exemption.

The description of the older § 744.23 control is supported by the record and is not independently disputed.

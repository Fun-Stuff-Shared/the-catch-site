## The story

On November 12 the State Department made public its first accounting of the U.S.-held account for Venezuela's oil money: $12.26 billion came in from January to September and $12.11 billion went back out at the Venezuelan central bank's request. Nearly all of it was sold as dollars through 20 Venezuelan banks to businesses and people the report does not name, and the report says the central bank uses the bolívars it takes in for government spending such as public payrolls. The report, dated October 29, followed months of Democratic demands for the audits officials had promised, and came out two days after the Times reported a confidential copy. President Trump says the oil money has paid for the war many times over and in July said it could go to the military, but the account pays out to Venezuela and no record shows billions reaching the United States; and Rubio told Congress KPMG audits every disbursement, while the report says KPMG's work, paid for with Venezuela's money, is not an audit. Democrats take both chambers of Congress on January 3, with the senators' questions about who bought the dollars still open.

## The plan

Headline: The U.S. took in $12 billion for Venezuela's oil. Nearly all of it went back.

What happened (sentence 1): the event, what it means, where it stands
1. What is the account? Under the January 9 order, buyers of Venezuelan oil, gold, minerals and fertilizer pay into a U.S. Treasury account; the money is Venezuela's, held by the U.S. [eo-14373-order-2026-01-09: "constitute property of the Government of Venezuela"]
2. How much? $12,263,715,308.36 in, $12,107,622,199.55 out, $168,558,474.25 left, January 3 to September 30 [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "$12,263,715,308.36"]
3. Who decides? The central bank asks; State and Treasury approve each payout [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "Any disbursement of funds from the Venezuela Custody Account requires authorization from the DoS and U.S. Treasury"]
4. Where it stands: State made it public November 12; dated October 29; the Times had a confidential copy November 10.

Where the money went (sentence 2): opens on "where did the $12.11 billion go?"
1. Three dollar channels to 20 banks, $11.96 billion allocated ($192.9 million of it in banknotes not yet released); vendors $133.1 million; $15.0 million and $192.9 million in banknotes still held on September 30 (figure: the payout ledger as a table).
2. What is a currency intervention? The central bank sells dollars to banks at a rate it sets and takes their bolívars; banks sell the dollars to customers [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "the BCV debits each bank's account for the corresponding VES value"]
3. Who got the dollars? Businesses $9.37 billion, people $2.87 billion, by the banks' data, which also carries $346 million of IMF money; food and consumer goods $3.35 billion first; State and KPMG hold the names, the public version gives none.
4. Where did the bolívars go? The report: to government spending "such as to fund public sector payrolls"; Rodríguez says 99 of every 100 dollars paid wages and pensions, on figures for two funds through November 12; nothing ties the two.
5. What did the $133 million buy? Medicines and PAHO $57.4 million; oil, power, coal and fertilizer operations $35.6 million; KPMG, lawyers and banknote logistics $28.8 million; memberships in regional central-bank bodies $1.7 million; passports, market data, embassy repairs, industrial chemicals and air-traffic links $9.5 million (table of the largest lines).
6. Which banks? Five of 20 took 83%; state-owned Banco de Venezuela the most, $2.88 billion. The rate of each sale is not published, so who gained from cheap dollars cannot be measured.

The Catch (sentence 4): opens on Trump's November 11 sentence
1. What he said November 11, after "got very little from any of these wars"; and on July 27, asked about the "$13 billion": "we've paid for that war many times over", "Goes toward running the country", "It can go to the military. You know, Congress has to approve it." That ties the claim to the account's money.
2. The account: by his own order Venezuela's; 98.7% paid back out at Caracas's request; nothing to a U.S. agency.
3. Outside the account: the "gift" cargo was bought at a discount (Post); the U.S. holds a 35% stake in NABEP's parent the White House values at "up to hundreds of billions" (link the August 31 story); no record shows a payment from it. No record shows billions reaching the U.S. from anywhere. The Catch says no more than that. Reference, one clause: an independent, partial estimate puts the operations' cost at $4.7 billion or more (Costs of War).
4. Second item, the audit: Rubio, June 3, "Every single disbursement is audited by KPMG", KPMG "paid by the Venezuelan government out of those funds"; the report: not an audit or attestation. Kozak's April line as the members quote it, one clause; BDO's $155,000 limited review of May and June for the central bank, results unpublished, one clause.
5. Third item, as a row: the Times' "mostly debts to other governments and internationals organizations" for the $130 million, repeated by El Diario; the public ledger shows $10.4 million (PAHO's $8.7 million included) to foreign central banks and international bodies, against "mostly" needing over $66 million. The Times saw the confidential copy.

## Why: the stated reasons

- The order: creditors' attachment would threaten U.S. aims; the U.S. holds the money "solely in a custodial and governmental capacity" [eo-14373-order-2026-01-09: "solely in a custodial and governmental capacity"].
- The report: the Qatar account let stored oil be sold and kept creditors off the first payments [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "to avoid creditor attachment while holding the initial Venezuelan oil deposit payments"].
- Rubio: "This is simply a way to divide revenue so that there isn't systemic collapse while we work through this recovery and transition" [independent-rubio-sfrc-2026-01-28: "This is simply a way to divide revenue so that there isn’t systemic collapse"].
- Interventions: the bolívars collected fund the state "without printing unbacked currency" [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "enabling the state to meet its budget needs without printing unbacked currency"].

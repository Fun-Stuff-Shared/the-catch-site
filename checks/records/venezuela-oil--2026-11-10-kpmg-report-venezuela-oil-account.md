## The explanations

Question 1: where did the $12.26 billion go?
- A. Into Venezuela's economy for Venezuelans: dollars sold through Venezuelan banks to businesses and people, under U.S. checks (the State Department and its report; an unnamed State official: "The report shows the process is working"). If true: a ledger of payouts to Venezuelan banks and vendors, recipient data. Should not find: large transfers to the U.S. government, or money parked.
- B. To the United States: "we're taking out billions and billions of dollars of oil ... we've paid for the cost of the war many, many times over already" (Trump, November 11; "We take hundreds of billions of dollars of oil", November 2). If true of the account: transfers from it to the U.S. Treasury or agencies. Should not find: nearly all of it paid back to Venezuela. If it refers to oil outside the account (the NABEP barrels, the October "gift" cargo), a priced, billions-scale flow to the U.S. somewhere in the record.
- C. To Venezuela's public wages and pensions: "De cada 100 dólares ... 99 se destinaron al pago de salarios ... y de pensiones" (Delcy Rodríguez, November 12). If true: payroll spending of that size. Should not find: the dollars going to private buyers with none to government bodies, unless the payroll is paid in bolívars.
- D. To a few well-placed Venezuelan firms buying cheap dollars (Ricardo Ríos; Primero Justicia; the Times' earlier reporting). If true: concentration of the dollars among few buyers at a favorable rate. Should not find: wide distribution across many recipients.
- E. Nobody can say: the trail stops at the banks ("It is unclear if the money stayed in the country or who the ultimate recipients were," the Times; Senate Democrats). If true: no recipient-level data. Should not find: a named, recipient-level accounting.
- F. Steered to politically connected U.S. firms (the Senate Democrats' stated risk). If true: large vendor payments to U.S. firms with ties to the administration. Should not find: vendor payments that are a small share of the total.
- G. It keeps the Rodríguez interim government running (Molotov on X: Washington "devolvió el 98,7 %" while keeping the interim government in place; Primero Justicia). If true: the money reaches the state's budget, including through state banks. Should not find: no path from the account to the state's finances.

## The story

The State Department published its first accounting of the account that holds Venezuela's oil money: $12.26 billion came in from January to September, and $12.11 billion went back out at the Venezuelan central bank's request. Almost all of it was sold as dollars to Venezuelan businesses and people through 20 banks, and the bolívars they paid went to the Rodríguez government, which says it spent them on wages and pensions. The report came out as Democrats, who won Congress, press for an accounting the State Department had promised since April. President Trump says the oil has paid for the war many times over; the account sent nothing to the U.S. government, and the report says KPMG's work, paid for with Venezuela's money, is not an audit. Democrats take both chambers on January 3, with the buyers of the dollars still unnamed.

## The plan

Headline: The U.S. took in $12 billion for Venezuela's oil. Nearly all of it went back to Venezuela.

What happened (sentence 1): the event, what it means, where it stands
1. What is the account? Under the January 9 order, buyers of Venezuelan oil, gold, minerals and fertilizer pay into a U.S. Treasury account; the money is Venezuela's, held by the U.S. [eo-14373-order-2026-01-09: "constitute property of the Government of Venezuela"]
2. How much? $12,263,715,308.36 in, $12,107,622,199.55 out, $168,558,474.25 left, January 3 to September 30 [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "$12,263,715,308.36"]
3. Who decides? The central bank asks; State and Treasury approve each payout [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "Any disbursement of funds from the Venezuela Custody Account requires authorization from the DoS and U.S. Treasury"]
4. Where it stands: State posted the report November 12; the Times had a confidential copy November 10.

Where the money went (sentence 2): opens on "where did the $12.11 billion go?"
1. Three dollar channels to 20 banks, $11.96 billion; vendors $133.1 million (figure: the payout ledger as a bar or table).
2. What is a currency intervention? The central bank sells dollars to banks at a rate it sets and takes their bolívars; banks sell the dollars to customers [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "the BCV debits each bank's account for the corresponding VES value"]
3. Who got the dollars? Businesses $9.37 billion, people $2.87 billion; food and consumer goods $3.35 billion first; names not given.
4. Where did the bolívars go? To the state: "to fund public sector payrolls"; Rodríguez says 99 of every 100 dollars paid wages and pensions.
5. What did the $133 million buy? Medicines and PAHO $57.4 million; energy and industry $37.2 million; KPMG, lawyers and banknote logistics $28.8 million; memberships in regional central-bank bodies $1.7 million; passports, market data and embassy repairs $7.9 million (table of the largest lines).
6. Biggest bank? State-owned Banco de Venezuela, $2.88 billion.

The Catch (sentence 4): opens on Trump's November 11 sentence
1. What he said, in full, and what it names (no channel).
2. The account: by his own order Venezuela's; 98.7% paid back at Caracas's request; nothing to a U.S. agency.
3. The barrels outside the account: the "gift" cargo was bought at a discount (Post); the NABEP terms are in the August 31 story (link). No record prices them in billions.
4. One qualifier: the report covers the account only.
Second point, as a row: the Times' "mostly debts to other governments and international organizations" for the $130 million; the public ledger shows $10.4 million to foreign central banks and international bodies.

The audit (sentence 4, second half): opens on "who checked the money?"
1. Kozak told the House in April that KPMG was "an independent auditor" doing quarterly audits (as the members quote him).
2. The report: not an audit; KPMG hired by State with Venezuela's money, paid $18.2 million from the account.

What happens next (sentence 5): Democrats take both chambers January 3; the senators' questions (beneficial owners, the KPMG contract) are open; the report promises later editions; GAO review opened in July (bne; lead). Link the Vitol story for who sold the oil.

Background held: the Qatar phase ($500 million, January 15 payouts of $75 million to four banks); the banknote program after the June earthquakes; the IMF $346 million mixed into recipient data; Citibank's vendor checks before April; deposits fell from $2.44 billion in May to $921 million in July; the 61.5% from two depositors (Vitol story); Question 3's estimates; Murphy's "at gunpoint"; the July 27 AML enforcement pause (senators); Greenberg Traurig's client.

## Why: the stated reasons

- The order: creditors' attachment would threaten U.S. aims; the U.S. holds the money "solely in a custodial and governmental capacity" [eo-14373-order-2026-01-09: "solely in a custodial and governmental capacity"].
- The report: the Qatar account let stored oil be sold and kept creditors off the first payments [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "to avoid creditor attachment while holding the initial Venezuelan oil deposit payments"].
- Rubio: "This is simply a way to divide revenue so that there isn't systemic collapse while we work through this recovery and transition" [independent-rubio-sfrc-2026-01-28: "This is simply a way to divide revenue so that there isn’t systemic collapse"].
- Interventions: the bolívars collected fund the state "without printing unbacked currency" [state-custody-report-2026-11-12: "enabling the state to meet its budget needs without printing unbacked currency"].

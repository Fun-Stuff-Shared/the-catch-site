### Finding — C2 [sig-rule]

**Claim:** “The Sept. 4 memo tells county boards to review the voter's signature on the envelope to make sure it is the name of the voter the ballot was issued to, and if clear evidence shows it is not, the ballot cannot be fixed. The memo also dropped an earlier line saying the envelope signature would not be compared with the voter's signature in their registration record.”

**Record:** `ncsbe-numbered-memo-2021-03-2026-09-04`

The memo identifies as a non-curable deficiency:

> “The voter’s signature on the application is the name of a person other than the voter assigned the application number on the container-return envelope.”

But immediately provides an exception:

> “If the voter’s signature is the same signature as an assistant’s signature on the application, then that is a curable deficiency and staff shall proceed in accordance with the cure process in Section 2 of this memo.” records(20261025-040146)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That every signature determined to belong to someone other than the voter creates a deficiency that cannot be cured. The memo expressly allows a cure when the signature is the same as an assistant’s signature.

The remainder of C2, including the removal of the earlier prohibition on comparing signatures, is supported. records(20261025-040146)

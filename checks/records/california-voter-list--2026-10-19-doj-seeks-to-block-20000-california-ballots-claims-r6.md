### C2 — [proposed-order] — Scope exceeds what the proposed order specifies

**Claim:** “The order DOJ asks for, in a proposed order filed Oct. 21, would require California and LA County to set aside the mail ballots of everyone on the list, confirm each one's citizenship without relying only on the voter's sworn registration, refuse to count the ballots of anyone found not to be a citizen, and process the rest under provisional-ballot rules. It names no deadline.”

**Record:** `doj-proposed-tro-2026-10-21`

**Exact words:**
> “If DEFENDANTS determine that the individual is not a U.S. citizen, DEFENDANTS are HEREBY RESTRAINED from tabulating that person’s vote and ENJOINED to take all steps necessary to ensure the vote-by-mail ballot is not tabulated, counted, and certified as valid.”

> “If the DEFENDANTS determine that the individual is a U.S. citizen, then DEFENDANTS are HEREBY ENJOINED to follow California law to conduct the remaining provisional ballot processing requirements.” records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That every ballot not rejected for confirmed noncitizenship would proceed through provisional-ballot processing. The proposed order directs that processing only for voters affirmatively determined to be U.S. citizens. It establishes no corresponding procedure for voters whose citizenship remains unresolved. Those ballots could remain on hold indefinitely.

The other elements of the claim, including the absence of a citizenship-determination deadline, are supported.

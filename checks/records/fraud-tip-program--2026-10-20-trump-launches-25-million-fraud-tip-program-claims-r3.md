**C3 — [terms-charges-defined]**

**Claim:** “Under the terms, a tip qualifies when it leads the Justice Department to obtain and unseal criminal charges; the department may, at its discretion, pay before the charges are unsealed.”

**Short form:** “Trigger: charges filed and unsealed”

**Record:** `doj-whip-terms-2026-10-20`

> “No reward is payable while the relevant criminal charge or charges remain under seal or otherwise non-public, unless the Department determines in its sole discretion that such a reward should be paid prior to unsealing.” records

**Finding:** The full claim is supported, but the short form makes unsealing sound like an absolute prerequisite. The terms expressly allow the department to pay before unsealing.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That no reward can be issued while criminal charges remain sealed.

**C33 — [mahamud-indicted-may]**

**Claim:** “An indictment against Mahamud was unsealed on May 20, 2026.”

**Short form:** “May 20: indictment unsealed”

**Record:** `cbsmn-2026-05-21`

> “Fahima Mahamud, the daycare's owner, was charged with one count each of wire fraud and conspiracy to defraud the United States through Minnesota's Child Care Assistance Program, or CCAP, according to a federal indictment unsealed Wednesday.” records

**Conflicting record:** `doj-mn-case-summaries-2026`

> “Fahima Mahamud, 50, of Minneapolis, Minnesota, was charged by Information with one count of wire fraud and one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States” records

**Finding:** CBS Minnesota describes an indictment, but the Justice Department's case summary identifies the charging instrument as an *Information*. These are different legal documents. The saved records conflict on the instrument, although they support the May 20 date.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That a grand jury indictment was definitively unsealed on May 20, when the Justice Department identifies an information instead.

**C55 — [law-cap-3m]**

**Claim:** “The reward law the program's terms cite caps any one reward at $3 million, unless the reward is for terrorism or another statute authorizes more.”

**Short form:** “Legal cap: $3 million per reward”

**Record:** `usc-28-530c`

> “no such reward shall exceed $3,000,000, unless—”

The law then provides two exceptions:

> “the reward is to combat domestic terrorism or international terrorism”

> “a statute should authorize a higher amount” records

**Finding:** The full claim correctly identifies both exceptions. The short form presents the $3 million ceiling without qualification.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That federal law categorically prohibits rewards exceeding $3 million, including rewards that qualify for its explicit exceptions.

**C57 — [one-pct-billion-over-cap]**

**Claim:** “One percent of $1 billion, Trump's own example, is $10 million, more than the $3 million cap in the reward law this program's terms rest on, so such an award would need a different statute.”

**Record:** `usc-28-530c`

> “no such reward shall exceed $3,000,000, unless—”

> “the reward is to combat domestic terrorism or international terrorism”

> “a statute should authorize a higher amount” records

**Finding:** The arithmetic and identification of the ordinary statutory ceiling are correct. However, the conclusion that an award exceeding $3 million necessarily requires a different statute is broader than the law supports.

The statute itself provides an exception for rewards combating domestic or international terrorism. It does not establish a categorical requirement for separate statutory authority in every case exceeding $3 million.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That a separate statute is the only possible legal authority for an award exceeding $3 million under 28 U.S.C. § 530C, when the provision also contains a terrorism exception.

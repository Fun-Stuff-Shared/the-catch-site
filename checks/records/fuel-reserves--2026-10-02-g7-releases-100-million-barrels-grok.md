## Claims to check

1. **“G7 is releasing 100 million new barrels, mostly diesel, on top of March.”**  
   Contradicted, or at least not supported, by the IEA October 7 primary: the ~100 million is “stocks that had been pledged but have not yet been released” from March. G7 Oct 2 already said “taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled.” Wright’s Oct 2 post (“the majority diesel”) and the White House fact sheet (“100 million barrels of refined diesel”) go beyond the G7 text I opened, which does not split crude vs diesel and does not say all 100 million is diesel.

2. **“The U.S. threatened a diesel export ban, then Trump said it was never on the table.”**  
   Both statements exist. Reuters Oct 1, three people: administration told France and Germany to release stocks or face a potential ban. Trump Oct 2, on camera per Politico: “It was never really ever on the table.” Macron, in secondary reports I did not re-open, said the discussion was not a threat. Primary public U.S. document of a ban order: not found. House bills to ban exports exist and have not been voted.

3. **“100 million barrels of diesel are en route to the United States.”**  
   Circulated Oct 2 (Eric Daugherty / Brooke Rollins attribution in the site’s prior record). G7 text does not say the barrels are diesel-only or bound for the U.S. France’s Oct 7 10 million barrels are “to distributors in France.” I found no bill of lading or IEA allocation to U.S. ports dated Oct 3–8.

4. **“IEA agreed to accelerate a new 100 million barrel intervention.”**  
   Headline language in several wires. The IEA statement I opened is acceleration and completion of March, plus readiness to release more “if and when required,” with the Governing Board still to meet.

5. **“SPR already awarded the last 40 million barrels on October 6.”**  
   The solicitation deadline is real. I found no award. June’s similar 40-million offer drew about 500,000 barrels (Reuters Sept 29, not re-opened in full this pass). Treating the 40 million as delivered would overstate the record.

6. **Arithmetic 400 − 325 = 75, yet IEA says ~100 million remaining.**  
   IEA itself notes some countries released more than pledged, which is why leftover pledged barrels can still be described as ~100 million. Without the country table, 75 and 100 are different measures (headline pledge vs remaining pledged volumes after over-release). Do not collapse them.

7. **Canada is releasing stockpile barrels.**  
   Canada republished the G7 statement. The Hub, Oct 5, says Canada has no strategic petroleum reserve and will stagger refinery maintenance (Canadian Press / Energy Minister Tim Hodgson spokesperson). I did not open that CP story. March IEA workbook in prior site work listed Canada as extra production, not public stocks.

8. **France’s 10 million barrels are additional to March.**

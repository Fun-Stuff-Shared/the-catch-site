### C2 — Law's enactment is not established by the record

**Claim:** “A 2023 state law, in force from Jan. 1, 2024, lets county commissioners act on any ‘reliable information’ that a voter has moved. Its examples are official mail returned as undeliverable, a homestead exemption filed at another address, and information from another state or county office that the voter no longer lives at the registered address.”

**Record:** `hb1310-2023`

The document identifies itself as:

> “HB1310 (As Sent to Governor) - 2023 Regular Session”

Its final section states:

> “SECTION 9. This act shall take effect and be in force from and after January 1, 2024.”

The document establishes what the legislation proposed and its intended effective date, but does not establish that the governor signed it or that it became law. The provisions concerning reliable information and the listed examples are accurately represented.  

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the cited legislative record establishes HB 1310 was enacted and took effect January 1, 2024. The record alone does not establish enactment.

### C3 — Short form states broader distribution than the record establishes

**Claim:** “For many years Mississippi relied mainly on Postal Service change-of-address data to find voters who had moved. In July 2025 the secretary of state's office announced that county election commissioners would also get Experian's commercial data.”

**Short form:** “July 2025: Experian data to all counties”

**Record:** `sos-experian-release-2025-07-21`

The secretary of state's July 21, 2025, announcement states:

> “Through this new partnership, county election commissioners will not only receive access to the NCOA information, but they will receive reliable commercial data from Experian’s long history as a credit reporting agency.”

The announcement describes future access for county election commissioners. It does not establish that Experian data had already been distributed to every county.

The full claim accurately describes the announcement, but the short form removes the distinction between announced access and completed distribution. records(20261120-004317)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Experian data was distributed to all Mississippi counties in July 2025, rather than that statewide access was announced.

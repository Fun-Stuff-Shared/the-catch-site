## Officials who acted

- **North Carolina State Board of Elections**, 3-2 party-line vote, Sept. 4, 2026. Approved updated Numbered Memo 2021-03 (Absentee Container-Return Envelope Deficiencies) and Numbered Memo 2020-23 (in-person return), the same day civilian absentee ballots began going out. Verified: NCSBE numbered-memo index lists 2021-03 “updated Sept. 4, 2026”; contemporaneous coverage (ABC11, CBS17, Carolina Journal, Democracy Docket). PDF: [https://dl.ncsbe.gov/sboe/numbermemo/2021/Numbered%20Memo%202021-03_Absentee%20Deficiencies.pdf](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/sboe/numbermemo/2021/Numbered%20Memo%202021-03_Absentee%20Deficiencies.pdf). From the memo, signed by Executive Director **Sam Hayes**: “This updated memo was approved by a vote of the State Board and issued on September 4, 2026.” The memo directs county staff to review returned absentee packages within one business day and, if the container-return (inner) envelope is unsealed or the ballot is outside it, spoil and reissue. Signature review: if the name on the application “appears to be the name of the voter assigned the application number … and not some other person, then that is not a deficiency even if the signature is illegible.”

- **Sam Hayes**, NCSBE executive director. Sept. 2, 2026 press release: “Election officials across North Carolina will start mailing absentee ballots out later this week…” and “All registered voters can choose how they wish to cast their ballot: by mail, in person during the 17-day early voting period, or in person on Election Day.” [https://www.ncsbe.gov/news/press-releases/2026/09/02/voting-2026-general-election-begins-week-nc](https://www.ncsbe.gov/news/press-releases/2026/09/02/voting-2026-general-election-begins-week-nc). Oct. 20 X post (account @NCSBE, 14:00 GMT): “We are pleased with yesterday's decision by the U.S. District Court denying preliminary injunction request and allowing the State Board’s absentee voting procedures, which are consistent with statute, to remain in place. See NCSBE Executive Director's full statement below.” [https://x.com/NCSBE/status/2112544436265624058](https://x.com/NCSBE/status/2112544436265624058). Democracy Docket later paraphrased the image as saying the opinion “does not change how they [voters] cast their ballots or the instructions they have already received.” I did not independently OCR the image.

- **Stacy Eggers IV**, Republican board secretary, Sept. 4 meeting. ABC11: “The voter experience in casting their ballots will not change.” “This numbered memo is simply guidance, updated guidance, I might add, as to what the board and staff at the county level will do and how they will process things.” CBS17 (same meeting): “It’s a ballot security issue to make sure that the voter’s will and intent is being followed through.” Also CBS17: counties “need it for this coming election.” Daily Tar Heel: “We have the statutory authority to act by numbered memo, as this board well knows and has done many a time.”

- **Siobhan O’Duffy Millen** (also reported as Siobhan Duffy O’Millen / Siobhan Millen), Democratic board member, Sept. 4. ABC11: “Some of it is putting our numbered memo in alignment with the rules.” “But some of it, I would say, is sneaking in a whole new provision about if Aunt Betty brings in your absentee ballot, they’re going to spoil it.” “Now, to my mind, we’re doing an end run around the process that we said we wanted to do and revising the numbered memo.” Democracy Docket: “I do not understand the need for secrecy.” “It affects whether your ballot counts and that’s huge.” CBS17: “Legislative review has to have a meaning. Legislative review doesn’t mean just rubber-stamping it.”

- **Jeff Carmon**, Democratic board member, Sept. 4. Democracy Docket: “What are we doing here?”

- **Jason Tyson**, NCSBE spokesman, emails and interviews Oct. 21–23. To WUNC: “Preliminary State Board data shows a slight increase in spoiled ballot rates in 2026 (3.1%) compared to 2022 (2.6%) at this stage in each election.” “Ballots can be spoiled for many reasons, including at the request of the voter (damaged ballot, spilled coffee on it, etc.) or for certain deficiencies related to ballot envelope issues, or missing witness, assistant, or notary signatures and information.” To WFAE: “It’s a matter of securing and making sure the voter’s intention is clear.” Follow-up email (WFAE paraphrase, not a quoted example on the page): someone could open an outer envelope, alter a ballot in an unsealed inner envelope, and reseal the outer envelope. To Democracy Docket, as of Oct. 23: “The new absentee ballot rules follow the plain text of state law, which requires ballots to be ‘sealed in the container-return envelope.’” “These new rules provide an added layer of security to help prevent any ballot tampering and have been affirmed by a federal court.” (The inner quotation is the board’s citation of statute.)

- **U.S. District Judge Thomas D. Schroeder**, M.D.N.C., Memorandum Opinion and Order, filed Oct. 19, 2026, Doc. 60, 72 pages. Hearing Oct. 14. “For the following reasons, the motion will be denied.” [https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/mail-in-ballots-nc-preliminary-injunction-order.pdf](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/mail-in-ballots-nc-preliminary-injunction-order.pdf). Verified on the PDF: “The Supreme Court has repeatedly admonished lower courts that they ‘should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election.’” (Carolina Journal and Democracy Docket both quote the next clause: “Here, ‘the election is not merely upcoming; it has already started.’”) “Neither of the two Challenged Provisions, which are clearly stated on the absentee ballot materials — that the voter sign his or her own name and place their ballot in a sealed envelope provided by the county board of election — is burdensome, much less unduly so.” “Plaintiffs’ proposed remedy eliminates any verification of the signature, permitting a voter to sign anyone’s name to the certification, even ‘Mickey Mouse.’” NC Newsline/Courthouse News: “[T]he absentee voter who fails to seal his or her ballot correctly has an opportunity to correct a mistake, and is not barred from participating in early voting or voting on election day.” The order traces the Feb. 2, 2026 declaratory ruling that the 2024 memo’s accept-if-either-envelope-is-sealed instruction was “not correct,” after *Wasserberg et al. v. NCSBE*, 5:24CV00578 (E.D.N.C.), a consent judgment in GOP-initiated litigation.

- **Adam Steele**, NCSBE associate general counsel, Sept. 4 (Carolina Journal summary, not a full transcript): state law requires procedural safeguards by instruction or rulemaking; the board had issued a February declaratory ruling and adopted rules in July that then went to legislative review after objections.

**Related NC / other-jurisdiction actions (not this memo):**

- Formal rules the board adopted in July 2026 were approved by the Rules Review Commission Aug. 27, then delayed for legislative review after objections (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 150B-21.3(b2)). Schroeder’s order: the board said the General Assembly would not meet to approve them until after the 2026 election. The Sept. 4 numbered memo put overlapping procedures in force anyway.

- *Wasserberg* (E.D.N.C. 5:24CV00578): RNC/NCGOP suit against the prior (Democratic-majority) board’s 2024 memo practice of counting ballots if either envelope was sealed; consent judgment required reconsideration.

- *Stein v. Hall*: fight over SB 382 transferring board appointments from the governor to the auditor. Court of Appeals 2-1 for the legislature Sept. 16, 2026; Stein petitioned the N.C. Supreme Court Oct. 22 (No. 325P26). That is the control of the board, not the envelope memo.

ABC11 quoted Forward Justice’s Kat Roblez on Sept. 4 that the blocked executive order meant “For right now there is no change” at the federal postal/mail-ID layer.

## Stated reasons

- **NCSBE / Eggers / Tyson / Feb. 2 declaratory ruling:** statute requires the ballot “sealed in the container-return envelope” (N.C.G.S. § 163-230.1(d)); chain-of-custody / tampering if someone accesses an unsealed inner envelope before the outer is sealed; uniform county processing; numbered-memo authority while formal rules sit in legislative review; photo-ID deficiencies remain curable.

- **Schroeder:** provisions are printed on the materials and not an undue burden; notice-and-reissue or in-person options; Purcell (election already underway); 18-day delay in suing; plaintiffs attack board execution of statutes they do not invalidate; proposed signature remedy would allow “Mickey Mouse.”

- **RNC/NCGOP:** years-long effort to enforce statutory envelope sealing after the prior board counted unsealed inner envelopes; election integrity; voter is not deprived of the franchise because they can recast or vote in person.

- **Stein / Democratic board members / plaintiffs / Common Cause:** last-minute party-line workaround of legislative review; technical errors unrelated to eligibility; burden on older and disabled voters; inner envelope is tight; unequal county application; signature standard is vague; spoil-and-reissue is not a true cure when time is short.

- **Hilary Harris Klein (SCSJ), as reported:** a 2026 statute on discounting certain ballots was written not to take effect until 2027; the memo pulled the practice forward. Treat as reported, not independently verified against the session-law text in this pass.

## Claims to check

1. **“Rejection rate twice as high as in 2022” (Stein, Oct. 20; amplified by DD video and some social posts).** Statewide same-stage NCSBE: 3.1% vs 2.6%, not double. Final 2022 NC rate was 2.2%. National 2022 rate was 1.5%. PolitiFact documented that an early DD version said 3% was double “the state saw” in 2022, then updated to “the entire nation.” Stein’s office cited that DD piece. **Verified mismatch:** doubling is not the statewide same-stage comparison Tyson published the next day.

2. **“Thousands more absentee ballots being rejected this election.”** Through Oct. 20, **fewer** ballots had been rejected than at the same 2022 point (1,349 vs 1,829) because mail volume fell. DD’s 3,942 is a full-election **projection** at 3% of a reduced 2022 base, not a current count. Common Cause’s “thousands … could be spoiled” is likewise conditional.

3. **“Tiny provisions” as an RNC phrase.** Verified in the **NCSBE** opposition brief. DD’s Oct. 19 ruling story attributed it to RNC/NCGOP.

4. **Signature matching vs name check.** Plaintiffs and some coverage describe “signature matching” against file signatures. The 2026 memo text, and Schroeder, describe a **name** check: whether the signature is the voter’s name, presumed valid if it appears to be that name even if illegible, absent “clear evidence” otherwise. WBTV’s Mecklenburg “signing requirements” 250 may mix sealing and signature; WFAE’s 259 is explicitly inner-envelope sealing.

5. **“Received but not counted” ≈ rejected.** WXII’s “nearly 2,000 received but not counted” and Fox Carolina’s county “received but not counted” are not the same as Tyson’s 1,349 rejected. Pending cure, unopened, or not-yet-reviewed ballots can sit in that bucket.

6. **High county rates (7–11% in Mecklenburg, Forsyth, etc.).** Common Cause monitor reports, not NCSBE official reason-coded files. Jones to WUNC: 8–11% in named counties; DD: 7–8% in Mecklenburg and Forsyth. Dickerson’s official Mecklenburg request-spoil rate so far was 3% of requests. Guilford’s director said volume was similar to prior years. **Do not treat monitor county peaks as statewide official rates.**

7. **Inner envelope “only became part of mail voting in 2023” (Change.org petition).** Unverified here. Photo ID with absentee is 2023-era; container-return envelopes are older statutory machinery. Treat as a petition claim.

8. **WXII “over two-thirds” of 156,000 sent ballots unreturned or problematic.** Unreturned is the normal early-cycle pattern (Cohen: 35.7% of applicants had successfully voted by Oct. 23). It is not a 67% rejection rate.

## Competing explanations

Each in the strongest form its holders give, with who holds it.

1. **Disenfranchisement by design (Stein, Common Cause, Elias plaintiffs, Democracy Docket, Rubin, Fuller).** GOP control of the board, after the auditor took appointments, used a same-day numbered memo to enforce a sealing trap the General Assembly had not yet allowed to take effect, targeting a mode Democrats use more, with a tight inner envelope that older and disabled voters fail, and a “cure” that many never complete. Jones: “systematic effort to make voting by mail more difficult.”

2. **Enforcing the statute the prior board had waived (NCSBE majority, RNC, NCGOP, Schroeder, Gruters, Eggers, Tyson).** Law already required a sealed container-return envelope. The 2024 memo’s either-envelope rule was “not correct.” *Wasserberg* forced a revisit. The memo is guidance to counties, not a new voter-facing ritual; instructions already say seal the inner envelope. Integrity and chain of custody justify spoil-and-reissue. Voters can recast or vote in person. Gruters: “commonsense.”

3. **Modest operational uptick, not a doubling (Tyson, PolitiFact, WUNC/WBTV fact frames, Woodhouse).** Spoiled rate is up about half a point at this stage. Mail volume is down. Many spoils are old reasons (witnesses, voter-requested replacement, coffee). Envelope sealing is one slice. Statewide official data contradict “twice” and “thousands more” as current facts.

4. **Purcell / too late to unwind (Schroeder, NCSBE brief).** Voting had started; 31,582 ballots were already cast under the memo by the hearing; reversing rules midstream would confuse voters who already recast. Plaintiffs waited 18 days. Even if merits were closer, an injunction now is the forbidden last-minute change.

5. **Legislative-review workaround, process not substance (Millen, Carmon, Stein Sept. 4, Klein as reported).** The real story is the board implementing objected-to rules by memo after enough objection letters sent the permanent rules to the General Assembly. “End run.” The sealing policy may be defensible as statute; the **timing and vehicle** are the abuse.

6. **County capacity and unequal application (Berg, Jones, plaintiffs’ equal-protection claim).** Wake can staff 24-hour notice; smaller counties may not. Monitor rates vary 2% to 11%. Jones: zip code determines whether the vote counts. Plaintiffs: “state-sanctioned free-for-all” on signatures.

7. **Harmless error / no fraud showing (Fuller, plaintiffs’ Materiality Provision claim).** An unsealed inner envelope inside a sealed, witnessed, signed outer package with ID is not evidence of ineligibility or tampering. Spoil-and-reissue punishes a flap, not a qualification defect.

8. **Mail voting is inherently the fraud vector (some replies under Stein’s Oct. 20 post; not an official NCSBE claim).** Example: users arguing absentee-by-mail should revert to excuse-only. This is a minority public line; it is not the board’s stated rationale.

9. **Volume drop is Trump/COVID reversion, not the memo (Carolina Journal related coverage; Tyson to PolitiFact on COVID plus Trump discouraging mail).** Requests down ~32%. Early **in-person** voting is up sharply (~38% vs 2022 at comparable dates). Holders say the headline “rejected mail” misses that the electorate is moving to in-person.

**Unresolved as of Oct. 24:** official reason-coded statewide spoil file (what share is inner-envelope vs witnesses vs ID vs voter-requested); how many spoiled voters will recast before Nov. 3; whether plaintiffs appeal the PI denial; what the General Assembly does with the delayed permanent rules in 2027.

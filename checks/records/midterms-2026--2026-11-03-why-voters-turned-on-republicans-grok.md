## Stated reasons

* **Trump, November 3, two versions the same night.** To the Journal, the war was "a piece of it," and he would do it again. To Fox Business, "I don't think it's the war, I think it's the gasoline prices." The next day he called Vance "largely" right because the war raised gasoline "short-term," and still said he would do it again to stop an Iranian nuclear weapon.
* **Trump, November 4.** He was not on the ballot, and if he had been, "we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE." On Fox the night of the election, AP reports, he also said the base "didn't like the candidates" he had endorsed because some were not fully loyal.
* **Vance, November 4.** "The reason we lost is the war." The mechanism he offers is persuasion, not turnout: 2024 Trump/Vance voters showed up and voted for Democrats to end the war, focus on the cost of living, "put America First," and change the relationship with Israel.
* **Navarro, November 6–9.** Tariffs "played no role" and "don't create inflation." The loss is not the trade policy.
* **Moore, November 6.** Voters "didn't like" the tariffs and officials know it.
* **Schumer, November 9, and Jeffries, November 4.** Costs, chaos, and corruption. Schumer names gasoline from "a war they never wanted," supermarket prices, tariffs, stock-trading, and the gold renovations. Jeffries: "We ran on affordability. We won on affordability."
* **Ossoff, November 3.** "Depravity, corruption, and incompetence."
* **Johnson, before the vote.** Gas is high because Hormuz is closed, and it would reopen "soon after the election." After the vote he points at the tax cut, spending cuts, and the border as a record voters did not reward.
* **The surveys' own ranking, which is not a politician's claim.** Corruption edges the cost of living as "crucial" (55 vs. 49 on the AP page). The economy is the top national issue (39), then

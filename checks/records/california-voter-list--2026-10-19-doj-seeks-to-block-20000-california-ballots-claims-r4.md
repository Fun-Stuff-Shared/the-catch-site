### C1 — [la-sample-birthplace]

**Claim:** “In the same sample, 38.9 percent of the records that meaningfully reported a birthplace listed the United States, which the county calls strong evidence of citizenship; across all 200, 25.8 percent did.”

**Finding: The percentages are supported, but the denominator and provenance are not fully supported.**

**Record: flores-decl-2026-10-20**

> “Registrar staff reviewed a sample of 200 records from the October List bearing Los Angeles County addresses.”

> “of the voters where birthplace was meaningfully reported, 38.9% indicate that they were born in the United States, which is strong evidence of citizenship” records

**Record: logan-opp-2026-10-20**

> “25.8% of the sampled voters record a place of birth in the United States, a strong indicator of citizenship status.”

The same filing states:

> “86 of the sampled individuals had ballots mailed, whereas, the ballots for the other 112 were not sent because of canceled or inactive status.” records

**What a reader would wrongly believe:**

1. **That 25.8% uses exactly 200 records as its denominator.** The opposition says “sampled voters,” without specifying the denominator. Its ballot figures account for 198 individuals (86 + 112). Mathematically, 25.8% of 198 corresponds to approximately 51 individuals, whereas 25.8% of 200 corresponds to 51.6 individuals. The record does not establish that the percentage applies to all 200 records.

2. **That birthplace information was entered by the registrants themselves.** Neither record establishes who supplied the birthplace information. Both describe what voter records indicated, not the origin of that information.

The 38.9% figure, its restricted denominator, and the county's characterization of U.S. birthplace as strong evidence of citizenship are supported.

The short form, “U.S. birthplace: 25.8% of the sample,” faithfully reflects the opposition's percentage without asserting a specific denominator.

### Findings

**C5 — [five-countries]**

**Claim:** “The rule requires a license to export advanced AI chips to or within Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil and Thailand, except when the ultimate consignee or end user is headquartered in the United States, or has a U.S.-headquartered ultimate parent, and the exporter files reports to BIS.”

**Short form:** “License for shipments to five countries, U.S. firms excepted.”

**Record:** `fr-pi-2026-23529`

**Exact words:** “The license requirements in this paragraph (a)(6)(iii)(A)(1) do not apply to exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) to an ultimate consignee or end user headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, the United States, provided the exporter, reexporter, or transferor follows the reporting requirements in § 743.10.” 

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form implies that U.S. firms are generally exempt from the five-country licensing requirements. The exemption depends on the headquarters of the ultimate consignee or end user, not simply the exporter’s nationality, and requires compliance with BIS reporting requirements. The full claim is supported; its short form overstates the exemption.

**C8 — [existing-90-days]**

**Claim:** “A data center already providing that much computing power to such companies must get a license within 90 days of the rule's effective date to keep doing so, or face future export restrictions.”

**Short form:** “90 days for existing data centers to get a license.”

**Record:** `fr-pi-2026-23529`

**Exact words:** “Any data center providing significant volume of compute power to companies headquartered, or with an ultimate parent headquartered, in Macau or Country Group D:5 prior to the publication date of this rule that wishes to continue providing these services must obtain a license with 90 days of the effective date of this IFR or face future export restrictions.” 

The relevant threshold is “more than 12,000,000 ‘aggregate TPP’ either at one data center or through ‘linked data centers’” for the specified customers. 

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form suggests existing data centers generally face a 90-day licensing deadline. The deadline concerns existing facilities providing the specified volume of computing power to or for companies tied to Macau or Country Group D:5 that wish to continue those services. The full claim preserves these restrictions; the short form does not.

**C10 — [effective-on-publication]**

**Claim:** “The rule takes effect on the day it is published in the Federal Register, which is scheduled for November 19.”

**Short form:** “Nov 19: published, takes effect.”

**Record:** `fr-pi-2026-23529`

**Exact words:**

“This document is scheduled to be published in the Federal Register on 2026-11-19.” 

“Effective date: This rule is effective [INSERT DATE OF PUBLICATION IN THE FEDERAL REGISTER].” 

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form presents publication and effectiveness on November 19 as established events rather than a scheduled publication and an effective date tied to actual publication. As of November 17, neither event has occurred. The full claim accurately distinguishes the effective-date provision from the scheduled publication date.

**C29 — [biden-rule-rescinded]**

**Claim:** “Biden's January 2025 rule restricted remote access to data centers; in May 2025 Trump's Commerce Department said it would not enforce it and would rescind it.”

**Short form:** “May 2025: Biden's rule dropped.”

**Records:** `nyt-loophole-2026-11-16`, `npr-loophole-2026-11-16`, `bis-diffusion-rescission-2025-05-13` and `fr-pi-2026-23529`.

**Exact words:**

The May 13, 2025 BIS announcement states: “BIS plans to publish a regulation formalizing the rescission and will issue a replacement rule in the future.” 

The November 2026 rule states: “Finally, the IFR formally rescinds the regulations implementing the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. The AI Diffusion Rule has never been enforced.” records(20261118-020143)

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** The short form can lead readers to believe that the Biden-era regulation was formally rescinded in May 2025. The record distinguishes the May 2025 announcement of non-enforcement and an intended rescission from the formal regulatory rescission accomplished by the November 2026 interim final rule.

The full claim correctly describes the May announcement. The short form loses that legally significant distinction.

## Officials who acted

- **Donald J. Trump, President.** Memorandum dated October 7, 2026, posted on the White House site and reported on October 9. Verified on the page: he establishes a "committee of inquiry" to investigate allegations that Federal Reserve Governor Lisa D. Cook "made false statements in connection with one or more mortgage instruments." Members are the Assistant to the President for Economic Policy, the Chairman of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, and the Director of the Office of Government Ethics. The committee reports whether there is "cause" for removal under 12 U.S.C. 242, "as construed in *Trump v. Cook*, 146 S. Ct. 2234 (2026)." Hearing: in person at the White House on November 5, 2026, "no longer than 4 hours," "closed to the public but shall be transcribed." Cook "shall appear." She may file a position statement at least three days before and a post-hearing statement by November 10, 2026. The committee then submits findings and a recommendation "as soon as practicable." The Attorney General is directed to publish the memo in the Federal Register. Reason, in his words: "As President, it is my job to ensure the laws are faithfully executed, including by firing subordinates who cannot be trusted to tell the truth and follow the law." https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/establishment-of-a-committee-of-inquiry-to-investigate-allegations-of-false-statements-by-lisa-denell-cook/
- **Same memorandum, named seats, not named in the text.** News organizations identify them as Kevin Hassett (Assistant to the President for Economic Policy), Andrea Lucas (EEOC chair), and Keith Sonderling (acting director of the Office of Government Ethics, also described as Labor secretary). I did not find a public statement from Hassett, Lucas, or Sonderling about serving on the panel. SCOTUSblog, October 9, 2026: https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/10/trump-announces-committee-investigation-of-fed-governor-lisa-cook/
- **Donald J. Trump, August 2025 firing, as recited in the Supreme Court syllabus** (June 29, 2026). The syllabus says that on August 20, 2025, the FHFA director posted a letter accusing Cook of mortgage fraud, and that Trump posted "Cook must resign, now!!!" and told reporters he would "fire her if she doesn't resign." Three days later he "purported to fire Cook for cause," writing that he had "reason to believe" she "may have made false statements on one or more mortgage agreements," that he lacked "confidence in [her] integrity," and that "faithfully executing the law requires [her] immediate removal from office." Those quotations are the Court's account of the complaint exhibits, which I read in the syllabus, not the August 25 letter itself. https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a312_5468.pdf
- **Chief Justice John Roberts, for the Court, *Trump v. Cook*, No. 25A312, decided June 29, 2026, 5–4.** Stay application denied. Verified in the syllabus: "The President failed to afford Cook the procedural protections to which she was entitled by statute. Without such protections, she could not properly dispute the charges the President laid against her." The Court "need not address Cook's constitutional due process argument." Majority: Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Jackson. Dissents: Thomas; Alito joined by Gorsuch; Barrett. SCOTUSblog vote line: https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/trump-v-cook-2/
- **Same opinion, footnote 8, as printed by Justia.** "Like the parties, we read the injunction to forbid the implementation of 'the President's letter of August 25' in which he purported to fire Cook—but not to forbid the President from trying again, if he chooses to do so." Justice Barrett's dissent, also on Justia, says the President "remains free to 'tr[y] again'" if he gives notice and an opportunity to respond, and that the Court "does not rule out mortgage fraud as sufficient cause." https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25a312/
- **D. John Sauer, Solicitor General, oral argument January 21, 2026.** From the search index of the official transcript, which I did not page through: "Deceit or gross negligence by a financial regulator in financial transactions is cause for removal." He said Cook, in a two-week period in 2021, told a lender that within 60 days she would occupy each of two properties "as her principal residence." https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2025/25a312_c0nd.pdf

## Stated reasons

- **Trump, October 7 memorandum:** faithful execution of the laws, "including by firing subordinates who cannot be trusted to tell the truth and follow the law," and a report on whether there is "cause" under 12 U.S.C. 242 as construed in *Trump v. Cook*. White House URL above.
- **Trump, August 25, 2025 letter, via the syllabus:** "reason to believe" she "may have made false statements on one or more mortgage agreements," lack of confidence in her integrity, and a claim that the law required immediate removal.
- **Pulte, August 15, 2025 referral:** apparent falsified residence status on the Ann Arbor and Atlanta loans "to potentially secure lower interest rates and more favorable loan terms."
- **Sauer, January 21, 2026:** deceit or gross negligence by a financial regulator in financial transactions is cause, and the public should not have rates set by someone who was "at best, grossly negligent in obtaining favorable interest rates for herself." Transcript URL above.
- **Roberts opinion:** for-cause protection is real. "Not only the fact of independence but also the appearance of independence is key." The question is whether the assigned cause "truly impl[ies] an unfitness for the place" or is an effort to secure a "more congenial" replacement. The Court did not decide whether these allegations meet that test. "In this opinion, we have not addressed the facts, as they have yet to be found." Justia text: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25a312/
- **Cook's lawyers:** the allegations are untrue, an inadvertent notation is not fraud, and the effort is to make the Fed "bend to his will." NBC and CBS URLs above.
- **Warren and Schumer:** control of the central bank and interest-rate policy, not ethics. Their October 9 statements above.

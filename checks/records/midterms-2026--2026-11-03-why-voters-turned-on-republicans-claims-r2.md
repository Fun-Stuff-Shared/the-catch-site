**C39 — [vance-pollster-question]**

**Claim:** “Vance's pollster asked defecting Trump voters what would cause them to come back; Vance then wrote that those four answers were why many voted for Democrats.”

**Record:** `vance-x-post-2026-11-04`

**Exact words:**
> “Another pollster—one of the smartest people I know in politics, told me yesterday we’d lose the generic ballot by 13 points.”

> “He asked those voters who had abandoned us what would cause them to come back on our team.”

**Problem:** Vance identifies the person as a pollster he knows, not necessarily one employed by him or his campaign. The record supports the polling question and Vance's subsequent interpretation, but does not establish the relationship implied by “Vance's pollster.”

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That the findings came from Vance's own campaign pollster rather than an unidentified pollster who shared results with him. records(1)

**C59 — [rigged-claims]**

**Claim:** “Trump said he would accept the results; claims of a rigged count came from activists such as Katie Miller and Laura Loomer, there was no wave of court challenges, and the FBI called one viral fraud claim false.”

**Record:** `ap-rigged-fact-check`

**Exact words:**
> “After President Donald Trump said he would accept the results of his party’s midterm rout, many of his loyalists followed suit”

> “Most notably, there was no day-after wave of court actions challenging the outcome”

**Additional record:** `usatoday-trump-doubts-2026-11-09`

**Exact words:**
> “President Donald Trump cast doubt on the results of the midterm election, pushing unsubstantiated accusations of rigged election machines in a social media post after previously accepting the blowout defeat for Republicans.”

**Problem:** The AP's account describes the immediate aftermath of November 3, specifically the absence of a *day-after* wave of court challenges. The claim omits that temporal limitation. The implication that election-rigging allegations remained confined to activists is also overtaken by Trump's November 9 accusations.

Trump's initial statement accepting the results is accurately reported. The problem is the broader impression created by presenting the initial response without its temporal scope.

**What a reader would wrongly believe:** That Trump's acceptance continued unchallenged and that the absence of widespread court challenges was established beyond the immediate post-election period. records(1) records(1)

## Competing explanations

Each is the strongest form I found, held by the people named.

- **The war is won, and economics finishes it.** Trump and Rubio. Iran’s forces are “decapitated” and “effectively over.” The blockade, Iron Gate, and Operation Economic Outcast impose enough pain that new strikes are unnecessary. The non-negotiable end is no nuclear weapon. Force returns only if Iran changes the picture. Rubio, November 13: victory “is ensuring that they never have a nuclear weapon.”
- **The same policy, stated as unfinished regime change.** Trump to Hewitt, three days later. “No hesitation.” Control of Hormuz is the reason not to strike today, and the regime still being in power when he leaves would be “a defeat.” “We don’t need a deal” because the money and the strait are already in American hands. This is not a peace explanation. It is a decision to keep squeezing without a negotiation.
- **A Gulf-requested pause while a deal is tested.** Trump on November 9, and a diplomat quoted by Axios and the Financial Times. Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, and Kuwait would rather keep the blockade than resume bombing. Trump will give talks a chance and reverse if Iran is not serious. Qatar’s prime minister’s own line is narrower: the Washington talks were “positive” and aimed at “reaching a deal.”
- **A political retreat dressed up as victory.** Schumer, Jeffries, Van Hollen, Vance’s post-election line, Batmanghelidj, Mortazavi. The midterms punished the war. “Effectively over” lowers the chance of a new bombing campaign without ending the blockade, admitting failure, or getting congressional authorization. Schumer: the public still has not been told “what the heck we’re fighting for,” and the purse is how Congress ends it.
- **Only renewed strikes solve it.** The view attributed to Netanyahu by people speaking to the Financial Times and Axios. Diplomacy leaves the regime and the missile force in place. He has pressed Trump to restart the war and was told, before and after the election, to wait. A second source tells Gulf News this is his preference, not a daily campaign underway now.
- **Washington is bluffing, and Iran is still fighting.** Rezaei, Mohebbi, and the IRGC. The talks are “fake news.” Iron Gate is “merely wordplay” and cannot collapse the economy. The answer is Iran’s seven conditions. Hormuz is closed. Drone attacks on Bahrain are the proof that Iran’s military is not over, and any U.S. strike draws a harder reply. Azizi and Sabet add the strategic version: the midterms vindicated waiting Trump out until 2028, so Tehran will not offer nuclear concessions or move first.
- **A stalemate neither side can call peace.** Kelly, Esfandiary, and the Al Jazeera November 13 explainer. Shooting is limited enough to look like a “virtual ceasefire,” and the political window for a big new campaign has narrowed, but Hormuz, the blockade, the missile inventory, and the nuclear file are open. The blockade is expensive for Washington. Economic pain may not produce capitulation. Al Jazeera’s frame is “no war, no peace,” with the administration divided between Vance’s diplomacy and Trump’s “we don’t need a deal.”
- **The statement is leverage, and the evidence cannot tell which kind.** Eurasia’s odds cut, and the Command Eleven rundown of November 11, which refuses to pick among a real pause, negotiating pressure, or cover for a later strike. The public record they put beside the sentence is the Qeshm strike, a blockade past 100 days, and Iron Gate. https://commandeleven.com/trump-iran-strikes-quite-unlikely-indicators/
- **The legal claim and its rejection.** The administration’s standing position is the May 1 letter: hostilities “have terminated,” so the War Powers clock stopped, and later strikes are Article II self-defense. Massie, Khanna, Kaine, Paul, and CAIR say the blockade, the strikes, and the forces offshore are still hostilities, Congress never authorized them, and the “terminated” letter is wordplay. A resolution did pass both chambers in June; Reuters says the administration ignored it and that lawyers do not agree it was binding. I found no new presidential report since November 3 that would restart or update that argument.

The contradiction inside the U.S. position is in the president’s own words, three days apart. On November 9 the war was “effectively over” and strikes were “quite unlikely.” On November 12 he said “No hesitation,” called regime survival a defeat, and said “We don’t need a deal.” The forces, the sanctions, and the blockade did not change in between.

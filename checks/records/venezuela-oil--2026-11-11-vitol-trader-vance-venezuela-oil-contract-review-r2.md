### 2. The Libya precedent does not establish that Washington previously selected oil marketers without competition

**What is missing or wrong:** The record correctly establishes that OFAC named Vitol and Qatar Petroleum in its April 2011 Libya authorization.

OFAC's April authorization permitted U.S. persons to transact with the named companies. It did not establish that Washington had selected those companies to market Libya's oil.

**Why it changes the story:** Question 3 treats the Libya precedent as supporting the administration's explanation that naming particular trading firms was routine.

It supports that explanation only as a matter of sanctions licensing. It does not establish a precedent for the U.S. government choosing another country's commercial oil-marketing counterparties without a tender.

The distinction matters because the challenged decision is not simply OFAC's issuance of a license. It is Washington's involvement in choosing who received the initial commercial opportunity.

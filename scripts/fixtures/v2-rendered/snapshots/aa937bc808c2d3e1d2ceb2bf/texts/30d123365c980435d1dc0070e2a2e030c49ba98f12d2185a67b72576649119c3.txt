USCIS’ Role in Determining Immigration Status
10. USCIS is the federal agency that oversees lawful immigration into the
United States. In this role, USCIS is responsible for adjudicating applications for
naturalization, lawful permanent residence, citizenship by operation of law, and other
immigration benefits.
11. In the course of its work, USCIS determines the immigration status of
individuals and whether those individuals are in fact United States citizens.
12. I submit this declaration to outline how USCIS identifies potential
noncitizens who appear on voter rolls.
USCIS’ Role in Federal Election Integrity
13. As part of its charge to oversee lawful immigration, USCIS receives state
voter rolls or information on state voter rolls from DHS to review for potential aliens
who unlawfully registered to vote or who voted in a federal election. USCIS conducts
extensive data analysis of these rolls and information to identify and match such
individuals. USCIS then refers certain matched individuals to Homeland Security
Investigations (HSI).
How USCIS Processes Alien or Noncitizen Voter Records
14. When presented with information on voter rolls from agencies like DHS,
USCIS processes that information to identify registered voters who are likely aliens.
15. This process is distinct from the Systematic Alien Verification for
Entitlements (SAVE) program.
16. The voting records USCIS receives contain information about the listed
individual such as name, mailing address, date of birth or age, social security number,
phone number, email, registration information, voter-history information, and more.
USCIS enhances that voter data through a reliable third-party data analytics and

investigative platform. The raw voting record data are combined with the enhanced data
and then paired with an internal USCIS alien number that may be the same individual.
USCIS then scores and ranks the potential identity match. The matches are scored and
ranked because the resulting data may be incomplete, outdated, or mismatched and the
potential matches need to be prioritized for manual review. The alien number may
include further identifying information like aliases.
17. Following the preliminary identity match, USCIS screens all potential
matches against Department of State and Social Security Administration data to remove
individuals who can be identified as United States citizens or as deceased and excludes
them from any further review.
18. USCIS then consolidates all match evidence and assigns a confidence level
for each match. A high confidence match means the match between the voting record
and USCIS alien record is strong and consistent with multifield evidence supporting a
strong identity link. At this point, USCIS has completed its initial review.
19. Based on the results of the initial review, FDNS investigators manually
review the matches to confirm the identity match and determine which registered voters
they believe are very likely to be aliens based on USCIS’ citizenship data. Part of the
FDNS investigation includes verifying whether the registered voter could have
potentially derived citizenship based on history or through any parents. Investigators also
confirm the match with U.S. Department of State systems to identify if any documents
(like passports) may not have been linked in USCIS systems, but which bear on the
individual’s citizenship status. A second-level review of the match and citizenship status
determination occurs as an additional safeguard.
20. After completion of that multilevel review, USCIS makes a final
determination on which matches to send to HSI for further assessment. After its
investigation, HSI may ask USCIS to perform additional manual review of the relevant

Evidence that Noncitizens in California Are Registered to Vote in California
21. On or around May 8, 2026, FDNS received a copy of the California Voter
rolls from HSI to review. The list referenced in Mr. McLoughlin’s declaration is the
same list FDNS received. See McLoughlin Decl. ¶ 20-21. I also understand that
California automatically mails vote-by-mail ballots to participate in a federal election to
anyone registered on California voter rolls. See 2021 Cal. Stat. ch. 312 (codified at Cal.
Elec. Code § 3000.5).
22. FDNS took that list and put it through the process identified above to
produce the most accurate results of likely noncitizens to whom California law requires a
vote by mail ballot to be sent for the November 2026 federal election. In particular,
FDNS analyzed the original file and then enhanced the data with third-party data. FDNS
then manually reviewed the data with immigration officials who are trained to determine
citizenship status. After those steps, FDNS compiled a refined list of individuals who the
agency believes are highly likely to be noncitizen aliens registered to vote in the 2026
federal election in California and then FDNS provided HSI with the refined list of
individuals. That list includes individuals described in Mr. McLoughlin’s declaration.
See McLoughlin Decl. ¶ 24. I understand that the California Secretary of State and Los
Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk were provided that list on October 13,
2026. Id. and Exhibit A.
23. USCIS, along with FDNS, continue to analyze additional potential
noncitizen voter records through the above-mentioned multilevel review process. For
example, USCIS has identified a separate batch of more than 17,000 voting records
potentially tied to noncitizens after an initial review of voter rolls. Historically speaking,
most voters in this initially flagged population will end up being referred to HSI after
USCIS completes its manual review. Our review is ongoing as USCIS receives records
on a rolling basis before the election date. USCIS intends to supplement the record with
additional information of likely noncitizen voting in the federal election. USCIS also

Conclusion
24. USCIS and FDNS receive bona fide information of individuals on voter
rolls to determine the likely citizenship status of those registered voters. USCIS then
processes those voter rolls to identify whether the registered voters are likely
noncitizens. Those processes resulted here in a culled list of persons who are likely to be
noncitizens and who registered to vote in the upcoming 2026 federal election. USCIS
sent that list to HSI for further investigation. My understanding is that list was delivered
to the California Secretary of State and Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County
Clerk on October 13, 2026. See McLoughlin Decl. ¶ 24 and Exhibit A.
25. Based on my training, experience, understanding of California law, the
above-described systems, and collaboration with HSI, I reasonably believe that the
individuals in the list presented to California (1) registered to vote in California, (2) were
sent a vote-by-mail ballot, and (3) are highly likely to be noncitizen aliens.
26. The statements made in this Declaration are based on the knowledge

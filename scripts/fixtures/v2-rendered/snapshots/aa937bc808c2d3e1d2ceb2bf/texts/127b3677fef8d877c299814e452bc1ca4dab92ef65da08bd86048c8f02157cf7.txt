Scopes Calibration Index: Oversight Log | Scopes

The governed, append-only record of how the index and its studies are run: every methodology version, every pre-registration logged before its outcome is known, and every correction, appended and dated. Rules are published in advance from a timestamped record, so a past value is never re-cut; corrections are appended, not overwritten. Each linked page is archived to the Wayback Machine, and the market record it reads from is fingerprinted daily at scopes.com/integrity.

Ohio Governor 2026 added to the Poll desk
The 2026 Ohio governor race joins the Poll desk as a two-venue cross-market: the Kalshi party binary (Ramaswamy for the Republicans, Acton for the Democrats) and the Polymarket party-win market, which price the same outcome. Gemini lists no Ohio governor market, so there is no third lane (Ohio sits between the Kalshi-only California and Wisconsin races and the three-lane Texas and Oregon races). As with every governor race there is no external benchmark, so the fair value is the Scopes Poll Average: the rules-based median of qualifying public polls (sample of at least 400, named matchup, unsponsored, one poll per pollster), published only once at least three distinct pollsters qualify. No other race or published value was changed.
Fraudulent Wedgewood Polling inputs removed from the Poll Average
Wedgewood Polling publicly admitted in October 2026 that it was a fictional operation that had published fabricated election surveys. Every Wedgewood input was removed from the Scopes Poll Average and the affected races recomputed. Georgia Senate 2026 continues to clear the publication gate (three or more distinct pollsters) on its remaining polls. Kansas Senate 2026 falls below the gate once Wedgewood is removed, so its poll-based fair value is withheld, with the remaining qualifying inputs still shown, until another pollster qualifies. The Poll Average is rules-based (objective inclusion filters, one poll per pollster, the median of two-party margins): no other input or published value was changed, and this removal is recorded here rather than overwritten.
Scorecard evidence page published; the significance test named
A method page now documents the scorecard number end to end: what one resolved divergence is (with a worked example), what is counted and what is set aside broken out by the rule that removed it and its effective date, how ties and stale prices and unresolved events are handled, the significance test (a two-sided exact binomial against p = 0.5) with its current sample, win share, 95% interval and p-value, why the scored set is not chosen after the fact, and a published CSV with a recipe that recomputes the headline to the row. Every figure is read live from the same record the scorecard and homepage read; none is hand typed. The significance claim wherever it appears now names the test and the sample ("statistically significant at the 95% level (binomial test, n = N)") and carries a one-sentence limitation: the test compares the market against the Scopes Fair Value on flagged divergences only and does not establish whether prediction markets are well calibrated in general. The scorecard computation, the record, and the daily digests are unchanged; this documents the number, it does not alter it.
Institutional entry points and pilot offer published
The institutions page opens with three doors (a reference mark for a contract at a date; how a venue or a series performs; the data in your systems) and a "Start with a pilot" section: the Series Market Quality offer (three series, thirty days, from $20,000, credited against an annual monitor begun within sixty days) with an example deliverable computed from the resolved record and labeled as such, never a sample client. The inquiry form records a conflicts-register entry for review and flags any inquiry from a venue Scopes measures, or an affiliate, before engagement. A build check bars customer-name claims and client logo displays until a named client consents in writing. No published value, benchmark, or scorecard count changed.
Pricing states introduced; self-serve API tiers retired
Every price on the site now reads from one availability table in three states: available now, by engagement, and licensed per client. A CI check asserts that no price outside that table, and no unavailable-style label, appears on any public page. The self-serve paid API tiers ($249, $649, $1,200) and their prepaid credit packs are retired in favor of API access licensed per client: their checkout was already gated off, the Stripe prices are archived, and any existing key is honored to the end of its term. The $249 research key is closed, replaced by the $49 subscriber plan or a license conversation. No published value, benchmark, or scorecard count changed.
Polymarket US (a CFTC-designated contract market, separate from global Polymarket, with its own books) and Gemini (Gemini Titan) are collected through their public market interfaces, without an account. Rights are set per venue: Polymarket US is treated like Polymarket, while Gemini market data is used under the Gemini Market Data Agreement, which bars its use in any benchmark, reference rate or index and bars redistribution. Gemini is therefore excluded from the benchmark families, shown with the attribution "Data provided by Gemini", and kept out of the published divergence aggregate. Crypto contracts are collected and labeled but left unscored until a crypto desk and its fair value are defined. A new venue enters the scorecard and the index only after it clears the existing evidence gate for a series. No past value was changed.
Desk names simplified; "Scope" reserved for the last-hour object
Each desk now goes by its plain name (Rates, Polls, Sports, Energy, Stocks) rather than the "Rate Scope" form. The word "Scope" is reserved for a forthcoming object: the last hour of a market before it resolves. A naming change only, with no effect on any published value, benchmark or scorecard count.
